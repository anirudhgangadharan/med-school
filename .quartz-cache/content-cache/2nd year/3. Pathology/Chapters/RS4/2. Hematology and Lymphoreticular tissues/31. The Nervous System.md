### 1. Classification of CNS Tumours

CNS tumours can be classified based on their cell of origin:

- **I. Tumours of Neuroglia (Gliomas)**
    - Astrocytoma (including Glioblastoma Multiforme)
    - Oligodendroglioma
    - Ependymoma
    - Choroid Plexus Papilloma
- **II. Tumours of Neurons**
    - Neuroblastoma
    - Ganglioneuroblastoma
    - Ganglioneuroma
- **III. Tumours of Neurons and Neuroglia**
    - Ganglioglioma
- **IV. Poorly-differentiated and Embryonal Tumours**
    - Medulloblastoma
    - Neuroblastoma
    - PNET (Primitive Neuroectodermal Tumour)
- **V. Tumours of Meninges**
    - Meningioma
    - Meningeal Sarcoma
- **VI. Nerve Sheath Tumours**
    - Schwannoma (Neurilemmoma)
    - Neurofibroma
    - Malignant Nerve Sheath Tumour
- **VII. Other Primary Intraparenchymal Tumours**
    - Haemangioblastoma
    - Primary CNS Lymphoma
    - Germ Cell Tumours
- **VIII. Miscellaneous Tumours**
    - Malignant Melanoma
    - Craniopharyngioma
    - Pineal Cell Tumours
    - Pituitary Tumours
- **IX. Tumour-like Lesions**
- **X. Metastatic Tumours**

### 2. Difference in CSF findings in Pyogenic Meningitis and Tuberculous Meningitis

|Feature|Acute Pyogenic (Bacterial) Meningitis|Chronic (Tuberculous) Meningitis|Source(s)|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Naked eye appearance**|**Cloudy or frankly purulent**|**Clear or slightly turbid**, forms fibrin coagulum on standing||
|**CSF pressure**|**Elevated (above 180 mm water)**|**Elevated (above 300 mm water)**||
|**Cells**|**1,000-10,000 neutrophils/µl**|**100-1000 mononuclears/µl** (lymphocytes)||
|**Proteins**|**Markedly raised**|**Raised**||
|**Glucose**|**Markedly reduced** (usually less than 40 mg/dl)|**Reduced** (usually less than 45 mg/dl)||
|**Bacteriology**|**Causative organisms present** (Gram's stain/culture)|**Tubercle bacilli present** (ZN staining)||

### 3. Berry Aneurysm

- **Definition:** Small dilatations affecting the **Circle of Willis** at the base of the brain.
- **Morphology/Clinical Relevance:**
    - Rupture is common and leads to **haemorrhage throughout the subarachnoid space**.
    - Causes a **rise in intracranial pressure** and produces **characteristic blood-stained CSF**.
    - Can lead to an **intracerebral haematoma** if blood tracks into the brain parenchyma.
    - Infarction in the affected arterial region may occur due to **vasospasm**.

### 4. Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM)

- **WHO Grade:** **Grade IV Astrocytoma**.
- **Etiology/Pathogenesis (Molecular Genetic Features of High-Grade Astrocytomas):**
    - Overexpression of **platelet-derived growth factor-a (PDGF-a)**.
    - Mutation of **p53**.
    - Mutation of two tumour-suppressor genes: **RB gene** and **p16**.
    - Chromothripsis (chromosome shattering) is found in up to **25% of gliomas**.
- **Gross Features:**
    - **Variegated appearance** with grey-white and yellow soft areas.
    - Foci of **haemorrhages and necrosis**.
    - **Distorts and infiltrates** the surrounding normal brain tissue.
    - Poorly defined, irregular appearance.
- **Microscopic Features:**
    - **Highly anaplastic and cellular appearance**.
    - Marked variation in cell types, including **fusiform cells**.
    - Characteristically, areas of **necrosis surrounded by a pseudopalisade layer of anaplastic tumour cells**.
    - May contain **multinucleate tumour giant cells**.

### 5. Oligodendroglioma

- **Gross Features:**
    - **Well-circumscribed**, grey-white and gelatinous mass.
    - Often has **cystic areas**.
    - May show foci of **haemorrhages and calcification**.
- **Microscopic Features:**
    - Composed of **uniform cells with round to oval nuclei**.
    - Characteristically surrounded by a **clear halo of cytoplasm** and well-defined cell membranes ("fried egg" appearance).
    - Tumour cells tend to cluster around native neurons, a feature called **satellitosis**.
    - Presence of varying degrees of **endothelial cell hyperplasia**.
    - Foci of **calcification** are typical.
    - Anaplastic changes can occur.

### 6. Schwannoma (Neurilemmoma)

- **Gross Features:**
    - **Firm, encapsulated**, and tan-translucent on cut surface.
    - Typically a **solid, sometimes cystic tumour**.
    - Produces **eccentric enlargement of the nerve root** from which it arises.
- **Microscopic Features:**
    - Composed of **fibrocellular bundles** forming a whorled pattern.
    - Consists of two main patterns:
        - **Antoni A areas:** Densely cellular and compact.
        - **Antoni B areas:** Loosely cellular and myxomatous.
    - Characteristic **nuclear palisading** forming **Verocay bodies**.
- **Clinical Relevance:**
    - Generally **solitary**.
    - Can occur in **cerebellopontine angle** (affecting the vestibular branch of the 8th nerve) or extradural sites.
    - Bilateral schwannomas are associated with **Neurofibromatosis type 2** (NF2, chromosome 22 disorder).
    - **Invariably benign** behaviour.

### 7. Verocay Bodies

- **Definition:** Characteristic structures found in **Schwannomas (Neurilemmomas)**.
- **Morphology:** They are formed by **nuclear palisading**, where the nuclei of tumour cells are arranged in parallel rows.
- **Significance:** A **pathognomonic microscopic feature** for diagnosing Schwannomas.
