1. Hashimotos thyroiditis
	**HASHIMOTO'S THYROIDITIS** _(Also known as Diffuse Lymphocytic Thyroiditis, Struma Lymphomatosa, Goitrous Autoimmune Thyroiditis)_
	
	**1. Definition & Key Features:**
	
	- A **chronic autoimmune disease** of the thyroid gland.
	- Characterised by a triad:
	    1. **Diffuse goitrous enlargement** of the thyroid.
	    2. **Lymphocytic infiltration** of the thyroid gland.
	    3. Presence of **thyroid autoantibodies**.
	- **Most common cause of goitrous hypothyroidism** in iodine-sufficient regions.
	
	**2. Epidemiology:**
	
	- **Predominantly affects females** (approx. 10-fold higher incidence).
	- Commonly seen between **30-50 years** of age.
	- Accounts for about **half of adolescent goitre cases**.
	- Higher incidence in regions with more iodine intake.
	
	**3. Aetiology & Pathogenesis (Autoimmune Basis):**
	
	- **Genetic Predisposition**:
	    - Familial occurrence.
	    - Associated with **HLA-DR3 and HLA-DR5 subtypes**.
	    - Reported association with **CTLA4**, a T-cell regulatory gene.
	- **Immune Destruction**:
	    - Involves initial activation of **CD4+ T helper cells**.
	    - Leads to infiltration of **CD8+ T cytotoxic cells** into thyroid parenchyma.
	    - **B cells are activated**, producing autoantibodies that destroy thyroid tissue.
	- **Key Autoantibodies**:
	    - **Thyroid microsomal autoantibodies** (against follicular cell microsomes).
	    - **Thyroglobulin autoantibodies**.
	    - **TSH receptor autoantibodies**: Primarily **inhibitory**, leading to hypothyroidism; rarely, may stimulate, causing alternating hypo/hyperthyroidism episodes.
	- **Associations**: Frequently seen with other autoimmune diseases (e.g., Graves' disease, SLE, Sjögren's syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis, Type 1 DM).
	
	**4. Clinical Features:**
	
	- **Painless, firm, moderately enlarged thyroid gland (goitre)**.
	- Typically presents with **hypothyroidism** (↓ T3/T4, ↓ RAIU).
	- **"Hashitoxicosis"**: A small percentage may initially present with a transient hyperthyroid phase.
	- **Risk of Malignancy**:
	    - **No increased risk of thyroid carcinoma**.
	    - However, there is an **increased frequency of malignant lymphoma** in these cases.
	- **Variant**: Subacute lymphocytic (painless or silent or postpartum) thyroiditis, often seen 3-6 months postpartum.
	
	**5. Morphological Features:**
	
	- **Gross Appearance (Classic Form)**:
	    - **Diffuse, symmetric, firm, and rubbery enlargement** of the thyroid (100-300 gm).
	    - Cut surface is **fleshy** with accentuated normal lobulations; gland retains its normal shape.
	- **Gross Appearance (Fibrosing Variant)**:
	    - Firm, enlarged thyroid with compression of surrounding tissues (less common, ~10% of cases).
	- **Microscopic Appearance (Classic Form)**:
	    - **Extensive lymphocytic, plasma cell, immunoblast, and macrophage infiltration**.
	    - Formation of **lymphoid follicles with prominent germinal centres**.
	    - **Atrophic thyroid follicles**, often lacking colloid.
	    - Presence of **Hurthle cells** (also called Askanazy cells, oxyphil cells, or oncocytes):
	        - Transformed follicular epithelial cells with **abundant oxyphilic/eosinophilic granular cytoplasm** (due to numerous mitochondria).
	        - Contain **large, bizarre nuclei**.
	    - Slight **fibrous thickening of the septa** separating thyroid lobules.
	- **Microscopic Appearance (Fibrosing Variant)**:
	    - Considerable **fibrous replacement** of thyroid parenchyma and a less prominent lymphoid infiltrate.
2. Thyroid malignancies
	**THYROID MALIGNANCIES**
	
	Primary thyroid cancers are predominantly of **follicular epithelial origin**, with a few arising from parafollicular C-cells. They are **twice as common in women**.
	
	---
	
	**1. GENERAL FEATURES**
	
	- Approximately **95% of all primary thyroid cancers are carcinomas**.
	- The **most common benign thyroid neoplasm is a follicular adenoma**.
	- Thyroid cancer has **4 major morphological types** with distinct clinical behaviours and prevalence:
	    - **Papillary carcinoma** (most frequent, best prognosis)
	    - **Follicular carcinoma**
	    - **Medullary carcinoma**
	    - **Undifferentiated (Anaplastic) carcinoma** (least common, most malignant)
	
	**2. AETIOLOGY & PATHOGENESIS**
	
	- **Iodine Excess and TSH**: In regions with widespread endemic goitre, iodine supplementation has been linked to an **increase in papillary cancer incidence**. Well-differentiated thyroid cancers often express TSH receptors and respond to T4 suppression of TSH.
	- **Genetic Basis**: Thyroid carcinoma is a multistep process involving genetic alterations, with distinct mutations for each type.

**4. OTHER THYROID MALIGNANCIES**
	- **Primary Lymphomas of Thyroid**: Comprise **less than 5%** of thyroid cancers and majority may evolve from **autoimmune (lymphocytic) thyroiditis**.
	- **Sarcomas**: Extremely rare.
	- **Metastatic Tumours**: About 20% of patients dying of metastasising malignancy have metastatic deposits in the thyroid gland, most commonly from **malignant melanoma, renal cell carcinoma, and bronchogenic carcinoma**.

**THYROID CARCINOMAS**

Primary thyroid cancers predominantly originate from **follicular epithelial cells**, with a smaller proportion arising from **parafollicular C-cells**. They are typically **twice as common in women**.

**1. PAPILLARY THYROID CARCINOMA (PTC)**

- **Frequency:** **75-85%** of all thyroid carcinomas (most common type).
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Occurs at **all ages**, including children and young adults, but incidence increases with advancing age.
    - **3 times more frequent in females** (Female/male ratio 3:1).
- **Aetiology/Pathogenesis:**
    - **Strong positive correlation with radiation exposure** (maximum relation to radiation).
    - **Genetic alterations**:
        - **RET gene overexpression** (mutation in RET gene on chromosome 10q) in ~20% of cases.
        - **NTRK1 gene rearrangement** (neurotrophic tyrosine kinase receptor 1 on chromosome 1q) in 5-10% of cases.
- **Clinical Presentation:**
    - Typically **slow-growing**.
    - Most often presents as an **asymptomatic solitary nodule**.
    - First presentation may be **cervical lymphadenopathy** due to spread to regional lymph nodes.
    - "**Lateral aberrant thyroid**" refers to well-differentiated metastasis of an occult PTC in a lateral cervical lymph node.
- **Gross Morphology:**
    - Ranges from microscopic foci to nodules up to 10 cm.
    - Generally **poorly delineated**.
    - Cut surface: **greyish-white, hard and scar-like**.
    - May transform into a **cyst with projecting papillae** (papillary cystadenocarcinoma).
- **Microscopic Morphology (Pathognomonic features are KEY):**
    - **Papillary pattern**: Papillae with fibrovascular stalks covered by a single layer of tumour cells. Often accompanied by follicles.
    - **Characteristic nuclear features**:
        - **Ground glass or optically clear appearance** (due to dispersed nuclear chromatin).
        - **Nuclear grooves** (often referred to as 'Orphan Annie eye' nuclei, though not explicitly named this in source).
        - **Prominent nucleoli**.
        - **Overlapping nuclei**.
    - **Psammoma bodies**: Small, concentric, calcified spherules found in ~50% of cases in the stroma.
    - Tumour cells have **clear or oxyphilic cytoplasm**.
- **Spread:**
    - **Common to regional lymph nodes**.
    - Invasion of capsule and intrathyroid lymphatics common, but **vascular invasion is rare**.
    - **Distant metastases are rare**.
- **Prognosis:** **Excellent**; 10-year survival rate is **80-95%**, regardless of pure papillary or mixed papillary-follicular histology.

**2. FOLLICULAR THYROID CARCINOMA (FTC)**

- **Frequency:** **10-20%** of all thyroid carcinomas (second most common).
- **Epidemiology:**
    - More common in **middle and old age**.
    - **Female preponderance** (Female/male ratio 2.5:1).
- **Aetiology/Pathogenesis:**
    - Positive correlation with **endemic goitre**.
    - Role of external radiation is unclear.
    - **Genetic alterations**:
        - **RAS family of oncogenes mutations** (HRAS, NRAS, KRAS) in ~50% of cases.
        - **PAX-8 and PPARγ-1 fusion-translocation**.
- **Clinical Presentation:**
    - Presents as a **solitary nodule** or an irregular, firm, nodular thyroid enlargement.
    - **Slow-growing**, but more rapid than papillary carcinoma.
- **Gross Morphology:**
    - May be a **solitary, circumscribed, adenoma-like nodule** or an obvious irregular cancerous enlargement.
    - Cut surface: grey-white with areas of haemorrhages, necrosis, and cyst formation. May extend to involve adjacent structures.
- **Microscopic Morphology (Diagnosis relies on invasion):**
    - **Follicular pattern**: Composed of follicles of various sizes; may show trabecular or solid patterns.
    - Tumour cells have **hyperchromatic nuclei** and cytoplasm resembling normal follicular cells.
    - Variants include **clear cell type and Hurthle cell (oxyphilic) type**.
    - **Differs from papillary carcinoma** in lacking papillae, ground-glass nuclei, and psammoma bodies.
    - **Pathognomonic microscopy**: **Vascular invasion** (into the capsule or vessels) and **direct extension** to adjacent structures are the significant features for diagnosis.
- **Spread:**
    - **Regional lymph node metastases are rare**.
    - **Distant metastases by haematogenous route are common**, especially to the **lungs and bones**. This may be the presenting feature.
- **Prognosis:** Intermediate prognosis; 10-year survival rate is **50-70%**.

**3. MEDULLARY THYROID CARCINOMA (MTC)**

- **Frequency:** **5%** of thyroid carcinomas.
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Equally common in **men and women** (Female/male ratio 1:1).
    - Common in **middle to old age**; familial cases also occur.
- **Aetiology/Pathogenesis:**
    - Arises from **parafollicular C-cells**.
    - **Genetic alteration**: **Point mutation in RET-protooncogene** seen in both familial (MEN2) and sporadic cases.
    - Familial cases generally have **C-cell hyperplasia** as a precursor lesion.
- **Distinctive Features:** Familial occurrence, secretion of calcitonin and other peptides, and amyloid stroma.
- **Clinical Presentation:**
    - Most present as a **solitary thyroid nodule**.
    - May first manifest as an **enlarged cervical lymph node**.
    - **Secretion of calcitonin** (hypocalcaemic hormone) and other peptides (e.g., prostaglandins, histaminase, somatostatin, VIP, ACTH).
    - Hormone elaborations can cause paraneoplastic syndromes like **carcinoid syndrome, Cushing's syndrome, and diarrhoea**.
    - Generally **slow-growing**.
- **Gross Morphology:**
    - Unilateral solitary nodule (sporadic) or bilateral and multicentric (familial).
    - Cut surface: **firm to hard, grey-white to yellow-brown** with areas of haemorrhages and necrosis.
- **Microscopic Morphology (Pathognomonic features are KEY):**
    - **Organoid pattern**: Nests of tumour cells separated by fibrovascular septa, similar to other neuroendocrine tumours. May also be arranged in sheets, ribbons, pseudopapillae, or small follicles.
    - Tumour cells are uniform, with structural and functional characteristics of C-cells; may be spindle-shaped.
    - **Amyloid stroma**: Present in most cases, stains positively with **Congo red** (shows apple-green birefringence under polarizing microscopy). Derived from altered calcitonin (prohormone).
    - Amyloid deposits may have irregular calcification, but **no regular lamellations seen in psammoma bodies**.
- **Spread:**
    - **Regional lymph node metastases may occur**.
    - **Distant organ metastases are infrequent**.
- **Prognosis:** Better in familial form than sporadic form; overall 10-year survival rate is **60-70%**.

**4. ANAPLASTIC (UNDIFFERENTIATED) THYROID CARCINOMA**

- **Frequency:** **Less than 5%** of all thyroid cancers.
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Predominantly found in **old age** (7th-8th decades).
    - Slightly more common in females (Female/male ratio 1.5:1).
- **Definition:** One of the **most malignant tumours in humans**.
- **Aetiology/Pathogenesis:**
    - May arise from **further dedifferentiation of differentiated papillary or follicular thyroid carcinoma**.
    - **Genetic alterations**: Inactivating **point mutation in p53 tumour suppressor gene** or mutation in gene coding for β-catenin pathway.
- **Clinical Presentation:**
    - **Widely aggressive and rapidly growing**.
    - Features at presentation typically involve **extensive invasion of adjacent soft tissue, trachea, and oesophagus**, leading to dyspnoea, dysphagia, and hoarseness.
- **Gross Morphology:**
    - Generally **large and irregular**, often invading adjacent strap muscles of the neck and other structures.
    - Cut surface: **white and firm** with areas of necrosis and haemorrhages.
- **Microscopic Morphology (Highly undifferentiated):**
    - Too poorly-differentiated to be placed in other histologic types.
    - May show a component of papillary or follicular carcinoma in better differentiated areas.
    - Composed of **3 types of cells** (occurring in varying proportions):
        1. **Small cell carcinoma**: Closely packed small cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and numerous mitoses; closely resembles malignant lymphoma.
        2. **Spindle cell carcinoma**: Spindle cells resembling sarcoma; may contain sarcomatous components (e.g., osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma).
        3. **Giant cell carcinoma**: Highly anaplastic giant cells with numerous atypical mitoses, bizarre and lobed nuclei, some assuming spindle shapes.
- **Spread:**
    - Metastasises to **regional lymph nodes**.
    - Metastasises to **distant organs** (e.g., lungs).
- **Prognosis:** **Worst prognosis**; 10-year survival rate is **5-10%** (median survival ~2 months).
