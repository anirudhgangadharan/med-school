### 1. Paget's Disease of Bone (Osteitis Deformans)

- **Definition:** A chronic, progressive, osteolytic and osteosclerotic bone disease of uncertain aetiology.
    - Described by Sir James Paget in 1877.
- **Aetiology:** Unclear, but implicated factors include:
    - **Slow-virus infection** (e.g., paramyxovirus like respiratory syncytial virus, measles) in osteoclasts.
    - **Genetic susceptibility:** Autosomal dominant inheritance, 7-10 fold higher prevalence in first-degree relatives, linkage to **RANK gene on chromosome 18q**.
- **Epidemiology:** Predominantly affects **males over 50 years**. Can be monostotic (one bone) or polyostotic (multiple bones).
- **Clinical Features:**
    - Monostotic form may be asymptomatic, discovered incidentally.
    - Polyostotic form presents with **pain, fractures, skeletal deformities**.
    - Occasionally, **sarcomatous transformation** can occur.
    - **Biochemicals:** Marked elevation of **serum alkaline phosphatase**; serum calcium usually normal to high.
- **Morphologic Features (Three Sequential Stages):**
    1. **Initial Osteolytic Stage:** Increased number of large osteoclasts leading to bone resorption.
    2. **Mixed Osteolytic-Osteoblastic Stage:** Imbalance between osteoblastic new bone formation and osteoclastic resorption.
        - Mineralisation of newly-laid matrix lags, leading to a **characteristic mosaic pattern or jigsaw puzzle appearance** of osteoid seams or cement lines.
    3. **Quiescent Osteosclerotic Stage:** Excessive bone formation results in compact, dense bone (osteosclerosis) over many years.
        - Radiologically: **"Cotton-wool appearance"** of affected bone. However, newly-formed bone is poorly mineralised, soft, and susceptible to fractures.
- **Clinical Relevance:** Different from Paget's disease of nipple and vulva.

### 2. Aneurysmal Bone Cyst

- **Definition:** An **expanding osteolytic lesion filled with blood**, appearing as a dilatation or distension.
- **Age/Location:** More common in **young patients under 30 years**. Most frequently involves **shafts of metaphyses of long bones** or the vertebral column.
- **Radiographic Appearance:** Characteristic **"ballooned-out expansile lesion"** underneath the periosteum.
- **Pathogenesis:** Unclear; suggested to arise from persistent local alteration in haemodynamics. **17p13 translocation** identified in some cases.
- **Clinical Features:** May enlarge over years, producing **pain, tenderness, and pathologic fracture**.
- **Morphologic Features:**
    - **Gross:** Large **haemorrhagic mass** covered by thinned-out reactive bone. The end of the long bone is expanded due to a cyst, with a tan and haemorrhagic inner wall.
    - **Microscopically:** Consists of **blood-filled aneurysmal spaces** of variable size, some lined by endothelium. Spaces are separated by connective tissue septa containing osteoid tissue, numerous **osteoclast-like multinucleate giant cells**, and trabeculae of bone.
- **Differential Diagnosis:** Must be distinguished histologically from **giant cell tumour (osteoclastoma)** and telangiectatic osteosarcoma.

### 3. Osteoclastoma (Giant Cell Tumour)

- **Definition:** A distinctive neoplasm with uncertain histogenesis. Contains a uniform distribution of **osteoclastic giant cells** spread in the stroma.
- **Age/Location:** Occurs in patients between **20 and 40 years** of age, with no sex predilection. Arises in the **epiphysis of long bones** close to the articular cartilage.
    - **Common Sites:** Lower end of femur, upper end of tibia (around the knee), lower end of radius, and upper end of fibula.
- **Clinical Features:** Pain (especially on weight-bearing and movement), noticeable swelling, and **pathological fracture**.
- **Radiographic Appearance:** Large, lobulated, and osteolytic lesion at the end of an expanded long bone with characteristic **"soap bubble" appearance**.
- **Morphologic Features:**
    - **Gross:** Eccentrically located in the epiphyseal end of an expanded long bone. Well-circumscribed, **dark-tan**, covered by a thin shell of subperiosteal bone. Cut surface is characteristically **haemorrhagic, necrotic, and honey-combed** due to focal cystic degeneration.
    - **Microscopically:**
        - **Large number of osteoclast-like giant cells** regularly scattered throughout the stroma.
        - Giant cells may contain up to 100 benign nuclei, similar to normal osteoclasts.
        - **Mononuclear stromal cells are the real tumour cells** and determine behaviour; they are uniform, plump, spindle-shaped or round to oval, but may show varying degrees of atypia and mitosis.
- **Differential Diagnosis:** Must be distinguished from other benign giant cell lesions like chondroblastoma, brown tumour of hyperparathyroidism, aneurysmal bone cyst, simple bone cyst, and metaphyseal fibrous defect.

### 4. Ewing's Sarcoma

- **Definition:** A **highly malignant small round cell tumour** of neuroectodermal origin. It is a member of the **small round cell tumour group**.
- **Age/Location:** Primarily affects children and young adults between **5 to 20 years** of age. Arises in the **medullary canal of diaphysis or metaphysis** of long bones.
    - **Common Sites:** Shafts of long bones (femur, tibia, humerus, fibula).
- **Morphologic Features:**
    - **Gross:** Typically located in the medullary cavity, producing **expansion of the affected diaphysis/metaphysis**, often extending into adjacent soft tissues. Tumour tissue is characteristically **grey-white, soft, and friable**. Can form a bulky mass extending into soft tissues, with cystic and necrotic areas.
    - **Microscopically:**
        - Composed of **small, uniform, round to oval cells**, slightly larger than lymphocytes.
        - Cells have **scanty, ill-defined cytoplasm** and hyperchromatic nuclei with **"salt and pepper" chromatin** and frequent mitoses.
        - Cytoplasm contains **glycogen** that stains positively with **Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS) reaction**.
        - Characteristically arranged around capillaries forming **pseudorosettes**.
        - Lacks the intercellular network of reticulin fibres. Areas of necrosis and acute inflammatory cell infiltration are often seen.
        - Consistently expressed cell surface marker: **CD99** (product of MIC-2 gene).
- **Cytogenetics:** Characterised by specific translocations, most commonly **t(11;22)(q24;q12) EWSR-FLI1 fusion gene**.
- **Spread:** Metastases early via **haematogenous route** to lungs, liver, other bones, and brain.
- **Prognosis:** Historically dismal, but combined radiotherapy and systemic chemotherapy have greatly improved outcome (**5-year survival rate 40-80%**).
