1. Chronic Myeloid Leukemia-Lab diagnosis and Blood picture
	**Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML): Laboratory Diagnosis & Blood Picture**
	
	- **Definition:** CML is a clonal myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN) characterized by **overproduction of cells of the myeloid series** from a pluripotent hematopoietic stem cell. It typically occurs in middle age.
	- **Phases:** CML has a natural history consisting of 3 phases: Chronic (Stable/Indolent), Accelerated, and Blastic (Blast Crisis). Most patients are diagnosed in the chronic phase.
	
	**Laboratory Diagnosis Overview**
	
	- Diagnosis is generally possible on the **blood picture alone**.
	- Bone marrow examination, cytochemical stains, and other investigations are helpful.
	- Modern diagnosis considers clinical, morphologic, cytogenetic, and molecular characteristics.
	
	**Peripheral Blood Findings**
	
	- **Anaemia:** Usually mild to moderate degree and is **normocytic normochromic** in type. Hemoglobin is usually <11 g/dL. Occasional normoblasts may be present.
	- **White Blood Cells:** Characteristically, there is **marked leucocytosis**. The white cell count is approximately **200,000/µl or more** at the time of presentation, often exceeding 100,000/µl.
	- **Differential Count (Chronic Phase):**
	    - Excessive proliferation of myeloid cells of **intermediate grade** (myelocytes and metamyelocytes) and **mature segmented neutrophils**. This results in a "myeloid precursors, neutrophils and myelocyte peak".
	    - Myeloblasts usually do not exceed **10%** of cells.

	- **Accelerated Phase (Blood):**
	    - Progressively rising leucocytosis.
	    - Blast count in blood or marrow is between **10-20%**.
	    - Marrow basophils are **20% or more**.
	    - Platelet count falling below 100,000/µl or persistent thrombocytopenia (<100 x 10^9/L) unrelated to therapy. Persistent thrombocytosis (>1000 x 10^9/L) uncontrolled by therapy may also be seen.
	- **Blast Phase/Crisis (Blood):**
	    - Blood or marrow blasts are **>20%**, fulfilling the definition of acute leukaemia.
	    - These blasts may be myeloid, lymphoid, erythroid or undifferentiated. Myeloid blast crisis resembles AML.
	    - Unlike AML, **Auer rods are NOT seen** in myeloblasts of CML in blast crisis.
	- **Platelets:** Count may be normal but is raised in about half the cases. Can be normal or increased in blood picture.
	
	**Bone Marrow Examination**
	
	- **Cellularity:** Generally **hypercellularity** with total or partial replacement of fat spaces by proliferating myeloid cells. Mild to marked hypercellularity.
	- **Myeloid Cells:** Predominate with an **increased myeloid-erythroid ratio**. Differential counts show predominance of myelocytes. Myeloblasts usually do not exceed 10% in chronic phase.
	- **Erythropoiesis:** Normoblastic but there is **reduction** in erythropoietic cells.
	- **Megakaryocytes:** Conspicuous but are usually **smaller** in size than normal. Markedly increased and abnormally large in Essential Thrombocythemia (MPN), but conspicuous and smaller in CML. May proliferate in sheets/clusters in accelerated phase.
	
	**Special Tests**
	
	- **Cytogenetics:**
	    - The characteristic chromosomal abnormality is the **Philadelphia (Ph) chromosome**.
	    - Seen in **90-95%** cases of CML.
	    - Ph chromosome results from a reciprocal balanced translocation between chromosome 22 and chromosome 9: **t(9;22)(q34;q11)**.
	- **Molecular:** The t(9;22) translocation forms the fusion gene, **BCR/ABL**. This fusion protein is a target for therapy (e.g., Imatinib).
	- **Cytochemistry:**
	    - Leucocyte alkaline phosphatase (NAP or LAP) score is very useful to distinguish CML from leukaemoid reaction.
	    - LAP score is **characteristically HIGH in leukaemoid reaction** but **DECREASED in chronic myeloid leukaemia**.
		
	**Clinical Context**
	
	- Patients often present in middle age with gradual symptoms like fatigue, weakness, weight loss, anorexia, and abdominal fullness/pain, often due to **marked splenomegaly**.
	- Organ infiltration may be present.
	- Prognosis is poor with accelerated phase or blast crisis.

2. Peripheral smear findings in Chronic Myeloid Leukemia
	**Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia (CML): Peripheral Blood Picture**
	
	- **Overview:** Diagnosis is generally possible on the **blood picture alone**. CML is a myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN) characterised by overproduction of myeloid cells. Patients are usually diagnosed in the **chronic/stable/indolent phase**.
	- **Natural History:** CML has 3 phases: Chronic, Accelerated, and Blastic (Blast Crisis). The blood picture changes depending on the phase.
	
	**Peripheral Blood Findings (Chronic Phase):**
	
	- **Anaemia:** Usually **mild to moderate** degree. It is typically **normocytic normochromic** in type. Hemoglobin is usually **<11 g/dL**. Occasional normoblasts may be present.
	- **White Blood Cells (WBCs):**
	    - Characteristic feature is **marked leucocytosis**.
	    - Total leukocyte count is approximately **200,000/µl or more** at presentation, often **exceeds 100,000/µl**.
	- **Differential Count:**
	    - Excessive proliferation of myeloid cells of **intermediate grade** (myelocytes and metamyelocytes) and **mature segmented neutrophils**. This results in a characteristic "**myeloid precursors, neutrophils and myelocyte peak**".
	    - Immature cells of **all maturation stages** are present.
	    - Myeloblasts and promyelocytes are typically **<10%** of cells. Myeloblasts usually do not exceed 10% in the peripheral blood.
	    - A characteristic feature is an **increase in the proportion of basophils**. Basophils can be increased **up to 10%**.
	    - Eosinophilia may also be present.
	- **Platelets:** Count may be **normal** but is **raised in about half the cases**. Can be normal or increased.
	
	**Peripheral Blood Findings (Accelerated Phase):**
	
	- Progressively rising leucocytosis.
	- **Blast count** in blood is between **10-20%**.
	- Platelet count falling **below 100,000/µl** (persistent thrombocytopenia) unrelated to therapy OR persistent thrombocytosis (>1000 x 10^9/L) uncontrolled by therapy.
	
	**Peripheral Blood Findings (Blast Phase / Blast Crisis):**
	
	- Blood picture **resembles acute leukaemia** and has poor prognosis.
	- **Blasts are ≥20%** in the blood. This fulfils the definition of acute leukaemia.
	- Blasts may be myeloid (most common, 70%), lymphoid (30%), erythroid or undifferentiated.
	- Myeloid blast crisis resembles AML, but **Auer rods are NOT seen** in the myeloblasts of CML blast crisis, unlike AML.
	
	**Clinical Relevance:**
	
	- The marked leucocytosis and myeloid cell proliferation often lead to significant **splenomegaly**. This is a key differentiating feature from leukaemoid reaction where massive splenomegaly is absent.
	- Symptoms like fatigue, weight loss, and abdominal fullness are often related to the high cell burden and splenomegaly.
	
	**Differential Diagnosis (Key Point):**
	
	- Distinguishing CML from a **myeloid leukaemoid reaction** is crucial.
	- In a leukaemoid reaction, leucocytosis is usually moderate, not exceeding 100,000/µl.
	- Immature cells in leukaemoid reaction are predominantly metamyelocytes and myelocytes (5-15%), with blasts fewer than 5%.
	- The **Leucocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) score** is a very useful distinguishing test.
	    - LAP score is **DECREASED** in CML (chronic phase).
	    - LAP score is **HIGH** in leukaemoid reaction.
	- The presence of the **Philadelphia (Ph) chromosome** (t(9;22)) or the **BCR/ABL fusion gene** is characteristic of CML and absent in leukaemoid reaction.

4. Auer rods
	**Auer Rods**
	
	- **Definition:** **Azurophilic needle-like structures** found within the **cytosol of myeloblasts**.
	- **Composition:** They are **peroxidase-positive**.
	- **Morphology:** Appear as **needle-like** structures.
	- **Diagnostic Significance (High Yield!):**
	    - They are a key finding in **Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML)**.
	    - Specifically seen in the myeloblasts of certain AML subtypes, such as **M2 and M3** (FAB classification).
	    - Crucially, **Auer rods are NOT seen** in the myeloblasts during the **blast crisis of Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia (CML)**. This is a vital point for differentiating AML from CML blast crisis.
	- **Differentiation:** Their presence helps to distinguish **myeloblasts** from lymphoblasts.
