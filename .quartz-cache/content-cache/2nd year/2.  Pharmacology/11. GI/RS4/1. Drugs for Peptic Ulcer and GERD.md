1. Proton pump inhibitors-name, Mechanism of action , uses and adverse effects
	### **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)**
	
	**I. Names:**
	
	- **Omeprazole** (prototype drug)
	- **Esomeprazole**
	- **Lansoprazole**
	- **Pantoprazole**
	- **Rabeprazole**
	- Ilaprazole (more potent than omeprazole)
	
	**II. Mechanism of Action:**
	
	- **Drug Class:** PPIs are **prodrugs**.
	- **Target:** They act on the **H+, K+-ATPase** (known as the **proton pump**). This is the **terminal enzyme** responsible for secreting H+ ions in the apical canaliculi of gastric parietal cells.
	- **Activation:**
	    - After absorption in the small intestine, PPIs enter the bloodstream and diffuse into the **parietal cells**.
	    - In the **acidic environment of the canaliculi** of the parietal cell, they are converted to their **active, charged form (sulphenamide)**.
	- **Inhibition:**
	    - The activated sulphenamide form **binds covalently with the SH group of the proton pump**.
	    - This leads to **irreversible inactivation** of the proton pump.
	- **Overall Effect:** PPIs are the **most powerful inhibitors of gastric acid secretion**. They inhibit **both fasting and stimulated acid secretion**. As they act at the **final step of acid secretion**, they are effective regardless of the stimulus.
	- **Duration of Action:** Despite a short half-life (~1.5 hours), acid secretion is suppressed for up to **24 hours** due to the irreversible inhibition of the proton pumps.
	
	**III. Clinical Uses (Therapeutic Relevance):**
	
	- **Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD):**
	    - **Most powerful acid suppressive agents**.
	    - Superior to H2-blockers, with **rapid onset of action and faster ulcer healing**.
	    - Standard therapy: Duodenal ulcers (4 weeks), gastric ulcers (6–8 weeks).
	    - **H. pylori-associated ulcers:** Essential component of **combination therapy** (two/three antimicrobials + PPI) for eradication.
	    - **Stress ulcers:** Prophylactic intravenous PPIs reduce incidence in critically ill patients.
	    - **NSAID-induced ulcers:** More effective than H2-blockers for prevention and treatment.
	- **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):**
	    - **Preferred agents** for GERD.
	    - More effective than H2-blockers in providing symptom relief, healing erosive oesophagitis, and preventing complications.
	    - Prolonged maintenance therapy may be needed for erosive oesophagitis or stricture.
	- **Zollinger-Ellison (Z-E) Syndrome:**
	    - Preferred agents for this hypersecretory condition.
	    - Higher doses are needed, and prolonged therapy is common in inoperable cases.
	- **Preoperative Use:**
		- To **reduce the risk of aspiration pneumonia**, especially before prolonged surgery.
	
	**IV. Adverse Effects:**
	
	- Generally **well-tolerated**.
	- **Common:** Headache, dizziness, diarrhoea.
	- **Rare:** Rashes, pruritus, abdominal pain, nausea, taste disturbance.
	- **Long-term concerns (Clinical Relevance):**
	    - **Thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, anaemia:** Mostly after prolonged use (>2 weeks), generally mild and reversible.
	    - **Optic and peripheral neuropathy:** May develop with use exceeding 4 weeks.
	    - **Serotonin syndrome:** Occurred when linezolid (a weak MAO inhibitor) was co-administered with serotonergic drugs like SSRIs.
	    - Increased risk of **cardiovascular thrombotic events** (with selective COX-2 inhibitors) by inhibiting PGI2 production.
	    - May precipitate **gout** due to hyperuricaemia (inhibits uric acid excretion).
	    - **Headache, dizziness, diarrhoea**.
	    - **Abdominal discomfort**.
	    - **Flatus**.
	
	**V. Administration Notes:**
	
	- Typically administered **orally 30 minutes before food** to allow activation in the stimulated parietal cells.
	- Available as enteric-coated forms or with sodium bicarbonate to prevent degradation by stomach acid.
	- Parenteral (i.v.) formulations are available for severe cases or specific needs (e.g., acute bleeding ulcers).
2. Omeprazole--mechanism of action and therapeutic uses
	### **Omeprazole: Mechanism of Action & Therapeutic Uses**
	
	**I. Names:**
	
	- **Omeprazole** (prototype drug)
	- Other Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) include Esomeprazole, Lansoprazole, Pantoprazole, Rabeprazole. Ilaprazole is noted as more potent than omeprazole.
	
	**II. Mechanism of Action (MOA):**
	
	- **Drug Class:** PPIs are **prodrugs**.
	- **Target:** They act on the **H+, K+-ATPase**, also known as the **proton pump**. This is the **terminal enzyme** responsible for secreting H+ ions in the apical canaliculi of gastric parietal cells.
	- **Activation:**
	    - After oral absorption from the small intestine, PPIs diffuse into the **parietal cells**.
	    - In the **acidic environment of the canaliculi** within the parietal cell, they are converted to their **active, charged form (sulphenamide)**. This activation is why they are given before food, as food stimulates acid secretion in the canaliculi, which is necessary for their activation.
	- **Inhibition:**
	    - The activated sulphenamide form **binds covalently with the SH group of the proton pump**, leading to its **irreversible inactivation**.
	- **Overall Effect:**
	    - PPIs are the **most powerful inhibitors of gastric acid secretion**.
	    - They inhibit **both fasting and stimulated acid secretion**.
	    - Because they act at the **final step of acid secretion**, they are effective regardless of the stimulus.
	    - Despite a short plasma half-life (~1.5 hours), acid secretion is suppressed for up to **24 hours due to this irreversible inhibition** of the proton pumps.
	    - They typically suppress acid production by about 80%–98% at commonly used doses.
	- **Specificity:** Omeprazole is a **highly specific drug** because its singular perceptible action is on the H+, K+-ATPase, which is localized specifically to gastric parietal cells.
	
	**III. Clinical Uses (Therapeutic Relevance):**
	
	- **Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD):**
	    - **Most powerful acid suppressive agents** for PUD.
	    - **Superior to H2-blockers** due to rapid onset and faster ulcer healing.
	    - Standard therapy: Duodenal ulcers require **4 weeks**; gastric ulcers require **6–8 weeks**.
	    - **H. pylori-associated ulcers:** Essential component of **combination therapy** (two/three antimicrobials + PPI) for eradication.
	    - **Stress ulcers (Curling ulcer): Prophylactic intravenous PPIs reduce the incidence** in critically ill patients.
	    - **NSAID-induced ulcers:** More effective than H2-blockers for prevention and treatment.
	- **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):**
	    - **Preferred agents** for GERD.
	    - More effective than H2-blockers in symptom relief, healing erosive oesophagitis, and preventing complications.
	    - Prolonged maintenance therapy may be needed for erosive oesophagitis or stricture.
	- **Zollinger-Ellison (Z-E) Syndrome:**
	    - **Preferred agents** for this hypersecretory condition.
	    - Higher doses are needed, and prolonged therapy is common in inoperable cases.
	- **Preoperative Use:** To **reduce the risk of aspiration pneumonia**, especially before prolonged surgery.
	
	**IV. Administration Notes:**
	
	- Typically administered **orally 30 minutes before food**.
	- Available as enteric-coated forms or with sodium bicarbonate to prevent degradation by stomach acid.
	- Parenteral (i.v.) formulations are available for acute bleeding ulcers or other severe indications.
3. Anti H pylori drug regimen
	 **Anti-_Helicobacter pylori_ Drug Regimens**
	
	**I. Introduction:**
	
	- _Helicobacter pylori_ (_H. pylori_) is a Gram-negative, rod-shaped bacterium **associated with gastritis, duodenal ulcer, gastric ulcer, and gastric carcinoma**.
	- The exact mechanism of mucosal inflammation and damage caused by _H. pylori_ is not fully clear, but **ammonia produced by its urease activity may directly damage cells**.
	
	**II. Rationale for Combination Therapy:**
	
	- **Combination therapy (triple/quadruple) is always recommended** for _H. pylori_ eradication.
	- **Objectives of combination therapy:**
	    - To **prevent or delay the development of resistant organisms**.
	    - To **prevent relapse** of the infection.
	    - To **promote rapid ulcer healing**.
	    - To **eradicate _H. pylori_ infection**.
	
	**III. Antimicrobial Agents Used:**
	
	- The antimicrobials commonly used in _H. pylori_ infection include:
	    - **Amoxicillin**.
	    - **Tetracycline**.
	    - **Clarithromycin**.
	    - **Metronidazole**.
	    - **Tinidazole**.
	- **Resistance develops rapidly to metronidazole and clarithromycin**, but **not to amoxicillin**.
	- **Amoxicillin should be avoided in patients with a history of penicillin allergy**.
	- Other anti-_H. pylori_ drugs are:
	    - **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)**.
	    - **H2-blockers**.
	    - **Colloidal Bismuth Subcitrate (CBS)**.
	
	**IV. Recommended Regimens (Standard):**
	
	- The duration of treatment can be 1 week or 2 weeks, with **14-day therapy generally being more effective**.
	
	1. **Triple Therapy (14 Days):**
	    
	    - **Lansoprazole** 30 mg twice daily (b.d.).
	    - **Clarithromycin** 500 mg twice daily (b.d.).
	    - **Amoxicillin** 1 g twice daily (b.d.).
	    - _Note: Metronidazole 400 mg TDS or tinidazole 500 mg BD can be combined with amoxicillin/clarithromycin and a PPI_.
	2. **Quadruple Therapy (14 Days):**
	    
	    - **Omeprazole** 20 mg twice daily (b.d.).
	    - **CBS** 120 mg four times daily (q.i.d.).
	    - **Tetracycline** 500 mg four times daily (q.i.d.).
	    - **Metronidazole** 400 mg three times daily (t.i.d.).
	
	**V. Post-Treatment Management:**
	
	- After completion of the recommended antimicrobial regimen, a **PPI should be continued for 6 more weeks to enhance ulcer healing**.
