1. Cyclosporine-MOA, uses and adverse effects
	**Cyclosporine: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. Mechanism of Action (MOA)**
	
	- **Class**: Calcineurin inhibitor; a polypeptide derived from _Beauveria nivea_.
	- **Intracellular Binding**: Cyclosporine **enters target cells** and **binds to an intracellular protein called cyclophilin**.
	- **Calcineurin Inhibition**: The resulting **cyclosporine-cyclophilin complex inhibits calcineurin**, which is a phosphatase enzyme.
	- **T-cell Activation Blockade**: This inhibition **blocks the activation of T cells by antigen**.
	- **Cytokine Suppression**: The ultimate effect is a **decreased production of IL-2 and other cytokines**, leading to **suppression of cell-mediated immunity**.
	
	**II. Therapeutic Uses**
	
	- **Organ Transplantation**: **Prevention and treatment of rejection episodes** in various organ transplantations, including **kidney, bone marrow, and liver**.
	- **Autoimmune Diseases**: Used in the treatment of several autoimmune conditions, such as:
	    - **Myasthenia gravis**
	    - **Rheumatoid arthritis**
	    - **Systemic lupus erythematosus**
	
	**III. Adverse Effects**
	
	- **Renal Toxicity**: **Nephrotoxicity** is a prominent adverse effect.
	- **Hepatic Toxicity**: **Hepatotoxicity** can occur.
	- **Gastrointestinal**: **Hypertrophy of gums**.
	- **Cardiovascular**: **Hypertension**.
	- **Metabolic**:
	    - **Hyperglycaemia**
	    - **Hyperlipidaemia**
	- **Dermatological**: **Hirsutism**.
	- **Infections**: **Increased susceptibility to infections** due to immunosuppression.
	- **Haematological**: **Bone marrow toxicity is minimal**.
	
	**IV. Clinical Relevance & Drug Interactions**
	
	- **Enhanced Nephrotoxicity**: Risk of nephrotoxicity is **increased** when co-administered with **aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, vancomycin, or NSAIDs**.
	- **Reduced Plasma Levels**: **Rifampicin, phenobarbitone, or phenytoin** can **reduce cyclosporine blood levels** by inducing its metabolism.
	- **Increased Plasma Levels**: **Erythromycin** can **increase cyclosporine plasma levels** by inhibiting its metabolism.
	- **Hyperkalaemia Risk**: Concomitant use with **potassium-sparing diuretics** can lead to **severe hyperkalaemia**.
