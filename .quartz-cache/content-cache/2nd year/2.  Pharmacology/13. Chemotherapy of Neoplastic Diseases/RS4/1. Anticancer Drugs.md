1. Cyclophosphamide-MOA , adverse effects and therapeutic uses
	1. **Cyclophosphamide: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. Mechanism of Action (MOA)**
	
	- **Class**: **Alkylating agent**, specifically a **Nitrogen Mustard**.
	- **Cell Cycle Specificity**: **Cell Cycle Non-Specific (CCNS) drug** – acts on both dividing and resting cells.
	- **Prodrug**: Cyclophosphamide is an **inactive prodrug** that undergoes **hepatic activation** (in the liver).
	- **Active Metabolites**: Produces active metabolites:
	    - **Phosphoramide mustard**: Responsible for the **cytotoxic (anticancer)** effect.
	    - **Acrolein**: Responsible for the specific adverse effect of haemorrhagic cystitis.
	- **DNA Damage**: The active alkylating metabolites form **covalent bonds** by introducing alkyl groups into nucleophilic sites on DNA bases. This leads to:
	    - **DNA cross-linking** (inhibits DNA replication).
	    - **Abnormal base pairing** (causes miscoding and defective protein synthesis).
	    - **Breaks in DNA strands**.
	    - The net result is **cell death**.
	- **Immunosuppression**: Primarily affects **B cells** and suppresses **humoral immunity**.
	
	**II. Adverse Effects**
	
	- **General Toxicity (Common to Cytotoxic Agents)**:
	    - **Bone Marrow Suppression**: Leading to myelosuppression (leukopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, aplastic anaemia) and increased risk of infection and bleeding.
	    - **Gastrointestinal (GI) Disturbances**: Nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, oral mucositis, diarrhoea, GI bleeding, and ulcers.
	    - **Alopecia** (hair loss, usually reversible upon discontinuation).
	    - **Skin Rashes** and dermatitis.
	    - **Gonadal Dysfunction**: Oligozoospermia and infertility in males; amenorrhoea and infertility in females.
	    - **Teratogenicity**: Can cause abortion or teratogenic effects, **contraindicated in pregnancy**.
	    - **Hyperuricaemia**: Risk of gout and urate stones (manage with hydration, allopurinol, corticosteroids).
	    - **Secondary Malignancies**: Rarely, long-term use can lead to secondary cancers (e.g., leukaemia).
	- **Specific Toxicity (High-Yield Point)**:
	    - **Haemorrhagic Cystitis**: A **severe, dose-limiting adverse effect** caused by the metabolite **acrolein** irritating the bladder mucosa.
	    - **Prevention**: Can be minimized by **adequate hydration** and coadministration of **Mesna** (2-mercaptoethane sulphonate), which binds to and inactivates acrolein in the urine.
	- **Hepatotoxicity**.
	
	**III. Therapeutic Uses**
	
	- **Anticancer (Chemotherapy)**: Often used in combination regimens for:
	    - **Lymphomas** (e.g., Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma).
	    - **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL)**.
	    - **Breast Cancer**.
	- **Immunosuppression (Key Clinical Relevance)**: Due to its powerful immunosuppressant effects, it's used in:
	    - **Organ Transplantation**: To prevent and treat graft rejection.
	    - **Autoimmune Diseases**:
	        - **Rheumatoid Arthritis**.
	        - **Nephrotic Syndrome**.
	        - **Multiple Sclerosis**.
	        - **Crohn's Disease**.
	        - **Autoimmune Haemolytic Anaemia**.
2. Methotrexate-therapeutic uses and adverse effects
	**Methotrexate: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. Therapeutic Uses**
	
	- **Anticancer (Chemotherapy)**:
	    - **Drug of choice** for **choriocarcinoma**.
	    - Used in **acute leukaemias**.
	    - Used in **Burkitt lymphoma**.
	    - Used in **breast cancer**.
	    - Note: It is a **Cell Cycle Specific (CCS) drug** and acts during the **S phase** of the cell cycle.
	- **Immunosuppression & Anti-inflammatory Effects**:
	    - **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)**: **Preferred DMARD** (Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drug) for RA. It has a relatively rapid onset of action compared to other DMARDs.
	        - Used at a **much lower dose** than in cancer chemotherapy (e.g., 7.5–25 mg once weekly).
	        - Exerts anti-inflammatory effects, inhibits neutrophil chemotaxis, and decreases production of proinflammatory cytokines by activated T cells.
	        - Prevents joint erosion.
	    - **Psoriasis**: Used in severe cases.
	    - **Inflammatory Bowel Disease**.
	    - **Polymyositis, Giant Cell Arteritis, Dermatomyositis**.
	    - **Organ Transplantation** (as an immunosuppressant).
	
	**II. Adverse Effects**
	
	- **General Toxicity (Common to Cytotoxic Agents)**:
	    - **Bone Marrow Suppression**: Leading to **leucopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, and aplastic anaemia**. This increases the risk of infection and bleeding.
	    - **Gastrointestinal (GI) Disturbances**:
	        - **Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea**.
	        - **Stomatitis, oral mucositis, GI bleeding, and ulcers** (due to damage to rapidly dividing epithelial cells of gut mucosa).
	    - **Alopecia** (hair loss, usually reversible upon discontinuation).
	    - **Dermatitis and skin rashes**.
	    - **Gonadal dysfunction** and infertility.
	    - **Teratogenicity**: **Contraindicated in pregnancy**.
	    - **Hyperuricaemia** (risk of gout/urate stones).
	    - **Secondary Malignancies** (rarely, long-term use can lead to secondary cancers like leukaemia).
	    - **Megaloblastic anaemia**: Due to its folate antagonist mechanism.
	- **Specific Toxicity/High-Yield Points**:
	    - **Megaloblastic Anaemia**: This is a direct consequence of its MOA as a folate antagonist.
	    - **Hepatotoxicity**: Can cause **hepatic fibrosis**. Liver function should be monitored periodically, and it is contraindicated in liver disease.
	    - **Pancytopaenia**.
	- **Management of Toxicity**:
	    - **Folinic Acid Rescue (Leucovorin Rescue)**: **Crucial for minimizing toxicity**. Folinic acid is an active coenzyme form that bypasses the metabolic block produced by Methotrexate, rapidly reversing its toxic effects on normal cells. This allows for the use of very high doses of Methotrexate for better antineoplastic effects.
	- **Drug Interactions (Clinical Relevance)**:
	    - **Salicylates, sulphonamides, tetracyclines, NSAIDs**: These drugs can **potentiate Methotrexate toxicity** by displacing it from plasma proteins and interfering with its renal excretion.
3. Vinca alkaloids - MOA , uses and adverse effects
	**Vinca Alkaloids: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. Mechanism of Action (MOA)**
	
	- **Class**: Natural products derived from the periwinkle plant.
	- **Cell Cycle Specificity**: **Cell Cycle Specific (CCS) agents**, acting primarily during the **M phase (mitotic phase)** of the cell cycle.
	- **Microtubule Interference**:
	    - Vinca alkaloids (vinblastine and vincristine) **bind to β-tubulin**.
	    - This binding **inhibits its polymerization into microtubules**.
	    - The result is the **absence of an intact mitotic spindle**.
	    - Ultimately, **cell division is arrested in metaphase**, leading to cell death.
	
	**II. Therapeutic Uses**
	
	- **Anticancer (Chemotherapy)**:
	    - Used in combination with other anticancer agents.
	    - **Vinblastine (V)**:
	        - **Hodgkin disease** (as part of MOPP/ABVD regimens).
	        - **Carcinoma of Breast**.
	        - **Testicular tumours**.
	    - **Vincristine (C)**:
	        - **Childhood leukaemias** (e.g., Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia - ALL).
	        - **Childhood tumours** (e.g., Wilms tumour, neuroblastoma).
	        - **Hodgkin disease**.
	
	**III. Adverse Effects (Toxicity)**
	
	- Vinca alkaloids, like other cytotoxic drugs, affect rapidly proliferating normal cells, including bone marrow, skin, hair, GI mucosa, and gonads.
	- **General Toxicity (Common to Cytotoxic Agents)**:
	    - **Bone Marrow Suppression**: Leukopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, and aplastic anaemia, increasing risk of infection and bleeding.
	    - Gastrointestinal (GI) disturbances: Nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, oral mucositis, diarrhoea.
	    - **Alopecia** (hair loss).
	    - Gonadal dysfunction: Oligozoospermia, amenorrhoea, and infertility.
	    - Teratogenicity (contraindicated in pregnancy).
	    - Secondary malignancies (e.g., leukaemia).
	    - Hyperuricaemia (risk of gout/urate stones).
	- **Specific Toxicity (High-Yield Distinction)**:
	    - **Vincristine (C)**:
	        - **Peripheral Neuritis**: Presents as paraesthesia (tingling/numbness).
	        - **Constipation**.
	        - **Minimal myelosuppressive action** (spares bone marrow compared to vinblastine).
	    - **Vinblastine (V)**:
	        - **Prominent Bone Marrow Suppression** (myelosuppression).
	        - Other side effects: Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea.
4. Antibiotics in cancer chemotherapy
	**Anticancer Antibiotics: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. General Mechanism of Action (MOA)**
	
	- **Direct Action on DNA**: Most anticancer antibiotics exert their effects by directly interfering with DNA.
	    - **Intercalation**: Drugs like **Dactinomycin, Doxorubicin, and Daunorubicin** insert themselves between DNA base pairs, **blocking DNA transcription**.
	    - **DNA Damage/Free Radical Formation**: **Bleomycin** binds to DNA and generates **free radicals, causing DNA strand breaks**.
	    - **Alkylating Action**: **Mitomycin C** is converted into a compound that acts as an **alkylating agent**, damaging DNA.
	
	**II. Cell Cycle Specificity**
	
	- Anticancer antibiotics are generally **Cell Cycle Non-Specific (CCNS)** drugs, meaning they can act on cells in any phase of the cell cycle (dividing and resting).
	    - _Exception:_ **Bleomycin** is noted as a **Cell Cycle Specific (CCS) drug** acting in the **M phase**.
	
	**III. Therapeutic Uses & Specific Adverse Effects**
	
	- **Actinomycin D**
	    
	    - **Uses**: Primarily for **Wilms tumour, Ewing sarcoma, and choriocarcinoma**.
	    - **Adverse Effects**: Prominent **bone marrow suppression** and **gastrointestinal (GI) side effects**.
	- **Mitomycin C**
	    
	    - **Uses**: Used in **GI tumours, cervix, and bladder cancer**.
	    - **Adverse Effects**: Causes **bone marrow suppression, GI side effects, and nephrotoxicity**.
	- **Bleomycin**
	    
	    - **Uses**: Effective in **testicular and ovarian tumours**, and **Hodgkin lymphoma** (often in the ABVD regimen).
	    - **Key Advantage**: Causes **very little bone marrow suppression** (bone marrow-sparing drug).
	    - **Specific Adverse Effects**:
	        - **Pulmonary Fibrosis**: A significant and potentially severe side effect.
	        - **Hyperpigmentation of the skin**.
	- **Doxorubicin & Daunorubicin (Anthracyclines)**
	    
	    - **Daunorubicin Uses**: Effective in **acute leukaemias**.
	    - **Doxorubicin Uses**: Active against **solid tumours**.
	    - **Specific Adverse Effects**:
	        - **Cardiotoxicity**: Can cause **cardiomyopathy** leading to congestive cardiac failure (CCF), hypotension, or arrhythmias.
	        - **Bone marrow suppression**.
	        - **GI disturbances**.
	    - **Clinical Relevance**: **Dexrazoxane**, an iron chelating agent, can be used to **reduce doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity**.
	- **Mithramycin**
	    
	    - **Uses**: Used to treat **hypercalcaemia with bone metastasis** by inhibiting osteoclasts.
	    - **MOA**: Inhibits osteoclasts to reduce serum calcium.
	
	**IV. General Toxicity of Cytotoxic Drugs (Applies to Anticancer Antibiotics)**
	
	- **Bone Marrow Suppression**: (Leukopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, aplastic anaemia) leading to increased risk of **infection and bleeding**.
	    - _Management_: Platelet transfusion, G-CSF, erythropoietin, bone marrow transplantation.
	- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Toxicity**: (Nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, oral mucositis, diarrhoea, GI bleeding, ulcers) due to damage to rapidly dividing epithelial cells.
	    - _Management of Nausea/Vomiting_: 5-HT3 antagonists (e.g., ondansetron), metoclopramide, dexamethasone.
	- **Alopecia** (hair loss, usually reversible).
	- **Immunosuppression**: Increased susceptibility to opportunistic infections.
	- **Gonadal Dysfunction**: Oligozoospermia, amenorrhoea, and infertility.
	- **Teratogenicity**: **Contraindicated in pregnancy** (can cause abortion or birth defects).
	- **Hyperuricaemia**: Risk of gout and urate stones (manage with hydration, allopurinol, corticosteroids).
	- **Secondary Malignancies**: Rare, long-term risk of developing secondary cancers (e.g., leukaemia).
