1. Give the pharmacological basis of use of digoxin in heart failure
	### **Digoxin in Heart Failure: Pharmacological Basis**
	
	**I. Introduction:**
	
	- **Cardiac Glycoside:** Digoxin is a glycosidic drug with **cardiac inotropic property**.
	- **Historical Context:** Historically, cardiac glycosides were the **primary drugs for congestive heart failure (CHF)** until the 1970s, and remain valuable when combined with other agents. William Withering established its action on the heart and its use in CHF in the early 20th century.
	
	**II. Mechanism of Action:**
	
	- **Primary Target:** Digoxin acts by **inhibiting the Na+, K+-ATPase enzyme** (the 'sodium pump') located on the cardiac cell membrane. This enzyme is also known as the digitalis receptor.
	- **Cascade of Effects:**
	    1. **Increased Intracellular Na+:** Inhibition of Na+, K+-ATPase leads to a rise in intracellular Na+ concentration.
	    2. **Reduced Ca2+ Extrusion:** This increased intracellular Na+ reduces the efficiency of the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger (NCX), which normally extrudes Ca2+ from the cell.
	    3. **Increased Intracellular Ca2+:** Consequently, more Ca2+ remains within the myocardial cell.
	    4. **Enhanced Ca2+ Storage & Release:** This excess Ca2+ is taken up and stored in the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR), leading to an increased amount of Ca2+ released during each action potential.
	    5. **Increased Myocardial Contractility:** The increased availability of Ca2+ for excitation-contraction coupling results in an **increased force of myocardial contraction (positive inotropic effect)**.
	
	**III. Pharmacological Actions & Therapeutic Benefits in CHF:**
	
	- **Cardiac Actions:**
	    - **Positive Inotropic Effect:**
	        - Increases the force of contraction, particularly prominent in the failing heart.
	        - Leads to **more complete emptying of failing/dilated ventricles**, thereby **increasing cardiac output (CO)**.
	        - Reduces end-diastolic volume and pressure, which improves left ventricular function (by Laplace's law).
	        - Decreases the diastolic size of the heart, reducing muscle fibre length and **decreasing myocardial oxygen requirement**.
	    - **Negative Chronotropic Effect:**
	        - **Reduces heart rate** (bradycardia), especially marked in CHF patients due to restoration of diminished vagal tone and abolition of sympathetic overactivity.
	        - Also directly depresses SA and AV nodes at higher concentrations.
	    - **Electrophysiological Effects (Therapeutic Range):**
	        - **Prolongs effective refractory period (ERP) and slows conduction in the AV node**. This is crucial for controlling ventricular rate in atrial fibrillation/flutter.
	        - Decreases automaticity in SA node and ectopic foci via vagal action.
	        - **ECG changes:** May produce prolongation of P-R interval, inversion of T wave, and depression of ST segment.
	- **Extracardiac Actions & Systemic Benefits:**
	    - **Diuresis:** Promptly causes diuresis in CHF patients due to **improvement in circulation and renal perfusion**, leading to excretion of retained salt and water.
	    - **Withdrawal of Compensatory Mechanisms:** Improved tissue perfusion leads to withdrawal of reflex sympathetic overactivity and inactivation of mechanisms retaining Na+ and water. This alleviates symptoms like dyspnoea and reduces cyanosis.
	    - **Relief of Symptoms:** Digoxin is effective in **relieving symptoms of systolic failure** and restoring cardiac compensation, particularly in patients with dilated hearts and low ejection fraction (EF). It mitigates low output symptoms like decreased capacity for muscular work.
	
	**IV. Clinical Relevance & Current Status:**
	
	- **Not First-Line:** Digoxin is **no longer a first-line drug** for heart failure and is typically used **in conjunction with other drugs in selected cases**.
	- **Specific Indications:** It is particularly valuable in patients with **systolic dysfunction** or when CHF is accompanied by **atrial fibrillation (AF) with a rapid heart rate**.
	- **Prognosis:** Large studies, including the DIG trial, found **no evidence that digoxin decreases overall mortality** in CHF patients, though it can reduce episodes of decompensation and heart failure deaths. It does not reverse or retard the pathological changes of heart failure.
	- **Narrow Margin of Safety:** Digoxin has a **high toxicity and a low therapeutic index** (1.5-3). Approximately 25% of patients develop toxic symptoms.
	
	**V. Administration Notes:**
	
	- Usually administered **orally**.
	- Dose adjustment is necessary in patients with **renal failure** as it is primarily excreted unchanged by the kidney.
	- Typically initiated with estimated maintenance doses (e.g., 0.125-0.25 mg/day) without a loading dose, reaching full response in 5-7 days.

	### **Aldosterone Antagonists (Spironolactone) in Heart Failure (CHF)**
	
	**I. Introduction & Pathophysiological Rationale:**
	
	- **Aldosterone** levels are often **increased in CHF** due to activation of the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) (a compensatory mechanism).
	- **Deleterious effects of excess Aldosterone in CHF:**
	    - **Sodium and water retention:** Leads to increased extracellular fluid volume and **increased cardiac preload**, worsening congestion.
	    - **Myocardial fibrosis:** Promotes **fibroblast proliferation and fibrotic changes** in the myocardium, contributing to pathological remodelling and worsening systolic dysfunction.
	    - **Potassium and Magnesium loss:** Causes **hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia**, significantly increasing the **risk of ventricular arrhythmias** and sudden cardiac death.
	
	**II. Mechanism of Action of Spironolactone (in CHF Context):**
	
	- **Competitive Antagonism:** Spironolactone is a **synthetic steroid** that acts as a **competitive antagonist** at the **intracellular mineralocorticoid receptors (MR)** in the cytoplasm of the late distal tubule and collecting duct cells (site 4).
	- **Inhibition of AIPs:** By binding to MR, spironolactone **inhibits the formation of Aldosterone-Induced Proteins (AIPs)**, which are normally induced by aldosterone to promote Na+ reabsorption and K+ secretion.
	- **Result:** This leads to **increased sodium (Na+) excretion** and **potassium (K+) retention**. Its action is most prominent when circulating aldosterone levels are high.
	
	**III. Benefits & Reasons for Use in Heart Failure (Clinical Relevance):**
	
	- **Retards Disease Progression & Improves Survival:** Large randomized trials (e.g., RALES, EPHESUS) have demonstrated that spironolactone and eplerenone **reduce overall mortality** and morbidity in moderate-to-severe CHF patients with reduced Ejection Fraction (EF). This prognostic benefit is a key reason for their use.
	- **Anti-Remodelling Effects:**
	    - **Antagonizes myocardial fibrosis** and **pathological ventricular remodelling** caused by aldosterone. This helps preserve cardiac function.
	- **Potassium Conservation:**
	    - **Prevents hypokalaemia** (K+ loss) induced by aldosterone and other diuretics (e.g., loop diuretics, thiazides). This **reduces the risk of life-threatening arrhythmias**.
	- **Fluid & Preload Reduction:**
	    - Promotes **sodium and water excretion**, thereby reducing fluid retention, oedema, and **decreasing preload** on the heart.
	- **Overcoming Diuretic Resistance:** Can **reverse resistance to loop and thiazide diuretics** that often develops in advanced heart failure due to secondary hyperaldosteronism.
	- **Add-on Therapy:** Currently established as **add-on therapy** to conventional CHF management (ACE inhibitors/ARBs, beta-blockers, and loop diuretics) in symptomatic mild to severe heart failure with reduced EF.
	- **Improved Tolerability (Eplerenone):** Eplerenone, a newer and more selective aldosterone antagonist, causes **fewer hormonal side effects** (e.g., gynaecomastia, impotence, menstrual irregularities) compared to spironolactone.
