1. Advantages of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (Fluoxetine) over tricyclic antidepressants (imipramine)
	**SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors) vs. TCAs (Tricyclic Antidepressants)**
	
	- **TCAs (e.g., Imipramine):** First-generation antidepressants.
	    - Inhibit reuptake of **Noradrenaline (NET)** and **Serotonin (SERT)**.
	    - Also interact with **muscarinic, α1 adrenergic, H1, 5-HT receptors**. Imipramine is the prototype.
	- **SSRIs (e.g., Fluoxetine):** Second-generation antidepressants, now often **first-line drugs**.
	    - **Selectively inhibit reuptake of Serotonin (SERT)**.
	    - In contrast to TCAs, they **do not interact significantly with cholinergic, adrenergic, or histaminergic receptors**.
	
	**Advantages of SSRIs (e.g., Fluoxetine) over TCAs (e.g., Imipramine)** (Developed to overcome TCA shortcomings, offer improved tolerability and safety)
	
	1. **Superior Side Effect Profile / Tolerability:**
	    
	    - **Fewer Anticholinergic Effects:** SSRIs produce **little or no anticholinergic side effects**. TCAs are potent anticholinergics causing dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, and urinary hesitancy. Imipramine is rated ++ for antimuscarinic effects.
	    - **Fewer Cardiovascular Side Effects:**
	        - SSRIs are **devoid of α1 adrenergic blocking action**, so **postural hypotension does not occur**. TCAs cause postural hypotension due to α1 blockade and inhibition of cardiovascular reflexes. Imipramine is rated ++ for hypotension. Suitable for elderly patients.
	        - **Less Cardiac Toxicity/Arrhythmias:** SSRIs **do not inhibit cardiac conduction**, so **overdose arrhythmias are not a problem**. TCAs interfere with intraventricular conduction and can cause arrhythmias. Imipramine is rated +++ for cardiac arrhythmia risk. SSRIs are **significantly safer** in this regard.
	    - **Less Sedation:** SSRIs produce **little or no sedation**. TCAs (like Imipramine) are often sedating. Imipramine is rated ++ for sedation.
	    - **No Weight Gain:** Weight gain is **not a problem with SSRIs**.
	    - **Overall:** SSRIs have **improved tolerability** and **very good patient acceptability** compared to TCAs.
	2. **Improved Safety in Overdose:**
	    
	    - TCAs have a **relatively low safety margin** and **fatalities are common** in overdose.
	    - SSRIs have an **improved safety margin in overdose**. Overdose arrhythmias are **not a problem**. (Note: Caution needed with Citalopram regarding Q-T prolongation risk in overdose).
	3. **Lower Seizure Risk:**
	    
	    - SSRIs have **practically no seizure precipitating propensity**.
	    - TCAs lower the seizure threshold and have a **significant seizure precipitation risk**. Imipramine is rated ++ for seizure precipitation.
	4. **Broader Range of Indications (Beyond Depression):**
	    
	    - SSRIs are used **extensively in anxiety, phobias, panic, OCD, and related disorders**.
	    - TCAs (like Imipramine) are now used primarily as **alternatives** for non-responders in depression. Their use in anxiety/OCD spectrum disorders is less common than SSRIs.
	    - **Fluoxetine** and Sertraline are the only antidepressants **clearly effective in juvenile depression** and approved for use in children (>7 years for Fluoxetine).
	
	**Specific Points on Fluoxetine**:
	
	- The **first SSRI** introduced.
	- Longest half-life (**t½ 2 days**, active metabolite t½ 7-10 days), making it **appropriate for poorly compliant patients**.
	- Slower onset of effect, considered less suitable if rapid effect needed.
	- Stimulant effect can potentially **worsen agitation**. Agitation is more frequent with Fluoxetine than other SSRIs.
