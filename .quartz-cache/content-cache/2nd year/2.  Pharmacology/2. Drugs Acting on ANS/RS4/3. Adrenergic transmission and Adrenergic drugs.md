1. Adrenaline therapeutic uses and adverse effects
	Here is a concise, high-yield, note-style summary of Adrenaline (Epinephrine), its therapeutic uses, and adverse effects, optimized for medical school exams:
	
	### **Adrenaline (Epinephrine)**
	
	**I. Core Mechanism of Action:**
	
	- A **non-selective adrenergic agonist** acting on **α1, α2, β1, β2**, and weak β3 receptors.
	- Its diverse clinical effects stem from widespread sympathetic stimulation.
	
	**II. Therapeutic Uses:**
	
	- **Anaphylactic Shock (Drug of Choice - LIFE-SAVING)**:
	    
	    - **Immediately administered via intramuscular (i.m.) route.** Adult dose: 0.5 mg. Paediatric doses: 0.3 ml (6-12 years), 0.15 ml (up to 6 years) of 1:1000 solution.
	    - **Rationale:** Rapidly raises **blood pressure (BP)**, counteracts **bronchospasm**, and reverses **laryngeal oedema**.
	    - If shock is immediately life-threatening and i.v. is unavoidable, must be diluted to 1:10,000 or 1:100,000 and infused slowly with constant monitoring.
	- **Cardiac Arrest**:
	    
	    - Used to **stimulate the heart** in cases due to drowning, electrocution, Stokes-Adams syndrome, and other causes.
	    - **Intravenous (i.v.) administration** is justified in this emergency, coupled with external cardiac massage.
	    - The 1:1000 solution should be diluted 10 times for i.v. injection.
	- **Acute Hypotension / Shock (e.g., Cardiogenic, Septic)**:
	    
	    - An **expedient measure to elevate very low BP** and maintain cerebral circulation.
	    - **Caution:** May reduce perfusion of kidneys and other vital organs.
	- **Bronchial Asthma / COPD (Acute Bronchospasm)**:
	    
	    - A **potent bronchodilator** (β2 action) and decongests bronchial mucosa (α action).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** While effective, **selective β2 agonists** (e.g., salbutamol) are now generally preferred to minimise cardiac side effects. Also used for bronchospasm in drug hypersensitivity.
	- **Other Allergic Disorders (Acute Hypersensitivity Reactions)**:
	    
	    - A **physiological antagonist of histamine**.
	    - Provides **quick relief** in urticaria and angioedema.
	    - **Ineffective** in delayed, retarded, or other types of allergies.
	- **Local Vasoconstrictor / Control of Local Bleeding**:
	    
	    - **Added to local anaesthetics** (e.g., lidocaine, 1:200,000 to 1:80,000 concentration) to **prolong duration of anaesthesia** and **reduce systemic toxicity** of the local anaesthetic.
	    - **Minimises local bleeding** in surgical/trauma-related situations, epistaxis, etc., when applied as compresses (e.g., 1:10,000 solution).
	- **Partial or Complete A-V Block**:
	    
	    - May be used as a **temporary measure** to maintain sufficient ventricular rate.
	    - Facilitates A-V conduction and shortens ERP of conducting tissues.
	    - Increases automaticity of ventricular pacemakers to maintain adequate idioventricular rate until pacemaker implantation.
	- **Insulin Hypoglycaemia**:
	    
	    - Can **rapidly reverse** by its hyperglycaemic action (β2-mediated glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Should only be used as an **expedient measure**; intravenous/oral glucose or glucagon is generally preferable due to adrenaline's strong cardiovascular effects.
	- **Glaucoma (Ocular)**:
	    
	    - Lowers intraocular tension (I.O.T.) by reducing aqueous humour formation and facilitating outflow.
	    - The ester prodrug, **dipivefrine**, is occasionally used as adjunctive medication in open-angle glaucoma.
	
	**III. Adverse Effects & Contraindications:**
	
	- **Cardiovascular:**
	    
	    - **Marked rise in BP** leading to potential **cerebral haemorrhage**.
	    - Risk of **ventricular tachycardia/fibrillation, angina, and myocardial infarction** with large doses or inadvertent i.v. injection.
	    - **Arrhythmias** (especially with halothane anaesthesia due to sensitisation of the heart).
	    - **Contraindicated in hypertensive patients**.
	    - **Contraindicated in hyperthyroid patients**.
	    - **Contraindicated in patients with angina**.
	    - **Avoid during halothane anaesthesia** due to risk of arrhythmias.
	    - **Avoid in patients receiving beta-blockers** (e.g., propranolol) as this can lead to a marked and dangerous rise in BP due to unopposed alpha-adrenergic action.
	- **Respiratory:**
	    
	    - Toxic doses can cause **pulmonary oedema** by shifting blood from systemic to pulmonary circulation.
	- **Central Nervous System (CNS):**
	    
	    - At clinically used doses, can cause **restlessness, apprehension, and tremor**.
	    - High doses can lead to **excitement, mental confusion, disorientation, tremors, convulsions, delirium, and hallucinations**.
	- **Ocular:**
	    
	    - Poor penetration of the cornea limits its topical use for mydriasis alone.
	    - Dipivefrine can cause significant ocular burning and other side effects.
	- **General/Other:**
	    
	    - Transient headache, palpitation, anxiety, and pallor can occur after subcutaneous/intramuscular injection.
	    - As a potent catecholamine, glucose/glucagon is generally preferred for hypoglycaemia due to adrenaline's strong cardiovascular effects.
