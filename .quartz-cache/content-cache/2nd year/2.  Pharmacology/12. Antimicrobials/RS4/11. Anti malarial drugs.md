	### **Antimalarial Drugs: Classification & Chloroquine**
	
	**I. Classification of Antimalarial Drugs**
	
	Antimalarial drugs can be classified based on their chemical structure or clinical utility.
	
	**A. Clinical Classification (Based on Parasite Life Cycle Stage Targeted):**
	
	3. **Tissue Schizontocidal Agents:** Act on liver stages of the parasite.
	    
	    - **Primary Tissue Forms (Pre-erythrocytic):** Prevent malarial infection and clinical attacks by targeting the pre-erythrocytic phase in the liver.
	        - **Primaquine:** Effective against all species, but generally not used in mass programs due to toxicity potential.
	        - **Atovaquone, Proguanil:** Act on primary forms.
	    - **Latent Tissue Forms (Hypnozoites):** Eradicate the persistent liver stages of _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_ to prevent relapses.
	        - **Primaquine:** Drug of choice for radical cure of _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_.
	        - **Tafenoquine:** Also effective against hypnozoites.
	4. **Blood Schizontocidal Agents:** Act on the erythrocytic stage of _Plasmodium_ to terminate the clinical attack.
	    
	    - **Rapid-acting and High-Efficacy:** Quickly reduce parasite biomass and resolve symptoms.
	        - **Artemisinins** (Artesunate, Artemether, Dihydroartemisinin, Arterolane): Most potent and rapid blood schizontocides, acting on a wide range of erythrocytic stages (ring to early schizonts), giving them the broadest time window of antimalarial action.
	        - **Chloroquine (CQ)**.
	        - **Quinine**.
	        - **Mefloquine**.
	        - **Amodiaquine**.
	        - **Lumefantrine**.
	        - **Atovaquone**.
	    - **Slow-acting and Low-Efficacy:** Used only in combination with rapid-acting agents.
	        - **Proguanil**.
	        - **Pyrimethamine**.
	        - **Sulfonamides/Sulfones** (Sulfadoxine, Dapsone).
	        - **Antibiotics** (Doxycycline, Clindamycin).
	5. **Gametocidal Agents:** Kill gametocytes in the patient's blood to reduce human-to-mosquito transmission.
	    
	    - **Primaquine:** Gametocidal to all _Plasmodia_ species.
	    - **Artemisinins:** Weak lethal action on early-stage gametes, but not mature ones. A single dose of primaquine is still recommended after ACT to kill all circulating gametes.
	    - **Chloroquine and Quinine:** Gametocidal against _P. vivax_ and _P. malariae_.
	
	**B. Classification Based on Clinical Utility (Objectives of Therapy):**
	
	- **Causal Prophylaxis:** Targets the pre-erythrocytic phase in the liver to prevent infection and clinical attacks. (e.g., Primaquine, Proguanil, Atovaquone).
	- **Suppressive Prophylaxis:** Suppresses the erythrocytic phase, preventing clinical manifestation of the disease. (e.g., Chloroquine, Mefloquine, Doxycycline, Atovaquone-Proguanil).
	- **Clinical Cure:** Achieved by erythrocytic schizontocides to terminate an acute malarial attack.
	- **Radical Cure:** Achieved by adding a tissue schizontocide (Primaquine) to a blood schizontocide to completely eradicate parasites (including hypnozoites) and prevent relapses in _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_ malaria.
	- **Prevention of Transmission (Gametocidal):** Achieved by drugs that kill or inhibit gametocytes to prevent the spread of infection to mosquitoes.
	
	---
	
	**II. Chloroquine (CQ)**
	
	**A. Mechanism of Action:**
	
	- **Chloroquine is a 4-aminoquinoline** and a rapidly acting erythrocytic schizontocide.
	- It is a **weakly basic drug** that **actively concentrates** in the acidic food vacuoles of susceptible intraerythrocytic plasmodia.
	- By accumulating in the acidic vacuoles, CQ raises the vacuolar pH and **interferes with the degradation of haemoglobin** by parasitic lysosomes.
	- CQ **inhibits the polymerization of toxic haem** (generated from haemoglobin digestion) into non-toxic parasite pigment haemozoin.
	- The free haem or its **complex with CQ then damages the plasmodial membranes**, leading to parasite lysis.
	- **Resistance** to CQ, especially in _P. falciparum_, is primarily mediated by an **efflux transporter** encoded by the _pfcrt_ gene, which pumps CQ out of the parasite's acidic vacuoles. The _pfmdr_ gene encoded P-glycoprotein may also be involved in some cases.
	
	**B. Uses:**
	
	- **Drug of choice for clinical cure of uncomplicated malaria** caused by _P. vivax_, _P. ovale_, _P. malariae_, and chloroquine-sensitive _P. falciparum_ and _P. knowlesi_. Fever typically resolves within 24-48 hours, and parasitaemia clears in 1-3 days.
	- For **radical cure of _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_ malaria, primaquine is administered concurrently** with CQ to destroy hypnozoites and prevent relapses.
	- Historically, CQ was an effective **chemoprophylactic agent** for all types of malaria except resistant _P. falciparum_. However, its use as suppressive prophylaxis is now limited in India.
	- **Extraintestinal amoebiasis (hepatic amoebiasis):** CQ is highly concentrated in the liver, making it effective for hepatic amoebiasis, but a luminal amoebicide must be added as it is not effective against intestinal forms.
	- **Anti-inflammatory properties:** Used in:
	    - **Rheumatoid arthritis**.
	    - **Discoid lupus erythematosus** (highly effective).
	    - **Lepra reaction**.
	    - **Photogenic reactions**.
	    - **Infectious mononucleosis** (for symptomatic relief).
	
	**C. Adverse Effects:**
	
	- **Common (at antimalarial doses):** Nausea, vomiting, skin rashes, **itching (most common complaint)**, headache, and visual disturbances.
	- **Parenteral administration** (especially rapid infusion) can cause serious effects such as **hypotension, confusion, cardiac arrhythmias, convulsions, and even cardiac arrest**.
	- **Prolonged administration in large doses** (e.g., for rheumatoid arthritis) can lead to:
	    - **Irreversible retinopathy** (due to selective accumulation in retina) and **corneal opacity**.
	    - Ototoxicity.
	    - Myopathy, cardiomyopathy, neuropathy, and rarely psychiatric disturbances.
	    - Regular ophthalmological examinations are required for long-term therapy (e.g., every 3-6 months).
	- **Contraindications/Precautions:**
	    - Should be avoided in patients with **epilepsy**.
	    - **Not to be co-administered with mefloquine** due to risk of QTc lengthening and cardiac arrests.
	- **Pregnancy:** Generally **safe for use during pregnancy**.

	**Drugs for Chloroquine (CQ)-Resistant _Plasmodium falciparum_ Malaria**
	
	**Key Concept: Artemisinin-Based Combination Therapies (ACTs)**
	
	- **First-line treatment** for uncomplicated _P. falciparum_ malaria, irrespective of CQ-resistance status.
	- **Rationale:** Artemisinins are rapid-acting, potent blood schizontocides that significantly reduce parasite biomass, while the longer-acting partner drug clears remaining parasites and prevents recrudescence. This strategy also **prevents emergence of resistance** to either component.
	- **Monotherapy** with oral artemisinin derivatives is **prohibited in India** to preserve their efficacy and prevent resistance.
	
	**Mechanism of Chloroquine Resistance:**
	
	- Primarily associated with **decreased ability of the parasite to accumulate CQ**.
	- Involves an **efflux transporter** encoded by the **pfcrt gene** (P.f. chloroquine-resistance transporter) in the acidic vacuole membrane, which pumps CQ out.
	- The **pfmdr gene** encoded P-glycoprotein (an energy-dependent ABC transporter) may also contribute to CQ resistance and confers resistance to other antimalarials like quinine, mefloquine, and halofantrine.
	
	---
	
	**I. Artemisinin-Based Combination Therapies (ACTs)**
	
	1. **Artemisinin Derivatives** (Artesunate, Artemether, Dihydroartemisinin, Arterolane):
	    
	    - **Activity:** Potent and rapid blood schizontocides, active against _P. falciparum_ strains resistant to other antimalarials. They act on a wide range of erythrocytic stages, from ring forms to early schizonts.
	    - **Mechanism of Action (MOA):**
	        - Cleavage of the **endoperoxide bridge** by haem iron within the parasite's acid vacuole leads to the generation of highly reactive **free radicals**.
	        - These free radicals bind to membrane proteins, cause lipid peroxidation, damage the endoplasmic reticulum, and inactivate **plasmodial sarcoplasmic-endoplasmic calcium ATPase (PfATP6)**, ultimately leading to parasite lysis.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Artesunate is the **first-choice parenteral drug for severe malaria**, superior to quinine in terms of faster parasite clearance, safety, tolerability, and lower mortality.
	2. **Lumefantrine** (paired with Artemether: **Artemether-Lumefantrine**):
	    
	    - **Activity:** High-efficacy, long-acting erythrocytic schizontocide, effective even in **multidrug-resistant _Pf_** areas, including mefloquine-resistant strains.
	    - **MOA:** Similar to halofantrine and mefloquine; it accumulates in infected red blood cells and likely binds to haem, forming a complex that damages parasitic membranes.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Widely used, including in North-Eastern India. **Must be administered with fatty food or milk** to enhance absorption. Generally well-tolerated, but can cause headache, dizziness, and GI upset.
	3. **Amodiaquine (AQ)** (paired with Artesunate: **AS/AQ**):
	    
	    - **Activity:** Properties similar to chloroquine, but effective in areas with CQ-resistant _Pf_. Its metabolite has a long half-life.
	    - **MOA:** Similar to chloroquine – inhibits haem polymerization.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Now marketed as fixed-dose combination (FDC) tablets in India. Side effects are similar to CQ, but neutropenia and hepatotoxicity are a concern with prolonged use.
	4. **Piperaquine** (paired with Dihydroartemisinin: **DHA-Piperaquine**, or Arterolane: **Arterolane-Piperaquine**):
	    
	    - **Activity:** A bisquinoline congener of CQ, more active against CQ-resistant _Pf_.
	    - **MOA:** Similar to chloroquine (inhibits haem polymerization). Its bulkier molecule is believed to be less susceptible to efflux by the parasite's transporters.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Good safety profile and high cure rates. Arterolane (a synthetic trioxolane) also acts rapidly and accumulates in the food vacuole.
	5. **Sulfadoxine + Pyrimethamine (S/P)** (paired with Artesunate: **AS-S/P**):
	    
	    - **Activity:** Effective against _Pf_, but efficacy against _P. vivax_ is low. Resistance to S/P is widespread, especially in South-East Asia, necessitating combination therapy.
	    - **MOA:** **Sequential blockade** of bacterial folate metabolism:
	        - **Sulfadoxine:** Competitively inhibits **bacterial folate synthetase** (structural analogue of PABA).
	        - **Pyrimethamine:** Inhibits **plasmodial dihydrofolate reductase (DHFRase)**.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Adopted as first-line treatment for uncomplicated _Pf_ malaria in most of India, but not in North-Eastern states due to high S/P resistance. Potential for serious cutaneous reactions (e.g., Stevens-Johnson syndrome) from sulfonamide.
	6. **Mefloquine (MQ)** (paired with Artesunate: **AS/MQ**):
	    
	    - **Activity:** Intrinsically fast-acting erythrocytic schizontocide, effective against CQ-sensitive and resistant plasmodia.
	    - **MOA:** Accumulates in infected RBCs and may bind to haem, damaging parasite membranes. Recent evidence suggests action in the parasitic cytosol. Resistance linked to enhanced translation of the **pfmdr1 gene**.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** While effective, its use is now less favoured due to neuropsychiatric side effects (e.g., seizures, psychiatric disorders) and the risk of **QTc prolongation** when combined with other drugs. Should be avoided in cerebral malaria.
	
	---
	
	**II. Alternative/Adjunctive Drugs (Often in Combination)**
	
	7. **Quinine:**
	    
	    - **Activity:** Oldest antimalarial, still effective against most CQ and multidrug-resistant _Pf_ strains, though resistance exists sporadically.
	    - **MOA:** Weak base, acts analogously to CQ by inhibiting **polymerization of haem to hemozoin**. Free haem or haem-quinine complex damages parasite membranes.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Can be used orally as an alternative to ACT in uncomplicated CQ-resistant _Pf_ malaria. Often combined with doxycycline or clindamycin for complete parasite clearance. It is the **drug of choice for _P. falciparum_ malaria during the first trimester of pregnancy**. Parenteral artemisinins are generally preferred for severe malaria.
	8. **Doxycycline:**
	    
	    - **Activity:** Slow-acting, weak erythrocytic schizontocidal action against all plasmodial species, including _Pf_ resistant to CQ, MQ, and S/P.
	    - **MOA:** Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit. (Though not explicitly detailed for plasmodia in sources, inferred from antibiotic class).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** **Never used alone** for malaria; always combined with quinine for CQ-resistant malaria. **Contraindicated in children (< 6 years) and pregnant women** due to deposition in developing teeth and bone.
	9. **Clindamycin:**
	    
	    - **Activity:** Bacteriostatic antibiotic with slow-acting erythrocytic schizontocidal property against all plasmodia, including multidrug-resistant _Pf_.
	    - **MOA:** Inhibits protein synthesis.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Markedly potentiates the antimalarial activity of quinine and artemisinin, always used in combination. **Can be used in children and pregnant women** (unlike doxycycline), making it a valuable alternative. Not used for prophylaxis.
	10. **Atovaquone + Proguanil** (fixed-dose combination: **MALARONE**):
	    
	    - **Activity:** Atovaquone is a rapidly acting erythrocytic schizontocide and also active against the pre-erythrocytic stage of _Pf_.
	    - **MOA:**
	        - **Atovaquone:** Collapses plasmodial mitochondrial membranes and interferes with ATP production.
	        - **Proguanil:** Inhibits plasmodial DHFRase and potentiates atovaquone's action, preventing resistance.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Used for treatment of uncomplicated CQ-resistant _Pf_ malaria and as prophylaxis for non-immune travellers.

	### **Artemisinin-Based Combination Therapy (ACT) for Uncomplicated _Plasmodium falciparum_ Malaria**
	
	**I. Core Principles of ACT**
	
	- **First-line treatment** for all uncomplicated _P. falciparum_ malaria cases, **irrespective of Chloroquine (CQ)-resistance status**.
	- **Rationale for Combination:**
	    - Artemisinins are **potent and rapid blood schizontocides**, quickly reducing parasite biomass and resolving symptoms.
	    - The **longer-acting partner drug** (e.g., lumefantrine, mefloquine, piperaquine, sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine) clears remaining parasites and **prevents recrudescence**.
	    - This combination strategy is crucial to **prevent the emergence of resistance** to either component.
	- **Monotherapy with oral artemisinin derivatives is prohibited in India** to preserve their efficacy and prevent resistance.
	- **Advantages of ACTs:** Rapid clinical and parasitological cure, high cure rates (>95%), very low recrudescence rates, and good tolerability profile.
	
	**II. Mechanism of Action (Artemisinins)**
	
	- Artemisinin compounds (e.g., Artesunate, Artemether, Dihydroartemisinin, Arterolane) possess an **endoperoxide bridge**.
	- This bridge is cleaved by **haem iron** within the parasite's acidic vacuole.
	- This cleavage generates **highly reactive free radicals**, which cause:
	    - Binding to membrane proteins.
	    - Lipid peroxidation.
	    - Damage to the endoplasmic reticulum.
	    - Inactivation of **plasmodial sarcoplasmic-endoplasmic calcium ATPase (PfATP6)**.
	- Ultimately, these actions lead to **parasite lysis**.
	- Artemisinins act on a **wide range of erythrocytic stages**, from ring forms to early schizonts, offering the broadest time window of antimalarial action.
	- They are also **lethal to early-stage malarial gametes**, helping to reduce transmission.
	
	**III. ACT Regimens for Uncomplicated _P. falciparum_ Malaria (Oral, 3-day courses, unless specified)**
	
	1. **Artemether-Lumefantrine (AL)**
	    
	    - **Drug Components:** Artemether (80 mg) + Lumefantrine (480 mg) as a fixed-dose combination (FDC).
	    - **Dosing:**
	        - **Adults & Children > 35 kg BW:** 4 tablets twice daily (BD) for 3 days.
	        - Children (dose adjusted by BW): 25-35 kg (3 tabs BD), 15-25 kg (2 tabs BD), 5-15 kg (1 tab BD).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:**
	        - **Recommended in North-Eastern states of India** due to high S/P resistance.
	        - Effective even in **multidrug-resistant _Pf_ areas**, including mefloquine-resistant strains.
	        - **Crucially, must be administered with fatty food or milk** to enhance lumefantrine absorption and ensure adequate blood levels.
	        - Generally well-tolerated; side effects include headache, dizziness, GI upset.
	        - Lumefantrine inhibits CYP2D6, caution with drugs metabolized by it.
	2. **Artesunate-Sulfadoxine + Pyrimethamine (AS-S/P)**
	    
	    - **Drug Components:** Artesunate (100 mg BD x 3 days) + Sulfadoxine (1500 mg) + Pyrimethamine (75 mg) single dose (on Day 1).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:**
	        - **Adopted as first-line treatment in most of India**, except North-Eastern states.
	        - Sulfadoxine and Pyrimethamine provide **sequential blockade of folate metabolism**.
	        - **Resistance to S/P is widespread**, especially in South-East Asia and parts of India, necessitating combination with artemisinin.
	        - Potential for serious cutaneous reactions (e.g., Stevens-Johnson syndrome) from sulfonamide component.
	        - **Can be used during 2nd and 3rd trimester of pregnancy** in India, with folate supplementation.
	3. **Artesunate-Mefloquine (AS/MQ)**
	    
	    - **Drug Components:** Artesunate (100 mg BD x 3 days) + Mefloquine (750 mg on Day 2, 500 mg on Day 3).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:**
	        - Historically **extensively used** in Southeast Asia and Africa.
	        - MQ-resistance is common in some regions (e.g., Thailand, Cambodia, Myanmar), but sporadic in India.
	        - **MQ has neuropsychiatric side effects** (e.g., seizures, psychiatric disorders), and can cause QTc prolongation, making this ACT **less favoured now**.
	        - **Contraindicated in cerebral malaria** due to neuropsychiatric adverse effects.
	4. **Artesunate-Amodiaquine (AS/AQ)**
	    
	    - **Drug Components:** Artesunate (e.g., 25/50/100 mg) + Amodiaquine (e.g., 67.5/135/270 mg) fixed-dose combination (FDC) tablets, daily x 3 days (dose ratios vary by age/BW).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:**
	        - Amodiaquine is similar to CQ but **effective in areas with CQ-resistant _Pf_**.
	        - Cofor-mulated FDC is **first-line therapy in many African countries**.
	        - Approved and marketed in India based on successful trials.
	        - Side effects similar to CQ; **neutropenia and hepatotoxicity** are concerns with prolonged use (not relevant for short-course ACT).
	5. **Arterolane-Piperaquine**
	    
	    - **Drug Components:** Arterolane (150 mg) + Piperaquine (750 mg) once daily x 3 days.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:**
	        - Arterolane is a novel synthetic trioxolane artemisinin congener developed in India.
	        - Piperaquine is a bisquinoline congener of CQ, more active against CQ-resistant _Pf_.
	        - Good safety profile and high cure rates (~95%).
	        - Considered an **effective and well-tolerated alternative ACT**.
	        - Dihydroartemisinin (DHA) is also coformulated with Piperaquine (DHA-Piperaquine), extensively evaluated in multidrug-resistant areas with high success.
	6. **Artesunate-Pyronaridine**
	    
	    - **Drug Components:** Artesunate + Pyronaridine (dose ratio 1:3 FDC).
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Pyronaridine resembles amodiaquine structurally, mechanism similar to CQ. Active against both CQ-sensitive and CQ-resistant _Pf_ and _P. vivax_. Clinical trials completed in India with high cure rates (>95%), but **not yet approved for use in India**.
	
	**IV. Alternative/Adjunctive Regimens for CQ-Resistant _Pf_ Malaria (when ACT is not feasible or appropriate)**
	
	- **Quinine + Doxycycline/Clindamycin**
	    
	    - **Quinine:** 600 mg (10 mg/kg) 8 hourly x 7 days.
	    - **Doxycycline:** 100 mg daily x 7 days (slow-acting).
	        - **Contraindicated in children (< 6-8 years) and pregnant women** due to dental and bone effects.
	    - **Clindamycin:** 600 mg 12 hourly x 7 days (slow-acting).
	        - **Can be used in children and pregnant women** (unlike doxycycline), making it a valuable alternative.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Quinine remains effective against most CQ-resistant _Pf_ strains, often used as an alternative to ACT. Doxycycline or clindamycin are added for complete parasite clearance. **Quinine + Clindamycin is the drug of choice for _P. falciparum_ malaria during the first trimester of pregnancy**.
	- **Atovaquone + Proguanil (MALARONE)**
	    
	    - **Activity:** Atovaquone is a rapid-acting erythrocytic schizontocide and also active against pre-erythrocytic stages of _Pf_. Proguanil potentiates its action and prevents resistance.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Used for treatment of uncomplicated CQ-resistant _Pf_ malaria and as prophylaxis for non-immune travellers in some countries (USA, UK), but **not in India** for treatment.
