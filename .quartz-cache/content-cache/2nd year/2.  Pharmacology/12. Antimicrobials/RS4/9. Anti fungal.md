1. Groups of drugs used in the treatment of fungal infections
## **ANTIFUNGAL DRUGS – Classification & Key Points**

### **1. Antibiotics**

- **Polyenes**
    
    - **Drugs:** Amphotericin B, Nystatin, Hamycin
        
    - **MOA:** Bind to **ergosterol** in fungal cell membrane → form pores → leakage of intracellular contents.
        
    - **Uses:**
        
        - Amphotericin B → severe systemic mycoses (cryptococcosis, mucormycosis, histoplasmosis).
            
        - Nystatin → topical/oral (not absorbed), for _Candida_ infections (oral thrush, vaginal candidiasis).
            
    - **ADR:** Amphotericin B → nephrotoxicity, electrolyte imbalance (↓K⁺, ↓Mg²⁺), fever/chills.
        
- **Echinocandins**
    
    - **Drugs:** Caspofungin, Micafungin, Anidulafungin
        
    - **MOA:** Inhibit **β-1,3-glucan synthase** → disrupt fungal cell wall synthesis.
        
    - **Uses:** Invasive _Candida_, _Aspergillus_.
        
    - **ADR:** Mild hepatotoxicity, infusion reactions.
        

---

### **2. Antimetabolite**

- **Flucytosine**
    
    - **MOA:** Converted to 5-fluorouracil in fungi → inhibits DNA & RNA synthesis.
        
    - **Uses:** In combination with Amphotericin B for cryptococcal meningitis.
        
    - **ADR:** Bone marrow suppression, GI upset.
        

---

### **3. Heterocyclic Benzofuran**

- **Griseofulvin**
    
    - **MOA:** Binds fungal microtubules → inhibits mitosis.
        
    - **Uses:** Dermatophyte infections (_tinea_, _microsporum_, _trichophyton_).
        
    - **ADR:** Hepatotoxicity, photosensitivity, teratogenic.
        

---

### **4. Azoles**

- **MOA:** Inhibit **lanosterol 14-α-demethylase** → block ergosterol synthesis → disrupt membrane.
    
- **Subclasses:**
    
    - **Imidazoles**
        
        - **Topical:** Clotrimazole, Econazole, Miconazole, Oxiconazole  
            → Used for dermatophytes, candidiasis.
            
        - **Systemic:** Ketoconazole  
            → Used for systemic & superficial mycoses (less used now due to hepatotoxicity, adrenal suppression).
            
    - **Triazoles**
        
        - **Fluconazole:** Cryptococcal meningitis (good CSF penetration), _Candida_.
            
        - **Itraconazole:** Blastomycosis, histoplasmosis, sporotrichosis.
            
        - **Voriconazole:** Invasive _Aspergillus_.
            
        - **Posaconazole:** Prophylaxis in immunocompromised, mucormycosis.
            

---

### **5. Allylamine**

- **Terbinafine**
    
    - **MOA:** Inhibits **squalene epoxidase** → blocks ergosterol synthesis.
        
    - **Uses:** Onychomycosis (nail fungal infections), dermatophytes.
        
    - **ADR:** Hepatotoxicity, taste disturbance.
        

---

### **6. Topical Agents**

- **Tolnaftate, Undecylenic acid, Benzoic acid, Ciclopirox olamine, Butenafine, Quiniodochlor, Sodium thiosulfate**  
    → Used for mild superficial fungal infections (tinea, candidiasis).
    

---

## **Mnemonics for Exams**

- **Polyenes punch holes** (bind ergosterol → pores).
    
- **Echinocandins end cell wall** (β-1,3-glucan synthase inhibition).
    
- **Azoles arrest ergosterol** (lanosterol demethylase block).
    
- **Allylamines attack early** (squalene epoxidase).
    
- **Flucytosine fools DNA** (nucleic acid synthesis inhibitor).
    
- **Griseofulvin grinds mitosis** (microtubules).

2. Topically used antifungal drugs and uses
## **1. Azoles (Imidazoles & Triazoles)**

**MOA:** Inhibit **14-α-demethylase** → ↓ ergosterol synthesis → membrane instability.  
**Examples & Uses:**

- **Clotrimazole** – Tinea corporis, cruris, pedis; cutaneous/vaginal/oral candidiasis.
    
- **Miconazole** – Similar uses; also for vulvovaginal candidiasis.
    
- **Ketoconazole** – Seborrheic dermatitis, pityriasis versicolor, dermatophytes.
    
- **Econazole / Oxiconazole / Sertaconazole** – Dermatophytes, Candida, pityriasis versicolor.
    

**Pearl:** Imidazoles are mostly fungistatic; ketoconazole shampoo for dandruff is common USMLE favorite.

---

## **2. Allylamines**

**MOA:** Inhibit **squalene epoxidase** → accumulation of squalene → membrane damage (fungicidal).  
**Examples & Uses:**

- **Terbinafine** – Tinea pedis, corporis, cruris (topical); oral for onychomycosis.
    
- **Naftifine** – Tinea pedis, corporis, cruris.
    
- **Butenafine** – Tinea infections (topical only).
    

**Pearl:** Allylamines are fungicidal (vs. azoles fungistatic).

---

## **3. Polyenes**

**MOA:** Bind **ergosterol** → pore formation → leakage of cell contents (fungicidal).  
**Examples & Uses:**

- **Nystatin** – Oral thrush (“swish & swallow”), cutaneous candidiasis, vaginal candidiasis, diaper rash.
    
- **Topical Amphotericin B** – Fungal keratitis, cutaneous leishmaniasis (rare topical use).
    

**Pearl:** Nystatin is too toxic for systemic use, but safe topically.

---

## **4. Hydroxyquinolines**

**MOA:** Chelate metals → inhibit metal-dependent fungal enzymes.  
**Example & Use:**

- **Quiniodochlor** – Dermatophytosis, superficial candidiasis, trichomoniasis (topical).
    

---

## **5. Fatty Acids**

**MOA:** Alter fungal cell membrane integrity.  
**Example & Use:**

- **Undecylenic acid** – Mild tinea pedis, corporis.
    

---

## **6. Other Miscellaneous Agents**

- **Tolnaftate** – Distorts hyphae; tinea corporis, cruris, pedis (ineffective against Candida).
    
- **Benzoic acid** – Acidic pH inhibits fungal growth; part of Whitfield’s ointment for tinea.
    
- **Ciclopirox olamine** – Chelates cations; tinea, Candida, pityriasis versicolor, onychomycosis (nail lacquer).
    
- **Sodium thiosulfate** – Releases sulfur compounds; tinea versicolor, some dermatophytes.
    
- **Selenium sulfide** – Cytostatic to epidermis; pityriasis versicolor, seborrheic dermatitis, tinea capitis adjuvant.

3. Advantages of liposomal amphotericin B?
	### **Liposomal Amphotericin B (L-AMB): Key Advantages**
	
	- **Improved Tolerability & Reduced Toxicity**:
	    
	    - Produces **milder acute reactions** on intravenous (i.v.) infusion (e.g., chills, fever, aches) compared to conventional Amphotericin B (C-AMB).
	    - **Lower nephrotoxicity**.
	    - Causes **minimal anaemia**.
	    - Can be used in patients who do not tolerate conventional AMB formulations.
	- **Targeted Delivery**:
	    
	    - Delivers Amphotericin B specifically to **reticuloendothelial cells** in the liver and spleen.
	    - **Clinical Relevance**: This makes it especially valuable for conditions like **kala-azar** (visceral leishmaniasis) and in immunocompromised patients, as the amastigotes of _Leishmania_ reside within macrophages in these organs.
	- **Enhanced Efficacy & Convenience**:
	    
	    - Maintains **similar clinical efficacy** to C-AMB for systemic mycoses and kala-azar.
	    - Can be infused at **higher rates** (3-5 mg/kg/day).
	    - **High cure rates** for kala-azar, including single-dose regimens (e.g., 90% cure at 5 mg/kg, 98% at 10 mg/kg).
	    - In combination therapy (e.g., with Miltefosine or Paromomycin for kala-azar), it yields **98-99% cure rates** and can **shorten treatment duration**.
	- **Specific Indications**:
	    
	    - Specifically indicated for **empirical therapy in febrile neutropenic patients** unresponsive to antibacterial antibiotics.
	    - Used in critically ill deep mycosis cases and **kala-azar**.
	
	**High-Yield Note**: While L-AMB offers significant advantages in terms of reduced toxicity and improved delivery, it is **many times costlier** than conventional Amphotericin B, which remains a significant factor in its widespread use, particularly in resource-limited settings.
