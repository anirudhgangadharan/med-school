1. Write the one drug regimen for multibacillary leprosy. Why drug combinations are used to treat leprosy?
	
	**Treatment of Multibacillary Leprosy (MBL)**
	
	- **One Drug Regimen:** There is **NO currently recommended one-drug regimen** for multibacillary leprosy. Historically, dapsone monotherapy was used, but this is **no longer practised**.
	- **Standard Regimen:** The standard treatment for MBL is **Multidrug Therapy (MDT)**, as recommended by the WHO and implemented under the National Leprosy Eradication Programme (NLEP).
	    - **Drugs:**
	        - **Rifampin:** 600 mg once a month (supervised).
	        - **Dapsone:** 100 mg daily (self-administered).
	        - **Clofazimine:** 300 mg once a month (supervised) + 50 mg daily (self-administered).
	    - **Duration:** **12 months**.
	
	**Why Drug Combinations (MDT) are Used in Leprosy:**
	
	The use of drug combinations is a fundamental principle in treating chronic infections requiring prolonged therapy, like leprosy, tuberculosis, HIV, and malaria. The reasons are primarily to overcome challenges associated with monotherapy:
	
	1. **Prevent Emergence of Drug Resistance:** _Mycobacterium leprae_ can develop resistance to individual drugs like dapsone and clofazimine when used alone.
	    - Combining drugs significantly **reduces the chance of resistant mutants surviving**.
	    - Mutation conferring resistance to one antimicrobial is independent of that conferring resistance to another. The chance of a bacillus being resistant to multiple drugs simultaneously is exceedingly low (e.g., 10⁻⁵ for drug P and 10⁻⁷ for drug Q means 1 in 10¹² is resistant to both).
	    - This prevents the selection and propagation of resistant strains, a major issue with historical dapsone monotherapy. Rifampin's inclusion in MDT helps prevent resistance development.
	2. **Effective Against Existing Resistance:** MDT is effective even in cases with **primary dapsone resistance** (contracted from a resistant source) or secondary dapsone resistance (developed during monotherapy).
	3. **Eliminate Microbial Persisters:** Some _M. leprae_ bacilli can become dormant and hide in tissues, becoming less susceptible to single drugs. These "persisters" can cause relapse after treatment withdrawal. MDT helps to **eliminate these persisters**, though some may still remain, necessitating defined treatment durations.
	4. **Shorten Treatment Duration:** MDT significantly **reduces the required treatment time** compared to historical monotherapy regimens.
	5. **Afford Quicker Symptom Relief and Stop Disease Progression:** The combined action leads to a **faster clinical response** and halts the worsening of the disease (e.g., skin lesions start regressing, nasal symptoms subside rapidly with Rifampin).
	6. **Higher Efficacy and Cure Rate:** Combination therapy achieves **better overall treatment success**.
	
	In summary, MDT is essential for treating MBL not just for symptom relief, but critically for **preventing resistance, eliminating persistent bacilli, and improving long-term cure rates**, thereby effectively controlling the disease at both individual and population levels.
