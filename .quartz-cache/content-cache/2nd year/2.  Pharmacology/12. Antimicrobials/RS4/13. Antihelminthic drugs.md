1. Albendazole uses
	### **Albendazole: Clinical Uses**
	
	**Key Concept:** Albendazole is a **broad-spectrum benzimidazole anthelmintic** effective against a wide range of intestinal and tissue helminthic infections. It is largely preferred due to its **single-dose administration** advantage in many infestations.
	
	#### **Major Indications (High Yield)**
	
	- **Intestinal Nematode Infestations:**
	    
	    - Highly effective against **roundworm** (_Ascaris lumbricoides_), **hookworm** (_Ancylostoma duodenale, Necator americanus_), **pinworm** (_Enterobius vermicularis_), **whipworm** (_Trichuris trichiura_), and **mixed worm infestations**.
	    - **Strongyloidiasis** (_Strongyloides stercoralis_): More effective than mebendazole. A 3-day course achieves nearly 50% cure, with a second course after 3 weeks curing practically all patients.
	    - _Clinical Point:_ While effective in trichuriasis, mebendazole is generally considered more effective for this specific infestation.
	- **Neurocysticercosis:**
	    
	    - **Anthelmintic of choice** for neurocysticercosis.
	    - **Preferred over praziquantel** due to:
	        - **Cheaper cost**.
	        - **Shorter treatment duration** (typically 8–15 days vs. 15–30 days for praziquantel).
	        - **Higher cure rates** (75–85% vs. 50–60% for praziquantel).
	        - **Better concentration** in brain and CSF.
	        - **Glucocorticoids** (given concurrently to suppress inflammatory reaction to dying larvae) **increase** albendazole sulphoxide plasma levels, whereas they decrease praziquantel levels.
	    - _Clinical Point:_ **Contraindicated in ocular cysticercosis** due to risk of blindness from inflammatory reaction to dead cysticerci.
	- **Hydatid Disease:**
	    
	    - **Drug of choice for medical therapy** in hydatid cyst.
	    - Often given **before and after surgery**, and for **inoperable cases**.
	    - Results in hydatid disease have been **superior to mebendazole**.
	
	#### **Other Important Uses**
	
	- **Cutaneous Larva Migrans:**
	    
	    - **Drug of choice**, kills larvae and relieves symptoms.
	    - Effective also for **visceral larva migrans**.
	- **Filariasis (Lymphatic):**
	    
	    - Has **adjuvant value** when combined with diethylcarbamazine (DEC) or ivermectin in lymphatic filariasis.
	    - A single dose combination (400 mg albendazole + DEC/ivermectin) given yearly is used in **mass programmes** to suppress microfilaraemia and disease transmission.
	    - Yearly mass treatment with 400 mg albendazole in children is recommended by WHO to lower worm burden.
	
	#### **Pharmacokinetic Note (Clinical Relevance)**
	
	- Albendazole is absorbed after oral administration, but absorption is **enhanced when taken with a fatty meal**, which is beneficial for tissue infections like neurocysticercosis and hydatid disease.
	- It is converted by first-pass metabolism to its **active sulfoxide metabolite**, which is widely distributed in the body, **enters the brain**, and exerts anthelmintic activity in tissues.
2. Ivermectin uses
	### **Ivermectin: Clinical Uses**
	
	**Key Concept:** Ivermectin is an **extremely potent semisynthetic anthelmintic** derived from _Streptomyces avermitilis_, primarily known for its efficacy against **nematodes** and certain **ectoparasites**.
	
	#### **Major Indications (High Yield)**
	
	- **Onchocerciasis (River Blindness):**
	    - **Drug of choice**.
	    - **Kills microfilariae** but has little effect on adult worms.
	    - Administered as a **single dose** at 6–12 month intervals.
	    - Produces **long-lasting reduction of microfilariae** counts in the eye and skin.
	    - Suppresses ocular inflammation/damage and lymphadenopathy with mild reactions.
	    - Used in **WHO's 'river blindness' control programme** in Africa and Latin America.
	- **Strongyloidiasis** (_Strongyloides stercoralis_):
	    - **Drug of choice** for single-dose treatment.
	    - A single 0.15–0.2 mg/kg dose yields the highest cure rate.
	    - Repeat doses may be given if stool remains positive.
	    - For hyperinfection syndrome in immunocompromised patients, daily treatment is needed until the syndrome subsides and larvae are eradicated.
	- **Cutaneous Larva Migrans:**
	    - **Highly effective** (single 0.2 mg/kg dose).
	- **Scabies and Pediculosis:**
	    - **Only oral drug effective** for these conditions.
	    - A single 0.2 mg/kg dose cures most patients.
	    - Used orally for scabies and pediculosis.
	
	#### **Other Important Uses**
	
	- **Filariasis (Lymphatic):**
	    - **Comparable to DEC** for bancroftian and brugian filaria.
	    - **Microfilaricidal** but not macrofilaricidal.
	    - Used in **mass chemotherapy programs** as a single annual dose (10–15 mg or 0.2 mg/kg), preferably with 400 mg albendazole, for 5–6 years.
	    - **Suppresses microfilaraemia**.
	- **Ascariasis:**
	    - **Highly effective** (single 0.2 mg/kg dose).
	    - Reduces the burden of other intestinal nematodes.
	- **Enterobius and Trichuris:**
	    - Moderate efficacy against these nematodes.
	    - Used as an add-on drug with albendazole/mebendazole in heavy trichuriasis.
	
	#### **Pharmacokinetic & Clinical Notes (Relevant to Uses)**
	
	- **Oral Administration:** Well absorbed orally.
	- **Distribution:** Widely distributed in the body.
	- **Long Half-life:** Sequestrated in liver and fat, with a **long terminal half-life of 48–60 hours**. This prolonged presence supports its single-dose or infrequent dosing regimens for many conditions.
	- **CNS Penetration:** Does not enter the CNS.
	- **Mechanism of Action (Brief):** Activates glutamate-gated chloride channels, leading to hyperpolarization and paralysis of worms. It also increases GABA transmission in worms.
	- **Contraindications/Precautions:** **Contraindicated in pregnancy and young children** due to safety not being established. It is also contraindicated in pregnant and lactating women for scabies/pediculosis.
