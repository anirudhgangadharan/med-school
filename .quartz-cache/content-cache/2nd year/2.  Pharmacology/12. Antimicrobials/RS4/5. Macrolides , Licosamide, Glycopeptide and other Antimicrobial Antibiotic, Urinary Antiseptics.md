1. What is pseudomembranous enterocolitis? Drug treatment for pseudomembranous colitis with the drug names
	**Pseudomembranous Enterocolitis**
	
	- **Definition/Cause:**
	    
	    - Caused by _**Clostridium difficile**_.
	    - Generally associated with the use of antibiotics.
	    - Commonly linked to antibiotics such as **clindamycin, tetracyclines, aminoglycosides, ampicillin**, etc..
	    - More common after colorectal surgery.
	    - The _C. difficile_ organism produces an **enterotoxin** which damages the gut mucosa, forming characteristic plaques.
	- **Drug Treatment:**
	    
	    - **Drugs of Choice:** **Metronidazole** and **Vancomycin** are the drugs of choice.
	    - **Preferred First-Line:** **Oral metronidazole** is often preferred.
	        - It is considered more effective, more convenient, and less toxic.
	        - This is because it is nearly completely absorbed from the upper bowel, meaning less active drug reaches the lower bowel compared to Vancomycin.
	        - Dose: **400-800 mg BD-TDS** for **10-14 days**.
	    - **Alternative/Second-Line:** **Vancomycin** may be used.
	        - It is indicated in **nonresponsive cases** or when the infection **recurs**.
