1. Laboratory diagnosis of intestinal amoebiasis
	**Laboratory Diagnosis of Intestinal Amoebiasis**
	- **Specimen Collection:**
	    - **Stool is specimen of choice**.
	    - Collect minimum of **three stool samples on alternate days** (within 10 days) due to intermittent shedding.
	    - Other samples: Rectal exudates, rectal ulcer tissues (collected by colonoscopy).
	    - **Examine immediately** within 1–2 hours or preserve (merthiolate iodine, formalin); **refrigeration not recommended**.
	- **Stool Macroscopy:**
	    - Differentiate from bacillary dysentery: Foul smelling, copious, dark red with blood/mucus, not adherent to container (Bacillary dysentery stool is bright red, odorless, adherent).
	- **Microscopy (Stool):**
	    - Direct examination (saline/iodine mount) for trophozoites and cysts.
	    - Poor sensitivity with single sample (25-60%); increases to ~90% with six samples.
	    - Sensitivity improved by concentration (formalin ether sedimentation).
	    - Permanent stains (trichrome, iron hematoxylin) demonstrate internal structures well.
	    - **Trophozoites:**
	        - **Gold standard microscopic test for active infection**.
	        - Present in freshly-passed dysenteric stool.
	        - Better appreciated in saline mount (motility seen). Iodine kills trophozoites.
	        - Presence of **RBCs in trophozoites** can differentiate _E. histolytica_ from other _Entamoeba_ species, but RBCs may be absent after dysentery ends.
	    - **Cysts:** Found in stool. Cysts and trophozoites are microscopically indistinguishable from _E. dispar/E. moshkovskii/E. bangladeshi_, except for RBCs in _E. histolytica_ trophozoites. Report as "cyst or trophozoite of _E. histolytica/E. dispar/E.moshkovskii/E. bangladeshi_ seen".
	- **Stool Antigen Detection (Coproantigen):**
	    - Use fresh or frozen stool (preservatives denature antigens).
	    - **ELISA:** Detects 170 kDa lectin antigen. **High sensitivity (>95%) and specificity (>95%)**. **Can differentiate pathogenic _E. histolytica_ (lectin antigen positive) from nonpathogenic _E. dispar_**.
	    - **ICT (Immunochromatographic test):** Rapid (20-30 mins). **Triage parasite panel** detects _E. histolytica/E. dispar_ (cannot differentiate), _Giardia lamblia_, and _Cryptosporidium parvum_ antigens simultaneously. Good sensitivity (83-96%) and specificity (99-100%).
	- **Molecular Diagnosis:**
	    
	    - **Gold standard test**.
	    - **Nested multiplex PCR:** Targets small subunit rRNA genes. **Can differentiate _E. histolytica_, _E. dispar_, and _E. moshkovskii_**. Sensitivity ~90%, specificity 90-100%.
	    - **Real-time PCR:** Targets 18S rRNA gene. **More sensitive**, can quantitate parasite load, faster than conventional PCR.
	    - **BioFire FilmArray:** Automated PCR system. Gastrointestinal panel detects _E. histolytica_ among other common diarrheal pathogens directly from stool.
	- **Serology (Serum):**
	    - **Amoebic Antigen:** ELISA detects 170 kDa lectin antigen. **Indicates recent/active infection**. Usually positive in early stage (sensitivity 65%).
	    - **Amoebic Antibody (IgG):** ELISA detects antibodies against lectin antigen. **Appear in later stages**. Less useful for early diagnosis.
	- **Culture (Stool):**
	    - Can be performed using polyxenic and axenic media (e.g., Diamond’s medium).
	- **Histology:**
	    - Intestinal biopsies stained with PAS or H&E can reveal trophozoites.
	- **Isoenzyme (Zymodeme) Analysis:**
	    - Can differentiate _E. histolytica_ from _E. dispar_.
	    - Technically difficult, time-consuming, **not routinely used**.
	- **Other Non-specific Findings:**
	    - Charcot-Leyden crystals in stool (diamond shaped, eosinophilic breakdown products).
	    - Moderate leukocytosis.
	    - Imaging (Colonoscopy): Can detect ulcers.
2. Lab diagnosis of Giardiasis
	**Laboratory Diagnosis of Giardiasis**
	
	- **Etiological Agent:** _Giardia lamblia_ (also known as _Giardia intestinalis_ or _Giardia duodenalis_).
	    
	- **Morphological Forms:** Pear-shaped trophozoite (pathogenic/feeding stage), Tetra-nucleated oval cyst (infective/diagnostic form).
	    
	- **Infective Form:** Cysts.
	    
	- **Diagnostic Form(s):** Cysts and Trophozoites in stool.
	    
	- **Specimen Collection:**
	    
	    - **Stool is the specimen of choice** for microscopy.
	    - Need **repeated stool examinations** (e.g., 3 samples) due to intermittent parasite excretion.
	    - Environmental samples (water) can be used for molecular methods.
	- **Microscopy (Stool):**
	    
	    - Considered the **gold standard for diagnosis**.
	    - Direct wet mount (saline/iodine) detects cysts and trophozoites.
	    - **Trophozoites:**
	        - Found in **freshly passed (acute) stool**.
	        - Pear-shaped, bi-nucleated.
	        - Exhibit characteristic **darting** or **falling leaf-like motility** in saline mount. Iodine kills trophozoites.
	    - **Cysts:**
	        - Found in **formed stool** (chronic cases or carriers).
	        - Oval shaped, tetra-nucleated. Internal structures seen clearer with iodine.
	    - **Concentration Techniques:** Recommended to increase the yield if parasite load is low. Formalin ether sedimentation is a common method.
	    - **Permanent Stains:** Trichrome or Giemsa stain can be used for better visualization of internal structures.
	- **Antigen Detection (Coproantigen) in Stool:**
	    
	    - **Highly sensitive (90-100%) and specific (99-100%)**.
	    - Useful for microscopy-negative samples and outbreak situations.
	    - **ELISA:** Detects _Giardia_ specific cyst wall protein antigens.
	    - **DFA (Direct Fluorescent Antibody):** Also uses labeled monoclonal antibodies; highly sensitive.
	    - **ICT (Immunochromatographic Test):** Rapid test (e.g., Triage parasite panel) that can simultaneously detect antigens of _Giardia_, _Cryptosporidium_, and _E. histolytica_. Sensitivity 83-96%, specificity 99-100%.
	- **Molecular Diagnosis:**
	    
	    - Detection of _Giardia_ nucleic acid (DNA/RNA) by **PCR or gene probes**.
	    - **Highly sensitive and specific**; can detect as low as 1-2 cysts.
	    - **BioFire FilmArray:** Automated multiplex PCR system with a gastrointestinal panel that includes _Giardia_.
	- **Culture:**
	    
	    - Can be cultivated in axenic media (e.g., Diamond’s medium, used for _E. histolytica_).
	    - Primarily used for **research purposes** and preparing antigens. **Not routinely used for diagnosis**.
	- **Clinical Relevance:**
	    
	    - Symptoms include foul-smelling diarrhea, flatus, belching, weight loss.
	    - Causes malabsorption.
	    - Intermittent shedding of cysts necessitates multiple stool samples.
	    - Antigen detection and molecular methods offer higher sensitivity than a single stool microscopy.
	- **Expected Questions/Clinical Scenarios:** Often involves a patient with recurrent foul-smelling diarrhea, gas, or weight loss, particularly with a history of consuming untreated water or recent travel. Lab diagnosis will focus on identifying the parasite (cysts/trophozoites), antigen, or nucleic acid in stool.
