1. A 13-year-old boy developed severe watery diarrhea and vomiting. The stool collected has a rice water type of appearance.
	
	It was sent for bacterial culture:
	
	a. What is the probable etiological agent of this condition?
	
	b. Describe in detail the pathogenesis of this condition.
	
	c. Describe the laboratory diagnosis of this organism.
	
	d. Add a note on prophylaxis and prevention.
	**Clinical Scenario: 13-year-old boy with severe watery diarrhea, vomiting, and rice water stool**
	
	This clinical presentation, particularly the description of "severe watery diarrhea and vomiting" and "rice water type of appearance" of the stool, is highly suggestive of an infection caused by **_Vibrio cholerae_**.
	
	**a. Probable Etiological Agent:**
	
	- The probable etiological agent is **_Vibrio cholerae_**.
	    - It causes **watery diarrhea** which can be severe, with vomiting.
	    - The stool is typically described as **non-bilious, slightly cloudy, and watery with mucus flakes**, resembling **rice water**.
	    - The onset of symptoms in food poisoning due to _V. cholerae_ is typically >16 hours.
	
	**b. Pathogenesis:**
	
	- The pathogenesis of _V. cholerae_ is mainly **toxin mediated**.
	- _V. cholerae_ is a **noninvasive** organism.
	- It produces a potent **cholera toxin**.
	- This toxin's mechanism involves a second messenger system, specifically **cAMP**.
	- The toxin causes the rapid loss of fluid and electrolytes from the intestine, leading to the characteristic watery diarrhea.
	- Being noninvasive, there is **no associated blood or pus cells** in the stool.
	- Progression of clinical manifestations is directly related to the loss of fluid and electrolytes.
	- Muscle cramps may occur due to electrolyte imbalance.
	- People with **blood group O** show increased susceptibility.
	
	**c. Laboratory Diagnosis:**
	
	- **Specimen:**
	    - Freshly collected **watery stool** is the specimen of choice for acute cases.
	    - Ideally collected **before starting antibiotics**.
	    - Rectal swab is preferred for convalescent patients or carriers.
	- **Transport:**
	    - Specimens should be transported **immediately**.
	    - If delay is expected, stool or rectal swabs should be inoculated into **transport media** such as:
	        - **Venkatraman-Ramakrishnan (VR) medium**
	        - **Cary-Blair medium** (also useful for _Salmonella_ and _Shigella_)
	        - Alkaline peptone water (if cholera suspected).
	- **Direct Microscopy:**
	    - **Gram staining** of mucus flakes shows **short curved comma-shaped gram-negative rods**.
	    - They may be arranged in parallel rows, described by Koch as **"fish in stream appearance"**.
	    - **Hanging drop** method demonstrates **darting motility**, also described as actively motile, frequently changing direction, shooting star, or swarming gnats motility.
	- **Culture:**
	    - _V. cholerae_ is **strongly aerobic** and non-fastidious, growing on ordinary media.
	    - To inhibit commensals, inoculate simultaneously onto **enrichment broth** and **selective media**.
	    - Media typically contain salt (0.5-1%) and have **alkaline pH** to stimulate growth and inhibit fecal commensals.
	    - **Enrichment Broth:** Alkaline peptone water (APW) or Monsur’s taurocholate tellurite peptone water. Incubate for 4-6 hours before subculture.
	    - **Selective Media:**
	        - **TCBS agar** (Thiosulfate citrate bile salts sucrose agar): Produces **sucrose fermenting yellow colored colonies**. This is a selective medium for _V. cholerae_.
	        - Bile salt agar, Monsur’s GTTT agar.
	        - **MacConkey agar**: Produces translucent **non-lactose fermenting (NLF) colonies**, which may become pink on prolonged incubation due to late lactose fermentation. MacConkey agar can be included when the enteric pathogen is uncertain as it supports other pathogens like _Shigella_ and _Salmonella_.
	    - Culture plates are incubated at 37°C.
	    - Culture smear shows short curved gram-negative bacilli.
	    - Hanging drop confirms darting motility.
	- **Identification:**
	    - Identification from colonies can be made by **conventional biochemical tests** or automated systems such as **VITEK or MALDI-TOF**.
	    - Biochemical characteristics: **Catalase positive**, **Oxidase positive**, **ICUT tests**: Indole (+), Citrate (+/–), Urease (–), TSI: A/A, gas (–), H2S (–). **String test positive**. Produces hemodigestion on blood agar.
	    - **Serogrouping:** Agglutinates with _Vibrio cholerae_ O1 antisera and Ogawa antisera (most common pattern). Can differentiate O1 and O139.
	    - **Serotyping:** Differentiates O1 serotypes Ogawa, Inaba, and Hikojima. Hikojima serotype agglutinates with both Ogawa and Inaba antisera.
	- **Antigen Detection:** Cholera dipstick assay is available.
	- **Molecular Methods:** Multiplex PCR can detect common diarrheal pathogens, including _V. cholerae_.
	- **Biotyping:** Differentiates classical and El Tor biotypes. El Tor is generally more resistant to bleaching powder.
	- **Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST):** Necessary for treatment guidance.
	
	**d. Prophylaxis and Prevention:**
	
	- The sources mention **water purification using bleaching powder** (6 mg/L) as a preventive measure.
	- While rehydration therapy (fluid therapy) is the **mainstay of treatment**, it is not described as prophylaxis.
	- Antibiotics are used for treatment but are not mentioned for prophylaxis in the sources.
	- No information on _V. cholerae_ vaccines is provided in the sources. Prevention via avoiding contaminated food/water is a general principle for enteric infections but not explicitly stated as prophylaxis for cholera in these excerpts.
2. Cholera-pathogenesis and laboratory diagnosis
	**Cholera: Pathogenesis and Laboratory Diagnosis**
	
	**1. Pathogenesis:**
	
	- **Causative Agent:** _**Vibrio cholerae**_, primarily serogroups **O1** and **O139**.
	    - Gram-negative bacilli.
	    - Short, curved, comma-shaped rods.
	    - **Darting motility**.
	- **Mode of Transmission:** Ingestion of **contaminated water or food**.
	- **Infective Dose:** **High**, typically **108 bacilli**, needed to overcome gastric acidity. _V. cholerae_ is extremely acid-labile.
	- **Key Virulence Factor:** **Cholera toxin**.
	    - Produced by _V. cholerae_ O1 and O139.
	    - Mechanism of action (not detailed in sources, but is the basis of pathogenesis): Affects epithelial cells to cause massive fluid secretion.
	- **Other Virulence Factors:**
	    - **Zona occludens toxin:** Disrupts tight junctions between mucosal cells.
	    - **Accessory colonization factors:** Aid in adhesion and colonization.
	    - Bacterial endotoxin (LPS): Immunogenic but **does not contribute to cholera pathogenesis**.
	- **Host Factors:** People with **blood group O** show increased susceptibility.
	- **Clinical Outcome (Pathogenesis leading to symptoms):** Infection can range from asymptomatic (75%) to mild diarrhea (20%) or **sudden onset explosive and life-threatening diarrhea** (5%). This severe diarrhea is characteristic of cholera.
	
	**2. Laboratory Diagnosis:**
	
	- **Specimen Collection:**
	    - **Fresh watery stool** is the specimen of choice for acute cases, ideally collected before antibiotics.
	    - Stool containing **mucus flakes** is preferred.
	    - Rectal swab is preferred for convalescent patients or carriers.
	- **Specimen Transport:**
	    - Should be transported **immediately**.
	    - If delay is expected (>1 hour), use **transport media** such as **Cary-Blair medium** or **alkaline peptone water**.
	- **Direct Microscopy:**
	    - **Wet mount:** Not specifically mentioned for cellular components of cholera stool, but generally used for stool.
	    - **Hanging drop:** Demonstrates the characteristic **darting motility** of _V. cholerae_ in liquid specimens.
	    - **Gram stain:** **Not routinely done** due to normal flora. In special situations, may show **gram-negative comma-shaped bacilli**.
	- **Culture:**
	    - _V. cholerae_ is strongly aerobic and non-fastidious.
	    - Specimens are inoculated into **enrichment broth** and **selective media** simultaneously to inhibit commensals.
	    - **Enrichment Broth:** Alkaline peptone water (APW) or Monsur’s taurocholate tellurite peptone water. Incubate 4-6 hours, then subculture.
	    - **Selective Media:**
	        - Media should contain salt (0.5-1%) and have alkaline pH.
	        - **MacConkey agar:** Produces **translucent non-lactose fermenting (NLF) pale colonies**, which may become pink on prolonged incubation (late lactose fermentation). Supports other enteric pathogens like _Shigella_ and _Salmonella_.
	        - (TCBS agar is standard but not mentioned in the provided sources for _V. cholerae_ diagnosis).
	    - Incubation: 37°C for 24 hours.
	- **Identification:**
	    - Gram stain of culture smear: Shows **short curved gram-negative bacilli**.
	    - Motility testing (hanging drop): Confirms **darting motility**.
	    - **Biochemical Tests:**
	        - **Catalase positive**.
	        - **Oxidase positive**.
	        - **ICUT:** Indole (+), Citrate (+/-), Urease (–).
	        - **TSI:** Alkaline slant/acid butt (A/A), **gas absent, H2S absent**.
	        - **String test positive**.
	    - Automated systems: MALDI-TOF and VITEK can be used.
	    - Biotyping (Classical/El Tor) and Serotyping (Ogawa/Inaba/Hikojima) for O1.
	    - Serogrouping (O1/O139).
	- **Antigen Detection:** **Cholera dipstick assay**.
	- **Molecular Methods:** **Multiplex PCR** detecting common diarrheal pathogens can include _V. cholerae_.
	- **Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST):**
	    - **Essential** due to increasing drug resistance.
	    - Performed on Mueller Hinton agar by disk diffusion test.

3. An adult male complains of passing loose watery stool and vomiting since one day.
	
	On examination, patient is dehydrated.
	
	a. What is the presumptive clinical diagnosis?
	
	b. Enumerate viral and bacterial causes of diarrhea.
	
	c. Laboratory diagnosis of cholera.
	
	**Clinical Scenario: Adult male with loose watery stool, vomiting, dehydration (since 1 day)**
	
	**a. Presumptive Clinical Diagnosis:**
	
	- The presumptive clinical diagnosis is **Acute Gastroenteritis** or **Infectious Diarrhea**.
	- Given the severity (dehydration) and watery nature of the stool with vomiting, a key consideration is **Cholera** caused by _**Vibrio cholerae**_, although "rice water stool" is not explicitly mentioned in _this_ scenario. Severe acute watery diarrhea leading to dehydration is characteristic of cholera.
	
	**b. Enumerate Viral and Bacterial Causes of Diarrhea:**
	
	- Acute diarrhea is commonly caused by viral, bacterial, or parasitic agents. Viral agents are the most common cause of acute infectious gastroenteritis worldwide.
	- **Viral Causes:**
	    - **Rotavirus** (most common viral cause overall, especially in children).
	    - **Norovirus** (also called Norwalk virus, important cause of epidemic viral gastroenteritis, especially in adults).
	    - Adenovirus.
	- **Bacterial Causes:**
	    - _**Vibrio cholerae**_ (causes severe watery diarrhea, cholera).
	    - _**Escherichia coli**_:
	        - Enterotoxigenic (_ETEC_) (common cause of watery diarrhea, traveler's diarrhea).
	        - Enteropathogenic (_EPEC_) (nontoxigenic, causes watery diarrhea).
	        - Enteroaggregative (_EAEC_) (causes watery diarrhea).
	        - Enterohemorrhagic (_EHEC_) (causes bloody diarrhea, e.g., O157:H7).
	    - _**Clostridium perfringens**_ (can cause watery diarrhea or food poisoning).
	    - _**Bacillus cereus**_ (can cause watery diarrhea or emetic type food poisoning).
	    - _**Staphylococcus aureus**_ (food poisoning, typically presenting with acute nausea and vomiting, but listed as bacterial cause of watery diarrhea).
	    - _**Aeromonas hydrophila**_ (causes watery diarrhea).
	    - _**Plesiomonas shigelloides**_ (causes watery diarrhea).
	    - _**Shigella**_ species (cause dysentery, but listed under acute diarrhea agents).
	    - _**Salmonella**_ (non-typhoidal) (cause inflammatory diarrhea, but listed under acute diarrhea agents).
	    - **_Campylobacter_ species** (primarily _C. jejuni_, cause diarrheal disease).
	    - _**Clostridioides difficile**_ (causes pseudomembranous colitis, often post-antibiotic use).
	
	**c. Laboratory Diagnosis of Cholera:**
	
	- **Specimen:** **Freshly collected watery stool** is the specimen of choice for acute cases. Ideally collected **before antibiotics**. Rectal swab for carriers or convalescent patients. Stool containing mucus flakes is preferred.
	- **Transport:** Transport **immediately**. If delayed (>1 hour), use transport media:
	    - **Cary-Blair medium**.
	    - **Alkaline peptone water (APW)**.
	    - Venkatraman-Ramakrishnan (VR) medium.
	    - Alkaline salt transport medium, Autoclaved sea water.
	- **Direct Microscopy:**
	    - **Gram staining** of mucus flakes (not routinely done on stool due to normal flora, but recommended in special situations): Shows **short curved comma-shaped gram-negative rods**. May be arranged in parallel rows ("fish in stream appearance").
	    - **Hanging drop preparation** (for liquid specimens): Demonstrates characteristic **darting motility** ("actively motile frequently changing direction", "shooting star", "swarming gnats motility").
	- **Culture:**
	    - _V. cholerae_ is strongly aerobic and non-fastidious, grows on ordinary media.
	    - Inoculate simultaneously onto **enrichment broth** and **selective media** to inhibit commensals. Media contain salt (0.5-1%) which stimulates growth and have alkaline pH.
	    - **Enrichment Broth:** Alkaline peptone water (APW) or Monsur’s taurocholate tellurite peptone water. Incubate 4-6 hours, then subculture. Avoid prolonged incubation.
	    - **Selective Media:**
	        - **TCBS agar** (Thiosulfate citrate bile salts sucrose agar): Produces **sucrose fermenting yellow colored colonies**.
	        - **MacConkey agar (MAC)**: **Translucent pale non-lactose fermenting (NLF) colonies**. May become pink on prolonged incubation (late lactose fermentation). Can be included if uncertain about enteric pathogen. Supports other enteric pathogens like _Shigella_ and _Salmonella_.
	        - Bile salt agar, Monsur’s GTTT agar.
	    - Incubation: 37°C for 24 hours.
	    - Culture smear: Short curved gram-negative bacilli.
	    - Motility testing (hanging drop from culture): Confirms darting motility.
	- **Identification:**
	    - By **conventional biochemical tests** or **automated systems** (VITEK or MALDI-TOF).
	    - Biochemical characteristics: **Catalase positive**, **Oxidase positive**. **ICUT tests:** Indole (+), Citrate (+/–), Urease (–). **TSI:** Alkaline slant/acid butt (A/A), **gas (–), H2S (–)**. **String test positive**. Produces hemodigestion on blood agar.
	    - **Serogrouping:** Agglutinates with _Vibrio cholerae_ O1 antisera. Differentiates O1 and O139.
	    - **Serotyping:** Differentiates O1 serotypes: Ogawa, Inaba, and Hikojima. Hikojima agglutinates with both Ogawa and Inaba antisera.
	    - Biotyping differentiates Classical and El Tor biotypes.
	- **Other Methods:**
	    - **Antigen detection:** Cholera dipstick assay available.
	    - **Molecular methods:** Multiplex PCR detecting common diarrheal pathogens can include _V. cholerae_.
	- **Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST):**
	    - **Essential** due to increasing drug resistance.
	    - Done on Mueller Hinton agar by disk diffusion test.
