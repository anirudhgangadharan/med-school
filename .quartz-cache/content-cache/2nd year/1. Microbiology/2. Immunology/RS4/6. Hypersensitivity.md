1. Type IV hypersenitivity reaction
	### Type IV Hypersensitivity Reaction
	**Type IV hypersensitivity**, also known as **Delayed-Type Hypersensitivity (DTH)**, differs fundamentally from other hypersensitivity reactions as it is **cell-mediated** rather than antibody-mediated. It is characterized by a **delayed onset**, typically appearing **48–72 hours** after antigen contact. The primary mediators are specific subsets of **T helper (TH) cells**, particularly **TDTH cells**, and **activated macrophages**.
	
	**I. Mechanism of Type IV Hypersensitivity** Type IV hypersensitivity involves two main phases:
	
	1. **Sensitization Phase (1-2 weeks initial exposure)**:
	    
	    - Initial exposure to an **allergen** (antigen).
	    - The allergen is processed by **Antigen Presenting Cells (APCs)**, and antigenic peptides are presented to **CD4 helper T cells**.
	    - Activated TH cells differentiate into **TDTH cells** (a specific subset of TH cells).
	    - These TDTH cells become **sensitized** and migrate to the site of antigen exposure, waiting for subsequent challenges.
	2. **Effector Phase (Re-exposure)**:
	    
	    - Upon re-exposure to the same antigen, the sensitized TDTH cells are activated and release a range of **cytokines**.
	    - **Key Cytokines and their Roles**:
	        - **Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ)**: The **key cytokine** of Type IV reaction. It **activates resting macrophages** into highly microbicidal activated macrophages. This enhances their phagocytic capabilities, increases MHC-II expression (making them efficient APCs), and boosts levels of oxygen radicals and nitric oxide for killing.
	        - **Interleukin-2 (IL-2)**: Acts in an autocrine manner to stimulate the **proliferation of TDTH cells**.
	        - **Monocyte Chemotactic and Activating Factor (MCAF) / TNF-β**: Help in the **migration of monocytes** from blood to the DTH site, where they transform into tissue macrophages.
	        - **Migration Inhibitory Factor (MIF)**: **Inhibits the migration of macrophages** _away_ from the DTH site, thus concentrating them at the area of inflammation.
	        - **IL-3 and Granulocyte-Monocyte Colony Stimulating Factor (GM-CSF)**: Help in the local synthesis and recruitment of monocytes.
	    - The recruitment and activation of these inflammatory cells, particularly **macrophages**, lead to **tissue injury** and the characteristic delayed inflammatory response.
	
	**II. Role: Protective vs. Tissue Damage** Type IV hypersensitivity has a dual role:
	
	- **Protective Response**: Under normal circumstances, it mediates defense against many **intracellular microorganisms** (e.g., _Mycobacterium tuberculosis_) and certain chemicals. The activated macrophages clear the pathogens with minimal tissue damage.
	- **Tissue Damage Response**: If intracellular microbes evade macrophage killing, the persistent and intensified delayed hypersensitivity reaction, characterized by enhanced phagocytic activity and release of lytic enzymes from activated macrophages, can lead to **nonspecific tissue destruction**.
	
	**III. Pathology: Granuloma Formation**
	
	- Continuous DTH in response to persistent or non-degradable antigens (e.g., in tuberculosis or leprosy) can lead to the formation of a **granuloma**.
	- Initially, TH cell infiltrates are progressively replaced by macrophages. These macrophages transform into large, flat **epithelioid cells** and may fuse to form **multinucleated giant cells** (e.g., Langhans giant cells), often induced by IFN-γ.
	- A granuloma typically consists of an inner zone of epithelioid cells, surrounded by lymphocytes, and a peripheral rim of fibroblasts and connective tissue.
	
	**IV. Factors Influencing Type I Hypersensitivity** (Note: Source titles this "Factors Influencing Type I Hypersensitivity" but content in this section also generally applies to immune responses. Focus is on factors that can lead to an exaggerated or particular type of immune response):
	
	1. **Genetic Makeup**: Host genetic factors play a significant role in mounting immune responses, with susceptibility to allergens often running in families.
	2. **Allergen Dose**: The dose of the allergen can impact the type and persistence of the immune response.
	3. **TH1 vs. TH2 Response Balance**: This balance is crucial. TH1 responses (producing IFN-γ) are generally inhibitory to Type I hypersensitivity (and by extension, can shift the balance in CMI); TH2 responses promote IgE-mediated allergic reactions.
	
	**V. Detection & Clinical Relevance**
	
	- **Tuberculin Test (Mantoux Test)**: The **prototype** of delayed hypersensitivity. In sensitized individuals (e.g., with prior _M. tuberculosis_ exposure), intradermal injection of tuberculin antigen elicits a localized induration (>5 mm) after 48-72 hours, indicating an intact CMI response.
	- **Contact Dermatitis**: Caused by haptens (e.g., nickel, poison oak) that complex with skin proteins to become immunogenic, eliciting a DTH response.
	- **Other Examples**:
	    - Granuloma formation in **tuberculosis** and **leprosy**.
	    - Immunity against **intracellular bacteria**, **viruses**, and **fungi**.
	    - Transplantation immunity and graft-versus-host (GVH) reaction.
