	### Functions of Complement
	
	The **complement system** is a group of serum proteins that, when activated, significantly **augment immune responses**. It serves as a crucial **bridge between innate and acquired immunity**.
	
	Its major effector functions include:
	
	2. **Target Cell Lysis (Cytolysis)**:
	    
	    - The **Membrane Attack Complex (MAC)**, formed by C5-C9 components, creates pores or channels in the cell membrane of target microbes.
	    - This leads to the **swelling, lysis, and death** of various pathogens and abnormal cells, including **bacteria, enveloped viruses, damaged cells, and tumor cells**.
	3. **Inflammatory Response**:
	    
	    - Complement by-products **C3a, C4a, and C5a** are potent **anaphylatoxins**.
	    - They bind to mast cell receptors, inducing **degranulation and release of inflammatory mediators** (e.g., histamine), which cause **vasoconstriction and increased vascular permeability**.
	    - **C3a and C5a** also act as **chemoattractants**, recruiting neutrophils to sites of immune complex deposition.
	4. **Opsonization**:
	    
	    - **C3b and C4b** function as major **opsonins**, coating immune complexes and particulate antigens.
	    - This coating **enhances phagocytosis** by phagocytic cells (e.g., macrophages, neutrophils) that express specific complement receptors (CR1, CR3, CR4).
	    - C3b-mediated opsonization is also crucial for **viral particles**.
	5. **Clearance of Immune Complexes**:
	    
	    - **C3b** plays a vital role in **removing immune complexes from the blood**.
	    - C3b-bound immune complexes are recognized by **complement receptor CR1 on red blood cells (RBCs)**. These RBCs then transport the complexes to the liver and spleen, where they are phagocytosed.
	6. **Viral Neutralization**:
	    
	    - Complement can neutralize viruses by **blocking their attachment sites** when coated on viral surfaces.
	    - It also contributes to viral neutralization through **C3b-mediated opsonization** of viral particles and **lysis of enveloped viruses**.
	    - Some serological tests, like the Sabin-Feldman Dye test for _Toxoplasma_, are complement-mediated neutralization tests.
