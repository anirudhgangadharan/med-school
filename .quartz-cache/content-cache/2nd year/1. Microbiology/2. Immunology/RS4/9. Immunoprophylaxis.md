1. Live and heat killed vaccines -names
	Immunodeficiency diseases leave the body vulnerable to infections. A critical aspect of managing these conditions, especially in medical school exams, involves understanding **vaccine types** and their implications for immunodeficient patients. Vaccines are broadly categorised by their preparation method.
	
	---
	
	### **Vaccine Types: Live Attenuated vs. Killed/Inactivated**
	
	**I. Live Attenuated Vaccines**
	
	- **Definition:** Contain **live, weakened (attenuated) organisms** that have lost their ability to cause full-blown disease but retain immunogenicity.
	- **Advantages:**
	    - Provide **stronger and long-lasting immunity**, closely mimicking natural infection.
	    - Often administered as a **single dose** (except Oral Polio Vaccine).
	    - Capable of inducing **mucosal immunity** by stimulating secretory IgA antibody production.
	- **Disadvantages/Precautions:**
	    - **Contraindicated in immunocompromised individuals** and **pregnant women** due to the risk of reversion to virulence or causing disease.
	    - Require **stringent cold chain** storage conditions to maintain effectiveness.
	- **Key Examples (High-Yield):**
	    1. **BCG vaccine** (Tuberculosis): Prepared from _Mycobacterium bovis_, providing protection against _M. tuberculosis_.
	    2. **MMR vaccine** (Measles, Mumps, Rubella):
	        - **Measles:** Schwartz, Edmonston-Zagreb, Moraten strains.
	        - **Mumps:** Jeryl-Lynn strain.
	        - **Rubella:** RA 27/3 strain.
	    3. **Oral Polio Vaccine (OPV)** (Sabin vaccine): Prepared in monkey kidney cell lines. Provides **herd immunity** due to fecal-oral spread of vaccine virus. The trivalent form has been replaced by bivalent (types 1 and 3) since 2015 due to eradication of serotype 2 and risk of vaccine-associated paralytic polio. **Most heat-sensitive vaccine**.
	    4. **Yellow Fever 17D vaccine:** Prepared in the allantoic cavity of chick embryos (consider **egg allergy** contraindication).
	    5. **Chickenpox vaccine** (Oka strain of varicella-zoster).
	    6. **Live Attenuated Influenza Vaccine** (intranasal).
	    7. **Japanese B Encephalitis vaccine** (SA 14-14-2 strain).
	    8. **Smallpox vaccine:** Unique in that it uses the **live, non-attenuated vaccinia virus** or cowpox virus, which are cross-reactive with variola virus. Historically significant as the first successful vaccine developed by Edward Jenner.
	
	**II. Killed/Inactivated Vaccines**
	
	- **Definition:** Contain organisms that are **grown in culture and then killed** (inactivated) using methods such as heat, phenol, formalin, or beta propiolactone.
	- **Advantages:**
	    - Generally **safer** than live vaccines, especially for immunodeficient individuals and pregnant women.
	    - More stable for storage.
	- **Disadvantages:**
	    - Typically **less efficacious** than live vaccines.
	    - Often require **larger doses, adjuvants**, and **multiple doses with boosters** to confer immunity.
	    - Do not induce mucosal immunity or cell-mediated immunity as effectively as live vaccines.
	- **Key Examples (High-Yield):**
	    1. **Injectable Polio Vaccine (IPV)** (Salk vaccine): Prepared by inactivating the virus with formalin. Requires multiple doses and boosters.
	    2. **Rabies vaccine:** Non-neural cell line derived vaccines are recommended, such as Purified Chick Embryo Cell (PCEC), Purified Vero Cell (PVC), and Human Diploid Cell (HDC) vaccines. Older **neural vaccines are encephalitogenic** and no longer used.
	    3. **Killed Kyasanur Forest Disease (KFD) vaccine:** Formalin-inactivated chick embryo vaccine.
	    4. **Hepatitis A vaccine** (inactivated).
	    5. **Killed Influenza Vaccine** (injectable): Prepared from strains grown in embryonated chick eggs or cell lines.
	    6. **Cholera vaccine** (Oral, killed whole-cell vaccine): Formulations like Shanchol and Euvichol.
	    7. **Pertussis vaccine** (Whole-cell): Often given as part of the **DPT (Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus) combined vaccine**.
	    8. **Typhoid vaccine** (Injectable).
	    9. **Plague vaccine:** Formalin-killed, recommended only for anticipated outbreaks.
	    10. **Q-Vax** (_Coxiella burnetii_ vaccine): Inactivated whole-cell vaccine, licensed in Australia.
	
	**III. Toxoid Vaccines** (A specialized type of inactivated vaccine)
	
	- **Definition:** Modified bacterial **exotoxins** that have been detoxified (e.g., with formalin) to lose virulence but retain their **immunogenicity**. They induce **neutralising antibodies**.
	- **Key Examples:**
	    
	    1. **Diphtheria toxoid (DT)** (from _Corynebacterium diphtheriae_).
	    2. **Tetanus toxoid (TT)** (from _Clostridium tetani_).
	    
	    - Often combined as **DPT vaccine**.
	
	**IV. Subunit/Recombinant Vaccines**
	
	- **Definition:** Contain only **specific antigenic components** of the pathogen (e.g., proteins, polysaccharides), often produced using **DNA recombinant technology**.
	- **Advantages:** Very safe, as they contain no whole pathogen.
	- **Key Examples:**
	    1. **Hepatitis B vaccine:** Uses Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) produced in Baker's yeast.
	    2. **Human Papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine:** Uses HPV L1 proteins.
	    3. **Meningococcal vaccine** and **Pneumococcal vaccine:** Prepared from capsular polysaccharide antigens.
	    4. **_Haemophilus influenzae_ type b (Hib) conjugate vaccine:** Uses polyribosyl ribitol phosphate (PRP) capsular antigen conjugated with adjuvants like diphtheria or tetanus toxoid to enhance immunogenicity in children.
