1. (DEC) Hetrazan provocation test
	Here's a concise, high-yield, note-style format for the DEC (Diethylcarbamazine) Hetrazan Provocation Test, optimized for medical school exams:
	
	---
	
	**DEC (Diethylcarbamazine) Hetrazan Provocation Test**
	
	- **Purpose/Principle**:
	    
	    - This test is performed to **stimulate nocturnal microfilariae** (e.g., _Wuchereria bancrofti_ or _Brugia malayi_) to appear in the peripheral blood during daytime hours.
	    - This facilitates **convenient blood collection** for microscopic examination, as microfilariae of these species typically exhibit nocturnal periodicity (present in peripheral blood predominantly at night).
	    - **Mechanism**: Oral administration of Diethylcarbamazine (DEC) causes the microfilariae to migrate into the peripheral circulation.
	- **Procedure**:
	    
	    - Patient takes a **DEC tablet orally** at a dose of **2 mg/kg**.
	    - Microfilariae are expected to appear in the peripheral blood within **15 minutes to 1 hour** after drug administration.
	- **Clinical Relevance**:
	    
	    - Primarily used in the diagnosis of **lymphatic filariasis** (e.g., caused by _W. bancrofti_ and _B. malayi_) in regions where nocturnal periodicity is prominent.
	    - Allows for **daytime blood sample collection**, making screening and diagnosis logistically simpler.
	- **Important Considerations/Contraindications**:
	    
	    - For **subperiodic _Wuchereria bancrofti_**, the microfilariae level may fall rather than rise after DEC provocation.
	    - **Absolutely contraindicated in _Onchocerca_ (onchocerciasis or "river blindness") and _Loa loa_ (loiasis or "African eye worm") infections**. This is a crucial point due to the risk of severe adverse reactions, including potentially fatal encephalopathy in _Loa loa_ infection due to massive microfilarial death in the brain.
