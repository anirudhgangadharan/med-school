1. Brucellosis - clinical features and lab diagnosis
	### **Brucellosis: Clinical Features & Laboratory Diagnosis (High-Yield Notes)**
	
	**Brucellosis**, also known as **undulant fever** or Malta fever, is a **highly contagious febrile illness** caused by _Brucella_ species. It is primarily a **zoonotic disease**, with human infection usually linked to **occupational or domestic exposure to infected animals or their products** (e.g., sheep, goat, cattle, or their unpasteurized dairy). _Brucella_ are **obligate aerobic, fastidious small gram-negative coccobacilli** that act as **facultative intracellular pathogens**, infecting macrophages and monocytes.
	
	---
	
	**I. Clinical Features**
	
	- **Classic Triad**: Brucellosis typically manifests as a **triad of fever, arthralgia, and hepatosplenomegaly**.
	- **Fever**: Often **undulant** (wavy pattern), hence the alternative name "undulant fever".
	- **Musculoskeletal**: Arthralgia (joint pain) is common.
	- **Reticuloendothelial System**: Hepatosplenomegaly (enlargement of liver and spleen) is a characteristic finding.
	- **Other Manifestations**: May also include malaise, fatigue, and weight loss (not explicitly detailed as primary symptoms in provided sources, but implied by systemic febrile illness).
	
	---
	
	**II. Laboratory Diagnosis**
	
	**A. Specimen Collection**
	
	- **Variety of Specimens**: _Brucellae_ can be recovered from **blood, bone marrow, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), joint fluid, or other tissues**.
	- **Timing**: Blood cultures should be collected **aseptically during the febrile period before starting antibiotics**.
	- **Multiple Samples**: Multiple blood cultures (8–10 mL per bottle, 2–3 times a day) collected over 3 consecutive days yield better results.
	- **Bone Marrow**: Bone marrow culture offers a **higher yield than blood culture** and remains positive even after antibiotic therapy has begun.
	
	**B. Culture (Gold Standard)**
	
	- **Media**: Blood agar, brain heart infusion (BHI) broth. Subcultures from positive blood culture bottles onto blood agar produce small, smooth, transparent, nonhemolytic colonies.
	- **Incubation**: Cultures are typically incubated at 37°C for 48 hours, but may require prolonged incubation (up to several weeks) due to the fastidious nature of _Brucella_.
	- **Biosafety**: **All laboratory procedures must be carried out with adequate biosafety precautions**, as _Brucella_ is a **highly infectious pathogen**.
	
	**C. Direct Microscopy & Motility**
	
	- **Gram Stain**: _Brucella_ appear as **non-capsulated, small, gram-negative coccobacilli**.
	- **Motility**: They are **nonmotile** by hanging drop test.
	
	**D. Identification**
	
	- **Biochemical Tests**:
	    - **Catalase positive**.
	    - **Oxidase positive**.
	    - **Urease test is rapidly positive** for _B. suis_ and _B. canis_.
	    - Species differentiation can also be based on CO2 requirement, H2S production, tolerance to bacteriostatic dyes, agglutination with specific antisera, and lysis by bacteriophages.
	- **Automated Systems**: **MALDI-TOF or VITEK** can be used for identification.
	
	**E. Serological Tests (Paramount Importance when Isolation is Difficult)**
	
	- **Standard Agglutination Test (SAT)**:
	    - **Principle**: Patient's sera are mixed with killed suspension of _B. abortus_ and incubated.
	    - **Diagnostic Titre**:
	        - In **non-endemic areas**: Titre of **≥ 1:160** along with compatible clinical manifestations is considered significant.
	        - In **endemic areas or following occupational exposure**: Titre of **≥ 1:320 or a rising titre** (four-fold) by repeating the test after 2-4 weeks is considered diagnostic.
	    - **Interpretation**: A positive SAT result indicates **either acute or chronic brucellosis**. It detects total antibodies (IgM + IgG) and **cannot differentiate between acute and chronic infection**.
	- **ELISA**:
	    - **High Sensitivity**: Highly sensitive test.
	    - **Antibody Detection**: Uses cytoplasmic proteins or LPS antigens to detect **IgM, IgG, and IgA antibodies individually**.
	    - **Utility**: Useful for diagnosis of **chronic brucellosis** and neurobrucellosis.
	    - **Confirmation**: **Results must be confirmed by SAT**.
	- **Dipstick Assays**: Anti-Brucella IgM dipstick assays are available for acute infection but are **less sensitive**.
	
	**F. Molecular Methods (PCR)**
	
	- **Assays Available**: PCR assays targeting genes like 16S-23S rRNA operon (_rrs-rrl_ gene), _Omp2_ gene, insertion sequence IS711, and protein BCSP31 are available.
	- **Advantages**: These methods are **rapid, sensitive, and specific**, and can also **differentiate between the _Brucella_ species and biovars**.
	- **Specimens**: Blood and tissues are ideal samples for PCR assays.
	- **Automated Systems**: The FilmArray BioThreat panel is an automated multiplex PCR system capable of simultaneously detecting _Brucella_ among other pathogens.
