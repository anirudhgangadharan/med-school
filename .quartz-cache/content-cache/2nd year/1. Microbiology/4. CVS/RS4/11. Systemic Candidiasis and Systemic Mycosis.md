1. Describe Germ tube test (Reynold's Braude phenomenon)
	**Germ Tube Test (Reynolds-Braude Phenomenon)**
	
	- **Purpose/Principle**:
	    
	    - A rapid and **specific test** used for the presumptive identification of **_Candida albicans_**.
	    - It detects the ability of _C. albicans_ yeast cells to form **true hyphae-like extensions (germ tubes)** when cultured under specific conditions.
	- **Procedure**:
	    
	    1. **Specimen**: A small amount of yeast colony (typically from a 24-hour culture) [Outside information: standard practice, not explicitly stated for this test, but implicitly from "Colonies are mixed"].
	    2. **Medium**: The yeast colony is mixed with a drop of **human or sheep serum**.
	    3. **Incubation**: The mixture is incubated at **37°C for 2 hours**.
	    4. **Examination**: A wet mount preparation is then examined under a microscope.
	- **Positive Result**:
	    
	    - The formation of **"germ tubes"**, which are described as **long tube-like projections extending from the yeast cells**.
	    - **Key Differentiator**: A true germ tube is characterized by the **absence of a constriction at its origin** from the parent yeast cell, distinguishing it from pseudohyphae. (Pseudohyphae, in contrast, are elongated yeast cells that remain attached after budding, showing constrictions at the points of attachment).
	- **Clinical Relevance/Notes**:
	    
	    - Despite its specificity, the test **may also be positive for _Candida dubliniensis_**.
	    - This test is crucial for rapid identification in clinical microbiology, aiding in the timely initiation of appropriate antifungal therapy for candidiasis, particularly in critically ill or immunocompromised patients [Outside information: This is the underlying clinical utility].
