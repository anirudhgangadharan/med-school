1. Give an outline of the lab diagnosis of bacterial disease
	
	### **I. Specimen Collection**
	
	- **General Principles**:
	    
	    - **Before Antibiotics**: Whenever possible, specimens should be collected **prior to administration of any antimicrobial agents**. If antibiotics are started, collect just before the next dose.
	    - **Aseptic Technique**: Strict aseptic conditions are essential to prevent contamination with commensal flora, especially for sterile sites like blood and CSF.
	    - **Sterile Containers**: Use **sterile, tightly sealed, leak-proof, wide-mouth, screw-capped containers**.
	    - **Adequate Volume**: Collect sufficient volume (e.g., 8–10 mL per blood culture bottle for adults) to increase yield.
	    - **Proper Labelling**: All specimens must be appropriately labelled with patient and clinical information.
	    - **Timely Transport**: Specimens should reach the laboratory **as soon as possible**, ideally within 1–2 hours, or be placed in appropriate transport media if delays are anticipated.
	    - **Rejection Criteria**: Samples that are improperly labelled, contaminated, insufficient in quantity, or delayed in transit may be rejected.
	- **Specimen Examples by Infection Type**:
	    
	    - **Bloodstream Infection/Sepsis**: Paired **blood culture** specimens from different venipuncture sites.
	    - **Meningitis**: **Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)** collected via lumbar puncture; blood culture is also useful.
	    - **Gastrointestinal Infections**: **Stool** (especially mucus flakes) or rectal swab; vomitus or suspected food for food poisoning.
	    - **Respiratory Tract Infections**: Sputum (spot and early morning), nasopharyngeal swabs/aspirates, bronchial wash, bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL), pleural fluid.
	    - **Skin & Soft Tissue Infections**: Pus, wound swab, aspirates from abscess, tissue bits.
	    - **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI)**: Midstream urine (clean catch), suprapubic aspirate, or catheterised urine.
	
	---
	
	### **II. Direct Detection**
	
	Direct examination of specimens provides rapid, presumptive identification.
	
	1. **Microscopy (Staining Techniques)**:
	    
	    - **Gram Stain**:
	        - **Principle**: Differentiates bacteria based on cell wall composition (peptidoglycan thickness).
	        - **Procedure**: Crystal violet (primary stain) → Gram's iodine (mordant) → Alcohol/Acetone (decolorizer) → Safranin (counterstain). Gram-positive appear violet, Gram-negative appear pink/red.
	        - **Uses/Clinical Relevance**:
	            - **Early Empirical Treatment**: Provides a preliminary clue (e.g., Gram-positive cocci, Gram-negative rods).
	            - **Fastidious Organisms**: Helpful for presumptive ID of slow-growing or fastidious organisms (e.g., _Haemophilus_).
	            - **Anaerobes**: Suggests anaerobic infection if organisms are seen but aerobic cultures are negative.
	            - **Quality Control**: Assesses sputum quality (pus cells vs. epithelial cells).
	            - **Fungi**: Useful for staining some fungi (e.g., _Candida_, _Cryptococcus_).
	    - **Acid-Fast Stain (Ziehl-Neelsen, Kinyoun, Auramine Phenol)**:
	        - **Principle**: Identifies organisms with **mycolic acid** in their cell walls that resist decolorisation by acid-alcohol.
	        - **Uses/Clinical Relevance**:
	            - **Mycobacteria**: Gold standard for **M. tuberculosis** (long slender, beaded red bacilli) and _M. leprae_ (less acid-fast, 5% H2SO4).
	            - **Nocardia**: Partially acid-fast.
	            - **Parasites**: Detects oocysts of _Cryptosporidium_, _Cyclospora_, _Cystoisospora_, _Microsporidia_.
	    - **Negative Staining**:
	        - **Principle**: Stains the background, leaving the organism (or its capsule) unstained.
	        - **Dyes**: **India ink** and **nigrosin**.
	        - **Uses**: Primarily for demonstrating bacterial or yeast capsules, notably for **Cryptococcus neoformans** (clear refractile halo).
	    - **Special Stains**:
	        - **Albert Stain**: Demonstrates **metachromatic granules** in _Corynebacterium diphtheriae_ (green bacilli with bluish-black granules).
	        - **M’Fadyean’s Reaction**: Demonstrates the **polypeptide capsule of _Bacillus anthracis_** (amorphous purple material surrounding blue bacilli).
	    - **Other Microscopy**:
	        - **Wet Mount/Hanging Drop**: Detects **motility** (e.g., darting motility of _Vibrio cholerae_, falling leaf-like motility of _Giardia_), and visualises parasites (trophozoites, cysts, eggs, larvae), pus cells, or RBCs.
	2. **Antigen Detection**:
	    
	    - **Principle**: Detects specific microbial antigens in clinical specimens using labelled antibodies.
	    - **Examples**:
	        - **Pneumococcus**: Capsular antigen in CSF/urine by latex agglutination or ICT.
	        - **Legionella**: Urinary antigen.
	        - _**Haemophilus influenzae**_: Type b capsular antigen in CSF/urine by latex agglutination or direct IF.
	        - _**Cryptococcus neoformans**_: Antigen in CSF.
	        - _**Vibrio cholerae**_: Cholera dipstick assay in stool.
	        - **Toxins**: Enterotoxins in stool (e.g., _C. difficile_, _S. aureus_).
	
	---
	
	### **III. Culture**
	
	Culture is considered the **gold standard** for many bacterial infections, confirming viability and allowing for susceptibility testing.
	
	3. **Culture Media**:
	    
	    - **Basal/Enriched Media**: Support growth of non-fastidious and fastidious organisms.
	        - **Blood Agar**: Most widely used, tests **hemolytic properties** (α-green, β-clear, γ-no change).
	        - **Chocolate Agar**: For fastidious organisms (e.g., _Haemophilus_, _Neisseria_), provides 5% CO2.
	    - **Selective/Differential Media**: Inhibit commensals and/or differentiate bacteria.
	        - **MacConkey Agar**: For Gram-negative rods, differentiates lactose fermenters (pink) from non-lactose fermenters (colourless).
	        - **Deoxycholate Citrate Agar (DCA) / Xylose Lysine Deoxycholate (XLD) Agar**: For _Salmonella_ (black centres) and _Shigella_.
	        - **Thiosulfate Citrate Bile Salts Sucrose (TCBS) Agar**: For _Vibrio_ species (yellow colonies for _V. cholerae_).
	        - **Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA)**: Primarily for fungi, but some bacteria can grow.
	        - **Thayer Martin / New York City Medium**: Selective for _Neisseria_ (e.g., _N. meningitidis_, _N. gonorrhoeae_).
	        - **Regan-Lowe / Bordet-Gengou Agar**: For _Bordetella pertussis_ (mercury drops appearance).
	        - **Buffered Charcoal Yeast Extract (BCYE) Agar**: For _Legionella_.
	        - **Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) Medium**: For _Mycobacterium tuberculosis_ (rough, tough, buff colonies).
	        - **_C. difficile_ Selective Media**: CCFA or CCYA (cefoxitin cycloserine fructose/egg yolk agar).
	        - **CLED Agar**: For urine cultures.
	4. **Culture Methods**:
	    
	    - **Solid Media**:
	        - **Streak Plate Method**: Most common, aims for **isolated colonies**.
	        - **Pour Plate Method**: For quantifying bacterial load (e.g., in urine, blood).
	    - **Liquid Media**:
	        - **Blood Culture Bottles**: Contain enriched broths (e.g., BHI), often in pairs (aerobic/anaerobic).
	        - **Automated Blood Culture Systems**: (e.g., BacT/ALERT, BACTEC) detect growth rapidly via CO2 production or fluorometric methods.
	        - **MGIT (Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube)**: Automated liquid culture for _M. tuberculosis_.
	5. **Colony Morphology**:
	    
	    - Observed features include **size, shape (circular, irregular), consistency (dry, moist, mucoid), density (opaque, translucent), colour (pigmentation), and hemolytic patterns**.
	    - **Examples**:
	        - _S. aureus_: Golden yellow, beta-hemolytic.
	        - _Pneumococcus_: Draughtsman or carrom coin appearance, alpha-hemolytic.
	        - _Klebsiella pneumoniae_: Large, dome-shaped, mucoid (due to capsule), pink.
	        - _B. pertussis_: Mercury drops or bisected pearls.
	        - _Bacillus anthracis_: Medusa head appearance, dry, wrinkled.
	6. **Culture Smear and Motility Testing**:
	    
	    - **Gram Stain of Colonies**: Confirms Gram reaction and morphology from isolated colonies.
	    - **Hanging Drop Test**: Demonstrates **bacterial motility** (e.g., darting, tumbling).
	
	---
	
	### **IV. Identification**
	
	- **Biochemical Identification**:
	    - **Catalase Test**: Differentiates _Staphylococcus_ (positive) from _Streptococcus_ (negative).
	    - **Oxidase Test**: Differentiates _Pseudomonas_, _Vibrio_, _Neisseria_ (positive) from Enterobacteriaceae (_E. coli_, _Klebsiella_) and _Acinetobacter_ (negative).
	    - **ICUT Tests** (Indole, Citrate, Urease, TSI): Standard for Gram-negative bacilli.
	    - **Specific Tests**:
	        - **Coagulase Test**: Differentiates _S. aureus_ (positive) from CoNS (negative).
	        - **CAMP Test**: For _Streptococcus agalactiae_ (Group B Strep).
	        - **Bile Esculin Hydrolysis**: For _Enterococcus_.
	        - **Optochin Sensitivity / Bile Solubility**: Differentiates _S. pneumoniae_ from viridans streptococci.
	        - **String Test**: For _Vibrio cholerae_.
	        - **Urease Test**: For _H. pylori_ (biopsy urease test).
	        - **X and V factor requirement**: For _Haemophilus_ species.
	        - **Antibiotic resistance/sensitivity**: (e.g., bacitracin for _S. pyogenes_).
	- **Automated Identification Systems**:
	    - **MALDI-TOF (Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption/Ionisation Time-of-Flight)**: Identifies organisms based on their **ribosomal protein patterns**, providing rapid and accurate results.
	    - **VITEK 2**: Automates identification and AST using **microbroth dilution** and advanced colorimetry.
	
	---
	
	### **V. Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST)**
	
	- **Purpose**: **Guides pathogen-directed therapy**, informs about drug resistance, and helps monitor treatment response.
	- **Methods**:
	    - **Disk Diffusion (Kirby-Bauer)**:
	        - **Principle**: Antibiotic diffuses from a disk into Mueller-Hinton agar, creating a **zone of inhibition**.
	        - **Interpretation**: Zone diameter indicates **sensitive (S), intermediate (I), or resistant (R)** based on CLSI/EUCAST guidelines.
	    - **Dilution Tests (Broth/Agar)**: Determines **Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)** (lowest drug concentration inhibiting visible growth) and **Minimum Bactericidal Concentration (MBC)** (lowest concentration killing the bacteria).
	    - **Epsilometer (E-test)**: Uses a strip with a predefined antibiotic gradient to determine MIC.
	    - **Automated AST Systems**: (e.g., VITEK 2, Phoenix) provide rapid MIC-based results.
	- **Molecular Methods for Drug Resistance**: Detect specific resistance genes.
	    - **_mecA_ gene**: For **MRSA** detection by PCR.
	    - **_van_ gene**: For **VRSA** (vancomycin-resistant _S. aureus_) and **VRE** (vancomycin-resistant _Enterococcus_).
	    - **GeneXpert**: For **rifampicin resistance** in _M. tuberculosis_.
	
	---
	
	### **VI. Serology**
	
	- **Principle**: Detects microbial antigens or host antibodies in serum/CSF, indicating current or past infection.
	- **Assays**:
	    - **Agglutination**:
	        - **Standard Agglutination Test (SAT)**: For **brucellosis** (detects IgM/IgG).
	        - **Widal Test**: For **enteric fever** (detects O and H antibodies against _Salmonella Typhi_).
	        - **Latex Agglutination**: For capsular antigens (e.g., _Pneumococcus_, _H. influenzae_, _Cryptococcus_), or detecting PBP2a for MRSA.
	        - **Weil-Felix Test**: For **rickettsial infections** (heterophile agglutination based on cross-reactivity with _Proteus_ antigens).
	        - **Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT)**: Gold standard for **leptospirosis**.
	    - **ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay)**: Widely used for antibody or antigen detection in various bacterial diseases (e.g., _Chlamydia_, _Mycoplasma pneumoniae_, _Brucella_, _Rickettsia_, _Helicobacter pylori_).
	    - **Immunochromatographic Test (ICT/Rapid Test)**: Simple, low-cost, rapid; used for antigen or antibody detection (e.g., _H. pylori_, _Syphilis_, _V. cholerae_, COVID-19 antigen).
	    - **VDRL (Venereal Disease Research Laboratory) / RPR (Rapid Plasma Reagin) Test**: Non-treponemal tests for **syphilis**.
	
	---
	
	### **VII. Molecular Methods**
	
	Molecular methods offer **high sensitivity and specificity** and are crucial for rapid diagnosis, particularly for fastidious or low-load pathogens.
	
	- **Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)**: Amplifies specific DNA sequences.
	    - **Real-time PCR (RT-PCR)**: Quantifies DNA/RNA in real-time, faster, and more sensitive. Used for **COVID-19**, **tuberculosis (GeneXpert)**, HIV, viral meningitis, etc..
	    - **Multiplex PCR**: Detects multiple pathogens simultaneously in one reaction (e.g., meningitis-encephalitis panel, gastrointestinal panel on BioFire FilmArray).
	- **BioFire FilmArray**: Automated multiplex nested PCR system with panels for respiratory, gastrointestinal, meningitis-encephalitis, and blood culture identification.
	- **GeneXpert**: Automated real-time PCR for rapid diagnosis and rifampicin resistance detection in _M. tuberculosis_.
	- **Line Probe Assay (LPA)**: Detects gene mutations for drug resistance in _M. tuberculosis_.
	- **16S rRNA gene sequencing**: Used for identification and differentiation of various bacterial species, especially for ambiguous or uncultivable organisms.
	- **Whole Genome Sequencing**: Determines the complete DNA sequence of an organism, primarily a research tool for epidemiology and understanding resistance.
2. Transport media and its uses
	Transport media are crucial for the proper handling of clinical specimens in microbiology, especially when there is an expected delay between collection and laboratory processing. They are designed to **maintain the viability of delicate microorganisms without allowing them to multiply**, thus preserving the integrity of the sample.
	
	**Key Characteristics and Purpose:**
	
	- **Prevent multiplication:** Bacteria do not multiply in transport media, they only remain viable.
	- **Maintain viability:** Essential for delicate organisms or when immediate processing is not possible.
	- **Inhibit normal flora:** Some transport media may contain inhibitory agents to suppress the growth of commensals, preventing them from overgrowing the suspected pathogen.
	- **Preserve morphology:** Help in maintaining the size and shape of parasitic structures.
	- **Neutralise antibacterial substances:** For specimens like blood, dilution in the medium can help neutralise antimicrobial substances.
	
	**Specific Transport Media and their Uses (Clinical Relevance):**
	
	- **Amies medium & Stuart's medium:**
	    - Used for **Neisseria**.
	    - **Stuart's medium** specifically mentioned for suspected **meningococcal meningitis** (for nasopharyngeal swabs, pus, or scrapings from rashes).
	    - **Charcoal-based Stuart's media** are preferred for _Bordetella pertussis_.
	    - **Charcoal-impregnated cotton swabs (Stuart's)** are useful for _Bordetella pertussis_ culture when delay is expected.
	- **Venkatraman-Ramakrishnan (VR) medium:**
	    - Used for **Vibrio cholerae**.
	- **Alkaline salt transport medium:**
	    - Used for **Vibrio cholerae**.
	- **Cary-Blair medium:**
	    - Used for **Vibrio cholerae**.
	    - Also useful for **Salmonella** and **Shigella**.
	    - Recommended for **stool cultures** if transport time exceeds 1 hour (up to 24 hours).
	    - Can be used for **Campylobacter** if delay is expected.
	    - Can be used for **Yersinia pestis** if delay in transportation is expected.
	- **Buffered glycerol saline:**
	    - Used for **Shigella** and **Salmonella**.
	- **Robertson's cooked meat (RCM) broth:**
	    - Used for **anaerobic cultures**; specimens should be put into RCM broth and transported immediately. It provides glutathione and unsaturated fatty acids to create a low redox potential for obligate anaerobes.
	- **Standard Mycoplasma fluid medium:**
	    - Contains fetal bovine serum and gelatine, used for **Mycoplasma pneumoniae** specimens to avoid drying.
	- **Viral transport medium (VTM):**
	    - Used for **influenza viruses** (nasopharyngeal swabs/lavage fluid, nasal aspirate, throat swab) and kept at 4°C during transport.
	    - Used for **SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19)** (throat/nasal swabs) and requires maintaining an adequate cold chain.
	    - Can be used for **Mycoplasma pneumoniae** specimens.
	- **Specific considerations for CSF:** CSF should **never be refrigerated** if bacteriological culture is required, as delicate pathogens like _H. influenzae_, pneumococci, or meningococci may die. If delay is expected, it may be kept in an incubator at 37°C. However, for molecular diagnosis, CSF can be kept in a freezer. CSF is considered a precious specimen and requires immediate transport (<15 minutes).
	
	**General Guidelines for Specimen Transport:**
	
	- **Immediate transport:** CSF, body fluids, ocular specimens, tissue specimens, suprapubic aspirate, and bone specimens should be transported within 15 minutes.
	- **Within 2 hours (general):** Most specimens should reach the laboratory within two hours for further processing.
	- **Exceptions:** Urine with preservative is acceptable up to 24 hours (otherwise within 2 hours), and stool/rectal swabs with transport medium are acceptable up to 24 hours.
	- **Storage before processing:** Blood cultures should be incubated immediately upon receipt. Sterile body fluids, bone, vitreous fluid, and suprapubic aspirates should be immediately plated and incubated at 37°C.
	- **Avoid formalin:** Specimens should never be sent in containers with formalin for microbiological analysis.
	- **Appropriate container:** Specimens should be collected in sterile, tightly sealed, leak-proof, wide-mouth, screw-capped containers.
	- **Labelling:** All specimens must be appropriately labelled with patient and specimen details.
	- **Prioritisation:** Certain precious specimens should be processed immediately (e.g., CSF, blood cultures).
