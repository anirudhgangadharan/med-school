1. Classify protozoan infections with examples
	In the context of medical microbiology, protozoan infections are a significant category of parasitic diseases. Protozoa are **unicellular eukaryotic cells** that carry out all physiological functions, possessing cellular organelles and metabolic pathways similar to other eukaryotes.
	
	Here is a concise, high-yield classification of medically important protozoan infections, optimised for medical school examinations:
	
	---
	
	### Classification of Protozoan Infections
	
	Protozoans are broadly classified based on their **morphology and motility**. A more modern classification is based on **molecular phylogeny**.
	
	**I. Morphological Classification (Clinical Relevance)**
	
	A. **Amoeba (Sarcodina)**
	
	- Characterized by **amoeboid movement** via pseudopodia.
	- **Examples**:
	    - _**Entamoeba histolytica**_:
	        - **Disease**: Intestinal Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery) and Extraintestinal Amoebiasis (e.g., **amoebic liver abscess** – characterised by "chocolate brown" pus without pus cells).
	        - **Diagnosis**: Detection of trophozoites (often with ingested RBCs) and cysts in stool microscopy, antigen detection, or molecular methods like PCR targeting rRNA genes.
	    - **Free-living Amoebae** (_Naegleria_, _Acanthamoeba_, _Balamuthia_):
	        - **Disease**: Opportunistic infections, primarily central nervous system (CNS) infections.
	            - _**Naegleria fowleri**_: Causes **Primary Amoebic Meningoencephalitis (PAM)**, a rapidly fatal infection often acquired from swimming in warm fresh water.
	            - _**Acanthamoeba**_: Causes **Granulomatous Amoebic Encephalitis (GAE)** (chronic) and **amoebic keratitis** (eye infection, often associated with contact lens use).
	            - _**Balamuthia mandrillaris**_: Also causes GAE.
	
	B. **Flagellates (Mastigophora)**
	
	- Characterized by **motility via flagella**.
	- **Examples**:
	    - **Intestinal Flagellate**: _**Giardia lamblia**_:
	        - **Disease**: Giardiasis, causing chronic diarrhea, steatorrhea, and malabsorption.
	        - **Diagnosis**: Detection of pear-shaped trophozoites (often with "falling leaf" motility on wet mount) and cysts in stool microscopy; antigen detection (ELISA, DFA); or PCR.
	    - **Genital Flagellate**: _**Trichomonas vaginalis**_:
	        - **Disease**: Trichomoniasis, a sexually transmitted infection causing vaginitis (frothy, foul-smelling discharge) or urethritis.
	        - **Diagnosis**: Detection of motile, pear-shaped trophozoites in wet mount of vaginal discharge (**"jerky motility"**); culture; or antigen detection.
	    - **Hemoflagellates**: (_Leishmania_, _Trypanosoma_):
	        - _**Leishmania**_ spp. (_Leishmania donovani_ most common):
	            - **Disease**: Leishmaniasis (Visceral Leishmaniasis/Kala-azar, Cutaneous Leishmaniasis, Mucocutaneous Leishmaniasis). _L. donovani_ causes Kala-azar, characterised by **fever, hepatosplenomegaly, pancytopenia, hypergammaglobulinemia, and weight loss**. Transmitted by **sand flies**.
	            - **Diagnosis**: Demonstration of **Leishman-Donovan (LD) bodies** (amastigotes within macrophages) in bone marrow or splenic aspirates; serology (e.g., rk39 ICT); or molecular methods.
	        - _**Trypanosoma**_ spp.:
	            - _**Trypanosoma brucei**_:
	                - **Disease**: African Sleeping Sickness (African Trypanosomiasis), characterised by fever, lymphadenopathy, and later CNS involvement (sleep cycle disturbance).
	                - **Transmission**: **Tsetse fly**.
	                - **Diagnosis**: Demonstration of trypomastigotes in blood, CSF, or lymph node aspirates.
	            - _**Trypanosoma cruzi**_:
	                - **Disease**: Chagas' Disease (American Trypanosomiasis), acute phase (fever, Romana's sign) and chronic phase (cardiomyopathy, megaesophagus, megacolon).
	                - **Transmission**: **Reduviid bug** (Kissing bug).
	                - **Diagnosis**: Demonstration of C-shaped trypomastigotes in blood (acute phase) or xenodiagnosis.
	
	C. **Apicomplexa (Sporozoa)**
	
	- **Obligate intracellular parasites** that reproduce asexually and sexually. Lack distinct motility structures.
	- **Examples**:
	    - _**Plasmodium**_ spp. (e.g., _P. falciparum_, _P. vivax_, _P. malariae_, _P. ovale_, _P. knowlesi_):
	        - **Disease**: Malaria, characterized by cyclic fever paroxysms, anemia, and splenomegaly. _P. falciparum_ causes severe (malignant) malaria with complications like **cerebral malaria**.
	        - **Transmission**: **Anopheles mosquito** (female) (outside source, but inferred by disease context).
	        - **Diagnosis**: Gold standard is **peripheral blood smear** microscopy (thick smear for detection, thin smear for speciation – e.g., _P. falciparum_ shows **multiple ring forms**, **accole forms**, and **banana-shaped gametocytes**; schizonts are typically not seen in peripheral blood). Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs) also common.
	    - _**Babesia**_ spp.:
	        - **Disease**: Babesiosis, a tick-borne illness resembling malaria, especially in immunocompromised individuals.
	        - **Diagnosis**: Detection of merozoites in RBCs in peripheral blood smear, often forming a **"Maltese cross"** tetrad arrangement.
	    - _**Toxoplasma gondii**_:
	        - **Disease**: Toxoplasmosis, usually asymptomatic, but can cause severe disease in immunocompromised individuals (**encephalitis** is common) and congenital infections (hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis).
	        - **Transmission**: Ingestion of oocysts (from **cat feces**) or tissue cysts (raw/undercooked meat).
	        - **Diagnosis**: Serology (IgM/IgG antibodies), microscopy of tissue biopsies (tachyzoites, tissue cysts), or PCR.
	    - **Opportunistic Coccidian Parasites**: (_Cryptosporidium_, _Cyclospora_, _Cystoisospora_):
	        - **Disease**: Cause **profuse watery diarrhea**, particularly in immunocompromised patients (e.g., HIV/AIDS).
	        - **Diagnosis**: Detection of **acid-fast oocysts** in stool microscopy (e.g., _Cryptosporidium_ oocysts are small and round, 4-6 µm).
	
	D. **Ciliates (Ciliophora)**
	
	- Characterized by **motility via cilia**.
	- **Example**:
	    - _**Balantidium coli**_:
	        - **Disease**: Balantidiasis, causing dysentery-like symptoms or asymptomatic carriage.
	        - **Diagnosis**: Detection of large, ciliated trophozoites (with characteristic kidney-shaped macronucleus and small micronucleus) or cysts in stool.
	
	**II. Molecular Classification (Phyla and Genera)**
	
	This classification organises protozoa into larger phylogenetic groups based on their genetic makeup.
	
	- **Phylum Archezoa**:
	    - Class Metamonada (Intestinal and Genital Flagellates): _Giardia_, _Enteromonas_, _Trichomonas_.
	- **Phylum Amoebozoa**:
	    - Class Archamoebae (Amoebas): _Entamoeba_, Free-living Amoebae (_Naegleria_, _Acanthamoeba_).
	- **Phylum Euglenozoa**:
	    - Class Kinetoplastea (Hemoflagellates): _Leishmania_, _Trypanosoma_.
	- **Phylum Apicomplexa**:
	    - Class Haemosporida: _Plasmodium_, _Babesia_.
	    - Class Coccidia (Opportunistic Coccidian Parasites): _Toxoplasma_, _Cryptosporidium_, _Cystoisospora_, _Cyclospora_.
	- **Phylum Ciliophora**:
	    - Class Litostomatea (Ciliates): _Balantidium_.
