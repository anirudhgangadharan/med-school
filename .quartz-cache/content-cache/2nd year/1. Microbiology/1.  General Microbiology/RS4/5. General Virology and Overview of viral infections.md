1. Viruses producing inclusion bodies
	
	Here is a concise, high-yield overview of viruses producing inclusion bodies, optimized for medical school examinations:
	
	---
	
	### Viruses Producing Inclusion Bodies
	
	**I. Definition & Diagnostic Significance**
	
	- **Definition**: Aggregates of virions, viral proteins, or other viral replication products within host cells.
	- **Staining Properties**: Confer altered staining properties to the host cell, making them visible under a light microscope after histopathological staining.
	- **Diagnostic Utility**: They are **characteristic of specific viral infections** and aid in presumptive identification, especially in tissue sections.
	
	**II. Types and Characteristics Based on Location**
	
	**A. Intracytoplasmic Inclusion Bodies**
	
	- **Staining**: Generally **acidophilic** (pink when stained with Giemsa or eosin methylene blue).
	- **Key Viruses & Specific Names**:
	    - **Rabies virus**: **Negri bodies**.
	        - **Characteristic**: Eosinophilic, with characteristic basophilic inner granules; commonly found in Purkinje cells of the cerebellum and pyramidal neurons of the hippocampus.
	        - **Clinical Relevance**: Pathognomonic for rabies, though absence does not rule out diagnosis.
	    - **Poxviruses**:
	        - **Variola virus**: **Paschen body**.
	        - **Vaccinia virus**: **Guarnieri bodies**.
	        - **Molluscum contagiosum virus**: **Molluscum bodies**.
	    - **Reovirus**: **Perinuclear cytoplasmic body**.
	
	**B. Intranuclear Inclusion Bodies**
	
	- **Staining**: Generally **basophilic**.
	- **Classification (Cowdry, 1934)**:
	    - **Cowdry type A inclusions**: Variable in size, granular appearance.
	        - **Herpes simplex virus (HSV)**: **Lipschultz body**.
	        - **Yellow fever virus**: **Torres body**.
	    - **Cowdry type B inclusions**: More circumscribed, amorphous or hyaline spheres, multiple in number.
	        - **Poliovirus**.
	        - **Adenovirus**.
	
	**C. Both Intracytoplasmic and Intranuclear Inclusion Bodies**
	
	- **Key Viruses & Specific Names**:
	    - **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**: **"Owl's eye" appearance**.
	        - **Characteristic**: Massive enlargement of infected host cells, with perinuclear cytoplasmic inclusions in addition to usual intranuclear inclusions.
	    - **Measles virus**: Seen within **multinucleated giant cells** (Warthin-Finkeldey cells).
