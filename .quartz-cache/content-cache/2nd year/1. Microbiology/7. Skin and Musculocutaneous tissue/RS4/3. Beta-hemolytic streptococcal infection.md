1. List the suppurative and non suppurative infections of streptococcus pyogenes
	**_Streptococcus pyogenes_ (Group A Streptococcus - GAS) Infections**
	
	_Streptococcus pyogenes_ is a significant human pathogen, responsible for a wide array of diseases classified into two main categories: **suppurative (pyogenic) infections** and **non-suppurative (post-infectious) sequelae**.
	
	**I. Suppurative (Pyogenic) Infections:** These are direct infections caused by the bacteria, often characterised by pus formation. _S. pyogenes_ produces various virulence factors including M protein, streptolysins O and S, hyaluronidase, and streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins (SPEs) that contribute to its pathogenicity.
	
	- **Pharyngitis (Streptococcal Sore Throat)**: A common upper respiratory tract infection.
	- **Scarlet Fever**: A toxin-mediated disease resulting from pharyngitis, characterised by a rash due to streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins (SPEs).
	- **Skin and Soft Tissue Infections (SSTIs):**
	    - **Impetigo (Pyoderma)**: Superficial skin infection, commonly presenting as thin, honey-coloured crusted lesions.
	    - **Erysipelas**: A diffuse, spreading infection of the superficial dermis, characterised by a sharply demarcated, raised, red, warm, and tender area.
	    - **Cellulitis**: A deeper infection of the dermis and subcutaneous tissue, with ill-defined borders.
	    - **Necrotizing Fasciitis**: A severe, rapidly progressive deep soft tissue infection, often referred to as "flesh-eating bacteria" due to extensive tissue necrosis. _S. pyogenes_ (especially M types 1 and 3 producing SPEs) is a common cause, accounting for nearly 60% of cases.
	    - **Streptococcal Myositis**.
	    - **Peritonsillar Abscess (Quinsy)**.
	- **Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS)**: A severe, life-threatening condition associated with SPEs, causing fever, rash, desquamation, and multi-organ involvement.
	- **Pneumonia**: Can occasionally cause pneumonia in previously healthy individuals, often associated with pleural effusion.
	- **Empyema**.
	- **Bacteremia/Sepsis**: Can lead to systemic spread and complications like endocarditis, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, and meningitis.
	- **Puerperal Sepsis**: Infection of the female genital tract, typically post-childbirth.
	- **Otitis Media** and **Sinusitis**.
	
	**II. Non-Suppurative (Post-Infectious) Sequelae:** These are autoimmune complications that develop days to weeks after an initial _S. pyogenes_ infection, resulting from molecular mimicry where antibodies against streptococcal antigens cross-react with human tissues.
	
	- **Acute Rheumatic Fever (ARF)**: A systemic inflammatory disease affecting the heart, joints, brain, and skin. It follows pharyngitis but not skin infections.
	- **Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)**: A kidney disease that can follow either pharyngitis or skin infections (impetigo). Notably, ASO (Anti-Streptolysin O) titres may be low in PSGN following pyoderma.
	- **Guttate Psoriasis**.
	- **Reactive Arthritis**.
	- **PANDAS (Pediatric Autoimmune Neuropsychiatric Disorders Associated with Streptococcal infections)**: A syndrome involving sudden onset or exacerbation of obsessive-compulsive disorder or tic disorders following _Streptococcus_ infection.
