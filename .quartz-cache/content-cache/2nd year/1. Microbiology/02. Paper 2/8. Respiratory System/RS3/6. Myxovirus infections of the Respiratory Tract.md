1. Antigenic drift and Antigenic shift
	**Antigenic Drift vs. Antigenic Shift (Influenza Viruses)**
	
	These are mechanisms by which influenza viruses undergo genetic variation, impacting host immunity and vaccine effectiveness.
	
	**1. Antigenic Drift**
	
	- **Mechanism:** A **minor change** occurring due to **point mutations** in the HA (Hemagglutinin) or NA (Neuraminidase) gene.
	    - Results in alteration of the amino acid sequence of the antigenic sites on HA/NA.
	    - Virus can **partially escape recognition** by the host's immune system.
	    - A new variant requires two or more mutations to be epidemiologically significant.
	- **Affected Viruses:** Seen in both **influenza virus type A and B**.
	- **Epidemiological Impact:** Results in **outbreaks and minor periodic epidemics**.
	    - Occurs more frequently, typically every 2–3 years.
	- **Clinical Relevance:** Requires frequent updates to the influenza vaccine composition.
	
	**2. Antigenic Shift**
	
	- **Mechanism:** An **abrupt, major drastic, discontinuous variation** in the sequence of a viral surface protein (HA/NA).
	    - Occurs due to **genetic reassortment** between genomes of two or more influenza viruses (e.g., human and animal strains) infecting the **same host cell**.
	    - Results in a **new virus strain, antigenically unrelated** to the predecessor strains.
	    - Antibodies developed against previous strains (from infection or vaccination) become **ineffective**.
	- **Affected Viruses:** Occurs **only in influenza A virus**.
	- **Epidemiological Impact:** Results in **pandemics and major epidemics**, e.g., H1N1.
	- **Clinical Relevance:** Represents a significant public health threat as it can infect a large proportion of the population who have no pre-existing immunity. Leads to the emergence of novel influenza strains capable of causing pandemics.
