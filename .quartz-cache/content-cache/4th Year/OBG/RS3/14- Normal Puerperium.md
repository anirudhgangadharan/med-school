1. Lochia (types).

**LOCHIA**

**DEFINITION** Lochia is the vaginal discharge occurring during the first fortnight of the puerperium, originating from the uterine body, cervix, and vagina.

**GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS**

- **Odor:** It has a peculiar offensive, fishy smell.
- **Reaction:** It is alkaline in reaction initially, tending to become acidic toward the end.
- **Amount:** The average amount of discharge during the first 5–6 days is estimated to be approximately 250 mL.
- **Normal Duration:** The discharge typically lasts for 15 days but may extend up to 3 weeks.

**TYPES OF LOCHIA** Depending upon the variation in the color of the discharge, lochia is classified into three distinct types:

**1. Lochia Rubra**

- **Duration:** Days 1 to 4.
- **Color:** Red.
- **Composition:** Consists of blood, shreds of fetal membranes and decidua, vernix caseosa, lanugo, and meconium.

**2. Lochia Serosa**

- **Duration:** Days 5 to 9.
- **Color:** Yellowish, pink, or pale brownish.
- **Composition:** Consists of fewer red blood cells (RBCs) but more leukocytes, wound exudate, cervical mucus, and microorganisms (specifically anaerobic streptococci and staphylococci).
- _Note:_ The presence of bacteria in lochia serosa is not pathognomonic of infection unless it is accompanied by clinical signs of sepsis.

**3. Lochia Alba**

- **Duration:** Days 10 to 15.
- **Color:** Pale white.
- **Composition:** Contains an abundance of decidual cells, leukocytes, mucus, cholesterin crystals, fatty and granular epithelial cells, and microorganisms.

**CLINICAL IMPORTANCE AND ABNORMALITIES** The character of the lochial discharge provides crucial information regarding the puerperal state of the mother. The vulval pads must be inspected daily to monitor for the following warning signs:

- **Odor:** A malodorous (foul-smelling) discharge is indicative of an infection. It should also raise the suspicion of a retained plug or cotton piece inside the vagina.
- **Amount:** A scanty or absent discharge signifies infection or lochiometra (accumulation of lochia within the uterus). Conversely, an excessive amount of discharge also indicates infection.
- **Color:** The persistence of red-colored discharge (lochia rubra) beyond its normal physiological limit signifies uterine subinvolution or retained bits of the conceptus.
- **Duration:** The persistence of lochia alba beyond 3 weeks strongly suggests the presence of a local genital lesion.

2. **POSTNATAL CARE AND CHECK-UP**

**DEFINITION** Postnatal care encompasses the systematic examination of the mother and the baby, along with appropriate advice provided to the mother during the postpartum period. The routine postnatal check-up is typically conducted at the end of the 6th week postpartum.

**AIMS AND OBJECTIVES**

- To assess maternal health and review or correct medical disorders such as diabetes, hypertension, or thyroid conditions.
- To detect and initiate early treatment for any gynecological conditions arising from the obstetric legacy.
- To evaluate the progress of the baby, including the infant's immunization schedule.
- To inquire about psychological issues like depression, as well as problems related to sexuality, contraception, and future pregnancies.
- To impart proper family planning guidance.

**PROCEDURE: EXAMINATION OF THE MOTHER**

- **General Physical Examination:** Routine examination involves the recording of maternal weight, pallor, and blood pressure.
- **Abdominal and Breast Examination:** The tone of the abdominal muscles is assessed, and an examination of the breasts is done if needed.
- **Pelvic Examination:** This is performed only when clinically indicated. A cervical smear (cytology) may be taken if it has not been done previously.
- **Investigations:** Laboratory investigations, such as hemoglobin estimation, may be advised depending upon the clinical need.

**PROCEDURE: EXAMINATION OF THE BABY**

- The examination of the newborn should be strictly conducted by a pediatrician.
- A well-attached baby clinic to the postpartum unit is an absolute necessity for this purpose.
- Appropriate immunization for the baby is initiated.

**ADVICE GIVEN DURING CHECK-UP**

- **General Activities:** If the patient is in sound health, she is permitted to resume her usual daily duties.
- **Postpartum Exercises:** The mother is advised to continue postpartum exercises for another 4 to 6 weeks to tone up the pelvic floor and abdominal muscles.
- **Maternal Vaccination:** Vaccinations such as MMR, Hepatitis B, and Tdap are administered or advised.
- **Infant Care:** The mother is instructed to periodically evaluate the baby's progress and is strongly encouraged to continue breastfeeding for 6 months.
- **Contraception:** Depending on her breastfeeding status, state of maternal health, and number of children, a suitable contraceptive method (such as POPs, DMPA, IUCD, or sterilization) is prescribed.

**MANAGEMENT OF COMMON POSTNATAL AILMENTS** Additional investigations and appropriate therapy are provided according to any abnormalities detected during the check-up.

- **Irregular Vaginal Bleeding:** Irregular or heavy bleeding 4-6 weeks postpartum is often the first menstrual period, especially in nonlactating women, and simple assurance is usually enough.
- **Cervical Ectopy (Erosion):** Hormone-induced ectopy during pregnancy takes about 12 weeks to regress; asymptomatic ectopy is re-evaluated at 6 weeks, and if it still persists, cauterization is considered.
- **Backache:** Mostly due to sacroiliac or lumbosacral strain; orthopedic lumbar backache is often successfully relieved by physiotherapy.
- **Uterine Descent and Incontinence:** A slight degree of uterine descent with cystocele, relaxed perineum, or urinary/anal stress incontinence are common findings. These can be effectively cured by rigorous pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT).
- **Mental Health:** Women should be evaluated for postpartum depression, and if grieving from a perinatal loss, they must be offered bereavement counseling, psychological support, and review of investigation reports for future pregnancy planning.
