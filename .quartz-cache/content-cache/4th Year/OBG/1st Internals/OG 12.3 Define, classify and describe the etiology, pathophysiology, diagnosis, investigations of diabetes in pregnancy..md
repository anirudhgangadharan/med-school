### **1. DEFINITION OF DIABETES IN PREGNANCY**

- **Diabetes Mellitus:** A chronic metabolic disorder resulting from either insulin deficiency (relative or absolute) or peripheral tissue resistance (decreased sensitivity) to the action of insulin.
- **Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM):** Defined as carbohydrate intolerance of variable severity with onset or first recognition during the present pregnancy (ACOG-2017). This definition is used irrespective of whether the condition persists after pregnancy or requires insulin for treatment.
- **Overt Diabetes:** Pre-existing diabetes (Type 1 or Type 2) or diabetes detected for the first time during pregnancy with classic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss) and high blood glucose levels.

---

### **2. CLASSIFICATION OF DIABETES IN PREGNANCY**

**General Classification**

- **Type 1:** Characterized by abrupt onset at a young age, absolute insulinopenia (beta-cell destruction), and genetic predisposition with autoantibodies. Patients have a high risk of Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA).
- **Type 2:** Characterized by late age onset, association with overweight women, and peripheral tissue (skeletal muscle, liver) insulin resistance leading to hyperinsulinemia.
- **Maturity Onset Diabetes of the Youth (MODY):** Due to beta-cell genetic mutation (glucokinase gene) with an autosomal dominant mode of inheritance.

**Priscilla White’s Classification** This classification is used to assess perinatal outcome and to formulate obstetric management based on the age of onset, disease duration, and presence of vasculopathy.

- **Class A:** Gestational diabetes. (A1: Fasting <105 mg/dL, managed on diet; A2: Fasting >105 mg/dL, requiring insulin).
- **Class B:** Onset >20 years, duration <10 years, no vascular disease.
- **Class C:** Onset 10-19 years, duration 10-19 years, no vascular disease.
- **Class D:** Onset <10 years, duration >20 years, benign retinopathy.
- **Class F:** Diabetic nephropathy.
- **Class H:** Coronary artery disease.
- **Class R:** Proliferative retinopathy.
- **Class T:** Renal transplant.

### **3. ETIOLOGY AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY**

**Mechanism of Insulin Resistance (Diabetogenic State)**

- Pregnancy is a state of chronic low-grade inflammation characterized by increased circulating levels of C-Reactive Protein (CRP) and Interleukin-6 (IL-6), which enhance insulin resistance.
- There is a combined effect of anti-insulin hormones: Human Placental Lactogen (hPL), estrogen, progesterone, free cortisol, and degradation of insulin by the placenta.
- Other factors like TNF-alpha (inversely correlated with insulin sensitivity) and leptin also decrease insulin sensitivity.
- To compensate, there is hypertrophy and hyperplasia of maternal pancreatic beta-cells, leading to hyperinsulinemia. However, when insulin secretion cannot overcome the resistance, maternal hyperglycemia results.
- Placental glucose transport to the fetus occurs via facilitated diffusion using the GLUT-1 transporter located in the syncytiotrophoblast.

**Accelerated Starvation and Lipid Alteration**

- With the "accelerated starvation" concept, there is rapid activation of lipolysis during short periods of fasting, generating Free Fatty Acids (FFA) for gluconeogenesis.
- Decreased HDL cholesterol is observed (especially in Type 1), reducing plasma antioxidant capacity and increasing oxidative stress, a potential factor for congenital malformations.
- Elevated maternal FFA transfers to the fetus, accelerating fetal triglyceride synthesis and causing fetal adiposity (macrosomia).

**Pathophysiology of Glycosuria**

- During normal pregnancy, the renal threshold for glucose is diminished due to increased glomerular filtration and impaired tubular reabsorption.
- As a result, physiological glycosuria is observed in 50% of normal pregnant women even when blood sugar levels are below 180 mg/dL. Fasting glycosuria or presence on multiple occasions after 20 weeks warrants a Glucose Tolerance Test.

### **4. DIAGNOSIS AND SCREENING**

**High-Risk Factors for GDM**

- Positive family history of diabetes in parents or siblings.
- Previous birth of a macrosomic baby (≥ 4 kg).
- Previous GDM, or history of stillbirth with pancreatic islet hyperplasia on autopsy, or unexplained perinatal loss.
- Presence of polyhydramnios or recurrent vaginal candidiasis in the present pregnancy.
- Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome (PCOS).
- Maternal obesity, age >30 years, and hypertension.

**Screening Strategy**

- All pregnant women should be screened for GDM between 24 and 28 weeks of gestation. Early screening is recommended for women with high-risk factors.

**Diagnostic Criteria for GDM (IADPSG - 75g 2-hour OGTT)** Diagnosis of GDM is confirmed if **one or more** of the following venous plasma glucose thresholds are met or exceeded:

- **Fasting:** ≥ 92 mg/dL.
- **1-Hour:** ≥ 180 mg/dL.
- **2-Hour:** ≥ 153 mg/dL.

**Diagnostic Criteria for Overt Diabetes (ADA)** Overt diabetes is diagnosed if any of the following criteria are met:

- Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL.
- 2-hour post-glucose (75g) value ≥ 200 mg/dL.
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5%.

_(Note: The DIPSI guidelines in India recommend a 1-step procedure giving 75g oral glucose regardless of the last meal, diagnosing GDM if the 2-hour venous plasma glucose is >140 mg/dL.)_

### **5. INVESTIGATIONS**

**Maternal Investigations**

- **HbA1c (Glycosylated Hemoglobin):** Measured in the first trimester. Levels ≥6.5% suggest overt diabetes. Value <6% is desirable for optimal control.
- **Capillary Blood Glucose Profiling:** Self-monitoring is essential. Target levels: Fasting ≤95 mg/dL; 1-hour postprandial ≤140 mg/dL; 2-hour postprandial ≤120 mg/dL.
- **Ophthalmoscopy:** Done in early pregnancy and repeated each trimester to detect and grade diabetic retinopathy (e.g., neovascularization or microaneurysms).
- **Renal Function Tests:** To rule out Class F diabetic nephropathy. Serum creatinine and 24-hour urine protein are monitored.
- **Urine Routine & Culture:** Regular testing for glycosuria, ketonuria, and asymptomatic bacteriuria.

**Fetal Investigations and Surveillance**

- **11-14 Weeks:** First-trimester combined screening (nuchal translucency, free β-hCG, PAPP-A) to rule out chromosomal anomalies.
- **16 Weeks:** Maternal Serum Alpha-Fetoprotein (MSAFP) to screen for neural tube defects.
- **20-22 Weeks:** A detailed high-resolution targeted ultrasonography and fetal echocardiography must be performed. Diabetic pregnancies have a 2-6 fold increase in structural anomalies (e.g., VSD, transposition of great vessels, neural tube defects, sacral agenesis).
- **Serial Ultrasonography (Every 2-3 weeks):** Essential for mapping the fetal growth profile (detecting macrosomia or FGR) and assessing amniotic fluid volume (detecting polyhydramnios).
- **Antepartum Fetal Surveillance (From 28 weeks):** Clinical assessment, Non-Stress Test (NST), Biophysical Profile (BPP), and umbilical artery Doppler velocimetry (especially useful in cases associated with maternal vasculopathy or pre-eclampsia).
