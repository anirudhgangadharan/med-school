### **1. INTRODUCTION AND ETIOLOGY**

Pregnancy induces a hyperdynamic circulation characterized by an increase in cardiac output, oxygen consumption, and heart rate, along with a decrease in systemic vascular resistance. These marked hemodynamic changes can mimic, unmask, or severely exacerbate underlying cardiac diseases.

**Etiology / Types of Heart Disease in Pregnancy:**

- **Rheumatic Valvular Lesions:** Constitute the majority of cases, with Mitral Stenosis (MS) being the predominantly encountered lesion (80%).
- **Congenital Heart Lesions:** Predominant conditions include Atrial Septal Defect (ASD), Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD), Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA), Pulmonary stenosis, Coarctation of aorta, and Fallot's tetralogy.
- **Other/Rare Causes:** Hypertensive, coronary cardiac (ischemic), thyrotoxic, or syphilitic heart diseases. Cardiomyopathies (such as peripartum cardiomyopathy) and myocardial infarction can also occur.

### **2. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (EFFECTS OF PREGNANCY ON HEART DISEASE)**

The marked hemodynamic alterations during pregnancy, particularly the increased cardiac output, have profound effects on the compromised heart.

- **Hemodynamic Load:** The hyperdynamic circulation increases the resting cardiac workload.
- **Intrapartum Stress:** Labor is associated with pain, stress, and anxiety, leading to tachycardia. Each uterine contraction squeezes out 400–500 mL of blood from the uterus into the maternal systemic circulation (autotransfusion).
- **Consequences:** In a woman with preexisting heart disease, this autotransfusion significantly increases: (a) Cardiac Output (CO), (b) Pulmonary pressures, and (c) Pulmonary congestion.
- **Timing of Cardiac Failure:** Cardiac failure commonly precipitates at three critical periods: around 30 weeks of gestation (when plasma volume peaks), during labor, and most frequently, immediately following delivery.
- **Risk Factors Precipitating Heart Failure:** Advanced maternal age, cardiac arrhythmias, left ventricular hypertrophy, history of previous heart failure, infections (urinary, dental, respiratory), anemia, hypertension, obesity, hyperthyroidism, dietary indiscretion, and multiple pregnancy.

### **3. CLASSIFICATION OF HEART DISEASE IN PREGNANCY**

**A. New York Heart Association (NYHA) Functional Classification** This classification assesses the patient's functional capacity and symptoms:

- **Grade I:** Uncompromised. No limitation of physical activity. Ordinary physical activity does not cause undue fatigue, palpitation, or dyspnea.
- **Grade II:** Slightly compromised. Slight limitation of physical activity. Comfortable at rest, but ordinary physical activity causes discomfort.
- **Grade III:** Markedly compromised. Marked limitation of physical activity. Comfortable at rest, but discomfort occurs with less than ordinary activity.
- **Grade IV:** Severely compromised. Inability to carry out any physical activity without discomfort. Symptoms of cardiac insufficiency or heart failure are present even at rest.

**B. WHO Classification of Maternal Cardiovascular Risk** This classification is used to predict pregnancy outcomes based on specific anatomical lesions and maternal risk:

- **WHO Class I (Low Risk):** Uncomplicated, small or mild lesions (e.g., pulmonary stenosis, PDA, mitral valve prolapse), successfully repaired simple lesions (ASD, VSD, PDA), and isolated ectopic beats.
- **WHO Class II (Moderate Risk):** Unoperated ASD or VSD, repaired tetralogy of Fallot, and most arrhythmias.
- **WHO Class II or III (Depending on Individual):** Mild left ventricular impairment, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, native or tissue valvular disease not in Class I/IV, Marfan syndrome without aortic dilatation, and repaired coarctation.
- **WHO Class III (Major Risk):** Mechanical valve, systemic right ventricle, Fontan circulation, unrepaired cyanotic heart disease, other complex CHD, Marfan syndrome with aortic dilatation 40–45 mm, and bicuspid aortic valve with aortic dilation 45–50 mm.
- **WHO Class IV (Pregnancy Contraindicated - Extremely High Risk):** Pulmonary arterial hypertension of any cause, severe systemic ventricular dysfunction (LVEF <30%, NYHA III-IV), previous peripartum cardiomyopathy with residual LV impairment, severe mitral stenosis, severe symptomatic aortic stenosis, Marfan syndrome with aorta dilated >45 mm, and native severe coarctation.

### **4. DIAGNOSIS (CLINICAL FEATURES)**

Diagnosis must carefully differentiate between the normal physiological changes of pregnancy and true pathological cardiac disease.

**Symptoms**

- _Physiological:_ Mild dyspnea, fatigue, and decreased exercise tolerance are common in normal pregnancy.
- _Pathological:_ Severe dyspnea, nocturnal cough, syncope, and chest pain strongly suggest underlying structural heart disease.

**Signs**

- _Physiological:_ Tachycardia, shift of the ventricular apex, loud first heart sound with splitting, a continuous murmur at the 2nd to 4th intercostal space (mammary souffle), and a systolic ejection murmur at the left sternal border due to hyperdynamic flow.
- _Pathological (Requires Investigation):_
    - Pansystolic, late systolic, loud ejection systolic, or diastolic murmurs.
    - Murmurs associated with a thrill.
    - Signs of cardiac enlargement and significant arrhythmias.

### **5. INVESTIGATIONS**

Symptoms must be carefully assessed and appropriate diagnostic modalities utilized to confirm the specific pathology.

- **Electrocardiography (ECG):** Used to detect T wave inversion, biatrial enlargement, and dysrhythmias.
- **Echocardiography (with Color Flow Doppler):** The most critical investigation. It identifies structural abnormalities (ASD, VSD), valve anatomy, valve area, valvular function, left ventricular ejection fraction, and pulmonary artery systolic pressure.
- **Chest Radiography (CXR):** Done using an abdominal lead shield. It may reveal cardiomegaly, increased pulmonary vascular markings, enlargement of pulmonary veins, and signs of congestive cardiac failure (CCF).
- **Cardiac MRI:** Utilized to delineate complex cardiac anatomy when it is not well-evaluated by standard echocardiography.
- **Cardiac Catheterization:** May be required in highly specific or complex cases for precise hemodynamic measurements.
