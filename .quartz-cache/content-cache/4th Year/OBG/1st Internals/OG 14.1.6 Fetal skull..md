### **1. Introduction**

The fetal skull is to some extent compressible and is mainly composed of thin, pliable tabular (flat) bones forming the vault. The vault is anchored to the rigid and incompressible bones at the base of the skull.

### **2. Areas of the Fetal Skull**

The skull is arbitrarily divided into several zones of obstetrical importance:

- **Vertex:** A quadrangular area bounded anteriorly by the bregma and coronal sutures, posteriorly by the lambda and lambdoid sutures, and laterally by lines passing through the parietal eminences.
- **Brow:** An area bounded on one side by the anterior fontanel and coronal sutures, and on the other side by the root of the nose and supraorbital ridges.
- **Face:** Bounded on one side by the root of the nose and supraorbital ridges, and on the other by the junction of the floor of the mouth with the neck.
- **Sinciput:** The area lying in front of the anterior fontanel; corresponds to the area of the brow.
- **Occiput:** Limited to the occipital bone.

### **3. Sutures of the Skull and their Importance**

Flat bones of the vault are united by non-ossified membranes called sutures.

- **Sagittal (Longitudinal) suture:** Lies between the two parietal bones.
- **Coronal sutures:** Run between the parietal and frontal bones on either side.
- **Frontal suture:** Lies between the two frontal bones.
- **Lambdoid sutures:** Separate the occipital bone and the two parietal bones.

**Obstetrical Importance of Sutures:**

1. Permit gliding movement of one bone over the other during molding of the head.
2. Digital palpation of the sagittal suture during internal examination in labor gives an idea of the manner of engagement (synclitism or asynclitism), the degree of internal rotation, and the degree of molding.

### **4. Fontanels**

Wide gaps in the suture lines are called fontanels. Of the 6 fontanels, two are of major obstetric significance.

**A. Anterior Fontanel (Bregma)**

- **Formation:** Formed by the joining of four sutures (frontal anteriorly, sagittal posteriorly, and two coronal sutures laterally).
- **Shape & Size:** Diamond-shaped. Its anteroposterior and transverse diameters measure approximately 3 cm each.
- **Ossification:** The floor is membranous and ossifies 18 months after birth (pathological if it fails to ossify after 24 months).
- **Obstetric Importance:**
    - Palpation through internal examination denotes the degree of flexion of the head.
    - Facilitates molding of the head.
    - Accommodates marked brain growth during the first year of life.
    - Reflects intracranial status (depressed in dehydration, elevated in raised intracranial tension).
    - Allows collection of blood/exchange transfusion via the superior longitudinal sinus or CSF from lateral ventricles.

**B. Posterior Fontanel (Lambda)**

- **Formation:** Formed by the junction of three sutures (sagittal suture anteriorly and two lambdoid sutures laterally).
- **Shape & Size:** Triangular in shape, measuring about 1.2 x 1.2 cm.
- **Ossification:** The floor is membranous but becomes bony at term (its nomenclature as a fontanel is technically a misnomer at birth).
- **Obstetric Importance:** Denotes the position of the head in relation to the maternal pelvis.

_(Note: A third fontanel, the Sagittal fontanel, is inconsistent, lies on the posterior third of the sagittal suture, and has no clinical importance)._

### **5. Diameters of the Fetal Skull**

**A. Anteroposterior Diameters (Depend on the attitude of the head):**

- **Suboccipitobregmatic (9.5 cm / 3¾"):** Extends from the nape of the neck to the center of the bregma. Presents in _complete flexion_ (Vertex presentation).
- **Suboccipitofrontal (10 cm / 4"):** Extends from the nape of the neck to the anterior end of the anterior fontanel. Presents in _incomplete flexion_ (Vertex presentation).
- **Occipitofrontal (11.5 cm / 4½"):** Extends from the occipital eminence to the root of the nose. Presents in _marked deflexion_ (Vertex presentation).
- **Mentovertical (14 cm):** Extends from the midpoint of the chin to the highest point on the sagittal suture. Presents in _partial extension_ (Brow presentation).
- **Submentobregmatic (9.5 cm):** Extends from the junction of the floor of the mouth/neck to the center of the bregma. Presents in _complete extension_ (Face presentation).

**B. Transverse Diameters:**

- **Biparietal diameter (9.5 cm):** Distance between the two parietal eminences. It is the maximum transverse diameter of the head.
- **Super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm):** The distance which passes through the brim during exaggerated parietal presentation (asynclitism).
- **Bimastoid diameter (7.5 cm / 3"):** The distance between the tips of the mastoid processes. This diameter is incompressible.

### **6. Circumferences of the Fetal Skull**

The circumference of the plane of engagement differs according to the attitude of the head:

- **Complete flexion (Suboccipitobregmatic + Biparietal):** 27.5 cm (11"). Shape is almost round.
- **Deflexed (Occipitofrontal + Biparietal):** 34 cm (13½"). Shape is oval.
- **Incomplete extension (Mentovertical + Biparietal):** 37.5 cm (15"). Shape is a bigger oval.
- **Complete extension (Submentobregmatic + Biparietal):** 27.5 cm (11"). Shape is almost round.

### **7. Molding of the Head**

- **Definition:** It is the alteration of the shape of the forecoming head while passing through the resistant birth passage during labor.
- **Mechanism:** There is compression of the engaging diameter of the head with corresponding elongation of the diameter at a right angle to it. The parietal bones tend to overlap the adjacent bones (occipital bone behind, frontal bones in front, and temporal bones at the sides). In the first vertex position, the right parietal bone tends to override the left.
- **Grading:**
    - _Grade-1:_ Bones touching but not overlapping.
    - _Grade-2:_ Overlapping but easily separated.
    - _Grade-3:_ Fixed overlapping.
- **Significance:** Slight molding is inevitable and beneficial, enabling the head to pass easily. Extreme molding (seen in disproportion) may produce severe intracranial disturbances like tearing of the tentorium cerebelli or subdural hemorrhage. The shape of the molding provides information about the position of the head. Molding disappears within a few hours after birth.

### **8. Caput Succedaneum**

- **Definition:** The formation of a diffuse, boggy swelling due to stagnation of fluid (serum) in the layers of the scalp beneath the girdle of contact (dilating cervix or vulval ring).
- **Mechanism:** As the head descends, the unsupported scalp over the dilating cervix is free from pressure, but the tissues in contact with the full circumference are compressed. This interferes with venous and lymphatic return, leading to fluid stagnation and edema. It usually occurs after the rupture of membranes.
- **Clinical Features:** Present at birth, soft/compressible, diffuse (spreads over and crosses suture lines), and changes position to the dependent part.
- **Significance:** It signifies a static position of the head for a long period. Location of the caput indicates the head's position in the pelvis and degree of flexion (e.g., in a left position, caput is on the right parietal bone; more posterior placement indicates greater flexion). It spontaneously disappears within 24 hours after birth and must be differentiated from a cephalhematoma, which appears hours after birth and does not cross suture lines.
