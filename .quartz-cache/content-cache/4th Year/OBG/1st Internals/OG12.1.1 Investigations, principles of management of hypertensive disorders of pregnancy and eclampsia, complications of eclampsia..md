### **1. Investigations for Hypertensive Disorders and Eclampsia**

The investigations are aimed at assessing the severity of the disease and monitoring end-organ dysfunction:

- **Urine Examination:** To detect proteinuria, tests include 24-hour urine total protein ($\ge$ 0.3 g), urine protein-creatinine (P/C) ratio (>0.3), or dipstick testing ($\ge$ 1+ persistent on two random samples),.
- **Blood Investigations:**
    - Complete blood count and Hematocrit to assess hemoconcentration,.
    - Platelet count (thrombocytopenia is defined as <100,000/µL),.
    - Renal function tests: Serum creatinine ($\ge$ 1.1 mg/dL or doubling of value) and uric acid ($\ge$ 4.5 mg/dL),,.
    - Liver function tests (LFT): Serum transaminases (AST, ALT) and LDH ($\ge$ 2 times the upper limit of normal),.
    - Coagulation profile (PT, PTT, fibrinogen) is done if platelet count is $\le$ 100,000/ml,.
- **Ophthalmoscopic Examination:** Essential in severe cases to detect retinal edema, constriction of arterioles, altered vein-arteriole ratio, hemorrhages, or papilledema,.
- **Fetal Wellbeing Assessment (Antenatal):** Includes Daily Fetal Movement Count (DFMC), Non-Stress Test (NST), Cardiotocography (CTG), and Biophysical Profile (BPP). Ultrasonography (USG) is used to monitor fetal growth (BPD, HC, AC, FL) and amniotic fluid volume. Doppler study of the umbilical artery (UA) and middle cerebral artery (MCA) helps evaluate uteroplacental perfusion,,,.
- **Neuroimaging (In Eclampsia):** Postpartum CT or MRI is indicated for cases with focal neurologic deficits, signs of neurologic injury, atypical presentation, or prolonged coma,.

### **2. Principles of Management of Hypertensive Disorders (PE/GH)**

Since the exact etiology of pre-eclampsia (PE) and gestational hypertension (GH) is obscure, treatment is empirical and symptomatic.

- **Objectives:** (1) Stabilize hypertension and prevent progression to severe PE, HELLP syndrome, or eclampsia. (2) Prevent complications. (3) Prevent eclampsia. (4) Deliver a healthy baby in optimal time. (5) Restore maternal health in the postpartum period.
- **General Care and Monitoring:**
    - Women without severe features can be managed in day-care or outpatient settings with regular BP checks and fetal surveillance, while PE with severe features mandates hospital admission,,.
    - Rest in the left-lateral position is encouraged to minimize vena caval compression, increase renal and uterine blood flow, and lower BP.
    - A normal diet with adequate protein (about 100 g/day) is given; fluids are not restricted, and low salt intake is permitted.
- **Antihypertensive Therapy:** Indicated when BP reaches severe levels (Systolic $\ge$ 160 mm Hg or Diastolic $\ge$ 110 mm Hg, MAP $\ge$ 125 mm Hg) to prevent maternal cardiovascular complications and stroke,,. First-line drugs include IV Labetalol, IV Hydralazine, or oral Nifedipine,,. Target BP is SBP 140-160 mm Hg and DBP 90-100 mm Hg.
- **Corticosteroid Therapy:** Administered to accelerate fetal lung maturity if delivery is planned at $\le$ 34 weeks,.
- **Seizure Prophylaxis:** Magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) is recommended for women with PE with severe features or imminent eclampsia,.
- **Definitive Treatment (Delivery):** Termination of pregnancy is the only definitive cure,. In non-severe cases, expectant management can continue until 37 weeks. In severe cases, delivery is recommended at or beyond 34 weeks after stabilization and steroid administration, or earlier if maternal/fetal deterioration occurs,,.

### **3. Principles of Management of Eclampsia**

Eclampsia is a life-threatening emergency requiring a multidisciplinary intensive care approach,.

- **First Aid and Transport:** Administer sedation (e.g., Midazolam 1-2 mg IV or Diazepam 10 mg IV) and a prophylactic loading dose of Magnesium Sulfate,. Stabilize the patient and urgently transfer her to an equipped tertiary care center with a trained attendant,,.
- **General and Supportive Care:**
    - Call for extra help and place the patient in a left lateral recumbent position to prevent aspiration,,.
    - Maintain oral airway, use a tongue blade to prevent tongue bite, and perform frequent suctioning,,.
    - Ensure oxygenation via face mask (8-10 L/min) and monitor pulse oximetry (SpO2 >95%),.
    - Catheterize the bladder to monitor hourly output,.
    - Administer IV fluids cautiously (crystalloids like Ringer's solution) not exceeding 2 liters in 24 hours to prevent pulmonary edema,. Broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., Ceftriaxone) are given to prevent infection.
- **Specific Medical Management:**
    - _Control of Seizures:_ Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4) is the drug of choice as it acts as a membrane stabilizer and neuroprotector. It effectively controls fits without depressing the mother or infant,,. It is continued for 24 hours following the last seizure or delivery, whichever is later,,.
    - _Control of Hypertension:_ IV Labetalol or Hydralazine is used to stabilize BP.
    - _Diuretics:_ Furosemide is used if pulmonary edema is present,.
- **Obstetric Management (Delivery):** Delivery is the definitive treatment and should be accomplished within 6-8 hours once the patient is stabilized, even if fits are not fully controlled,,.
    - If the cervix is favorable, surgical induction (ARM) with or without oxytocin is done,.
    - Cesarean section is performed for obstetric indications, unfavorable cervix, or if fetal distress/uncontrolled fits are present,,.

### **4. Complications of Eclampsia**

The complications are grave and contribute significantly to maternal and perinatal mortality.

- **Maternal Complications:**
    - _Neurological:_ Cerebral hemorrhage, cerebral edema, occipital lobe ischemia (disturbed vision/blindness), coma, and stroke,,.
    - _Cardiopulmonary:_ Acute left ventricular failure, pulmonary edema, aspiration or hypostatic pneumonia, Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), and cardiopulmonary arrest,.
    - _Hepatic and Renal:_ Hepatic necrosis, subcapsular liver hematoma, HELLP syndrome, oliguria, and acute renal failure (AKI),,.
    - _Hematological:_ Thrombocytopenia, Disseminated Intravascular Coagulopathy (DIC).
    - _Obstetric & Traumatic:_ Tongue bite injuries, injuries from falling out of bed, placental abruption, postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), postpartum shock, and puerperal sepsis,,.
- **Fetal and Neonatal Complications:**
    - Prematurity (either spontaneous or iatrogenic induction).
    - Intrauterine asphyxia and hypoxia due to placental insufficiency, infarction, or retroplacental hemorrhage.
    - Adverse effects of medications used to control maternal convulsions.
    - High perinatal mortality ranging from 10-20%.
