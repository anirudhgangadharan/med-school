### **1. Oral Contraceptives**

**A. Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs)**

- **Failure Rate:** Highly effective. The failure rate for perfect use is 0.1 per 100 Women Years (HWY), while typical use failure rate is 8 to 9 per HWY.
- **Minor Side Effects:**
    - _Estrogen-related:_ Nausea, vomiting, headache, chloasma, mastalgia (heaviness/tenderness in the breast), leukorrhea (due to excessive cervical mucus).
    - _Progestin-related:_ Leg cramps, weight gain (anabolic effect, though low-dose COCs rarely cause this), acne, hypomenorrhea, and diminished libido (often due to vaginal dryness).
    - _Menstrual Abnormalities:_ Breakthrough Bleeding (BTB) is common in early cycles due to subthreshold blood levels of hormones or missed pills, and amenorrhea (post-pill amenorrhea lasting >6 months occurs in <1% of cases).
- **Major Complications:**
    - _Vascular (Venous Thromboembolism - VTE):_ Overall risk is 3-4 times higher than in non-users, though absolute risk is very small compared to pregnancy. Risk is lowest with COCs containing levonorgestrel/norethisterone and highest with third-generation progestins.
    - _Arterial Thrombosis:_ Increased risk of myocardial infarction and stroke, especially in women >35 years who smoke, or have hypertension or diabetes.
    - _Hypertension:_ Current low-dose COCs rarely cause significant hypertension, but can aggravate pre-existing hypertension (systolic BP rise due to increased plasma angiotensinogen).
    - _Neoplastic Risks:_ Increased risk of breast cancer by 25% during use (disappears after cessation) and an increased risk of cervical cancer with prolonged use (>5 years).
    - _Hepatobiliary:_ Susceptibility to cholestatic jaundice is increased in women with a prior history of idiopathic recurrent jaundice.

**B. Progestin-Only Pills (POPs / Minipill)**

- **Failure Rate:** Perfect use is 0.3 per HWY, and typical use failure rate is around 9 per HWY. Failure is more common in young women compared to women over 40.
- **Side Effects:** Breakthrough bleeding, acne, mastalgia, headache, and amenorrhea (seen in about 20-30% of cases). Simple cysts of the ovary may occur but do not require surgery. If a pregnancy occurs, the risk of an ectopic pregnancy is relatively high (1 in 10).

**C. Non-Steroidal Oral Contraceptive (Centchroman / Saheli)**

- **Failure Rate:** About 1 to 4 per 100 women years of use.
- **Side Effects:** Mostly devoid of significant adverse metabolic and hormonal effects. The primary side effect is a tendency toward oligomenorrhea.

### **2. Male Contraception**

**A. Barrier Method (Male Condom)**

- **Failure Rate:** Perfect use failure rate is 2 per HWY, while typical use has a high failure rate of 15 to 18 per HWY.
- **Side Effects/Disadvantages:** Allergic reaction to latex, inadequate sexual pleasure, and the risk of accidental breakage or slipping off during coitus.

**B. Permanent Method (Vasectomy)**

- **Failure Rate:** Highly effective with a failure rate ranging from 0.1 to 0.15% (or 0.5% in some data for typical use).
- **Complications/Drawbacks:**
    - Sterility is _not immediate_. Additional contraceptive protection (e.g., condom) is required for about 2-3 months (or until semen is free of sperm).
    - Frigidity or impotence may occur, but it is almost entirely psychological.
    - Surgical complications are minimal and usually not life-threatening (e.g., local hematoma or infection).

**C. Experimental / Pharmacological Male Contraception**

- **Hormonal (GnRH analogs/Testosterone):** Produces a decline in sperm density but causes marked loss of libido (requiring testosterone add-back therapy).
- **Gossypol (Chemical):** Side effects include fatigue, decreased libido, delayed recovery of sperm count, and serious risks of hypokalemic paralysis and cardiac arrhythmias.

### **3. Emergency Contraception (Postcoital Contraception)**

- **Copper IUCD:** The gold standard for emergency contraception. Must be inserted within 5 days. Failure rate is 0 to 1% (or <0.1%). Side effects mimic general IUCD insertion (cramping, bleeding).
- **Ulipristal Acetate (UPA, 30 mg):** Failure rate is 0 to 1% (or 1-2%). Side effects are generally well tolerated, but contraindicated in severe hepatic dysfunction and severe asthma.
- **Levonorgestrel (LNG, 1.5 mg single dose or 0.75 mg two doses):** Failure rate ranges from 0-1% to 0.6-2.6%. Side effects include nausea and vomiting, though less intense than estrogen-containing regimens.
- **Combined Hormonal Regimen (Yuzpe Method):** Failure rate is 0 to 2%. Drawbacks include intense nausea and vomiting (requires antiemetics like metoclopramide).
- **Mifepristone (RU-486, 100 mg):** Failure rate is 0 to 0.6%. Minor side effects include nausea, vomiting, headache, and cramps. It may also cause vasomotor symptoms and endometrial hyperplasia due to unopposed estrogen effects.

### **4. Intrauterine Contraceptive Devices (IUCD)**

**A. Failure Rates**

- _Cu-T 380A:_ Perfect use 0.6 per HWY; typical use 0.8 per HWY. Devices with <300 mm² of copper have a higher failure rate.
- _LNG-IUS (Levonorgestrel Intrauterine System):_ Perfect use 0.1 per HWY; typical use 0.1 per HWY.
- Overall failure rate of modern IUCDs is between 0.5 and 2 per HWY.

**B. Immediate Side Effects**

- **Cramp-like pain:** Transient but can be severe, lasting 30-60 minutes post-insertion.
- **Syncopal attack:** More common in nulliparous women or if the device distends the uterine cavity.
- **Uterine Perforation:** Incidence is 1 in 1,000 insertions; partial or complete perforation usually occurs at the time of insertion, notably higher risk during the lactational period when the uterus is small and soft.

**C. Remote / Late Complications**

- **Abnormal Menstrual Bleeding:** For copper devices, menorrhagia, prolonged periods, and intermenstrual bleeding are common, potentially leading to anemia. Conversely, LNG-IUS users experience a 60% reduction in blood loss, with 50% developing amenorrhea and 25% oligomenorrhea after 24 months.
- **Pelvic Pain:** Proportionate to the degree of myometrial distension.
- **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID):** Risk is highest in the first month following insertion (due to bacteria carried during the procedure) and in women with multiple sexual partners. Actinomycosis is a rare association with copper devices.
- **Spontaneous Expulsion:** Occurs in about 5% of cases, commonly within the first few months or during menstruation. The rate is higher following puerperal or postabortal insertions.
- **Ectopic Pregnancy:** While IUCDs prevent uterine pregnancies highly effectively, if a pregnancy _does_ occur with the device in situ, the relative risk of it being ectopic is significant (0.02%). Other risks of pregnancy with an IUCD include miscarriage, preterm labor, and sepsis.
- **Missing Threads:** Caused by curling of the thread inside the canal, tearing, unnoticed expulsion, uterine perforation into the peritoneal cavity, or the device being pulled up by an enlarging pregnant uterus.
