### **1. CLASSIFICATION OF CLINICAL FEATURES**

The clinical features of pregnancy are traditionally categorized into three groups based on their diagnostic reliability:

- **Presumptive Symptoms and Signs:** Features mainly appreciated by the woman herself (e.g., amenorrhea, morning sickness).
- **Probable Signs:** Objective clinical findings elicited by the physician (e.g., uterine changes, Braxton-Hicks contractions, immunological tests).
- **Positive or Absolute Signs:** Undeniable proof of pregnancy (e.g., auscultation of fetal heart sounds, palpation of fetal parts, ultrasound evidence).

### **2. CLINICAL FEATURES IN THE FIRST TRIMESTER (0–12 WEEKS)**

**Subjective Symptoms**

- **Amenorrhea:** Cessation of menstruation during the reproductive period in a healthy female is due to pregnancy until proved otherwise. Scanty bleeding at the time of the expected period may occur until the decidua vera and capsularis fuse; this is termed the _placental sign_.
- **Morning Sickness (Nausea and Vomiting):** Present in about 70% of cases, commonly appearing soon after the missed period and rarely lasting beyond 16 weeks.
- **Frequency of Micturition:** A troublesome symptom between the 8th and 12th weeks. It occurs due to the bulky, exaggerated anteverted uterus resting on the bladder, pelvic congestion, and altered maternal osmoregulation causing polyuria. It subsides after 12 weeks as the uterus straightens up into the abdominal cavity.
- **Breast Discomfort:** Feeling of fullness and a "pricking sensation" is evident as early as 6–8 weeks, especially in primigravidae.
- **Fatigue:** A frequent early symptom.

**Objective Signs**

- **Breast Changes:** Evident between 6 and 8 weeks in primigravidae. There is enlargement, vascular engorgement (visible delicate veins under the skin), and increased pigmentation of the primary areola and nipple. Hypertrophied sebaceous glands around the nipple become prominent, known as _Montgomery’s tubercles_. Thick yellowish secretion (colostrum) can be expressed by the 12th week.
- **Pelvic and Vaginal Signs:**
    - _Jacquemier’s (or Chadwick’s) Sign:_ Dusky, bluish hue of the vestibule and anterior vaginal wall visible at about 8 weeks due to local vascular congestion.
    - _Vaginal Signs:_ The walls become softened, and a copious non-irritating mucoid discharge appears at 6 weeks.
    - _Osiander’s Sign:_ Increased vaginal pulsation felt through the lateral fornices at 8 weeks.
- **Cervical Signs:**
    - _Goodell’s Sign:_ Softening of the cervix as early as 6 weeks. The pregnant cervix feels like the "lips of the mouth," whereas the non-pregnant cervix feels like the "tip of the nose".
- **Uterine Signs:**
    - _Size and Shape:_ Enlarges to the size of a hen's egg at 6 weeks, a cricket ball at 8 weeks, and a fetal head by 12 weeks. The non-pregnant pyriform shape becomes globular by 12 weeks.
    - _Piskacek’s Sign:_ Asymmetrical enlargement of the uterus due to lateral implantation, causing one half to feel firmer than the other (symmetry is restored later).
    - _Hegar’s Sign:_ Present in two-thirds of cases, demonstrable between 6 and 10 weeks. On bimanual examination, the abdominal and vaginal fingers seem to appose because the upper uterine body is enlarged by the fetus, the lower uterine body is empty and extremely soft, and the cervix is comparatively firm.
    - _Palmer’s Sign:_ Regular and rhythmic uterine contractions elicited during bimanual examination as early as 4–8 weeks.

_(Note: Diagnosis is confirmed via Immunological hCG tests and TVS identifying a gestational sac by 4.5-5 weeks, yolk sac by 5.5 weeks, and fetal cardiac activity by 6 weeks.)_

### **3. CLINICAL FEATURES IN THE SECOND TRIMESTER (13–28 WEEKS)**

**Subjective Symptoms**

- Amenorrhea continues, while nausea, vomiting, and frequency of micturition usually subside.
- **Quickening (Feeling of life):** The perception of active fetal movements by the mother, usually felt around the 18th week (about 2 weeks earlier in multiparae).
- Progressive enlargement of the lower abdomen.

**Objective Signs**

- **General Examination:**
    - _Chloasma (Pregnancy mask):_ Pigmentation over the forehead and cheeks may appear at about 24 weeks.
    - _Breast Changes:_ Secondary areola (an outer zone of less marked, irregular pigmentation) appears at about 20 weeks. Montgomery's tubercles extend to the secondary areola.
- **Abdominal Examination:**
    - _Cutaneous Changes:_ Linea nigra (a linear pigmented zone from symphysis pubis to ensiform cartilage) is visible by 20 weeks. Striae gravidarum (pink and white stretch marks) appear on the lower abdomen and flanks.
    - _Fundal Height:_ The uterus becomes an abdominal organ. Fundal height is midway between the symphysis pubis and umbilicus at 16 weeks, at the level of the umbilicus at 24 weeks, and at the junction of the lower third and upper two-thirds between the umbilicus and ensiform cartilage at 28 weeks.
    - _Uterine Palpation:_ The uterus feels soft, elastic, and ovoid. _Braxton-Hicks contractions_ (painless, irregular contractions) can be felt.
    - _Fetal Parts and Movements:_ Fetal parts are distinctly palpable by the 20th week. Active fetal movements can be felt by the examiner by the 20th week.
    - _External Ballottement:_ Elicited as early as the 20th week when the fetus is relatively smaller than the amniotic fluid volume.
- **Auscultation:** Fetal Heart Sounds (FHS) can be detected with a standard stethoscope between 18 and 20 weeks. (Doppler ultrasound can pick it up by 10 weeks).

### **4. CLINICAL FEATURES IN THE THIRD TRIMESTER (29–40 WEEKS)**

**Subjective Symptoms**

- Amenorrhea persists.
- Progressive abdominal enlargement produces mechanical discomforts such as palpitation or dyspnea following exertion.
- **Lightening:** At about the 38th week, especially in primigravidae, a sense of relief from pressure symptoms occurs due to the engagement of the presenting part.
- Frequency of micturition reappears (due to the descending presenting part pressing on the bladder).
- Fetal movements are more pronounced.

**Objective Signs**

- **Cutaneous Changes:** Pigmentation and striae become increasingly prominent.
- **Uterine Shape:** Changes from cylindrical to spherical beyond the 36th week.
- **Fundal Height:**
    - At 32 weeks: At the junction of the upper and middle third between the umbilicus and ensiform cartilage.
    - At 36 weeks: Rises to the level of the ensiform cartilage.
    - At 40 weeks: Drops down back to the 32-week level due to the engagement of the presenting part. (Engagement distinguishes a 40-week pregnancy from a 32-week pregnancy).
- **Symphysis Fundal Height (SFH):** Measured in centimeters from the upper border of the symphysis pubis to the top of the fundus. After 24 weeks, SFH in cm roughly corresponds to the gestational age in weeks (up to 36 weeks).
- **Palpation:** Braxton-Hicks contractions are highly evident. Fetal parts are easily palpated, allowing the examiner to confidently determine the fetal lie, presentation, and position.
- **Auscultation:** FHS is distinctly heard in areas corresponding to the fetal presentation and position.

### **5. SUMMARY OF DIAGNOSTIC SIGNS**

For quick exam revision, the diagnostic features of pregnancy are summarized into three tiers:

1. **Presumptive Symptoms and Signs:**
    - Amenorrhea, frequency of micturition, morning sickness, fatigue, breast changes, skin changes, and quickening.
2. **Probable Signs:**
    - Abdominal enlargement, Braxton-Hicks contractions, external ballottement, outlining the fetus, changes in uterine size/shape/consistency, Jacquemier's sign, Goodell's sign, Osiander's sign, Hegar's sign, internal ballottement, and positive immunological tests.
3. **Positive or Absolute Signs:**
    - Palpation of fetal parts and perception of active fetal movements by the examiner (at about the 20th week).
    - Auscultation of fetal heart sounds (FHS).
    - Ultrasound evidence of the embryo/fetus.
    - Radiological demonstration of the fetal skeleton (16th week onwards).
