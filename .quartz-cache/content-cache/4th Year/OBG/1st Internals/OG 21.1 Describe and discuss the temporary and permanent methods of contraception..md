### **1. Introduction and Definition**

- **Definition:** Contraception includes all measures, temporary or permanent, designed to prevent pregnancy due to the coital act.
- **Ideal Contraceptive:** An ideal contraceptive method should be highly (100%) effective, acceptable, safe, reversible, cheap, simple to use, requiring minimal motivation, and should have non-contraceptive benefits.
- **Contraceptive Efficacy:** Measured by the Pearl Index, which calculates the pregnancy failure rate per Hundred Women Years (HWY) of use.

### **2. Classification of Contraceptive Methods**

Contraceptive methods are broadly divided into two main categories:

- **Temporary Methods:** Barrier methods, Natural contraception, Intrauterine contraceptive devices (IUCDs), and Steroidal contraception.
- **Permanent Methods:** Tubal occlusion (Female) and Vasectomy (Male).

### **3. Temporary Methods of Contraception**

Temporary methods are used to postpone or space births, though couples who desire no more children also frequently use them.

**A. Barrier Methods** These prevent sperm deposition in the vagina or sperm penetration through the cervical canal via mechanical or chemical means.

- **Male Condom:** Made of polyurethane or latex (e.g., 'Nirodh' in India). It is widely practiced, cheap, disposable, and has no side effects. A major advantage is protection against Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs), HPV, and HIV. The typical failure rate is 15/HWY.
- **Female Condom (Femidom):** A polyurethane pouch lining the vagina and external genitalia with a flexible ring at each end. It offers protection against STIs, HIV, and PID, but is expensive.
- **Diaphragm:** An intravaginal latex or silicone cup with a flexible metal spring ring. It must be fitted by medical personnel (unless using single-size types like Caya) and should be used with chemical spermicidal agents.
- **Vaginal Contraceptives (Chemicals):** Spermicides (e.g., nonoxynol-9) available as foams, gels, creams, tablets, and sponges (Today sponge). They act as surfactants to immobilize or kill sperm but should ideally be used in combination with mechanical barriers, as they have high failure rates in isolation.

**B. Natural / Fertility Awareness Methods**

- **Rhythm Method:** Based on identifying the fertile period and abstaining from intercourse during that time. Estimated by calendar rhythm, basal body temperature (biphasic pattern), or cervical mucus changes.
- **Coitus Interruptus (Withdrawal):** Withdrawal of the penis shortly before ejaculation. Requires self-control and carries a high failure rate (20/HWY) due to sperm in precoital secretions.
- **Lactational Amenorrhea Method (LAM):** Highly effective in the first 6 months postpartum if the woman is _exclusively_ breastfeeding and remains amenorrheic (failure rate <2%).

**C. Intrauterine Contraceptive Devices (IUCDs)** A safe, effective, and Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARC).

- **Types:**
    - _Copper Devices:_ Cu-T 380A (effective for 10 years), Multiload 375 (5 years).
    - _Hormone-releasing (LNG-IUS):_ Mirena (52 mg levonorgestrel, effective for 5-7 years), Skyla (13.5 mg).
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces a nonspecific inflammatory reaction in the endometrium. Macrophages cause phagocytosis of sperm. Copper ions prevent blastocyst implantation and impede sperm transport. LNG-IUS causes strong endometrial suppression and makes cervical mucus thick and scanty.
- **Timing of Insertion:** Interval (2-3 days after menses), Postabortal (immediately after termination), or Immediate postpartum (post-placental, intracesarean, or within 48 hours of delivery).
- **Complications:** Cramp-like pain, abnormal menstrual bleeding, expulsion (5-10%), uterine perforation, and missing threads.

**D. Steroidal / Hormonal Contraceptions**

- **Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs/Pills):** Contains an estrogen (ethinyl estradiol) and a progestin (levonorgestrel, desogestrel, drospirenone).
    - _Mechanism:_ Inhibits ovulation by suppressing FSH and LH release (negative feedback on the Hypothalamopituitary axis). Produces static endometrial hypoplasia preventing implantation, and makes cervical mucus thick and viscid.
    - _Regimen:_ One tablet daily for 21 days starting day 1 of the cycle, followed by a 7-day hormone-free interval.
    - _Benefits & Contraindications:_ Highly effective (0.1 HWY), provides good cycle control, and reduces the risk of ovarian and endometrial cancer. Contraindicated in women >40 who smoke, and those with a history of arterial/venous thrombosis, liver disease, or estrogen-dependent cancer.
- **Progestogen-only Pills (POPs/Minipill):** Contains low-dose progestin. Taken daily without a break. Ideal for lactating women as it does not affect milk composition, and suitable for patients with hypertension or contraindications to estrogen.
- **Injectable Progestins:**
    - _DMPA (Depot Medroxyprogesterone Acetate):_ 150 mg IM every 3 months.
    - _NET-EN:_ 200 mg IM every 2 months.
    - _Pros & Cons:_ Eliminates daily pill intake, safe during lactation, and reduces endometrial cancer risk. Drawbacks include irregular bleeding, amenorrhea, delayed return of fertility (6-12 months), and reversible loss of bone mineral density.
- **Implants:** Nexplanon (single rod, etonogestrel) or Norplant-II (two rods, levonorgestrel). Inserted subdermally in the inner arm. Highly effective LARC lasting for 3 years ("reversible sterilization"). Main drawback is irregular bleeding/amenorrhea.
- **Vaginal Rings & Transdermal Patches:** Deliver hormones steadily. NuvaRing is used for 3 weeks and removed for 1 week.

**E. Non-steroidal Oral Contraceptive**

- **Centchroman (Saheli):** A non-steroidal, potent antiestrogenic compound. Taken twice a week for 3 months, then once a week. Prevents implantation by creating asynchrony between the zygote and endometrium. Highly safe, devoid of metabolic side effects, and safe during lactation.

### **4. Emergency Contraception (Postcoital Contraception)**

Used following unprotected intercourse, condom rupture, or missed pills to prevent accidental conception. It is not effective as a regular method.

- **Copper IUCD:** The gold standard and most effective method. Inserted within 5 days of unprotected intercourse. Failure rate is <0.1%.
- **Ulipristal Acetate (UPA):** A selective progesterone receptor modulator. 30 mg single oral dose taken within 120 hours. Failure rate 1-2%.
- **Levonorgestrel (LNG):** 1.5 mg single dose (or two 0.75 mg doses 12 hours apart) taken within 72-120 hours of coitus.
- **Mifepristone (RU-486):** 100 mg single dose within 5 days. Prevents implantation.
- **Yuzpe Regimen:** Combined oral contraceptive pills (2 tablets stat, 2 tablets after 12 hours).

### **5. Permanent Methods of Contraception (Sterilization)**

Sterilization is a highly effective, safe, and permanent method for couples who have completed their families.

**Pre-sterilization Counseling** Couples must be adequately counseled together regarding the permanency of the procedure, occasional failure rates, surgical risks, and options for alternative Long-Acting Reversible Contraceptives (LARC). Reversal is possible via microsurgery but success (pregnancy) rates vary and carry an ectopic pregnancy risk.

**A. Male Sterilization (Vasectomy)** Resection of a segment of both vas deferens and ligation of the cut ends.

- **Advantages:** Can be done in clinics under local anesthesia, quick (20 mins), highly effective, lower cost and fewer complications compared to female sterilization.
- **No-Scalpel Vasectomy (NSV):** The modern preferred technique. The vasa are fixed under the skin with a ringed clamp. A puncture is made with dissecting forceps to deliver the vasa, which is then ligated and excised. It involves less tissue trauma and no suturing is required.
- **Precaution:** Sterility is _not_ immediate. Semen is stored in the distal channels. An alternative contraceptive must be used for 3 months or about 20 ejaculations, until semen analysis confirms the absence of sperm.

**B. Female Sterilization (Tubectomy)** Occlusion or resection of both fallopian tubes. It is the most popular terminal contraception method globally.

- **Timing:** Puerperal (24-48 hours post-delivery), Interval (beyond 3 months postpartum/postabortal), or concurrent with Cesarean Section or MTP.
- **Approaches:**
    - _Abdominal (Laparotomy/Minilaparotomy):_ The mainstay in developing countries due to technical simplicity. Through a small suprapubic incision, the tubes are identified and ligated.
    - _Laparoscopic Sterilization:_ Done via single or double puncture using local/general anesthesia. Tubes are occluded using silastic rings (Fallope ring), Filshie clips (titanium lined with silicone), or bipolar electrocoagulation. Permits quick turnover and short hospital stay (3-4 hours).
    - _Vaginal:_ Accessed through posterior colpotomy. Best for interval cases with a small uterus, but requires greater surgical skill.
    - _Transcervical:_ Hysteroscopic placement of a microcoil (Essure) into the tubes. Requires 3 months of backup contraception until scar tissue totally occludes the tube.
- **Surgical Techniques (Ligation):**
    - _Pomeroy's Method (Most common):_ A loop of the tube (junction of proximal and middle third) is tied with absorbable catgut and excised. Ends pull apart when the suture absorbs. Lowest failure rate (0.1-0.5%).
    - _Irving Method:_ Medial cut end is buried in the myometrial wall, and distal end in the mesosalpinx.
    - _Madlener Technique:_ Loop is crushed and tied with non-absorbable silk without excision. Abandoned due to high failure rates (up to 7%).
- **Hazards:** Immediate surgical/anesthetic complications. Late complications include 'post-ligation syndrome' (pelvic pain, congestive dysmenorrhea, menorrhagia) and occasionally spontaneous reanastomosis leading to ectopic pregnancies or regular pregnancies.
