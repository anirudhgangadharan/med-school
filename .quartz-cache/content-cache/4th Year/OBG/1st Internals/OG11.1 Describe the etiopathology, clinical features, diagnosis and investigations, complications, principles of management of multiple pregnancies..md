### **1. Introduction and Etiopathology**

**Definition and Varieties** Multiple pregnancy is defined as the simultaneous development of more than one fetus (twins, triplets, quadruplets, etc.) in the uterus. Twins are the most common variety.

- **Dizygotic (DZ) Twins (80%):** Results from the fertilization of two separate ova. They always have a dichorionic diamniotic (D/D) placenta.
- **Monozygotic (MZ) Twins (20%):** Results from the cleavage of a single fertilized ovum. The placenta's membrane status (chorionicity) depends on the timing of cleavage:
    - _<72 hours:_ Diamniotic-dichorionic (D/D) (25-30%).
    - _Days 4-7:_ Diamniotic-monochorionic (D/M) (70-75%).
    - _Days 8-12:_ Monoamniotic-monochorionic (M/M) (1-2%).
    - _>13 days:_ Conjoined (Siamese) twins (<1%).

**Etiology and Risk Factors** The frequency of monozygotic twins remains constant globally. The incidence of dizygotic twins varies and is influenced by:

- **Maternal Age and Parity:** Increased incidence in women over older age groups and those with higher parity (especially from the fifth gravida onward).
- **Heredity/Family History:** More often present on the maternal side.
- **Iatrogenic/Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART):** Drugs used for ovulation induction (gonadotropins 12-15%, clomiphene citrate 7-9%) and IVF/ET procedures significantly increase the incidence of both DZ and MZ twins. Day 5 blastocyst transfers have a higher rate of MZ twinning than Day 3 cleavage stage transfers.

**Maternal Physiological Changes** Multiple pregnancy imposes physical changes in excess of those seen in singleton pregnancies. There is an increased weight gain, tidal volume, and glomerular filtration rate. Plasma volume increases by an additional 500 mL without a corresponding increase in red cell volume, leading to exaggerated hemodilution and anemia.

### **2. Clinical Features**

**Symptoms**

- History of ovulation-inducing drugs or ART.
- Family history of twinning.
- Nausea and vomiting occur with increased frequency and severity.
- Unusual weight gain and mechanical distress such as palpitations, dyspnea, varicosities, and hemorrhoids.

**Signs**

- _General:_ Increased prevalence of anemia and pre-eclampsia (25%).
- _Abdominal Inspection:_ The abdomen is unduly enlarged and takes on a "barrel shape" rather than a normal pyriform shape.
- _Abdominal Palpation:_
    - Fundal height is greater than the period of amenorrhea (evident from midpregnancy).
    - Girth of the abdomen at the umbilicus is more than the normal average at term (100 cm).
    - Fetal bulk seems disproportionately larger in relation to the fetal head size.
    - Palpation of too many fetal parts, or finding two fetal heads or three fetal poles.
- _Auscultation:_ Simultaneous auscultation of two distinct Fetal Heart Sounds (FHS) located at separate spots with a silent area in between, provided there is a heart rate difference of at least 10 beats per minute.
- _Internal Examination:_ One head may be felt deep in the pelvis, while another is located abdominally.

### **3. Diagnosis and Investigations**

**Differential Diagnosis** Includes polyhydramnios, big baby, fibroid or ovarian tumor coexisting with pregnancy, and maternal ascites with pregnancy.

**Imaging and Biochemical Tests** Clinical diagnosis is confirmed by ultrasonography (USG).

- **Ultrasonography (TVS/TAS):** The diagnostic modality of choice. It confirms the diagnosis as early as the 10th week, determines viability, and detects vanishing twins. **Determination of chorionicity** is crucial and best done at 11-14 weeks:
    - _Dichorionic:_ Thick intertwin septum (>2 mm) with a triangular projection of chorionic villi at the base called the **"lambda" or "twin peak" sign**.
    - _Monochorionic:_ Thin dividing membrane (<2 mm) forming a **"T" sign**. USG is also used for pregnancy dating, anomaly screening, growth monitoring (every 3-4 weeks), placental localization, and Doppler studies for Twin-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS).
- **Biochemical Tests:** Maternal serum hCG, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), and unconjugated estriol are approximately double those of a singleton pregnancy. First-trimester combined tests and second-trimester quadruple tests are used for aneuploidy screening.

### **4. Complications**

Multiple pregnancies are high-risk and present numerous complications.

**Maternal Complications**

- _During Pregnancy:_ Exaggerated nausea/vomiting, anemia (iron and folate deficiency leading to megaloblastic anemia), pre-eclampsia (increased 3-fold), polyhydramnios (10%, more common in MZ twins), antepartum hemorrhage (placenta previa and placental abruption), malpresentation (first baby is vertex in 70%, both vertex in 50%), and preterm labor (50%).
- _During Labor:_ Early rupture of membranes, cord prolapse (five times more common), prolonged labor, increased operative interference (cesarean delivery, forceps), and **postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)** (due to uterine atony from overdistension, a larger placental site, and longer separation time).
- _During Puerperium:_ Subinvolution, puerperal infection, failing lactation, and thromboembolism.

**Fetal Complications**

- High miscarriage rate, prematurity (80%), low birth weight, discordant growth, intrauterine death of one fetus, and an increased rate of congenital anomalies (e.g., neural tube defects, structural cardiac anomalies). Perinatal mortality is 4-5 times higher than in singletons.

**Specific Complications of Monochorionic Twins** Monochorionicity poses higher risks than monozygosity itself.

- **Twin-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS) (10-15%):** The donor twin bleeds into the recipient via placental vascular anastomoses, leading to oligohydramnios/growth restriction in the donor ("stuck twin") and polyhydramnios/heart failure in the recipient.
- **Twin Anemia-Polycythemia Sequence (TAPS):** Chronic, slow transfusion leading to severe hemoglobin discordance without fluid discordance.
- **Twin Reversed Arterial Perfusion (TRAP) Sequence (1%):** An "acardiac perfused twin" receives blood from a normal "pump" cotwin via arterio-arterial anastomoses, causing high-output cardiac failure in the pump twin.
- **Dead Fetus Syndrome:** Death of one twin causes acute hypotension and thromboplastin release into the surviving twin's circulation, risking cerebral palsy and DIC.
- **Monoamniotic cord entanglement (1-2%):** Extremely high perinatal mortality (50%).
- **Conjoined Twins:** Thoracopagus, craniopagus, etc., carrying high mortality depending on organ sharing.

### **5. Principles of Management**

**Antenatal Management and Fetal Surveillance**

- Early diagnosis and establishment of chorionicity are the essence of successful management.
- _Diet and Supplementation:_ Increase caloric intake by 300 Kcal/day above singleton requirements. Enhance iron (100-200 mg/day), folic acid (5 mg), and calcium supplementation.
- _Visits:_ Dichorionic twins require 8 ANC visits; monochorionic twins require 11 visits. Routine bed rest, prophylactic tocolytics, or cervical cerclage do not prevent preterm delivery and are not recommended.
- _Fetal Surveillance:_ Serial USG every 3-4 weeks for DCDA twins and every 2 weeks for MCDA twins to assess fetal growth profile, BPP, and Doppler velocimetry. Corticosteroids are administered for fetal lung maturity if delivery is expected before 34 weeks.

**Timing of Delivery**

- Uncomplicated Dichorionic Diamniotic (DCDA): 37-38 weeks.
- Uncomplicated Monochorionic Diamniotic (MCDA): 36-37 weeks.
- Monochorionic Monoamniotic (MCMA) and TTTS: 32-34 weeks.
- Triplets: 35-36 weeks.

**Management During Labor**

- Delivery must take place in an equipped hospital with an intensive neonatal care unit.
- Establish an IV line, ensure careful electronic fetal monitoring, limit analgesic drugs (epidural is preferred to facilitate manipulation), and perform an internal examination immediately upon membrane rupture to exclude cord prolapse.

**Delivery of the Babies and Third Stage Management**

- **First Baby:** Delivered as in a singleton pregnancy. The maternal end of the cord must be clamped securely to prevent the second twin from bleeding out via placental anastomoses (in MZ twins). _Do not administer intravenous ergometrine with the delivery of the first baby_. Leave 8-10 cm of the cord.
- **Second Baby:** Assess lie and presentation immediately.
    - _Longitudinal/Cephalic:_ Low rupture of membranes with low-dose IV oxytocin infusion; deliver spontaneously or with forceps.
    - _Breech:_ ARM followed by gentle breech extraction.
    - _Transverse:_ Internal Podalic Version (IPV) followed by gentle breech extraction (the only accepted indication for IPV in modern obstetrics).
    - Urgent delivery of the second baby is indicated for severe vaginal bleeding, cord prolapse, fetal distress, or if the first baby was delivered under general anesthesia.
- **Third Stage:** The risk of PPH is high. Routine administration of 0.2 mg methergine IV or 10 IU oxytocin IM is given immediately _after_ the delivery of the second baby. The placenta is delivered by controlled cord traction, and an oxytocin drip is continued for at least 1 hour postpartum.

**Indications for Cesarean Section**

- Both fetuses (or the first fetus) presenting as noncephalic (breech or transverse).
- Associated obstetric indications (e.g., severe pre-eclampsia, placenta previa, cord prolapse of the first baby).
- Monoamniotic twins, conjoined twins, or monochorionic twins with TTTS.
- Fetal complications like severe IUGR or a second baby weighing <1500 g.
- Collision of heads at the brim preventing engagement.
