### **1. Introduction to Isoimmunization**

Red cell alloimmunization (isoimmunization) occurs when an Rh-negative mother is sensitized to Rh-positive fetal red blood cells, leading to the production of maternal IgG antibodies. These antibodies cross the placenta and cause hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN). The management aims to accurately diagnose fetal affection, monitor for fetal anemia, and intervene timely to prevent fetal hydrops and intrauterine death.

### **2. Antenatal Diagnosis and Investigation Protocol**

The objective is to identify at-risk pregnancies, detect sensitization early, and forecast fetal affection.

- **Initial Blood Grouping:** Blood for ABO and Rh grouping must be routinely tested at the first antenatal visit (first trimester) and repeated at 28 weeks.
- **Husband's Blood Grouping:** If the mother is Rh-negative, the husband's blood group must be tested.
    - If the husband is Rh-negative: Compatible mating; no risk of Rh isoimmunization.
    - If the husband is Rh-positive: His genotype (homozygous vs. heterozygous) should be determined. A homozygous father (DD) will always have Rh-positive children, whereas a heterozygous father (Dd) has a 50% chance of having an Rh-negative child.
- **Maternal Antibody Detection:** Done via Indirect Coombs' Test (ICT).
    - **Primigravida:** Tested at 12th, 20th, 28th, and 36th weeks.
    - **Multigravida:** Tested at monthly intervals up to 24 weeks, and every 2 weeks thereafter.
- **Determination of Fetal Rh Status:** Cell-free fetal DNA (cffDNA) analysis from maternal plasma can accurately genotype the fetal RhD status as early as 10 weeks. This noninvasive test has largely replaced amniocentesis or chorionic villus sampling for this purpose. If the fetus is Rh-negative, no further monitoring for isoimmunization is required.

### **3. Fetal Surveillance in Immunized Mothers**

Once the mother is immunized (ICT positive) and carrying an Rh-positive fetus, she must be managed in a specialized fetal medicine unit.

- **Antibody Titration:** Quantitative estimation of IgG antibodies is done periodically.
    - A safe level is <4 IU/mL. Levels >10 IU/mL or a sudden marked rise in titer (e.g., 1:16 or 1:32) is considered critical and predictive of moderate to severe HDFN, requiring intensive fetal monitoring.
- **Doppler Ultrasonography (MCA PSV):** This is the mainstay of noninvasive fetal surveillance.
    - Peak Systolic Velocity (PSV) of the fetal Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) is measured.
    - An MCA PSV > 1.5 Multiples of the Median (MoMs) for the corresponding gestational age strongly predicts moderate to severe fetal anemia.
    - Serial USG is also done every 2-3 weeks from 20 weeks to detect signs of hydrops (ascites, scalp edema, pleural effusion).
- **Cordocentesis (Fetal Blood Sampling):** Indicated when MCA PSV is >1.5 MoMs. It confirms the diagnosis by directly measuring fetal hematocrit, hemoglobin, reticulocyte count, and direct Coombs' test.
- **Amniocentesis:** Historically used for spectrophotometric analysis of bilirubin ($\Delta$OD450) on Liley's chart, but has now been largely replaced by MCA PSV Doppler studies.

### **4. Management of the Immunized Pregnancy**

- **Expectant Management:** For mild affection where antibody titers remain low and MCA PSV is normal, the pregnancy is carefully monitored.
- **Intrauterine Fetal Transfusion (IUFT):**
    - **Indication:** Fetal hematocrit <30% or a hemoglobin deficit of $\geq$ 2 g/dL from the mean for gestational age.
    - **Procedure:** Intravascular Transfusion (IVT) into the umbilical vein is the preferred route. It is usually performed between 18 and 35 weeks under USG guidance.
    - **Result:** Corrects fetal anemia and improves survival to >95%.
- **Other Medical Therapies:** High-dose Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG) (1,000 mg/kg weekly) or plasmapheresis may be used in severe early-onset cases to reduce maternal antibody levels.
- **Timing and Mode of Delivery:**
    - _Mild Affection:_ Pregnancy can be continued up to 38 weeks, followed by induction of labor.
    - _Severe Affection:_ Deliver around 34-36 weeks. Antenatal corticosteroids (Betamethasone) are administered prior to delivery if <34 weeks to accelerate fetal lung maturity.
    - _Mode:_ Amniotomy (low rupture of membranes) and oxytocin induction are effective near term. Cesarean section is reserved for unfavorable cervix or obstetric indications.

### **5. Prophylaxis (Prevention of Isoimmunization)**

For Rh-negative, unimmunized mothers carrying an Rh-positive fetus.

- **Routine Antenatal Anti-D Prophylaxis (RAADP):** Administration of 300 µg (1500 IU) Anti-D immunoglobulin at 28-30 weeks reduces third-trimester sensitization to 0.1%.
- **Postpartum Prophylaxis:** 300 µg of Anti-D immunoglobulin IM must be given within 72 hours of delivering an Rh-positive, Direct Coombs' Test (DCT) negative baby. This neutralizes up to 30 mL of fetal whole blood.
- **Other Indications:** 50 µg IM (first trimester) or 300 µg IM (after 12 weeks) is given within 72 hours following miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, MTP, amniocentesis, CVS, placental abruption, or external cephalic version.
- **Kleihauer-Betke Test:** An acid elution test to quantify fetomaternal hemorrhage. Adult RBCs appear as "ghosts," while fetal RBCs appear dark red. If the bleed exceeds 30 mL, an additional 10 µg of Anti-D is given for every 1 mL of fetal whole blood.

### **6. Management of the Neonate**

A neonatologist and NICU setup are essential at the time of delivery.

- **Cord Blood Examination:** At delivery, cord blood must be collected for:
    - _Clotted blood (3 mL):_ ABO and Rh grouping, Direct Coombs' Test (DCT), and serum bilirubin.
    - _EDTA blood (2 mL):_ Hemoglobin, hematocrit, reticulocyte count, and peripheral smear.
- **Adjuvant Therapy (Phototherapy):** Started immediately to degrade bilirubin by photo-oxidation into water-soluble isomers (lumirubin) that are excreted in urine and bile.
- **Exchange Transfusion:** A life-saving procedure to remove sensitized RBCs, circulatory bilirubin, and maternal antibodies, while correcting anemia.
    - **Indications:**
        - Cord blood bilirubin >4 mg/dL and cord Hb <11 g/dL.
        - Rapid rise of bilirubin (>1 mg/dL/hour) despite phototherapy.
        - Total bilirubin reaching 20 mg/dL or more.
    - **Blood used:** Rh-negative, ABO compatible with the baby (or group O), fresh (<7 days old), cross-matched with maternal serum.
    - **Volume:** Approximately 160 mL/kg body weight.
