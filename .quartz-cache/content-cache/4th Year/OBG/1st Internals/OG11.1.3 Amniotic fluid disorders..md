### **1. Introduction to Amniotic Fluid Disorders**

Amniotic fluid is dynamic and is completely replaced every 3 hours. The normal volume of amniotic fluid at term is about 600-800 mL. Abnormalities in the volume of amniotic fluid, either excessive (Polyhydramnios) or deficient (Oligohydramnios), are associated with significant perinatal morbidity and mortality.

### **2. Polyhydramnios (Syn: Hydramnios)**

**Definition and Incidence**

- **Anatomical Definition:** A state where liquor amnii exceeds 2,000 mL.
- **Clinical Definition:** Excessive accumulation of liquor amnii causing discomfort to the patient and/or requiring imaging to substantiate the clinical diagnosis.
- **Sonographic Definition:** Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI) > 25 cm (more than 95th centile) and/or a Maximum Vertical Pocket (MVP) > 8 cm.
- **Incidence:** Varies from 1 to 2% of cases, more common in multiparae.

**Etiology** The exact cause is often an imbalance between absorption and excessive production of liquor amnii.

- **Fetal Anomalies (20%):**
    - _Anencephaly (50% of anomalous cases):_ Due to transudation from exposed meninges, absent swallowing reflex, and suppression of fetal antidiuretic hormone causing excessive urination.
    - _Open spina bifida:_ Increased transudation from meninges.
    - _Gastrointestinal:_ Esophageal or duodenal atresia (prevents swallowing).
    - _Others:_ Facial clefts, neck masses, hydrops fetalis (immune or nonimmune), and fetal infections (TORCH, parvovirus B19).
    - _Aneuploidy and genetic syndromes (e.g., Muscular dystrophy)_.
- **Placenta:** Chorioangioma (tumor causing increased transudation).
- **Multiple Pregnancy:** Ten times more common than singleton overall; often affects the second sac in monozygotic twins. Seen in the recipient twin in Twin-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS).
- **Maternal Causes:** Diabetes mellitus (30% cases, leads to maternal/fetal hyperglycemia $\rightarrow$ fetal diuresis), cardiac or renal disease (placental edema $\rightarrow$ transudation).
- **Idiopathic:** 50-60% of cases have no identifiable cause.

**Clinical Types and Grading**

- **Chronic (Most common):** Insidious onset over a few weeks.
- **Acute (Extremely rare):** Sudden onset within a few days.
- **Grading by MVP:** Mild (8-11 cm), Moderate (12-15 cm), Severe (>16 cm).

**Clinical Features (Symptoms and Signs)**

- **Symptoms (Mechanical):** Dyspnea (patient may remain sitting), palpitation, edema of legs, varicosities in legs/vulva, and hemorrhoids.
- **Signs:**
    - _General:_ Dyspnea in supine position, evidences of pre-eclampsia (edema, hypertension, proteinuria).
    - _Inspection:_ Markedly enlarged, globular, barrel-shaped abdomen with tense, shiny skin and large striae.
    - _Palpation:_ Fundal height greater than the period of amenorrhea, abdominal girth >100 cm at term, elicitable fluid thrill, unidentifiable fetal parts, and easily elicited external ballottement.
    - _Auscultation:_ Fetal heart sounds (FHS) are muffled/indistinct.
    - _Internal Examination:_ Cervix is pulled up and may be partially taken up; tense bulging membranes felt.

**Diagnosis and Investigations**

- **Differential Diagnosis:** Multiple pregnancy (twins), huge ovarian cyst, and maternal ascites.
- **Ultrasonography (USG):** Confirms diagnosis (MVP > 8 cm, AFI > 25 cm). Excludes multiple fetuses, identifies lie/presentation, and screens for major congenital malformations.
- **Blood Tests:** ABO and Rh grouping, postprandial blood sugar (or GTT for diabetes).
- **Amniotic Fluid:** Estimation of alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) to rule out open neural tube defects.

**Complications**

- **Maternal:**
    - _Pregnancy:_ Pre-eclampsia (25%), malpresentation, premature rupture of membranes (PROM), preterm labor, and accidental hemorrhage (placental abruption).
    - _Labor:_ Early rupture of membranes, cord prolapse, uterine inertia, increased operative delivery, retained placenta, and severe postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) due to uterine atony.
    - _Puerperium:_ Subinvolution and puerperal infection.
- **Fetal:** Perinatal mortality is ~50%, primarily due to prematurity, congenital abnormalities (40%), cord prolapse, and placental abruption.

**Management**

- **Mild Polyhydramnios:** Usually transient in mid-trimester; requires no active treatment except extra bed rest.
- **Severe Polyhydramnios:** Requires admission to a high-risk hospital unit.
    - _Supportive Therapy:_ Bed rest with backrest, treatment of pre-eclampsia/diabetes. Indomethacin (COX-2 inhibitor) may be used short-term to reduce fetal urine production, but prolonged use risks premature ductus arteriosus closure.
    - _Uncomplicated Cases (No fetal anomalies):_ Wait for spontaneous term delivery if responsive. If unresponsive with severe distress <37 weeks, perform **amnioreduction** (amniocentesis). Slow decompression (500 mL/hr) relieves distress and prevents sudden abruption. If >37 weeks, **induction of labor** via controlled amniotomy (with oxytocin stabilization) is done.
    - _Complicated by Fetal Anomaly:_ Termination of pregnancy irrespective of gestational age via PGE2 gel followed by controlled amniotomy.
    - _During Labor:_ Check for cord prolapse upon rupture of membranes. Oxytocin infusion if contractions are sluggish. Give prophylactic IV methergine 0.2 mg at delivery of the anterior shoulder to prevent PPH.

### **3. Acute Polyhydramnios**

- Extremely rare, occurs before 20 weeks of gestation.
- Usually associated with monozygotic twins (TTTS) or placental chorioangioma.
- **Features:** Acute abdomen (abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting).
- **Treatment:** Often results in spontaneous abortion. Repetitive amnioreduction or laser ablation (for TTTS) can improve outcomes.

### **4. Oligohydramnios (Syn: Oligoamnios)**

**Definition** An extremely rare condition where the liquor amnii is deficient (<200 mL at term). Sonographically, it is defined as a Maximum Vertical Pocket (MVP) < 2 cm or an AFI < 5 cm (below the 5th percentile). AFI between 5 and 8 cm is termed borderline oligohydramnios.

**Etiology**

- **Fetal Conditions:** Chromosomal/structural anomalies, renal agenesis, obstructed uropathy, spontaneous rupture of membranes (most common cause), intrauterine infection, IUGR, postmaturity, and amnion nodosum (failure of secretion by amnion cells).
- **Maternal Conditions:** Hypertensive disorders, uteroplacental insufficiency, dehydration, and Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APLAS).
- **Drugs:** Prostaglandin inhibitors (NSAIDs/Indomethacin) and ACE inhibitors.

**Clinical Features and Diagnosis**

- **Signs:** Uterine size is much smaller than the period of amenorrhea, fetal movements are decreased, uterus feels "full of fetus" due to scanty liquor, and malpresentation (breech) is common.
- **USG Diagnosis:** Largest liquor pool < 2 cm or AFI < 5 cm. Visualization of normal fetal bladder filling/emptying rules out urinary tract abnormalities. Oligohydramnios with symmetric FGR strongly suggests chromosomal anomalies.

**Complications**

- **Fetal:** Abortion, physical deformities due to compression/adhesions (altered skull shape, wry neck, club foot, limb amputation), fetal pulmonary hypoplasia (cause or effect), umbilical cord compression, and high fetal mortality.
- **Maternal:** Prolonged labor (due to inertia) and increased operative interference due to fetal distress/malpresentation.

**Management**

- Evaluate rigorously for fetal congenital anomalies. If major structural anomalies are present, delivery is planned.
- Isolated oligohydramnios in the third trimester with a normal fetus may be managed conservatively.
- **Maternal Hydration:** Oral water intake (fetal hydration) and bed rest in the left lateral position can increase amniotic fluid volume.
- **Amnioinfusion:** Infusion of warm saline into the uterine cavity may be done during labor to decrease variable decelerations and prevent cord compression, though routine prophylactic amnioinfusion for meconium is not recommended.
