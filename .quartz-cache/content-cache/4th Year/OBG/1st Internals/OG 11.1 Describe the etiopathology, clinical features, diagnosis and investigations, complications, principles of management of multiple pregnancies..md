### **1. Introduction and Definition**

Multiple pregnancy is defined as the simultaneous development of more than one fetus in the uterus. While twins (two fetuses) are the most common, triplets, quadruplets, quintuplets, or sextuplets may also occur.

### **2. Etiopathology of Multiple Pregnancy**

**Types and Genesis of Twins**

- **Dizygotic (DZ) Twins (80%):** Also known as fraternal or binovular twins. They result from the fertilization of two separate ova by two separate sperms during a single ovarian cycle. They have two placentae, two chorions, and two amnions (Diamniotic-dichorionic).
- **Monozygotic (MZ) Twins (20%):** Also known as identical or uniovular twins. They result from the fertilization of a single ovum. The chorionicity depends on the timing of cleavage of the fertilized ovum:
    - **<72 hours (before inner cell mass differentiation):** Diamniotic-dichorionic (D/D) (25-30%).
    - **Days 4–7 (at differentiation of inner cell mass):** Diamniotic-monochorionic (D/M) (70-75%).
    - **Days 8–12 (when amnion is established):** Monoamniotic-monochorionic (M/M) (1-2%).
    - **>13 Days:** Conjoined (Siamese) twins (<1%).

**Etiological Factors** The frequency of monozygotic twins remains fairly constant globally (1 in 250) and is thought to be related to environmental factors. Dizygotic twinning is influenced by several factors:

- **Race:** Highest in Nigeria (1 in 20) and lowest in Japan (1 in 250).
- **Heredity:** More common if there is a family history on the maternal side.
- **Maternal Age:** Peaks at 30-35 years, after which the incidence sharply declines.
- **Parity:** Incidence increases with parity, especially from the 5th gravida onwards.
- **Iatrogenic (ART Procedures):** Drugs used for ovulation induction cause multiple fetuses. Incidence is 12-15% following gonadotropin therapy, 7-9% with clomiphene citrate, and 5% with letrozole. Day 5 blastocyst transfer has a significantly higher rate of MZ twinning compared to cleavage-stage day 3 transfers.

### **3. Clinical Features**

**Symptoms**

- Exaggerated minor ailments of normal pregnancy, including increased nausea and vomiting in the early months.
- Cardiorespiratory embarrassment such as palpitations or shortness of breath in later months.
- Increased swelling of the legs, varicose veins, and hemorrhoids.
- Unusual rate of abdominal enlargement.
- Perception of excessive fetal movements.

**Signs**

- **General Examination:** Higher prevalence of anemia, unusual weight gain (not explained by pre-eclampsia or obesity), and evidence of pre-eclampsia (in 25% of cases).
- **Abdominal Examination:**
    - **Inspection:** The abdomen is unduly enlarged and takes on a "barrel shape" rather than the normal cylindrical shape.
    - **Palpation:** Fundal height is greater than the period of amenorrhea. Abdominal girth at the umbilicus is more than the normal average at term (100 cm). The fetal bulk feels disproportionately larger compared to the size of the fetal head. Palpation of too many fetal parts, two fetal heads, or three fetal poles makes the diagnosis almost certain.
    - **Auscultation:** Simultaneous auscultation of two distinct Fetal Heart Sounds (FHS) by two observers at separate spots, with a silent area in between and a rate difference of at least 10 beats per minute, is a certain clue.
- **Internal Examination:** One fetal head may be felt deep in the pelvis, while the other is located via abdominal examination.

### **4. Diagnosis and Investigations**

- **History:** Eliciting a history of ovulation-inducing drugs, ART, or a maternal family history of twinning.
- **Ultrasonography (USG):** This is the diagnostic technique of choice. It can confirm the diagnosis as early as the 10th week of pregnancy.
    - Determines viability and can identify a "vanishing twin" in the second trimester.
    - Determines chorionicity (best at 11-14 weeks): A thick septum (>2 mm) with a triangular projection at the base indicates a "lambda" or "twin peak sign" (dichorionic). A thin dividing membrane (<2 mm) forming a "T sign" indicates monochorionic diamniotic pregnancy.
    - Aids in pregnancy dating, surveying fetal anomalies, monitoring fetal growth profile (every 3-4 weeks), determining lie/presentation, conducting Doppler studies (for twin transfusion), and assessing amniotic fluid.
- **Biochemical Tests:** Maternal serum hCG, alpha-fetoprotein, and unconjugated estriol are approximately double those in singleton pregnancies, but these are not clearly diagnostic.

### **5. Complications**

**Maternal Complications**

- **During Pregnancy:** Increased frequency and severity of nausea/vomiting, anemia (due to increased iron and folate requirements), pre-eclampsia (3x increased risk), polyhydramnios (10%, more common in MZ twins), antepartum hemorrhage (placenta previa due to larger placenta, and abruption), malpresentation, preterm labor (50%), and mechanical distress (palpitations, dyspnea).
- **During Labor:** Early rupture of membranes, cord prolapse (5x more common, especially in the second twin), increased operative interference (CS, forceps), intrapartum bleeding, and a major risk of postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) due to uterine atony and a large placental site.
- **During Puerperium:** Increased incidence of subinvolution, infection, lactation failure, and thromboembolism.

**Fetal and Neonatal Complications**

- Increased miscarriage rate, prematurity (80%), low birth weight, discordant growth (weight difference of 25% or more), vanishing twin, intrauterine death of one fetus (which may lead to fetus papyraceus or maternal DIC), fetal anomalies (2-4%), asphyxia, and stillbirth.

**Specific Complications of Monochorionic Twins**

- **Twin-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS):** Imbalance via arteriovenous anastomoses where the recipient twin becomes larger, hypervolemic, and polyuric (polyhydramnios), while the donor becomes smaller, anemic, and oliguric (oligohydramnios).
- **Twin Anemia-Polycythemia Sequence (TAPS):** Chronic hemoglobin discordance due to small anastomoses.
- **Dead Fetus Syndrome:** Death of one twin leading to cerebral palsy or DIC in the survivor due to acute hypotension and thromboplastin transfer.
- **Twin Reversed Arterial Perfusion (TRAP):** An acardiac perfused twin receives blood from a normal co-twin.
- **Cord Entanglement:** High risk in monoamniotic twins, leading to high perinatal mortality.
- **Conjoined Twins:** Incomplete division of the embryo (e.g., thoracopagus, craniopagus).

### **6. Principles of Management**

**Antenatal Management**

- **Diet & Supplements:** Increased dietary supplement of 300 Kcal per day above singleton requirements, increased protein, iron therapy (100-200 mg/day), and folic acid (5 mg).
- **Rest and Hospitalization:** Early cessation of work and rest at home. Routine admission is not required for bed rest alone, but admission is mandated for complications. DCDA twins require around 8 ANC visits, whereas MCDA twins require 11 visits.
- **Fetal Surveillance:** Serial USG every 3-4 weeks (or every 2 weeks for monochorionic twins) for growth, AFI, and Doppler velocimetry (after 30-32 weeks).
- **Planned Delivery Timing:** Uncomplicated DCDA twins at 37-38 weeks, MCDA twins at 36 weeks (or 34-37 weeks), and MCMA/TTTS twins at 32-34 weeks following prophylactic corticosteroids.

**Management During Labor**

- Deliver in a well-equipped hospital with intensive neonatal care, cross-matched blood ready, and an IV line established.
- Careful electronic fetal monitoring is essential. Use epidural analgesia preferentially.
- **Delivery of the First Baby:** Conducted according to normal labor guidelines. Clamp the cord at two places and cut in between to prevent exsanguination of the second twin through placental anastomoses. Do NOT administer IV ergometrine at this stage.
- **Delivery of the Second Baby:**
    - Assess lie and presentation clinically and by USG.
    - If longitudinal and cephalic: perform low rupture of membranes, start oxytocin if necessary, and allow spontaneous or assisted vaginal delivery.
    - If breech: perform assisted breech delivery or breech extraction.
    - If transverse lie: perform internal podalic version (IPV) and breech extraction under anesthesia.
    - Urgent delivery of the second baby is indicated for severe bleeding, cord prolapse, fetal distress, or if the first baby was delivered under general anesthesia.

**Management of the Third Stage**

- Administer 0.2 mg methergine IV or 10 IU oxytocin IM following the delivery of the second baby to minimize the high risk of PPH.
- Deliver the placenta by controlled cord traction.
- Continue oxytocin infusion for at least 1 hour post-delivery and monitor the mother closely for 2 hours.

**Indications for Cesarean Section**

- **Obstetric Indications:** Placenta previa, severe pre-eclampsia, previous cesarean section, cord prolapse of the first baby, abnormal uterine action.
- **Twin-Specific Indications:** First fetus is non-cephalic (breech or transverse), IUGR, conjoined twins, monoamniotic twins, monochorionic twins with TTTS, and collision of heads at the brim.
- **For the Second Twin:** If Twin B is >20% larger than Twin A and non-cephalic, prompt cervical closure after the first birth, fetal distress, fetal weight <1500g, or severe vaginal bleeding.
