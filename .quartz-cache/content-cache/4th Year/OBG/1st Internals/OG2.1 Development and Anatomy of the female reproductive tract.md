### **1. DEVELOPMENT OF THE FEMALE REPRODUCTIVE TRACT**

**Genetic Differentiation**

- The bipotential gonad develops into an ovary about two weeks later than testicular development, representing a default pathway due to the absence of the SRY gene (sex-determining region of the Y chromosome),.
- In the absence of Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH) and testosterone, the Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts develop, and the Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts regress,.

**External Genitalia**

- Developed simultaneously with internal organs from the urogenital sinus and cloacal structures.
- **Clitoris:** Develops from the genital tubercle,.
- **Labia Minora:** Develops from the genital folds (urogenital membrane),.
- **Labia Majora:** Develops from the labioscrotal swellings,.
- **Vestibule & Bartholin’s Glands:** Derived from the urogenital sinus,,.

**Internal Genitalia**

- **Fallopian Tubes:** Developed from the cephalic vertical and most of the intermediate horizontal parts of the respective paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct,.
- **Uterus and Cervix:** Developed from the fusion of the intermediate horizontal and adjoining vertical parts of the Müllerian ducts, forming the uterovaginal canal,.
    - Fusion begins at 7–8 weeks and completes by the 12th week.
    - The myometrium and endometrial stroma are derived entirely from the mesoderm of the paramesonephric ducts.
- **Vagina:** Has a composite origin:
    - _Upper four-fifths:_ Mucosa develops from the endoderm of the canalized sinovaginal bulb, while the musculature develops from the mesoderm of the fused paramesonephric ducts,.
    - _Lower one-fifth (below hymen):_ Developed from the endoderm of the urogenital sinus,.
    - _Vaginal Introitus (external orifice):_ Formed from the ectoderm of the genital folds after rupture of the urogenital membrane,.

**Ovary**

- Develops from the gonadal ridge; the cortex and covering epithelium arise from the coelomic epithelium, and the medulla arises from the mesenchyme,.
- Germ cells are endodermal in origin and migrate from the yolk sac to the genital ridge between 20 and 30 days,.

### **2. ANATOMY OF THE EXTERNAL GENITALIA (VULVA/PUDENDUM)**

The vulva includes all visible external genital organs in the perineum.

- **Mons Veneris (Mons Pubis):** A pad of subcutaneous adipose connective tissue lying in front of the pubis, covered by hair in adult females,.
- **Labia Majora:** Two elevations of skin and subcutaneous tissue bounding the vulva laterally, homologous to the male scrotum,,. Contains sebaceous glands, sweat glands, and hair follicles.
- **Labia Minora:** Two thin, fat-free folds of skin lying just within the labia majora,. They divide anteriorly to enclose the clitoris (forming prepuce and frenulum) and fuse posteriorly to form the fourchette,. They are devoid of hair follicles and are homologous to the ventral aspect of the penis.
- **Clitoris:** A small cylindrical erectile body (1.5–2.5 cm) consisting of a glans, a body, and two crura. It is homologous to the male penis,.
- **Vestibule:** A triangular space bounded anteriorly by the clitoris, posteriorly by the fourchette, and laterally by the labia minora,. Contains four openings:
    1. _Urethral opening:_ 1-1.5 cm below the pubic arch,.
    2. _Vaginal orifice:_ Incompletely closed by the hymen (a mucous membrane septum),.
    3. & 4. _Bartholin’s ducts (paired):_ Open outside the hymen at the junction of the anterior 2/3 and posterior 1/3 of the vestibule,.
- **Bartholin’s Glands (Greater Vestibular Glands):** Pea-sized (0.5 cm) compound racemose glands situated in the superficial perineal pouch. They secrete alkaline mucus during sexual excitement and correspond to the bulbourethral gland in males,,,.

### **3. ANATOMY OF THE INTERNAL GENITALIA**

**Vagina**

- **Structure & Dimensions:** A fibromusculomembranous sheath directed upwards and backwards at a 45° angle to the horizontal,. The anterior wall is 7 cm long, and the posterior wall is 9 cm long,.
- **Fornices:** Clefts formed at the vaginal vault due to the projection of the uterine cervix. There are 4 fornices: one anterior, one posterior (the deepest), and two lateral,.
- **Histology:** Lined by stratified squamous epithelium devoid of any secreting glands,.
- **Vaginal Secretion & Flora:** The pH is acidic (4–5.5) during the reproductive period,. The acidic environment is maintained by Doderlein’s bacilli, which convert the glycogen present in exfoliated squamous cells into lactic acid,.

**Uterus**

- **Structure & Position:** A hollow, pyriform muscular organ situated between the bladder and rectum,. Its normal position is anteverted (90° to the vagina) and anteflexed (120° to the cervix),.
- **Dimensions:** Measures about 8 cm long, 5 cm wide at the fundus, and 1.25 cm thick. Weight is 50–80 g,. Total cavity length is 6–7 cm,.
- **Parts:**
    - _Body (Corpus):_ Includes the fundus and the body proper. The superolateral angles are the cornua, where the fallopian tubes, round ligaments, and ovarian ligaments attach,,.
    - _Isthmus:_ A constricted part measuring 0.5 cm, bounded above by the anatomical internal os and below by the histological internal os,.
    - _Cervix:_ A cylindrical structure measuring 2.5 cm, divided into supravaginal and vaginal (portio vaginalis) portions,,.
- **Layers of the Uterine Body:**
    1. _Perimetrium:_ Serous coat investing the organ,.
    2. _Myometrium:_ Thick bundles of smooth muscle,.
    3. _Endometrium:_ Mucous lining consisting of a lamina propria and a single layer of ciliated columnar epithelium with simple tubular glands. There is no submucous layer,,.

**Fallopian Tubes (Oviducts)**

- **Structure & Dimensions:** Paired tubes measuring about 10 cm in length, situated in the medial three-fourths of the upper free margin of the broad ligament,.
- **Parts (From Medial to Lateral):**
    1. _Intramural/Interstitial:_ Lies in the uterine wall (1.25 cm long, 1 mm diameter),.
    2. _Isthmus:_ Almost straight (2.5-4 cm long, 2.5 mm diameter),.
    3. _Ampulla:_ Tortuous (5 cm long); typical site of fertilization,,.
    4. _Infundibulum:_ Wide end (1.25 cm long, 6 mm diameter) opening into the peritoneal cavity via the abdominal ostium, surrounded by 20-25 radiating fimbriae (including the ovarian fimbria),.
- **Histology:** Three layers: Serous (peritoneum), Muscular (inner circular, outer longitudinal), and Mucous (partly ciliated columnar and partly secretory non-ciliated "peg cells"),.

**Ovaries**

- **Structure & Position:** Paired, intraperitoneal sex glands measuring about 3 x 2 x 1 cm,. Located in the ovarian fossa on the lateral pelvic wall in nulliparae,.
- **Attachments:** Attached to the broad ligament via the mesovarium, to the lateral pelvic wall via the infundibulopelvic ligament, and to the uterus via the ovarian ligament,.
- **Histology:**
    - _Cortex:_ Covered by a single layer of cubical cells (germinal epithelium) underlain by the tunica albuginea. Contains the functional units: primordial, maturing, and Graafian follicles, as well as the corpus luteum and corpus albicans,.
    - _Medulla:_ Contains loose connective tissue, unstriped muscles, blood vessels, nerves, and "hilus cells" (homologous to male interstitial cells),.

### **4. BLOOD AND NERVE SUPPLY OF PELVIC ORGANS**

- **Vulva:** Supplied by the internal pudendal and femoral arteries,. Somatic nerve supply is from the ilioinguinal, genitofemoral, and pudendal nerves,.
- **Vagina:** Supplied by the vaginal, cervicovaginal branch of the uterine, middle rectal, and internal pudendal arteries,. Venous drainage is to the internal iliac and internal pudendal veins,.
- **Uterus:** Primarily supplied by the uterine arteries (branch of the anterior division of the internal iliac artery), which cross anteriorly and above the ureters, and by the ovarian arteries,,. Nerve supply is sympathetic (T10-L1) and parasympathetic (S2-S4 via pelvic nerve ending in Frankenhauser's ganglia),.
- **Ovaries:** Supplied by the ovarian artery (branch of the abdominal aorta),. Venous drainage is via the pampiniform plexus into the ovarian veins, draining to the inferior vena cava on the right and the left renal vein on the left,. Nerve supply is sympathetic from the T10 segment,.
