### **1. Definition and Types of Urinary Incontinence**

Urinary incontinence is defined as the objectively demonstrable involuntary loss of urine so as to cause hygienic and/or social inconvenience for day-to-day activity.

**Types of Urinary Incontinence in Women:**

- **Stress urinary incontinence (SUI):** Involuntary loss of urine with effort, physical exertion, sneezing, or coughing.
- **Urgency urinary incontinence:** Involuntary loss of urine associated with urgency or a sudden, compelling desire to void.
- **Mixed urinary incontinence:** Associated with both urgency and physical exertion.
- **Overactive bladder (OAB):** Urinary urgency, usually accompanied by frequency and nocturia, with or without urge incontinence, in the absence of UTI or obvious pathology.
- **Overflow incontinence:** Associated with chronic urinary retention.
- **Functional incontinence:** Due to cognitive, functional, or mobility impairments with an intact urinary system.
- **Occult stress incontinence:** Observed only after the reduction of coexistent pelvic organ prolapse.

### **2. Genuine Stress Incontinence (GSI) / Stress Urinary Incontinence (SUI)**

**Definition:** GSI is defined by the International Continence Society (ICS) as the involuntary urethral loss of urine when the intravesical pressure exceeds the maximum urethral pressure in the absence of detrusor activity.

**Etiopathogenesis and Risk Factors** GSI is primarily an anatomic problem resulting from a distortion of the normal urethrovesical anatomy.

- **Urethral Hypermobility (80%):** Descent of the bladder neck and proximal urethra (which normally lie intra-abdominally above the pelvic floor) prevents the adequate transmission of intra-abdominal pressure to the urethra during stress.
- **Intrinsic Sphincter Deficiency (20%):** Lowered urethral pressure at rest falls below the intravesical pressure.
- **Risk Factors:** Childbirth trauma causing damage to the levator ani and pubocervical fascia, prolonged second stage of labor, operative vaginal delivery (forceps), increasing age, postmenopausal estrogen deficiency, obesity (BMI $\geq$ 30), prior pelvic surgery, and chronic medical conditions like COPD or diabetes.

**Clinical Features**

- Patients are usually parous or postmenopausal, presenting with brief, involuntary escape of a small amount of urine exactly coinciding with stress (coughing, sneezing, laughing).
- Patients are fully aware of the leakage, and there is no prior urge to void.

**Diagnosis and Investigations**

- **Local Examination:** A stress test with a full bladder reveals a spurt of urine from the external meatus upon coughing. Q-tip test showing marked upward elevation (>30°) indicates urethral hypermobility.
- **Pad Test:** A 1-hour extended pad test with provocative exercises (an increase of >1 g is significant).
- **Midstream Urine Analysis:** Essential to rule out UTI.
- **Uroflowmetry:** Urinary flow rate remains normal with minimal residual urine in GSI.
- **Cystometry (Urethral Pressure Profile):** Normal filling cystometry. The pathognomonic sign is a significant lowering of urethral closure pressure compared to intravesical pressure during strain.

**Principles of Management** **A. Conservative Management**

- **Pelvic Floor Muscle Training (PFMT):** Kegel exercises (squeeze and release 10-15 times, up to 50 contractions daily) strengthen the rhabdosphincter and pelvic floor muscles.
- **Biofeedback and Vaginal Devices:** Use of vaginal cones, pessaries, or bladder neck support prosthesis.
- **Pharmacotherapy:** Estrogen replacement in postmenopausal women and sympathomimetic drugs ($\alpha$-adrenergic agonists like Imipramine) to improve urethral tone.
- **Weight reduction** for obese patients and management of chronic cough/constipation.

**B. Surgical Management** The principle is to restore normal anatomy by elevating the bladder neck into the abdominal pressure zone and strengthening its support.

- **Retropubic Cystourethropexy:** Burch colposuspension (open or laparoscopic) sutures the endopelvic fascia to Cooper's (iliopectineal) ligament. It is the gold standard for open procedures.
- **Midurethral Slings (Procedure of Choice):** Placement of a tension-free synthetic mesh under the midurethra.
    - _Tension-Free Vaginal Tape (TVT):_ Passed blindly through the retropubic space.
    - _Transobturator Tape (TOT):_ Passed through the obturator foramen, minimizing bladder and bowel injury risks.
- **Periurethral Bulking Agents:** For patients with Intrinsic Sphincter Deficiency (ISD), bovine collagen or polyacrylamide hydrogel can be injected to produce an obstructive effect.

### **3. Urge Incontinence and Overactive Bladder (OAB)**

**Definition:** Involuntary leakage of urine accompanied by urgency. OAB is an unstable bladder shown objectively to contract spontaneously or on provocation during the filling phase.

**Etiology & Pathophysiology:** Largely functional and psychosomatic, but may be neurogenic (detrusor hyperreflexia seen in multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease) or due to surgery/UTI. It results from acetylcholine-induced stimulation of detrusor muscarinic receptors.

**Clinical Features & Diagnosis:**

- Large amount of involuntary urine loss with a strong urge, frequency (>8 times/day), and nocturia.
- **Cystometry:** Urge is provoked at a lower bladder volume (100-175 mL) with true detrusor pressure rising >15 cm $H_{2}O$ during filling.

**Management:**

- **Bladder Retraining & Behavioral Therapy:** Fluid restriction, reducing caffeine, and timed voiding.
- **Pharmacotherapy:** Antimuscarinic/anticholinergic drugs (Oxybutynin, Tolterodine, Solifenacin, Darifenacin) are the first line of defense to inhibit bladder contractility. $\beta$-3 adrenergic agonists (Mirabegron) may also be used.
- **Advanced Therapies:** Intravesical Botulinum toxin A injections or sacral neuromodulation for refractory cases.

### **4. Overflow Incontinence and Voiding Disorders (Retention of Urine)**

**Definition:** Difficulty emptying the bladder leading to acute (inability to void >12 hours) or chronic (>50% volume retained) retention. Overflow incontinence occurs when prolonged, neglected retention causes overdistension, pulling open the internal sphincter.

**Causes of Urinary Retention in Gynecology:**

- **Postoperative:** Edema, reflex spasm of levators/sphincters (common after colporrhaphy or perineorrhaphy), denervation during radical hysterectomy (Wertheim's operation).
- **Gynecological/Mechanical:** Retroverted gravid uterus, impacted uterine fibroid or ovarian tumor in the Pouch of Douglas, cervical fibroid, cryptomenorrhea (hematocolpos).
- **Neurological/Drugs:** Multiple sclerosis, epidural anesthesia, antidepressants.

**Management:**

- **Acute Retention:** Intermittent catheterization using a soft rubber catheter with strict asepsis. If unable to void after 8 hours, continuous drainage for 24-48 hours via Foley catheter is indicated.
- **Chronic Retention/Overflow:** Continuous catheter drainage with measurement of post-void residual urine. Address the underlying obstructive pathology.

### **5. Urinary Tract Infections (UTI) in Gynecology**

**Prevalence & Pathogenesis:** Females are highly prone to UTI due to a short urethra (4 cm), close proximity to the vulva and anus, sexual intercourse, and catheterization. _Escherichia coli_ is responsible for 80-90% of cases. Stasis of urine, particularly in pregnancy due to hormonal and anatomical changes, increases the risk of ascending pyelonephritis.

**Clinical Types:**

- **Urethritis:** Dysuria, frequency, urgency, and scalding pain. Commonly caused by _Chlamydia_, _Trichomonas vaginalis_, and _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_.
- **Cystitis:** Painful micturition (especially terminal), suprapubic tenderness, and constitutional symptoms.
- **Urethral Syndrome:** Chronic nonspecific urethritis mimicking UTI but with negative cultures; treated with progressive urethral dilatation or tetracyclines.

**Management:** Midstream clean-catch urine for culture and sensitivity must dictate therapy. Typical oral regimens include Nitrofurantoin, Norfloxacin, or appropriate antibiotics.

### **6. Dysuria and Urethral Caruncle**

**Dysuria:** Difficulty or pain in passing urine.

- **Causes:** Cystitis (terminal pain), specific urethritis (scalding pain throughout), urethral caruncle, prolapsed mucosa, trauma from catheterization, or 'honeymoon cystitis'.

**Urethral Caruncle:**

- A benign, pedunculated, angry red-looking mass protruding from the posterior wall of the external urethral meatus, predominantly seen in postmenopausal women.
- **Symptoms:** Dysuria, irregular vaginal bleeding, dyspareunia.
- **Treatment:** Excision biopsy followed by cauterization or suturing and short-term catheterization.

### **7. Painful Bladder Syndrome (PBS) / Interstitial Cystitis**

- **Definition:** Chronic inflammatory condition causing suprapubic pain related to bladder filling, accompanied by frequency and urgency, in the absence of UTI or other pathology.
- **Diagnosis:** Diagnosis of exclusion. Cystoscopy with distension reveals petechial hemorrhages with oozing.
- **Management:** Behavioral therapy, avoiding trigger diets (spices, caffeine). Drugs like Pentosan Polysulfate repair the glycosaminoglycan layer. Intravesical instillations (DMSO, heparin, botulinum toxin) or tricyclic antidepressants are also used.
