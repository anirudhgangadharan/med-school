### **1. CRITERIA AND DIAGNOSIS OF HEART DISEASE IN PREGNANCY**

**Clinical Features (Symptoms and Signs)** The marked hemodynamic changes in pregnancy (increased cardiac output, hyperdynamic circulation) can mimic or unmask cardiac disease.

- **Symptoms:** Dyspnea, nocturnal cough, decreased exercise tolerance, fatigue, syncope, and chest pain.
- **Signs:** Tachycardia, shift of the ventricular apex, loud first heart sound with splitting, and a third heart sound (S3).
- **Pathological Murmurs:** A systolic ejection murmur at the left sternal border or a continuous murmur (mammary souffle) can be physiological, but pansystolic, late systolic, loud ejection systolic, or diastolic murmurs associated with a thrill indicate structural disease.

**Investigations**

- **Chest Radiography (with lead shield):** Shows cardiomegaly, increased pulmonary vascular markings, enlarged pulmonary veins, or congestive cardiac failure (CCF).
- **Electrocardiography (ECG):** May reveal T wave inversion, biatrial enlargement, or dysrhythmias.
- **Echocardiography (Color flow Doppler):** The gold standard for structural abnormalities, valve anatomy/area, left ventricular ejection fraction, and pulmonary artery systolic pressure.

**Risk Stratification Criteria** Risk assessment is heavily reliant on functional capacity and specific anatomical pathology:

- **NYHA Classification (Functional Capacity):**
    - _Grade I:_ Uncompromised, no limitation of physical activity.
    - _Grade II:_ Slightly compromised; ordinary physical activity causes discomfort.
    - _Grade III:_ Markedly compromised; discomfort with less than ordinary activity.
    - _Grade IV:_ Severely compromised; discomfort even at rest.
- **WHO Classification (Maternal Risk):**
    - _WHO I (Low Risk):_ Uncomplicated, mild lesions (e.g., small PDA, mild pulmonary stenosis, MVP).
    - _WHO II/III (Moderate/High Risk):_ Mild LV impairment, Marfan syndrome (normal aorta), unoperated ASD/VSD.
    - _WHO IV (Pregnancy Contraindicated):_ Pulmonary arterial hypertension, severe LV dysfunction (EF <30%), peripartum cardiomyopathy with residual impairment, severe MS/AS, and Marfan syndrome with aortic dilation >45 mm.

### **2. ADVERSE EFFECTS AND COMPLICATIONS**

**Maternal Complications** Cardiac failure commonly occurs around 30 weeks of gestation, during labor, or immediately following delivery due to the autotransfusion of 400-500 mL of blood from the contracting uterus into the systemic circulation.

- **Major Complications:** Congestive cardiac failure (CCF), pulmonary edema, pulmonary embolism, active rheumatic carditis, subacute bacterial endocarditis, and myocardial infarction.
- **Mortality:** In cyanotic lesions or pulmonary hypertension (Eisenmenger's syndrome), maternal mortality can be up to 50%.

**Risk Factors Precipitating Cardiac Failure**

- Infections (urinary tract, dental, respiratory), anemia, obesity, hypertension, arrhythmias, hyperthyroidism, and the use of beta-mimetic drugs.

**Fetal and Neonatal Complications**

- In acyanotic (rheumatic) lesions, fetal outcome is usually good.
- In cyanotic heart diseases, fetal loss is significantly high (up to 45%) due to abortion, severe intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR), and prematurity.
- The risk of congenital cardiac disease in the fetus is increased by 3–10% if either parent has a congenital lesion.

### **3. MANAGEMENT DURING PREGNANCY (ANTENATAL CARE)**

**General Principles**

- A multidisciplinary team approach involving an obstetrician, cardiologist, and neonatologist is mandatory. Delivery must occur in a tertiary hospital.

**Preconceptional Counseling and Therapeutic Termination**

- Pregnancy is absolutely contraindicated (WHO Category IV) in conditions like primary pulmonary hypertension, Eisenmenger's syndrome, and pulmonary veno-occlusive disease.
- If pregnancy occurs in these conditions, therapeutic termination by suction evacuation (MVA) or D&E is recommended within 12 weeks.

**Antenatal Supervision and Medical Therapy**

- **Rheumatic Heart Disease:** Injection Benzathine Penicillin (Penidure LA-12) is given every 4 weeks throughout pregnancy and puerperium to prevent recurrent rheumatic fever.
- **Prosthetic Valves (Anticoagulation):** Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH) is started in the first trimester. Warfarin carries teratogenic risks (Warfarin embryopathy).
- Avoid excess weight gain, anemia, and infections.

### **4. MANAGEMENT DURING LABOR (INTRAPARTUM CARE)**

Spontaneous onset of labor is awaited; induction is employed only in selected cases for obstetric indications.

**First Stage of Labor**

- **Position:** Left lateral recumbent position to minimize aortocaval compression.
- **Analgesia:** Epidural analgesia is the preferred method for pain relief.
- **Fluid Management:** Strict restriction of IV fluids to less than 75 mL/hour to prevent volume overload and pulmonary edema.
- **Oxygen and Monitoring:** Continuous oxygen administration (5-6 L/min), pulse oximetry, and cardiac monitoring. If maternal pulse exceeds 110 bpm between contractions, rapid digitalization with IV digoxin 0.5 mg is indicated.
- **Prophylaxis for Endocarditis:** Administer IV Amoxicillin (2 g) or Ceftriaxone (1 g). Clindamycin (600 mg IV) is used for penicillin-allergic patients.

**Second Stage of Labor**

- Maternal pushing (Valsalva) must be strictly avoided. The second stage should be cut short by applying prophylactic forceps or ventouse. Ventouse is advantageous as it does not require lithotomy position, which would otherwise increase venous return and cardiac load.

**Third Stage of Labor**

- **Avoid Ergometrine:** Intravenous ergometrine (Methergine) is strictly contraindicated to prevent sudden overloading of the heart from squeezed uterine blood.
- **Uterotonics:** Oxytocin (IM or infusion) is the preferred drug to prevent postpartum hemorrhage (PPH).
- **Diuresis:** Aggressive diuresis with IV Furosemide may be required.

**Indications for Cesarean Delivery** Vaginal delivery is generally preferred. Cesarean section is reserved for standard obstetric indications or specific high-risk cardiac lesions:

- Coarctation of aorta, aortic dissection or aneurysm.
- Dilated aortic root > 4 cm (e.g., Marfan syndrome).
- Severe symptomatic aortic stenosis.
- Patients on Warfarin treatment within two weeks of delivery.

### **5. MANAGEMENT DURING PUERPERIUM AND CARDIAC FAILURE**

**Puerperium**

- The patient must be closely observed in an ICU/HDU setup for the first 24 hours.
- Administer oxygen, record hourly vitals (pulse, BP, respiration), and use diuretics if indicated.

**Management of Cardiac Failure** If cardiac failure develops, manage with:

- Propped up position and oxygen administration.
- **Preload reduction:** IV Furosemide (40-80 mg).
- **Afterload reduction:** Hydralazine.
- **Inotropes/Rate control:** Digoxin 0.5 mg IM/IV.
- Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO) in severe refractory cases (e.g., pulmonary hypertension or peripartum cardiomyopathy).
