1. Congestive Cardiac Failure in children - mention the drugs used
**PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT OF CONGESTIVE CARDIAC FAILURE (CCF) IN CHILDREN**

The pharmacological management of congestive cardiac failure (CCF) in children involves a four-pronged approach: (i) reducing cardiac work, (ii) augmenting myocardial contractility, (iii) improving cardiac performance by reducing preload and afterload, and (iv) correcting the underlying cause.

The drugs used in the management of CCF are systematically categorized into the following high-yield groups:

**1. DIURETICS (Preload Reducers)** Diuretics are the first line of management in congestive failure, acting to reduce blood volume, decrease venous return, and reduce ventricular filling.

- **Furosemide (Loop Diuretic):** This is the most commonly used diuretic. Its action starts within 20 minutes of administration. It effectively relieves edema, pulmonary congestion, and liver enlargement.
    - _Dose:_ 1–3 mg/kg/day orally or 1 mg/kg/dose intravenously (IV). (Parenteral dose can range from 0.5 to 1.5 mg/kg/dose).
- **Spironolactone (Potassium-Sparing Diuretic):** Frequently used in combination with furosemide to prevent potassium and magnesium loss and to reduce the risk of arrhythmias.
    - _Dose:_ 1 mg/kg orally every 12 hours or 1 to 4 mg/kg/day in two divided doses.

**2. INOTROPIC AGENTS (Augment Myocardial Contractility)** These drugs increase the force of myocardial contraction and improve cardiac output.

- **Digoxin:** Digoxin is the cornerstone of management for pediatric CCF. It improves cardiac contractility, reduces cardiac work, and decreases cardiac size.
    - _Digitalizing Dose (Total):_ Varies by age. Premature neonates: 0.04 mg/kg; Term neonates: 0.05 mg/kg; 1 to 12 months: 0.08 mg/kg; 1 to 3 years: 0.06 mg/kg; >3 years: 0.04 mg/kg.
    - _Administration:_ Half the digitalizing dose is given stat, and the remaining half is divided into two doses given at 8-hour intervals.
    - _Maintenance Dose:_ Generally 1/4th to 1/3rd of the total digitalizing dose, given as a single or two divided doses daily.
- **Intravenous Inotropes (Catecholamines):** Used in acute care or refractory failure.
    - _Dopamine:_ At <5 µg/kg/min, it improves renal blood flow and causes natriuresis. Higher doses increase myocardial contractility.
    - _Dobutamine:_ Started at 2.5–15 µg/kg/min; effectively increases contractility and can be used as a 24-hour continuous infusion.
- **Phosphodiesterase Inhibitors:** Amrinone and Milrinone. These combine inotropic effects with peripheral vasodilation (inodilators) and do not increase the risk of rhythm disturbances.
- **Calcium Sensitizers:** Levosimendan acts as a potent inodilator, useful in acute care settings.

**3. VASODILATORS & ACE INHIBITORS (Afterload Reducers)** These drugs counteract compensatory vasoconstriction, decrease systemic vascular resistance (afterload), and increase cardiac output without increasing cardiac work.

- **ACE Inhibitors:** Captopril and Enalapril are highly effective in infants and children. They act synergistically with diuretics (particularly furosemide) by suppressing the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis.
- **Other Vasodilators:**
    - _Sodium Nitroprusside:_ Used in acute care as an intravenous infusion; acts on both venous and arterial systems.
    - _Nitrates:_ Isosorbide dinitrate (oral) or nitroglycerin can be used as preferential venodilators.

**4. BETA-BLOCKERS** Beta-blockers help mitigate the inappropriate sympathetic drive and tachycardia seen in heart failure.

- **Agents:** Carvedilol (preferred due to added peripheral vasodilatory effects) and Metoprolol.
- _Indication:_ Highly useful in children with dilated cardiomyopathy who continue to have tachycardia.
- _Dose:_ Carvedilol is started at a low dose of 0.08 to 0.4 mg/kg/day, increasing gradually to a maximum of 1.0 mg/kg/day.

**5. SUPPORTIVE MEDICATIONS**

- **Sedatives:** Morphine, pethidine, phenobarbital, chloral hydrate, or diazepam may be used to control restlessness and anxiety, thereby decreasing the metabolic demands and cardiac work.
- **Antibiotics:** Indicated to treat any co-existing or precipitating respiratory infections.
- **Iron / Blood Transfusion:** For correction of severe anemia (which precipitates failure). Packed cells (3-5 ml/kg) are given slowly, usually preceded by IV furosemide to prevent circulatory overload.

**STEPWISE ALGORITHM FOR PEDIATRIC CCF MANAGEMENT (High Yield)** According to the standard step-wise approach:

- _Step 1:_ Diuretics (Furosemide) to reduce blood volume and preload.
- _Step 2:_ Digoxin to improve contractility and decrease cardiac size.
- _Step 3:_ ACE Inhibitors (Captopril/Enalapril) while monitoring potassium levels.
- _Step 4:_ Vasodilators (e.g., Nitrates or Sodium nitroprusside).
- _Step 5:_ Intermittent IV Dopamine or Dobutamine (for refractory cases).
- _Step 6:_ Beta-blockers (e.g., Propranolol) or steroids if active myocarditis is present.
- _Step 7:_ Heart transplantation for end-stage failure.
