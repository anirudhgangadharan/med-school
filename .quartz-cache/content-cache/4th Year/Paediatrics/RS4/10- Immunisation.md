1. BCG vaccine — dose, route and schedule of administration, complications.
 **BCG VACCINE**

**Type of Vaccine** It is a live attenuated bacterial vaccine derived from subcultures of _Mycobacterium bovis_ (Calmette-Guérin strain). In India, the Danish 1331 strain is used.

**Dose**

- **Neonates (< 1 month of age):** 0.05 mL.
- **Infants (> 1 month) and Children:** 0.1 mL.

**Route and Site of Administration**

- **Route:** Strictly Intradermal. Inadvertent subcutaneous injection can cause persistent ulceration and lymphadenopathy.
- **Site:** Left upper arm, specifically at the insertion of the deltoid muscle.
- **Technique:** Administered using a special tuberculin syringe (26-gauge needle) to raise a clear, flat 5–7 mm wheal.

**Schedule of Administration**

- **National Immunization Program:** Administered at birth; if missed, catch-up vaccination is recommended till 1 year of age.
- **Indian Academy of Pediatrics (IAP) Schedule:** Administered at birth; catch-up is recommended till 5 years of age.

**Complications / Adverse Reactions** Complications are generally uncommon and often result from faulty technique or administration in an immunocompromised host:

- **Local Reactions:** Deep ulceration of the vaccination site with superadded bacterial infection, local discharging sinus, or keloid formation.
- **Glandular Involvement (BCGosis/BCG adenitis):** Ipsilateral, persistent, and progressive simple or suppurative lymphadenitis (usually axillary or cervical lymph nodes) with abscess formation.
- **Disseminated BCG Disease:** A severe complication manifesting 6–12 months after vaccination in children with severe cellular immunodeficiency (e.g., SCID, symptomatic HIV).
- **Osteomyelitis:** Rare, usually seen in immunocompromised individuals.
- **Scrofuloderma:** Exudative, crusted skin lesions resulting from direct extension or discharge of necrotic material from a caseating lymph gland into the skin.
