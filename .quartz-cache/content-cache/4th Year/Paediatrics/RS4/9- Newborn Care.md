1. Definition of prematurity, LBW and SGA
**DEFINITIONS: PREMATURITY, LBW, AND SGA**

**1. Prematurity (Preterm Neonate)**

- **Definition:** A neonate born before **37 completed weeks** (or < 259 days) of gestation, irrespective of the birth weight.

**2. Low Birth Weight (LBW)**

- **Definition:** A neonate whose birth weight is **less than 2500 grams**, irrespective of the gestational age.
- _Sub-classifications:_
    - **Very Low Birth Weight (VLBW):** Birth weight < 1500 grams, irrespective of gestational age.
    - **Extremely Low Birth Weight (ELBW):** Birth weight < 1000 grams, irrespective of gestational age.

**3. Small for Gestational Age (SGA) / Small for Date (SFD)**

- **Definition:** A neonate whose birth weight is **less than the 10th percentile** for that specific period of gestation.
- It can also be defined as an infant weighing less than two standard deviations below the population norms on an intrauterine growth chart.
- _Note:_ For practical purposes, SGA and Intrauterine Growth Retardation (IUGR) are often considered synonymous.

1. Respiratory Distress in Neonates and their causes
**RESPIRATORY DISTRESS IN NEONATES**

**Definition** Respiratory distress in neonates is defined as the presence of tachypnoea (respiratory rate >60 breaths per minute) accompanied by lower chest retractions, expiratory grunting, and cyanosis. Flaring of the alae nasi is also a prominent clinical sign.

**Assessment of Severity** The severity of respiratory distress can be objectively evaluated using standard clinical scoring systems, such as:

- **Downe's Score:** Evaluates respiratory rate, cyanosis, grunting, retractions, and air entry. A score >6 is suggestive of impending respiratory failure.
- **Silverman-Anderson Score:** Evaluates upper chest retraction, lower chest retraction, xiphoid retraction, nares dilatation, and expiratory grunt.

**ETIOLOGY (CAUSES OF RESPIRATORY DISTRESS)** The causes are broadly classified into Pulmonary (Respiratory) and Non-pulmonary (Extra-pulmonary) conditions.

**1. Pulmonary Causes (Airway & Lung Parenchyma)**

- **Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS) / Hyaline Membrane Disease (HMD):** The most common cause in preterm neonates (especially <34 weeks gestation), caused by a primary deficiency of pulmonary surfactant.
- **Transient Tachypnoea of the Newborn (TTN):** Common in term neonates and those born via elective caesarean section before 39 weeks. It results from delayed clearance of foetal lung fluid.
- **Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS):** Occurs in term or post-term neonates, or those who are small for gestational age (SGA). It is caused by the aspiration of meconium-stained amniotic fluid into the lungs, leading to airway obstruction and chemical pneumonitis.
- **Pneumonia:** Can be congenital or acquired. Common pathogens include _E. coli_, _S. aureus_, _K. pneumoniae_, and Group B Streptococcus.
- **Air Leak Syndromes:** Pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, and interstitial emphysema. Often presents as sudden clinical deterioration, particularly in neonates on positive pressure ventilation or with MAS.
- **Upper Airway Obstruction:** Choanal atresia, Pierre Robin syndrome (micrognathia and glossoptosis), laryngomalacia, laryngeal webs, or vascular rings pressing on the trachea.
- **Surgical/Congenital Malformations:** Diaphragmatic hernia (typically presents with a scaphoid abdomen and bowel loops in the thorax), tracheoesophageal fistula (TEF), pulmonary hypoplasia, and congenital lobar emphysema.

**2. Non-Pulmonary Causes**

- **Cardiac Causes:** Congenital heart diseases (e.g., Transposition of Great Vessels [TGV] presenting on day one, Patent Ductus Arteriosus [PDA]) and Congestive Cardiac Failure (CCF).
- **Neurological Causes:** Perinatal asphyxia (Hypoxic Ischemic Encephalopathy), intracranial haemorrhage, and meningitis. These neonates are usually lethargic and have poor neonatal reflexes.
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Hypothermia, severe hypoglycaemia, and metabolic acidosis.
- **Haematological Causes:** Severe anaemia (e.g., resulting from acute blood loss or haemolysis) or polycythaemia.

**HIGH YIELD EXAM POINTS (Differentiating Major Causes):**

- _Premature neonate + onset <6 hours + ground-glass opacity on X-ray =_ **RDS (HMD)**.
- _Term neonate + Elective LSCS + prominent vascular markings and fluid in fissures on X-ray =_ **TTN**.
- _Post-term/SGA neonate + meconium-stained liquor + patchy infiltrates on X-ray =_ **MAS**.
- _Excessive frothing from mouth + choking on first feed =_ **Tracheoesophageal Fistula (TEF)**.
- _Scaphoid abdomen + respiratory distress + bowel sounds in chest =_ **Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia**.
