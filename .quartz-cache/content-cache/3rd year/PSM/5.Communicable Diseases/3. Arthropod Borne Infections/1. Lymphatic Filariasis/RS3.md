1. Mass drug administration of lymphatic filariasis
	**MASS DRUG ADMINISTRATION (MDA) IN LYMPHATIC FILARIASIS**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION & OBJECTIVE**
	
	- **Definition:** Mass Drug Administration (MDA) is the recommended large-scale preventive chemotherapy strategy to interrupt the transmission of lymphatic filariasis and eliminate it as a public health problem.
	- **Target:** The strategy aims to reduce the prevalence of microfilaraemia in the human population to less than 1%, which is hypothesized to be sufficient to stop transmission.
	
	**2. DRUG REGIMENS** MDA involves the administration of a single dose of antifilarial medicines given together annually. The recommended regimens are:
	
	- **Two-drug regimen:**
	    - Diethylcarbamazine citrate (DEC) at 6 mg/kg body weight + Albendazole 400 mg.
	    - _OR_ Ivermectin 150–200 mcg/kg + Albendazole 400 mg.
	- **Triple-drug regimen (IDA):** Ivermectin + DEC + Albendazole (currently being administered in specific settings in India).
	
	**3. SCHEDULE AND DURATION**
	
	- **Dose:** Administered as a single dose.
	- **Frequency:** Given annually.
	- **Duration:** Must be conducted annually for a period of 4 to 6 years (or 5 years or more) to successfully interrupt the transmission cycle.
	
	**4. TARGET POPULATION & EXCLUSIONS**
	
	- **Target:** The entire at-risk population in endemic areas.
	- **Exclusions (High-Yield):** The drugs are contraindicated and strictly NOT to be given to:
	    1. Pregnant women.
	    2. Children below 2 years of age.
	    3. Seriously ill persons.
	
	**5. MECHANISM OF ACTION**
	
	- These medicines have a limited effect on the adult parasites (macrofilariae).
	- However, they are highly effective in clearing the microfilariae from the bloodstream, thereby preventing the spread of parasites to the vector mosquitoes.
	
	**6. IMPLEMENTATION IN INDIA (National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme)**
	
	- **Launch & Evolution:** India launched the annual MDA campaign in 2004 using a single dose of DEC tablets. In 2007, the strategy was upgraded to the co-administration of DEC plus Albendazole.
	- **Timing:** The mass drug administration typically commences in the month of November.
	- **Coverage Requirement:** To successfully reduce the microfilaria load in the community and interrupt transmission, the population coverage with MDA must be maintained **above 80 per cent persistently for 5 to 6 years**.
	- **Surveillance:** Microfilaria surveys are conducted in all implementation units through night blood surveys (in 4 sentinel and 4 random sites) prior to MDA to assess the reduction in microfilaria load.
