1. Lymphatic filariasis - causes
	**CAUSES AND AETIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS OF LYMPHATIC FILARIASIS**
	
	**1. CAUSATIVE AGENTS (THE PARASITES)**
	
	- Lymphatic filariasis is caused by infection with three closely related nematode worms: _Wuchereria bancrofti_, _Brugia malayi_, and _Brugia timori_.
	- About 90 per cent of all lymphatic filariasis cases globally are caused by infection with _W. bancrofti_.
	- The adult worms live in the lymphatic vessels of the human host, while their offspring, the microfilariae (Mf), circulate in the peripheral blood.
	- The microfilariae of _W. bancrofti_ and _B. malayi_ occurring in India typically display a nocturnal periodicity, meaning they appear in large numbers in the peripheral blood at night (maximum density between 10 pm and 2 am) as a biological adaptation to the nocturnal biting habits of the vector mosquitoes.
	
	**2. THE VECTORS (MOSQUITOES)**
	
	- All three infections are transmitted to man by the bites of infective mosquitoes.
	- No less than 5 genera of mosquitoes are involved in transmission globally.
	- **For _W. bancrofti_:** _Culex_, _Anopheles_, and _Aedes_ mosquitoes serve as vectors. In India, the main vector for Bancroftian filariasis is _Culex quinquefasciatus_ (formerly _C. fatigans_).
	- **For _Brugia_ species:** _Mansonia_, _Anopheles_, and _Coquillettidia_ mosquitoes serve as vectors. In India, the main vectors for Brugian filariasis are _Mansonia_ mosquitoes, specifically _M. annulifera_ and _M. uniformis_.
	
	**3. RESERVOIR OF INFECTION & HOSTS**
	
	- **Hosts:** Man is the definitive host (where the sexual cycle occurs) and the mosquito is the intermediate host (where the asexual developmental cycle occurs).
	- **Human Reservoir:** In humans, the source of infection is an individual with circulating microfilariae in their peripheral blood. A considerable proportion of people are asymptomatic but have blood positive for microfilariae; these carriers are an important source of infection in the community.
	- **Animal Reservoir:** Human filariasis is not usually a zoonosis, and there is no evidence that _W. bancrofti_ has animal reservoirs in India. While animal reservoirs of _Brugia_ are present in monkeys, cats, and dogs, they are believed to acquire their infections from man and are not regarded as important sources of infection to humans.
	
	**4. MODE OF TRANSMISSION**
	
	- **Bite of Infected Vector:** The disease is transmitted by the bite of infected vector mosquitoes.
	- **Biological Transmission (Cyclo-developmental):** The transmission is strictly biological and "cyclo-developmental" in nature. This means the disease agent (microfilaria) undergoes cyclical developmental changes inside the body of the arthropod to become an infective third-stage larva, but it does _not_ multiply in the mosquito.
	- **Portal of Entry:** During a blood meal, the infective mosquito deposits the parasite near the site of the puncture. The parasite then passes through the punctured skin, or penetrates the skin on its own, to finally reach the human lymphatic system where it matures into an adult worm.
