1.  Three communicable/nutritional diseases caused by toxin in the food 
	**THREE DISEASES CAUSED BY TOXINS IN FOOD**
	
	**1. LATHYRISM (NEUROLATHYRISM)**
	
	- **Toxin:** Beta oxalyl amino-alanine (BOAA).
	- **Source:** Seeds of the pulse _Lathyrus sativus_, commonly known as Khesari dhal.
	- **Epidemiological Risk:** Seen mostly in poor agricultural labourers who consume this cheap pulse in large quantities (forming over 30% of the diet) over a period of 2 to 6 months.
	- **Clinical Features:** A crippling disease of the nervous system causing progressively developing spastic paralysis of the lower limbs. It progresses through specific clinical stages: Latent stage $\rightarrow$ No-stick stage $\rightarrow$ One-stick stage $\rightarrow$ Two-stick stage $\rightarrow$ Crawler stage.
	- **Prevention:** Banning the crop, removing the toxin through steeping (soaking in hot water) or parboiling, and educating the public.
	
	**2. EPIDEMIC DROPSY**
	
	- **Toxin:** Sanguinarine (a toxic alkaloid).
	- **Source:** Consumption of mustard oil or other edible oils adulterated (accidentally or deliberately) with the seeds/oil of _Argemone mexicana_ (prickly poppy).
	- **Pathogenesis:** The toxin interferes with the oxidation of pyruvic acid, leading to its accumulation in the blood.
	- **Clinical Features:** Sudden, non-inflammatory, bilateral swelling of the legs, often associated with diarrhoea. Complications include dyspnoea, cardiac failure, and glaucoma. It carries a high mortality rate of 5 to 50 per cent.
	- **Detection:** Nitric acid test or Paper chromatography can detect argemone oil in edible oils.
	
	**3. AFLATOXICOSIS**
	
	- **Toxin:** Aflatoxins (a group of mycotoxins; $B_1$ and $G_1$ are the most potent hepatotoxins).
	- **Source:** Produced by the fungi _Aspergillus flavus_ and _A. parasiticus_ which infest improperly stored foodgrains (groundnut, maize, wheat, rice, tapioca) under high moisture (>16%) and temperature (11-37°C) conditions.
	- **Clinical Features:** Ingestion leads to acute hepatotoxicity. Long-term consumption is associated with human liver cirrhosis, infantile cirrhosis, and possesses significant carcinogenic properties (liver cancer).
	- **Prevention:** Ensuring proper storage of foodgrains after drying to keep moisture content below 10 per cent.
	
1. Mention food sanitation measures against food poisoning  
		**FOOD SANITATION MEASURES AGAINST FOOD POISONING**
		The prevention and control of bacterial food poisoning primarily depend on strict food sanitation, proper temperature control, and surveillance. The following are the high-yield measures:
		
	**1. FOOD SANITATION MEASURES**
	
	- **Meat Inspection:** Food animals must be free from infection, which is ensured by thorough examination by veterinary staff both before and after slaughter.
	- **Personal Hygiene:** A very high standard of personal hygiene must be maintained by all individuals engaged in the handling, preparation, and cooking of food.
	- **Screening of Food Handlers:** Anyone suffering from infected wounds, boils, diarrhoea, dysentery, or throat infections must be strictly excluded from food handling. Medical inspection of food handlers is routinely required.
	- **Safe Food Handling Techniques:**
		- The handling of ready-to-eat foods with bare hands should be reduced to an absolute minimum.
		- The time interval between the preparation and consumption of food should be kept as short as possible.
		- Food must be thoroughly cooked so that heat penetrates the centre, leaving no cool spots; most food poisoning organisms are destroyed at temperatures over 60°C.
		- Milk, milk products, and egg products must be properly pasteurized.
		- Cross-contamination must be avoided by separating raw meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs from ready-to-eat foods, and by using separate cutting boards.
	- **Sanitary Improvements:** Complete sanitization of all work surfaces, utensils, and equipment is essential. Food premises must be kept strictly free from rats, mice, flies, and dust.
	- **Health Education:** Food handlers must be actively educated regarding clean habits and personal hygiene, particularly the importance of frequent and thorough hand washing.
	
	**2. REFRIGERATION AND TEMPERATURE CONTROL**
	
	- Proper temperature control is critical to prevent bacterial multiplication and toxin production.
	- **The Golden Rule:** _"Cook and eat the same day"_.
	- **Danger Zone:** Foods held between 10°C (50°F) and 49°C (120°F) are in the danger zone for rapid bacterial growth and should never be left in warm pantries.
	- **Cold Storage:** Cold is bacteriostatic at temperatures below 4°C (40°F); therefore, refrigeration temperatures must not exceed this level.
	
	**3. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL SURVEILLANCE**
	
	- Food samples must be obtained periodically from food establishments and subjected to laboratory analysis to ensure standards are met.
	- Continuing surveillance is mandatory to detect unsatisfactory conditions early and avoid widespread outbreaks of food-borne diseases.
1. Differential diagnosis of cholera and food poisoning  
**DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS OF CHOLERA AND FOOD POISONING**

Food poisoning can sometimes present with severe gastrointestinal symptoms and may be mistaken for cholera, acute bacillary dysentery, and chemical (arsenic) poisoning. Differentiating between the two is critical for epidemiological investigation and clinical management. 

Below is the high-yield differentiation based on epidemiological and clinical features:

| **Feature**                 | **Cholera**                                                                                            | **Food Poisoning**                                                                  |
| :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Epidemiology**         | Occurs often in epidemic form associated with other cases in the neighbourhood; secondary cases occur. | Often a single group of persons who shared a common meal; no secondary cases occur. |
| **2. Incubation Period**    | From a few hours upto 5 days.                                                                          | 1 to 24 hours.                                                                      |
| **3. Onset**                | Starts with purging.                                                                                   | Starts with vomiting.                                                               |
| **4. Nausea and Retching**  | None.                                                                                                  | Present.                                                                            |
| **5. Vomiting**             | Projectile, effortless, watery and continuous.                                                         | Often a single, severe vomit; may be mucus and blood-streaked.                      |
| **6. Stools**               | Copious, rice-watery, and inoffensive.                                                                 | Frequent; may contain mucus and blood, and are offensive.                           |
| **7. Tenesmus**             | None.                                                                                                  | Yes.                                                                                |
| **8. Abdominal Tenderness** | None.                                                                                                  | Distinct.                                                                           |
| **9. Dehydration**          | Very marked.                                                                                           | Less constant.                                                                      |
| **10. Muscular Cramps**     | Constant and severe.                                                                                   | Often seldom.                                                                       |
| **11. Surface Temperature** | Subnormal.                                                                                             | Often raised, upto 100-102°F.                                                       |
| **12. Urine Output**        | Suppressed.                                                                                            | Often seldom suppressed.                                                            |
| **13. Blood Picture**       | Leucocytosis.                                                                                          | Normal.                                                                             |
