1. Primary prevention of amoebiasis **(RS2)** (D09).
	1. **PRIMARY PREVENTION OF AMOEBIASIS**
	
	The primary prevention of amoebiasis is essentially based on measures aimed at preventing the contamination of water, food, vegetables, and fruits with human faeces.
	
	**1. SANITATION**
	
	- **Excreta Disposal:** The safe disposal of human excreta is a crucial factor in the prevention and control of amoebiasis.
	- **Personal Hygiene:** The elementary sanitary practice of washing hands with soap and water after defecation and before eating must be strictly promoted.
	- **Community Implementation:** Sanitary systems for excreta disposal should be selected and constructed with the cooperation of the local community, taking into consideration the local customs, practices, and available resources.
	
	**2. WATER SUPPLY**
	
	- **Protection of Sources:** Protection of water supplies against faecal contamination is critical because amoebic cysts can survive for several days and weeks in water.
	- **Inefficacy of Chlorination (High-Yield):** Amoebic cysts are **not** killed by chlorine in the amounts normally used for municipal water disinfection.
	- **Filtration and Boiling:** Sand filters are quite effective in removing amoebic cysts from water. Therefore, water filtration and boiling are considered far more effective than chemical treatment for the prevention of water-borne amoebiasis.
	
	**3. FOOD HYGIENE**
	
	- **General Protection:** Environmental measures must include the strict protection of food and drinks against faecal contamination.
	- **Disinfection of Raw Foods:** Uncooked vegetables and fruits can be disinfected by soaking them in an aqueous solution of acetic acid (5-10 per cent) or full-strength vinegar.
	- **Washing:** In most instances, thorough washing with detergents in running water is sufficient to remove amoebic cysts from fruits and vegetables.
	- **Screening of Food Handlers:** Since food handlers are major transmitters of amoebiasis, they must be periodically examined, appropriately treated if infected, and rigorously educated in food hygiene practices, especially regarding hand washing.
	
	**4. HEALTH EDUCATION**
	
	- In the long term, a great deal of disease prevention and control can be accomplished through continuous and effective health education of the public.
