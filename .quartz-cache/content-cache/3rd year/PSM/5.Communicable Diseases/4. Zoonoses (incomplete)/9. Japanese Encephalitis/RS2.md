1. Japanese encephalitis—natural history/specific protection.
	**JAPANESE ENCEPHALITIS (JE)**
	
	**I. NATURAL HISTORY OF JAPANESE ENCEPHALITIS**
	
	Japanese encephalitis is a mosquito-borne viral encephalitis. Its natural history follows the epidemiological triad of agent, host, and environmental factors interacting to produce the disease.
	
	**A. Epidemiological Determinants**
	
	- **Agent:** The causative agent is the Japanese encephalitis virus, a Group B arbovirus (Flavivirus).
	- **Animal Hosts and Reservoir (Zoonosis):**
	    - **Pigs:** Act as major vertebrate hosts and "amplifiers" of the virus. They circulate the virus heavily in their blood without showing overt clinical symptoms, infecting mosquitoes that feed on them.
	    - **Birds:** Ardeid birds like pond herons, cattle egrets, ducks, and poultry are also involved in the natural transmission cycle.
	    - **Bovines:** Cattle and buffaloes act as "mosquito attractants" but do not play a significant role as natural reservoirs.
	    - **Man:** Man is merely an incidental or "dead-end" host. There is no man-to-man transmission.
	- **Vectors:** The disease is transmitted by Culicine mosquitoes, most notably _Culex tritaeniorhynchus_, _C. vishnui_, and _C. gelidus_. These mosquitoes are zoophilic (prefer vertebrate hosts) and breed profusely in irrigated rice fields, shallow ditches, and pools,.
	- **Environmental Factors:** The disease is predominantly rural and agricultural, strongly linked to flooded rice fields. Transmission intensifies during the rainy season when vector populations peak,.
	
	**B. Incubation Period & Transmission**
	
	- **Extrinsic Incubation Period:** After a female mosquito feeds on a viremic host, it takes 9-12 days for the virus to replicate and make the mosquito infective.
	- **Intrinsic Incubation Period (In Man):** Varies from 5 to 15 days following the infective mosquito bite.
	
	**C. Clinical Course & Spectrum of Disease** The ratio of overt clinical disease to inapparent (subclinical) infection is about 1:250, meaning cases of severe encephalitis represent only the "tip of the iceberg",. The clinical course in symptomatic individuals is divided into three stages:
	
	1. **Prodromal Stage (1-6 days):** Acute onset of fever, headache, lethargy, malaise, and gastrointestinal disturbances.
	2. **Acute Encephalitic Stage:** High fever (38°C to 40.7°C), nuchal rigidity, focal CNS signs, convulsions, signs of raised intracranial pressure, altered sensorium, and progression to coma,.
	3. **Late Stage and Sequelae:** Active inflammation subsides, and temperature normalizes. Convalescence is prolonged, and residual neurological deficits are common. The case fatality rate is high, varying between 20 to 40 per cent,.
	
	---
	
	**II. SPECIFIC PROTECTION & PREVENTION**
	
	Since there is no specific cure for JE, specific protection and prevention are the mainstays of controlling the disease.
	
	**A. Vaccination of Human Population** Vaccination of populations at risk is highly recommended. Three types of vaccines are currently in large-scale use:
	
	- **Types of Vaccines:**
	    1. Mouse brain-derived, inactivated vaccine (Nakayama or Beijing strains),.
	    2. Cell culture-derived, inactivated vaccine (Beijing P-3 strain).
	    3. Cell culture-derived, live attenuated vaccine (SA 14-14-2 strain).
	- **Immunization Schedule in India:** The live attenuated vaccine (SA 14-14-2) is extensively used in India and is integrated into the Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) in endemic districts for children aged 1 to 15 years. A single primary dose provides good protection, followed by a single booster dose given at an interval of about 1 year.
	- **Age constraints:** Vaccination is not recommended for children under 6 months of age due to potential interference with passively acquired maternal antibodies.
	
	**B. Vaccination of Swine** Immunizing pigs (the amplifier host) is highly effective for both public health and economic reasons. It prevents viremia in pigs, thereby breaking the transmission cycle to mosquitoes. Both inactivated and modified live virus vaccines are used for swine.
	
	**C. Vector Control**
	
	- **Space Spraying/Fogging:** Aerial or ground fogging with ultra-low-volume (ULV) insecticides (e.g., malathion or fenitrothion) is an effective outbreak control measure,.
	- **Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS):** All affected villages should undergo IRS. Spraying must cover vegetation around houses, breeding sites, and animal shelters. Uninfected villages within a 2 to 3 km radius should also be sprayed as a preventive barrier,.
	- **Biological Control:** Using larvivorous fishes in water bodies is part of the integrated vector control strategy.
	
	**D. Personal Prophylaxis**
	
	- Using mosquito nets (especially fine-mesh bed-nets),,.
	- Wearing full-sleeved clothes and full pants to cover the body and prevent mosquito bites,.
	- Keeping pigs away from human dwellings or securely in pigsties, especially from dusk to dawn, which aligns with the peak biting time of vector mosquitoes.
1. Vectors of Japanese Encephaliits
	**VECTORS OF JAPANESE ENCEPHALITIS (JE)**
	
	**1. Primary Vectors (High-Yield Species)**
	
	- Japanese Encephalitis is primarily a mosquito-borne viral disease transmitted by **Culicine mosquitoes**.
	- The most notable incriminated species are **_Culex tritaeniorhynchus_, _Culex vishnui_, and _Culex gelidus_**.
	- In addition to Culicine mosquitoes, some anopheline mosquitoes have also been implicated as vectors of JE.
	
	**2. Regional Importance in India**
	
	- In South India specifically, **_C. tritaeniorhynchus_ and _C. vishnui_** are considered the most important vectors for the transmission of the disease.
	
	**3. Vector Bionomics and Habits**
	
	- **Breeding Sites:** These mosquitoes generally breed in shallow ditches, pools, and irrigated rice fields. Irrigated rice fields are recognized as probably the most important breeding places. Transmission occurs principally in rural agricultural locations where flooding irrigation is practiced.
	- **Feeding Habits:** The vectors are predominantly **zoophilic**, meaning they prefer to feed primarily on vertebrate animal hosts rather than humans. They feed heavily on pigs (which act as the major "amplifier" hosts for the virus) and certain birds (like pond herons and cattle egrets).
	- **Extrinsic Incubation Period:** A female mosquito gets infected after taking a blood meal from a viraemic host. Following an incubation period of **9 to 12 days** within the mosquito, it becomes capable of transmitting the virus to other hosts.
	- **Human Infection:** Man acquires the infection incidentally through the bite of an infected mosquito and serves as a "dead-end" host, meaning man-to-man transmission does not occur.
