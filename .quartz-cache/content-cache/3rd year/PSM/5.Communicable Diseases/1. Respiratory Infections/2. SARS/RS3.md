1. Severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS)—preventive measures.
	**SEVERE ACUTE RESPIRATORY SYNDROME (SARS): PREVENTIVE MEASURES**
	
	**Introduction** Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS) is a highly communicable viral disease caused by a novel strain of Coronavirus. Since there is currently no vaccine available against SARS, control and prevention rely entirely on appropriate detection and strict protective measures.
	
	**High-Yield Preventive Measures for SARS Control:**
	
	**1. Case Identification and Contact Tracing**
	
	- **Prompt Detection:** Rapid identification of persons suspected of having SARS, along with tracking their movements and close contacts.
	- **Comprehensive Screening:** Comprehensive identification of all suspected SARS cases to halt the chain of transmission.
	
	**2. Isolation Practices**
	
	- **Hospital Isolation:** Effective and strict isolation of suspected and confirmed SARS patients in hospitals.
	- **Timing of Isolation:** Evidence shows that when symptomatic cases are isolated within 5 days of the onset of illness, secondary transmission is significantly minimized.
	
	**3. Infection Control & Personal Hygiene**
	
	- **Hand Hygiene:** Strict adherence to simple hygienic measures, such as thorough hand-washing after touching patients.
	- **Use of Masks:** Use of appropriate and well-fitted masks by patients and healthcare providers.
	- **Standard Precautions:** Introduction of strict infection control measures within the hospital environment. The virus can survive on plastic surfaces for at least 24 hours at room temperature, making surface disinfection critical.
	
	**4. Protection of Healthcare Workers (HCWs)**
	
	- **Medical Staff Safety:** Appropriate protection must be ensured for all medical staff treating SARS patients.
	- **Aerosol-Generating Procedures:** Special care and barrier precautions are required during procedures that generate aerosols (e.g., endotracheal intubation, bronchoscopy, and nebulization treatments), as these can amplify the transmission of the SARS coronavirus.
	
	**5. International Travel Restrictions**
	
	- **Exit Screening:** WHO recommends the exit screening of international travellers to reduce the opportunities for further international spread associated with air travel during an epidemic period.
	
	**6. Notification and Reporting**
	
	- **Information Sharing:** Timely and accurate reporting and sharing of disease information with other authorities and governments is essential for global containment.
	- **WHO Notification:** Under the International Health Regulations, every single confirmed case of SARS must be reported to the World Health Organization (WHO).
