1. Secondary prevention of rheumatic heart disease
	**SECONDARY PREVENTION OF RHEUMATIC HEART DISEASE (RHD)**
	
	**1. AIM AND RATIONALE**
	
	- **Definition:** Secondary prevention refers to the prevention of recurrences of Rheumatic Fever (RF) to avert the progression to or worsening of Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD).
	- **Significance:** It is the most practicable, feasible, and cost-effective approach for disease control, especially in developing countries where primary prevention is difficult to implement.
	- **Mechanism:** It works by preventing group A streptococcal sore throat infections, thereby completely preventing the recurrence of RF and subsequent RHD.
	
	**2. DRUG OF CHOICE AND DOSAGE (HIGH-YIELD)**
	
	- **Drug:** Benzathine Benzyl Penicillin given via deep intramuscular (IM) injection.
	- **Frequency:** Administered at intervals of **3 weeks**.
	- **Dosage:**
	    - **Adults:** 1.2 million units.
	    - **Children:** 600,000 units.
	
	**3. DURATION OF PROPHYLAXIS (EXAM ESSENTIALS)** The duration of secondary prophylaxis strictly depends on the clinical status and presence/severity of cardiac involvement:
	
	- **For RF without Carditis:** Must be continued for at least **5 years** after the last attack OR until the child reaches **21 years of age**, whichever is later.
	- **For RF with Mild Carditis:** (e.g., mild mitral regurgitation or healed carditis). Must be continued for **10 years** after the last attack OR at least until **40 years of age**, whichever is longer.
	- **For Severe Valvular Disease or Post-Valve Surgery:** Demands **life-long** treatment.
	
	**4. PROGRAMMATIC CHALLENGES & IMPLEMENTATION**
	
	- **Compliance:** The single most crucial problem in secondary prevention is patient compliance, as penicillin prophylaxis is a painful, long-term affair.
	- **Delivery:** Studies indicate that it is highly successful, inexpensive, and cost-effective when implemented effectively through the existing primary health care systems.
1. Prevention of rheumatic fever (heart disease)
	**PREVENTION OF RHEUMATIC FEVER AND RHEUMATIC HEART DISEASE (RHD)**
	
	The prevention of Rheumatic Fever (RF) and Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD) requires a multi-pronged approach broadly categorized into primary prevention, secondary prevention, and non-medical measures.
	
	**1. PRIMARY PREVENTION**
	
	- **Aim:** To prevent the very first attack of RF by promptly identifying and adequately treating group A beta-haemolytic streptococcal throat infections ``.
	- **Target Group:** Concentrates on "high-risk" groups such as school-age children who should be kept under surveillance for streptococcal pharyngitis ``.
	- **Drug of Choice:**
	    - A single intramuscular (IM) injection of **Benzathine Benzyl Penicillin** (1.2 million units for adults; 600,000 units for children) ``.
	    - Alternatively, Oral Penicillin (Penicillin V or Penicillin G) administered for 10 days `,`.
	- **Alternative Drugs (in case of Penicillin allergy):** First-generation cephalosporins (e.g., cefadroxil or cefalexin), erythromycin, clarithromycin, or azithromycin ``.
	- **Limitations:** While theoretically simple, primary prevention is logistically difficult and expensive to achieve in developing countries because many throat infections are inapparent, unreported, or lack reliable laboratory confirmation `,`.
	
	**2. SECONDARY PREVENTION (HIGH-YIELD)**
	
	- **Aim:** Prevention of recurrences of RF in individuals who have already had an attack ``.
	- **Significance:** It is the most practicable, feasible, and cost-effective approach for disease control, especially in developing countries `,`.
	- **Drug & Dosage:** **Benzathine Benzyl Penicillin** administered via IM injection at intervals of **3 weeks** ``.
	    - _Adults:_ 1.2 million units ``.
	    - _Children:_ 600,000 units ``.
	- **Duration of Prophylaxis (Exam Essential):**
	    - **For RF without Carditis:** Must be continued for at least **5 years** after the last attack OR until the child reaches **21 years of age**, whichever is later ``.
	    - **For RF with Mild Carditis:** (e.g., mild mitral regurgitation or healed carditis). Must be continued for **10 years** after the last attack OR at least until **40 years of age**, whichever is longer ``.
	    - **For Severe Valvular Disease or Post-Valve Surgery:** Requires **life-long** treatment ``.
	- **Programmatic Challenge:** The most crucial problem is patient compliance, as penicillin prophylaxis is a painful, long-term affair ``.
	
	**3. NON-MEDICAL MEASURES (SOCIO-ECONOMIC)**
	
	- **Rationale:** RF is a social disease. Penicillin prophylaxis alone will not lead to effective control without raising the standards of living ``.
	- **Measures:** Focused on improving living conditions, providing better housing, reducing overcrowding, and breaking the poverty-disease-poverty cycle ``.
	
	**4. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EVALUATION**
	
	- **Best Indicator:** The prevalence of RHD in school children is considered the best indicator for evaluating the control programme ``.
	- **Method:** Periodic surveys conducted on random samples of schools (children in the 6–14 years age group) at 5-year intervals, ideally with a sample size of 20,000 to 30,000 children ``.
