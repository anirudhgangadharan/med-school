1. Define accident. Enumerate the types of accident. Give an account of primary factors associated with accidents/describe in detail the risk factors and preventive measures for accidents. 
	**DEFINITION OF ACCIDENT**
	An accident has been defined as "an unexpected, unplanned occurrence which may involve injury". A WHO Advisory Group defined an accident as an "unpremeditated event resulting in recognizable damage". Furthermore, it is described as that "occurrence in a sequence of events which usually produces unintended injury, death or property damage". 
	
	**TYPES AND CLASSIFICATION OF ACCIDENTS**
	Accidents and injuries represent a major epidemic of non-communicable disease. They can be broadly classified based on "intentionality" into unintentional and intentional injuries.
	**1. Unintentional Injuries**
	*   **Road Traffic Accidents (RTAs):** The leading cause of injury deaths, especially among children and young adults aged 5-29 years,.
	*   **Domestic (Home) Accidents:** These take place in the home or its immediate surroundings and include drowning, burns (thermal, chemical, electrical), falls, poisoning, injuries from sharp instruments, and animal bites.
	*   **Industrial Accidents:** Associated with hazardous working conditions, machinery, and exposure to toxic/chemical substances in factories, mines, and agricultural settings,.
	*   **Railway Accidents:** Primarily caused by human failure leading to crashes and train derailments,.
	**2. Intentional Injuries**
	*   **Self-harm:** Suicides (e.g., poisoning, hanging, drowning),.
	*   **Interpersonal and Collective Violence:** Homicide, sexual assault, neglect, abandonment, child maltreatment, and war/conflicts.
	
	**PRIMARY FACTORS ASSOCIATED WITH ACCIDENTS (MULTICAUSATION)**
	Accidents are a complex phenomenon of multiple causation, typically grouped into Human (Host) and Environmental factors.
	
	**A. Human Factors (Host Factors)**
	Most authorities consider human factors as the most important, being responsible for up to 85 to 90 per cent of all accidents,.
	*   **Demographics:** Age and sex play a major role. Younger age groups are more prone to accidents, and males are almost three times more likely to be involved and killed in car crashes than females,.
	*   **Physical and Medical Conditions:** Fatigue, sudden illness, heart attacks, impaired vision, and lack of experience or inadequate physical capabilities,.
	*   **Psycho-social Factors:** Risk-taking behaviour, impulsiveness, defective judgement, aggressiveness, poor perception, and family dysfunction.
	*   **Precipitating Factors (Substance Abuse):** Heightened emotional tension, and the consumption of alcohol and drugs (like barbiturates and cannabis) drastically impair driving ability and increase the likelihood of severe crashes,.
	*   **Specific Behavioural Risks (RTAs):**
	    *   *Speed:* Higher average speeds directly increase crash likelihood and severity.
	    *   *Distracted driving:* Use of mobile phones (including hands-free) increases the risk of a crash by approximately four times,.
	    *   *Lack of protective gear:* Non-use of seat-belts and motorcycle helmets,.
	
	**B. Environmental Factors**
	*   **Road Conditions:** Defective or narrow roads, poor lighting, defective layouts of cross-roads, and unmarked speed-breakers.
	*   **Vehicle Conditions:** Excessive speed capabilities, old and poorly maintained vehicles, overloaded buses, and a high density of two- or three-wheelers,.
	*   **Traffic Mix:** In developing countries, large numbers of pedestrians and animals share unsegregated roadways with fast and slow-moving vehicles.
	*   **Other Factors:** Bad weather, social pressure (e.g., travelling in groups), and notably, the inadequate enforcement of existing traffic laws,.
	
	**PREVENTIVE MEASURES FOR ACCIDENTS**
	Accidents don't just happen; they are caused, and studies show that up to 98% of them are preventable,. A comprehensive, intersectoral approach is required:
	
	**1. Data Collection and Research**
	*   A basic reporting system supplemented by special surveys is required to collect detailed environmental data (road, vehicle, weather) and identify the chain of events leading to the accident.
	*   Epidemiological research (Accidentology) is essential to correlate accident experiences with personal attributes, evaluate control measures, and find ways to alter human behaviour.
	
	**2. Safety Education**
	*   Safety education must begin with school children to curb the fatalistic attitude that accidents are inevitable.
	*   Drivers must be trained in safe driving and vehicle maintenance.
	*   Young people need education on risk factors, traffic rules, and first aid. 
	
	**3. Promotion of Safety Measures**
	*   **Seat-belts:** Use of seat-belts reduces fatalities and non-fatal injuries by approximately 50 per cent.
	*   **Helmets:** Wearing a motorcycle helmet correctly reduces the risk of death by almost 42 per cent and severe head injury by over 69 per cent. 
	*   **Child Restraints:** Children should be prohibited from sitting in the front seats of cars; correctly installed child restraints reduce infant deaths by approximately 70 per cent,.
	
	**4. Control of Alcohol and Drugs**
	*   Strict laws establishing blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limits of 0.05 g/dl or below are highly effective.
	*   Enforcing sobriety checkpoints and random breath testing can reduce alcohol-related crashes by about 20 per cent.
	
	**5. Elimination of Causative Environmental Factors**
	*   Improvement of road infrastructure, imposition of speed limits (e.g., 30 km/h zones in residential and school areas), and marking of danger points to eliminate hazards,.
	
	**6. Enforcement of Laws**
	*   Strict implementation of legislation regarding driving tests, medical fitness to drive, speed limits, compulsory seat-belt and helmet wearing, and regular vehicle inspections. 
	*   Adopting legislative measures to ban texting and mobile phone use while driving.
	
	**7. Primary Care and Trauma Services**
	*   Emergency care must begin at the accident site, continue during transportation, and conclude in fully equipped specialized trauma care hospitals,.
	
	**8. Rehabilitation Services**
	*   Provision of medical, social, and vocational rehabilitation to prevent, reduce, or compensate for disability and handicap resulting from injuries.

	**EPIDEMIOLOGY OF ROAD TRAFFIC INJURIES (RTI)**
	
	**1. GLOBAL BURDEN**
	
	- **Mortality:** Road traffic deaths continue to rise steadily, reaching 1.19 million globally in 2021.
	- **Vulnerable Age Group:** RTIs are the absolute leading cause of death for children and young adults aged 5 to 29 years, and the 12th leading cause across all ages. Two-thirds of all deaths occur among the working-age population (18-59 years).
	- **Victim Profile:** Motorcyclists and other powered 2/3-wheeler riders account for 30% of deaths, occupants of 4-wheelers account for 25%, pedestrians for 21%, and cyclists for 5%.
	- **Geographical Distribution:** 90% of road traffic deaths occur in low and middle-income countries (LMICs). The WHO South-East Asia Region accounts for 26% of all global fatalities.
	- **Gender:** Males are almost 3 times more likely to be killed in a car crash than young females.
	
	**2. THE INDIAN SCENARIO (HIGH-YIELD STATISTICS - 2023 DATA)**
	
	- **Magnitude:** In 2023, India reported 4.80 lakh road accidents, resulting in 1.74 lakh deaths and 4,62,825 serious injuries. This amounts to a staggering 55 crashes and 20 fatalities every hour.
	- **Specific Causes:** Over-speeding caused the maximum deaths (1,17,682), followed by wrong-side overtaking (9,432), drunk driving (3,674), and distracted driving/mobile use (2,884). Non-use of helmets caused 54,000 deaths and non-use of seat-belts caused 16,000 deaths.
	- **Demographics:**
	    - _Age:_ 55.5% of deaths were in the below 35 years age group.
	    - _Gender:_ 85.8% of accidental deaths were in men and 14.2% in women.
	    - _Geography:_ 68.5% of victims were from rural areas compared to 31.5% from urban areas.
	- **State-wise Distribution:** Uttar Pradesh had the highest accidental mortalities (13.7%), while Kerala topped the country in the sheer number of road accidents (10.0%).
	- **Vehicle Type:** Two-wheelers accounted for 44.8% of deaths, and pedestrians accounted for nearly 20% of fatalities.
	
	**3. RISK FACTORS & DETERMINANTS**
	
	- **Speed:** An increase in average speed directly increases both the likelihood of a crash and the severity of its consequences. Pedestrians have a <50% chance of surviving an impact of 45 km/h or above.
	- **Drink-Driving:** The risk of a crash increases significantly above a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.04 g/dl.
	- **Lack of Protective Gear:** Not wearing motorcycle helmets or car seat-belts/child restraints massively increases fatality risk.
	- **Distracted Driving:** Drivers using a mobile phone are approximately 4 times more likely to be involved in a crash.
	- **Environmental Factors in Developing Countries:** Unique risks include large numbers of pedestrians and animals sharing the road with vehicles, poorly maintained vehicles, low driving standards, overloaded buses, disregard for traffic rules, and defective roads/lighting.

	**PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF ACCIDENTS IN INDIA**
	
	Accidents are a complex phenomenon of multiple causation (human and environmental) and are highly preventable. 98% of accidents are considered preventable. An intersectoral approach is required, encompassing the following measures:
	
	**1. Data Collection**
	
	- There must be a basic reporting system for all accidents, heavily relying on police records as a starting point.
	- National data should be supplemented by special surveys and in-depth studies to understand the exact circumstances, risk factors, and environmental conditions (road, weather, vehicle) leading to the crash.
	
	**2. Safety Education**
	
	- The fatalistic attitude that accidents are "inevitable" must be curbed. As the saying goes, _"If accident is a disease, education is its vaccine"_.
	- Safety education must begin with school children. Drivers require strict training in safe driving and vehicle maintenance.
	
	**3. Promotion of Safety Measures**
	
	- **Seat Belts:** Mandatory use reduces the number of fatalities and non-fatal injuries by approximately 40-50% for front-seat passengers.
	- **Safety Helmets:** Proper use reduces the risk of head injury by up to 69% and fatalities by 42%.
	- **Children:** Children should be prohibited from sitting in the front seats of cars and must use appropriate child restraints.
	- **Vehicle Design:** Use of door locks and laminated high-penetration resistance windscreens.
	
	**4. Control of Alcohol and Other Drugs**
	
	- Alcohol impairs driving ability and is a direct cause of 30 to 50% of severe road accidents.
	- Strict enforcement of sobriety checkpoints and random breath testing can reduce alcohol-related crashes by about 20%. Drugs like barbiturates and amphetamines must also be avoided.
	
	**5. Primary Care and Trauma Management**
	
	- Emergency care must begin at the accident site, continue during transportation, and conclude in the hospital emergency room.
	- An Accident Services Organization and fully equipped specialized trauma care hospitals should be established in all major cities.
	
	**6. Elimination of Causative Factors (Engineering)**
	
	- Factors that predispose to accidents must be structurally eliminated, such as improving road layouts, imposing strict speed limits, and clearly marking danger points.
	
	**7. Enforcement of Laws**
	
	- Strict State enforcement of legislation is critical. This includes rigorous driving tests, checking medical fitness to drive, speed limit enforcement, compulsory seat-belt/helmet laws, and regular inspection of vehicles.
	
	**8. Rehabilitation Services**
	
	- Survivors must receive medical, social, and occupational rehabilitation to prevent, reduce, or adequately compensate for disability and handicap.
	
	**9. Accident Research (Accidentology)**
	
	- The future of accident prevention lies in research to gather precise information, correlate accident experience with personal/environmental attributes, and evaluate the efficiency of current control measures.

	**ROAD TRAFFIC ACCIDENTS IN INDIA: EPIDEMIOLOGICAL TRIAD ANALYSIS & CONTROL MEASURES**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION: MAGNITUDE OF THE PROBLEM**
	
	- Road traffic accidents (RTAs) represent a major epidemic of non-communicable disease, driven by rapid motorization, urbanization, and changing lifestyles.
	- **Indian Scenario (2023 Data):** India reports about 4.80 lakh road accidents annually, resulting in 1.74 lakh deaths and 4.62 lakh serious injuries. This translates to a staggering 55 crashes and 20 fatalities every hour.
	
	**2. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL TRIAD ANALYSIS** Accidents are a complex phenomenon of multiple causation. To understand and control them, they must be analysed using the Epidemiological Triad: Host, Agent, and Environment.
	
	**A. HOST FACTORS (Human Factors)** Human failure is responsible for up to 85 to 90 per cent of all accidents.
	
	- **Demographic Profile:**
	    - **Age:** Younger age groups are highly prone; 55.5% of RTA deaths in India occur in individuals below 35 years of age.
	    - **Sex:** Males are almost 3 times more likely to be killed in a crash than females. In India, 85.8% of accidental deaths are in men.
	- **Physiological & Medical Conditions:** Fatigue, sudden illness, heart attacks, and impaired vision directly contribute to poor driving.
	- **Psychosocial & Behavioural Factors:**
	    - Over-speeding (the single largest cause, responsible for 1,17,682 deaths in 2023).
	    - Drink-driving (alcohol impairs driving ability and increases crash risk).
	    - Distracted driving, especially mobile phone use (responsible for nearly 2,884 deaths).
	    - Risk-taking behaviour, impulsiveness, aggressiveness, and inexperience.
	- **Lack of Body Protection:** Non-use of seatbelts (16,000 deaths) and not wearing helmets (54,000 deaths) drastically increases fatality.
	
	**B. AGENT FACTORS (Vehicle/Mechanical Factors)** The vehicle or machine acts as the primary agent of injury.
	
	- **Vehicle Condition:** Large numbers of old, poorly maintained vehicles on the roads.
	- **Vehicle Type:** High involvement of vulnerable vehicles like two-wheelers (accounting for 44.8% of deaths in India).
	- **Overloading:** Overloaded buses and goods vehicles significantly compromise stability and braking (12,000 deaths in 2023).
	
	**C. ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS** These are the physical and social surroundings that facilitate the accident.
	
	- **Road Infrastructure:** Defective and narrow roads, poor street lighting, and defective layouts of cross-roads and speed-breakers.
	- **Traffic Conditions (Mixed Traffic):** Developing countries like India face unique challenges where fast-moving vehicles, slow-moving vehicles (bullock carts), pedestrians, and animals share the same unsegregated roadway.
	- **Weather:** Bad weather conditions (fog, heavy rain) reducing visibility and road grip.
	- **Social Environment:** Widespread disregard for traffic rules, social pressure (e.g., travelling in groups), and inadequate enforcement of existing laws.

	**3. SOLUTIONS AND CONTROL MEASURES** Since RTAs are multifactorial, controlling them requires a comprehensive, intersectoral approach addressing the triad.
	
	**A. Addressing Host Factors (Human Behaviour)**
	
	- **Safety Education:** Must begin with school children. Drivers need training in safe driving, vehicle maintenance, and first aid ("If accident is a disease, education is its vaccine").
	- **Promotion of Safety Measures:**
	    - _Helmets:_ Mandatory and correct use of standard motorcycle helmets reduces the risk of death by 42% and severe head injury by 69%.
	    - _Seat-belts:_ Compulsory use reduces fatal and non-fatal injuries among front-seat passengers by 40-50%.
	    - _Child Restraints:_ Prohibiting children in front seats and using correct child restraints.
	- **Enforcement of Laws:** Stringent enforcement of speed limits, driving tests, medical fitness tests, and strict laws against distracted driving (mobile phones).
	- **Control of Alcohol/Drugs:** Implementing strict blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limits (0.05 g/dl or below), enforcing sobriety checkpoints, and random breath testing.
	
	**B. Addressing Agent Factors (Vehicles)**
	
	- **Vehicle Safety Standards:** Regular and mandatory inspection of vehicles for roadworthiness.
	- **Safety Features:** Incorporating proper vehicle designs, door locks, and laminated high-penetration resistance windscreen glass.
	- **Strict action against overloading** of transport and passenger vehicles.
	
	**C. Addressing Environmental Factors (Roads & Infrastructure)**
	
	- **Engineering & Road Layout:** Improvement of road designs, widening of streets, and correcting defective cross-roads.
	- **Traffic Segregation:** Creating dedicated walking and cycle paths to separate vulnerable road users (pedestrians/cyclists) from heavy/fast vehicular traffic.
	- **Marking Hazards:** Proper illumination/lighting of streets and clear marking of danger points and speed zones (e.g., 30 km/h zones near schools and residential areas).
	
	**D. Comprehensive Health Systems Approach (Tertiary Care & Research)**
	
	- **Primary Trauma Care:** Establishing an "Accident Services Organization" and specialized trauma care hospitals. Emergency care must seamlessly span the accident site, transportation, and hospital emergency rooms.
	- **Rehabilitation Services:** Medical, social, and occupational rehabilitation to reduce disability and handicap in survivors.
	- **Accidentology (Data & Research):** Establishing a robust basic reporting system taking police records as a starting point, supplemented by epidemiological surveys to constantly monitor risk factors and evaluate the efficiency of control measures.
1. Domestic accident prevention. 
	
	**PREVENTION OF DOMESTIC ACCIDENTS**
	
	**1. DEFINITION AND SCOPE**
	
	- **Definition:** A "domestic accident" is defined as an accident which takes place in the home or in its immediate surroundings, and generally includes all accidents not connected with traffic, vehicles, or sport.
	- **Common Causes (High-Yield):** The most frequent causes are (1) drowning, (2) burns (by flame, hot liquid, electricity, crackers, chemicals), (3) falls, (4) poisoning (drugs, insecticides, kerosene), (5) injuries from sharp or pointed instruments, and (6) bites and other injuries from animals.
	
	**2. GENERAL PREVENTIVE MEASURES**
	
	- **Safety Education:** Carelessness is the predominant factor in accidents. The problem can be effectively tackled through intensive safety education and health education directed towards safety in the home.
	- **Target Vulnerable Groups:** Unintentional injuries such as poisoning, drowning, burns, and maltreatment primarily affect small children, while the elderly are highly prone to falls.
	
	**3. SPECIFIC PREVENTIVE STRATEGIES**
	
	**A. Prevention of Drowning**
	
	- **Risk Factors:** Children under 5 years have the highest mortality rates, often associated with a lapse in adult supervision. Infants left alone in a bathtub or children living near open water sources (ditches, ponds, pools, wells) are at special risk.
	- **Engineering Methods:** This is the most effective strategy. It includes:
	    - Building four-sided pool fences or barriers to prevent access to standing water.
	    - Covering wells or open cisterns.
	    - Emptying buckets and baths immediately after use, and storing them upside down.
	- **Education:** Individual and community education on drowning awareness and learning water survival skills.
	
	**B. Prevention of Burns**
	
	- **Risk Factors:** 80-90% of burns occur at home, particularly in domestic kitchens from upset receptacles containing hot liquids, flames, or cookstove explosions. Other risks include open fire cooking, unsafe cookstoves, loose clothing, and use of kerosene (paraffin).
	- **Preventive Actions:** Address specific hazards like inherently unsafe cookstoves and inadequate safety measures for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and electricity. Educate vulnerable populations and train communities in proper first-aid.
	
	**C. Prevention of Falls**
	
	- **Risk Factors:** Highly prevalent in children (from rooftops, balconies, windows, stairs) and adults over 60 years of age.
	- **Prevention in Children:** Effective interventions include engineering modifications of nursery furniture and playground equipment, as well as legislation for the use of window guards.
	- **Prevention in the Elderly:** Programs should include:
	    - Screening within living environments for fall risks.
	    - Home assessment and environmental modifications (e.g., removing tripping hazards).
	    - Clinical interventions such as medication review, treating low blood pressure, and providing Vitamin D and calcium supplementation.
	    - Prescription of appropriate assistive devices and balance retraining/muscle strengthening by a trained health professional.
	
	**D. Prevention of Poisoning**
	
	- **Risk Factors:** The most common agents are household chemicals, kerosene, prescription drugs, and pesticides. Accidental ingestion of kerosene and therapeutic drugs is a leading cause of poisoning among pre-school children.
	- **Preventive Actions:** Safe storage of prescription drugs, household chemicals, and agricultural pesticides out of the reach of children to prevent unintentional morbidity and mortality.

	**EPIDEMIOLOGY AND PREVENTION OF ACCIDENTS**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION AND DEFINITIONS**
	
	- **Definition:** An accident is defined as "an unexpected, unplanned occurrence which may involve injury". The WHO Advisory Group (1956) defined it as an "unpremeditated event resulting in recognizable damage".
	- **Public Health Significance:** Accidents represent a major epidemic of non-communicable disease in the present century and are a leading cause of death and disability globally. They are no longer considered purely "accidental" but rather predictable events with their own natural history that follow the epidemiological pattern of agent, host, and environment interacting together.
	
	**2. MEASUREMENT OF THE PROBLEM** The magnitude of accidents is assessed using specific epidemiological indices:
	
	- **Mortality:** Measured by proportional mortality rate, number of deaths per million population, death rate per 1,000 registered vehicles, or deaths per million vehicle-miles. (A person "killed" in a road traffic accident is defined as one who died outright or within 30 days as a result of the accident).
	- **Morbidity:** Measured in terms of "serious injuries" and "slight injuries". The severity is often assessed using the "Abbreviated Injury Scale".
	- **Disability:** Estimated using event-type indicators (restricted activity days) and person-type indicators (limitation of mobility). The WHO uses the International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) to estimate disability.
	
	**3. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS (CAUSATION)** Accidents are a complex phenomenon of multiple causation, broadly grouped into human and environmental factors.
	
	**A. Human Factors (Host)** Human failure is responsible for up to 85 to 90 per cent of all accidents.
	
	- **Age and Sex:** Road traffic injuries are the leading cause of death for children and young adults aged 5-29 years. Males are almost 3 times as likely to be killed in a car crash as young females.
	- **Physical and Physiological Factors:** Lack of experience (highest accident rates in the first few months of employment or driving), fatigue, impaired vision, and sudden illness (e.g., heart attack).
	- **Psychological Factors:** Carelessness, inattentiveness, overconfidence, risk-taking behaviour, impulsiveness, delayed decision-making, and aggressiveness.
	- **Alcohol and Drugs:** Drinking and driving significantly increases crash risk. Risk increases sharply above a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.04 g/dl. Drugs like barbiturates and amphetamines also impair driving ability.
	- **Distracted Driving:** The use of mobile phones while driving (including text messaging) increases the risk of a crash by approximately four times due to longer reaction times and impaired lane keeping. Hands-free phones are not much safer than hand-held sets.
	
	**B. Environmental Factors**
	
	- **Road Conditions:** Defective or narrow roads, poor lighting, and defective layout of cross-roads and speed-breakers.
	- **Vehicle Conditions:** Old and poorly maintained vehicles, lack of safety features.
	- **Developing Country Specifics:** In countries like India, the traffic stream is highly mixed. Fast-moving vehicles share the roadway with slow-moving vehicles, large numbers of pedestrians, and animals. There is widespread disregard for traffic rules, overloaded buses, and a high density of two-wheelers.
	
	**4. TYPES OF ACCIDENTS** Accidental injuries are broadly classified based on "intentionality":
	
	- **Unintentional Injuries:** Road traffic injuries, poisonings, falls, fire and burn injuries, and drowning.
	- **Intentional Injuries:** Self-harm (suicide), interpersonal violence, and collective violence (war).
	
	_High-Yield Indian Statistics for Road Traffic Accidents (RTAs):_ During 2023, India reported about 4.80 lakh road accidents resulting in 1.74 lakh deaths (amounting to 55 crashes and 20 fatalities every hour). Over-speeding caused the maximum deaths, while two-wheelers accounted for 44.8% of fatalities and pedestrians nearly 20%. Kerala reported the highest number of accidents, whereas Uttar Pradesh had the highest accidental mortalities.
	
	**5. PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF ACCIDENTS** Accidents are preventable. A comprehensive, intersectoral approach is required:
	
	- **1. Data Collection:** A basic reporting system for all accidents is essential, supplemented by special surveys and police records to understand the chain of events and risk factors.
	- **2. Safety Education:** Often stated that "if accident is a disease, education is its vaccine". Education must begin with school children and extend to drivers regarding safe driving, vehicle maintenance, and first aid.
	- **3. Promotion of Safety Measures:**
	    - _Seat Belts:_ Compulsory use reduces fatalities and non-fatal injuries by approximately 50%.
	    - _Helmets:_ Correct use reduces the risk of death by 42% and severe head injury by over 69%.
	    - _Child Restraints:_ Installing child seats reduces deaths among infants by 70%. Children under 12-15 years should be prohibited from sitting in the front seats.
	- **4. Control of Alcohol and Drugs:** Enforcing drink-driving laws with sobriety checkpoints and random breath testing. Laws establishing BAC limits of 0.05 g/dl or below are highly effective at reducing alcohol-related crashes.
	- **5. Primary Care and Emergency Services:** Emergency care must begin at the accident site and continue through transportation to a fully equipped trauma center. Specialized trauma care hospitals should be established in all major cities.
	- **6. Elimination of Causative Factors:** Improving road infrastructure, marking danger points, and imposing strict speed limits (e.g., 30 km/h zones around schools and residential areas).
	- **7. Enforcement of Laws:** Strict implementation of legislation regarding driving tests, speed limits, mandatory seat-belt and helmet wearing, and periodic medical re-examination of older drivers.
	- **8. Rehabilitation Services:** Medical, social, and occupational rehabilitation to limit disability and prevent handicap in injured victims.
	- **9. Accident Research (Accidentology):** Gathering precise information correlating accidents with personal attributes and environments to evaluate the efficiency of control measures.
	
	**6. DOMESTIC ACCIDENTS** Domestic accidents occur in the home or its immediate surroundings. Common causes and their prevention include:
	
	- **Drowning:** The 3rd leading cause of unintentional injury death globally. Prevention involves engineering methods (covering wells, four-sided pool fences), laws on alcohol use near water, and community education on water survival skills.
	- **Burns:** Thermal burns (scalds, flames) account for 180,000 deaths annually. Prevention includes safer cookstoves, reducing reliance on open fires/kerosene, and first-aid education (e.g., using cool running water, not applying paste or ice).
	- **Falls:** The second leading cause of unintentional injury death. Prevention for children includes window guards and nursery modifications; for the elderly, it includes medication reviews, correcting visual impairment, and prescribing assistive devices.
	- **Poisoning:** High burden from pesticides, kerosene, and prescription drugs, especially among children and in rural agricultural economies.
	1. Primary factors associated with accidents. 
	**PRIMARY FACTORS ASSOCIATED WITH ACCIDENTS**
	
	Accidents are a complex phenomenon of multiple causation. The primary aetiological factors responsible for accidents are broadly classified into two main categories: **Human factors** and **Environmental factors**.
	
	**I. HUMAN FACTORS (HOST FACTORS)** Human factors are considered much more important than environmental factors in accident causation, being responsible for up to 85 to 90 per cent of all accidents. These can be subdivided as follows:
	
	- **Demographic and Physical Factors:**
	    - **Age:** Younger age groups and the very old are more prone to accidents.
	    - **Sex:** Studies indicate that women tend to have fewer accidents than men when performing comparative jobs.
	    - **Education & Experience:** Lack of education and experience are major factors; for example, in industrial settings, approximately 50 per cent of employees have accidents in their first 6 months of employment.
	    - **Physical Capabilities:** The physical capabilities of the worker or driver may not meet the job requirements, such as inadequate visual acuity or impaired hearing.
	- **Medical Conditions:**
	    - Fatigue, sudden illness, heart attacks, and impaired vision directly contribute to accidents.
	- **Psycho-social and Psychological Factors:**
	    - Key psychological traits include risk-taking behaviour, impulsiveness, defective judgement, delay in decisions, aggressiveness, and poor perception.
	    - Other mental factors include carelessness, inattentiveness, overconfidence, ignorance, emotional or mental stress, family dysfunction, and a general "accident proneness".
	- **Physiological Factors (Work/Driving related):**
	    - **Time of day:** Accidents are typically at a minimum at the beginning of the day and increase gradually as fatigue sets in.
	    - **Working hours:** An increase in daily or weekly working hours directly correlates with an increase in accidents.
	- **Precipitating Behavioural Factors:**
	    - Heightened emotional tension.
	    - Consumption of alcohol and drugs.
	    - Increased vulnerability and/or exposure to risk situations.
	- **Lack of Body Protection:**
	    - Failure to use protective gear such as safety helmets and seat belts.
	
	**II. ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS** Environmental factors encompass the physical surroundings, the vehicles or machinery involved, and the prevailing weather or regulatory conditions.
	
	- **Factors Relating to the Road/Transit:**
	    - Defective or narrow roads.
	    - Defective lay-out of cross-roads and speed-breakers.
	    - Poor street lighting and lack of familiarity with the route.
	- **Factors Relating to the Vehicle:**
	    - Excessive speed and low driving standards.
	    - Use of old, poorly maintained vehicles.
	    - Large numbers of 2 or 3 wheelers and overloaded buses.
	- **Traffic and Weather Conditions:**
	    - Mixed traffic scenarios where slow-moving vehicles, fast-moving vehicles, pedestrians, and animals share the same space.
	    - Bad weather conditions.
	    - Special traffic conditions, social pressure (e.g., travelling in groups), and the use of stolen vehicles.
	- **Regulatory Factors:**
	    - Inadequate enforcement of existing traffic and safety laws.
	- **Industrial Environmental Factors (Specific to Workplaces):**
	    - Adverse workplace conditions such as extreme temperature, poor illumination, high humidity, and excessive noise.
	    - Unsafe machines directly account for 10 to 20 per cent of all industrial accidents.
	1. Accidentology.  
	**ACCIDENTOLOGY**
	
	**1. DEFINITION**
	
	- **Accidentology** is the specialized branch of scientific research dedicated to the comprehensive study of accidents, their causation, and their prevention.
	- It is considered the future of accident prevention, shifting the focus from viewing accidents as inevitable occurrences to analyzing them systematically.
	
	**2. SCOPE AND OBJECTIVES (HIGH-YIELD)** The primary concerns and key areas of investigation in accidentology include:
	
	- **Data Gathering:** Collecting precise and detailed information regarding the extent, type, and specific characteristics of various accidents.
	- **Correlation Studies:** Analyzing the complex relationship between accident occurrences and two major epidemiological variables:
	    - _Personal attributes_ (human factors such as age, experience, psychological state, and physiological capabilities).
	    - _Environmental contexts_ (physical surroundings, road layouts, vehicle conditions, or workplace settings) in which the accidents occur.
	- **Behavioural Modification:** Investigating and developing new, better methods for altering human behaviour to reduce risk-taking and human failure.
	- **Environmental Engineering:** Actively seeking out strategies to make environments systematically safer.
	- **Evaluation:** Measuring and evaluating the efficiency and effectiveness of implemented accident control measures and safety regulations.
	
	**3. PUBLIC HEALTH SIGNIFICANCE**
	
	- Since accidents are a complex phenomenon resulting from multiple causation (the interaction of agent, host, and environmental factors), accidentology provides the much-needed evidence base to formulate effective preventive strategies, enforce safety laws, and improve emergency and rehabilitation services.
