1. **RISK FACTORS OF STROKE**
	
	Epidemiological studies indicate that strokes do not occur completely at random; they are preceded by several identifiable risk factors that operate over many years. These factors can be broadly classified into medical/physiological, lifestyle/behavioural, and non-modifiable host factors.
	
	**1. THE PRINCIPAL RISK FACTOR (HIGH-YIELD)**
	
	- **Hypertension:** High blood pressure is considered the single most important and main risk factor for both cerebral thrombosis and cerebral haemorrhage.
	
	**2. OTHER MODIFIABLE & MEDICAL FACTORS**
	
	- **Transient Ischaemic Attacks (TIA):** These are episodes of focal, reversible neurological deficits of sudden onset lasting less than 24 hours. They are due to microemboli and serve as a strong warning sign of an impending stroke.
	- **Cardiac Abnormalities:** Conditions such as left ventricular hypertrophy and cardiac dilatation are significant contributors to stroke risk.
	- **Diabetes:** The presence of diabetes mellitus severely increases the susceptibility to cerebrovascular events.
	- **Elevated Blood Lipids:** High levels of blood lipids and cholesterol are strongly associated with the atherosclerotic process leading to stroke.
	- **Associated Vascular Diseases:** Nearly three-quarters of stroke patients have a personal history of associated diseases in the cardiovascular system or diabetes, indicating that stroke is often an acute incident in a slowly progressive generalized vascular disease.
	
	**3. LIFESTYLE & BEHAVIOURAL FACTORS**
	
	- **Obesity:** General obesity and specifically "central obesity" (indicated by a high waist-to-hip ratio) strongly contribute to the risk.
	- **Smoking:** Cigarette smoking is a well-established risk factor that accelerates vascular damage.
	- **Alcohol Consumption:** Harmful or excessive alcohol intake significantly raises the risk of cerebrovascular disease.
	- **Oral Contraceptives:** The use of oral contraceptive pills is linked to an increased risk of stroke in women.
	
	**4. NON-MODIFIABLE (HOST) FACTORS**
	
	- **Age:** Incidence rates of stroke rise steeply with advancing age. Globally, about 47 per cent of all stroke deaths occur in individuals over 70 years of age. _(Exam Note: In India, however, about one-fifth of all strokes occur below the age of 40, which is termed "strokes in the young", attributed to the younger population demographics and a shorter overall life expectancy)._
	- **Sex:** The incidence rates of stroke are consistently higher in males than in females across all age groups.
	- **Genetics:** Genetic factors and familial predispositions also play a role in determining an individual's risk for stroke.
