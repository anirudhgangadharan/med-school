## Ante Natal Case (ANC) Visits

## Schedule of Visits

According to National guidelines (including Park, ICMR, NHP, and standard OB/GYN references):

- **Minimum 4 Visits (WHO, Park):**
    
    - **1st Visit:** As soon as pregnancy detected (ideally before 12 weeks)
        
    - **2nd Visit:** 14–26 weeks
        
    - **3rd Visit:** 28–34 weeks
        
    - **4th Visit:** 36 weeks or later
        

**Indian Standard/Traditional Schedule:**

- **Monthly:** Up to 28 weeks
    
- **Every 2 weeks:** 28–36 weeks
    
- **Weekly:** 36 weeks to delivery
    

**Early/later/frequent visits as per risk status!**

## Objectives of ANC Visits (Antenatal Care)

1. **Promote, protect, and maintain maternal health during pregnancy**
    
2. **Early detection of risk factors (high risk cases)** and prompt management
    
3. **Screen, diagnose and manage medical, obstetric, and fetal complications**
    
4. **Ensure the birth of a healthy baby**
    
5. **Reduce maternal, perinatal, and neonatal mortality & morbidity**
    
6. **Educate mother on nutrition, hygiene, rest, breastfeeding, and childcare**
    
7. **Psychological support & counseling**
    
8. **Immunization (TT, other as per protocol)**
    
9. **Prepare mother/family for safe delivery & parenthood**
    
10. **Promote family welfare and birth spacing guidance**
    

_Similar objectives found in Park (2025), Dutta's Textbook of Obstetrics, Williams OB, and RCOG/NICE/WHO guidelines._

## Components Per Visit (Essential Steps)

- **Antenatal registration:** Comprehensive history and risk assessment
    
- **Routine exams:** Weight, BP, abdominal palpation, fetal heart, urine (albumin, sugar), hemoglobin, blood grouping, VDRL, HIV, HBsAg, GDM screening
    
- **Immunization:** TT/TD
    
- **Iron, folic acid, calcium supplements**
    
- **Ultrasound scans** as per protocol
    
- **Advice:** Diet, rest, birth preparedness, danger signs, breastfeeding
    

## Risk Approach in ANC

- **Definition:** Identifying and providing special care to 'high-risk pregnancies' needing extra attention
    

**High-Risk Factors:**

- Maternal age <18 or >35
    
- Primi >30 years/short stature (≤140cm)
    
- Twins, multiple pregnancies
    
- Previous stillbirth, abortions, C-section, difficult labor
    
- Malpresentations, hydramnios, FGR
    
- Pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, anemia
    
- Antepartum hemorrhage
    
- General illnesses: heart disease, diabetes, TB, HIV, renal, hypertension
    
- Obesity, underweight, poor socioeconomic status
    

**Risk Approach Steps:**

1. **Early Identification** during first visit and subsequent follow-ups
    
2. **Special Surveillance/Care/Referral** for high risk cases
    
3. **Regular Review** to catch evolving risks
    
4. **Ensure delivery in well-equipped centers**
    

**Purpose:** Deliver basic care to all, special/additional care to those most at risk, improve outcomes, optimally use resources.[ihatepsm](https://ihatepsm.com/blog/risk-approach-antenatal-care)

## Objectives of Antenatal Care (Summary)

- **Healthy mother and baby at end of pregnancy**
    
- **Early diagnosis** of pregnancy
    
- **Screening & detection** of risk factors or complications early
    
- **Ensure safe delivery**
    
- **Educate and empower** mothers
    
- **Reduce maternal/neonatal mortality and morbidity**
    
- **Encourage institutional deliveries**

## Postnatal Care (PNC)

## Objectives

## For Mother

- Prevent/treat complications (bleeding, infections, depression)
    
- Promote physical, mental, and social recovery
    
- Initiate/improve breastfeeding and family planning
    
- Early detection of postnatal danger signs
    
- Immunization (as per protocol)
    
- Counsel on nutrition, rest, personal hygiene
    

## For Baby

- Prevent/treat complications (asphyxia, hypothermia, sepsis)
    
- Ensure normal growth and development
    
- Early detection of abnormalities (congenital or acquired)
    
- Immunization as per schedule
    
- Counsel/provide guidance on newborn care (exclusive breastfeeding, danger signs)
    

---

## PNC Strategy

- **Minimum 4 PNC Visits recommended by WHO:**
    
    - Within 24 hours of birth
        
    - Day 3 (48–72 hours)
        
    - Days 7–14
        
    - 6 weeks
        
- **Each visit:** Assess both mother and baby; offer counseling; screen for complications; check for emotional and social support needs
    
- **Promote home visits** by health workers (esp. for home births)
    
- **Encourage early initiation and exclusive breastfeeding**
    

---

## APGAR Scoring System

- **Purpose:** Assess newborn’s physical status at **1 and 5 minutes** after birth
    
- **5 components:** Appearance (color), Pulse (HR), Grimace (reflex), Activity (tone), Respiration
    
- **Each parameter scored 0, 1 or 2**
    

|Criteria|0|1|2|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Appearance**|Blue/pale|Body pink, extremities blue|Pink all over|
|**Pulse**|Absent|< 100 bpm|≥ 100 bpm|
|**Grimace**|No response|Grimace only|Cough/sneeze/cry|
|**Activity (Tone)**|Limp/floppy|Flexion of extremities|Active movement|
|**Respiration**|Absent|Weak/irregular/gasping|Strong cry|

- **Interpretation:**
    
    - 7–10: Normal
        
    - 4–6: Moderately depressed
        
    - 0–3: Severely depressed
        

_Scored at 1 and 5 minutes; repeated at 10 minutes if <7 at 5 minutes._

## High-Yield Points for Exams

- Antenatal care is essential for both **early detection and management of maternal and fetal complications**.
    
- WHO now recommends **minimum 8 ANC contacts** for optimal outcomes, but Indian national guidelines focus on minimum 4 core visits.[who](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549912)
    
- **Risk approach** ensures cost-effective and targeted care.
    
- **APGAR scoring** is a rapid clinical tool for immediate newborn assessment and triage.
    
- **PNC** is as important as ANC, focusing equally on maternal and newborn well-being, complication prevention, and health education.

Here is a high-yield, concise summary of important procedures after delivery of the child, based on Park’s Community Medicine, standard Obstetrics texts, and recent guidelines:

---

## Procedures After Delivery of Child

## Immediate Care of the Newborn

1. **Clear Airway**
    
    - Suction mouth first, then nose with bulb syringe to clear secretions.
        
2. **Dry and Warm**
    
    - Immediately dry the baby to prevent hypothermia.
        
    - Place baby on mother’s abdomen or radiant warmer.
        
3. **Apgar Scoring**
    
    - Assess at 1 minute and 5 minutes using Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, Respiration (score 0–10).
        
4. **Immediate Breastfeeding**
    
    - Initiate breastfeeding within the first hour of birth.
        
5. **Eye Care**
    
    - Apply antibiotic eye ointment (e.g., erythromycin) to prevent Ophthalmia neonatorum.
        
6. **Vitamin K Injection**
    
    - Administer 1 mg IM vitamin K to prevent Hemorrhagic Disease of Newborn.
        
7. **Identification**
    
    - Tag or identify baby properly to prevent mix-ups.
        

---

## Immediate Care of the Mother

1. **Delivery of Placenta**
    
    - Ensure complete expulsion of placenta and membranes.
        
2. **Uterine Massage**
    
    - Massage uterus immediately after placenta delivery to promote contraction and reduce postpartum hemorrhage.
        
3. **Examination of Placenta**
    
    - Check placenta and membranes for completeness.
        
4. **Check for and Control Bleeding**
    
    - Observe for postpartum hemorrhage; manage accordingly.
        
5. **Repair of Episiotomy or Lacerations**
    
    - Inspect birth canal and repair tears or episiotomy.
        
6. **Initiate Breastfeeding**
    
    - Assist and encourage early breastfeeding for uterine contraction and bonding.
        
7. **Monitoring**
    
    - Monitor vital signs, urine output, and vaginal bleeding for at least 2 hours.
        
8. **Prophylactic measures**
    
    - Administer oxytocin if required for uterine contraction.
        
    - Provide pain relief as needed.
        
    - Counsel regarding family planning and postnatal care.
