# XVIII. PREVENTION OF HYPERTENSION

## A. Primary Prevention

**i) Population Strategy**

**(a) Nutrition:**

◦        Reduction of salt intake to average — not more than 5 g per day

◦        Moderation of fat intake

◦        Avoidance of high alcohol intake

◦        Restriction of energy intake appropriate to body needs

**(b) Weight Reduction:**

◦        Prevention and correction of overweight

**(c) Exercise Promotion:**

◦        Regular physical activity

**(d) Behavioural Change:**

◦        Reduction of stress and smoking

◦        Modification of personal lifestyle

◦        Yoga and transcendental meditation could be profitable

**(e) Health Education:**

◦        Advice on all risk factors and related at-risk behaviours

**(f) Self-Care:**

◦        To take his own BP and keep a logbook of his readings

◦        Brochures also to be useful for standalone purposes

**ii) High Risk Strategy**

•        To prevent the attainment of levels of BP at which the introduction of treatment would be considered

•        Family history of HTN and history of BP from childhood may be used to identify individuals at risk

## B. Secondary Prevention

**i) Early Case Detection**

•        Effective method as seen in population screening

**ii) Treatment**

•        Aim to reduce BP below 140/90 mmHg and ideally to 120/80 mmHg

•        To reduce incidence of stroke and complications

**iii) Prevent Complications**

•        Education should be directed to patients, families, and community

## C. Tertiary Level

•        Disability limitation and rehabilitation

•        No disability and follow-up treatment

# XIX. LEVELS OF CARE
