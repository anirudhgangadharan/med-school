**ACUTE DIARRHOEAL DISEASE (ADD): HIGH-YIELD VIVA NOTES**

**I. DEFINITION & CLINICAL TYPES**

- **Definition:** Diarrhoea is defined as the passage of loose, liquid, or watery stools, usually more than three times a day. The recent change in consistency is more important than the frequency.
- **Four Clinical Types:**
    1. **Acute Watery Diarrhoea:** Lasts several hours to days. Main danger is dehydration and weight loss. Pathogens include _V. cholerae_, Enterotoxigenic _E. coli_ (ETEC), and Rotavirus.
    2. **Acute Bloody Diarrhoea (Dysentery):** Marked by visible blood in the stools. Main dangers are intestinal mucosa damage, sepsis, and malnutrition. Most common cause is _Shigella_.
    3. **Persistent Diarrhoea:** Lasts 14 days or longer. Main dangers are malnutrition, serious non-intestinal infection, and dehydration.
    4. **Diarrhoea with Severe Malnutrition:** Associated with marasmus or kwashiorkor. Main dangers are severe systemic infection, dehydration, heart failure, and vitamin/mineral deficiency.

**II. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS**

- **Agent Factors:**
    - _Viruses:_ Rotavirus is the leading cause of severe, dehydrating diarrhoea in children aged <5 years globally. Other viruses include astroviruses, adenoviruses, and enteroviruses.
    - _Bacteria:_ ETEC (major cause of traveller's diarrhoea), _Campylobacter jejuni_, _Shigella_, _Salmonella_, and _V. cholerae_.
    - _Parasites:_ _Entamoeba histolytica_, _Giardia intestinalis_, and _Cryptosporidium_ (important in malnourished or HIV/AIDS patients).
- **Host Factors:** Highest incidence is between 6-11 months of age, which coincides with the weaning period, declining maternal antibodies, and the infant starting to crawl. Malnutrition and diarrhoea form a vicious circle.
- **Environmental Factors:** Transmitted primarily by the faecal-oral route (water-borne, food-borne, or direct transmission via fingers and fomites). Bacterial diarrhoeas peak during the warmer, rainy season, whereas viral diarrhoeas (like Rotavirus) peak during winter in temperate climates and occur year-round in tropical areas.

**III. ASSESSMENT OF DEHYDRATION (IMNCI GUIDELINES)** Examiners frequently ask how to classify dehydration in a clinic setting.

- **Severe Dehydration (Needs Plan C):** Two or more of the following signs: Lethargic or unconscious; Sunken eyes; Not able to drink or drinking poorly; Skin pinch goes back _very slowly_ (>2 seconds).
- **Some Dehydration (Needs Plan B):** Two or more of the following signs: Restless/irritable; Sunken eyes; Drinks eagerly/thirsty; Skin pinch goes back _slowly_.
- **No Dehydration (Needs Plan A):** Not enough signs to classify as some or severe dehydration.

**IV. MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES (SHORT-TERM)**

- **Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT):** The sheet anchor of treatment.
    - _Reduced Osmolarity ORS:_ Recommended by WHO/UNICEF to reduce stool output and vomiting.
    - _Composition (mOsm/L):_ Sodium (75), Chloride (65), Glucose, anhydrous (75), Potassium (20), Citrate (10). Total osmolarity = 245 mOsm/L.
- **Intravenous Rehydration (Plan C):** Indicated for severe dehydration or shock.
    - _Fluid of choice:_ Ringer's Lactate (Hartmann's solution). If unavailable, normal saline can be used but is inferior.
    - _Dosage:_ 100 ml/kg body weight.
    - _Schedule for infants (<12 months):_ 30 ml/kg in 1 hour, then 70 ml/kg in 5 hours.
    - _Schedule for children (>12 months):_ 30 ml/kg in 30 minutes, then 70 ml/kg in 2.5 hours.
- **Zinc Supplementation:** Reduces duration and severity of the episode and prevents future episodes for 2-3 months. Dose: 10 mg/day for infants <6 months; 20 mg/day for children >6 months, given for 10-14 days.
- **Appropriate Feeding:** "Resting the gut" is a mistaken assumption; normal food and exclusive breast-feeding must continue during diarrhoea to prevent malnutrition.
- **Chemotherapy / Drugs NOT to use:** Antibiotics are generally NOT needed except for specific cases (cholera, shigella, amoebiasis). Purgatives, steroids, neomycin, charcoal, bismuth, and motility inhibitors (like atropine/tincture opium) are dangerous and strictly contraindicated.

**V. PREVENTION AND CONTROL (LONG-TERM)**

- **MCH Care Practices:** Promotion of exclusive breast-feeding for the first 6 months, appropriate weaning practices, and maternal nutrition.
- **Immunization:** Measles vaccination (can prevent up to 25% of diarrhoeal deaths in under-5 children) and Rotavirus vaccination.
- **Vitamin A Supplementation:** Reduces diarrhoeal mortality and complications.
- **WASH Interventions:** Safe disposal of human excreta, safe drinking water supply, and promoting hand-washing with soap before preparing food and after defecation.
