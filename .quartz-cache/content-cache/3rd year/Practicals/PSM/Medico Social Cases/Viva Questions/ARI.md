**ACUTE RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS (ARI) VIVA: HIGH-YIELD EXAM NOTES**

**I. DEFINITION AND BURDEN OF DISEASE**

- **Definition:** Acute respiratory infection (ARI) is an inflammation of the respiratory tract anywhere from the nose to the alveoli, presenting with a wide range of symptoms.
- **Classification:** Clinically divided into Acute Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (AURI) (e.g., common cold, pharyngitis, otitis media) and Acute Lower Respiratory Tract Infection (ALRI) (e.g., epiglottitis, bronchitis, pneumonia).
- **Public Health Importance:** Pneumonia continues to kill more children under five worldwide than any other single infectious disease. In India, ARI is a major cause of death in states with high infant and child mortality rates, and a leading cause of paediatric hospital admissions.

**II. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS**

- **Agent Factors:**
    - _Viruses:_ Respiratory syncytial virus (most common viral cause in infants), rhinoviruses, adenoviruses, influenza, and measles.
    - _Bacteria:_ _Streptococcus pneumoniae_ (most common bacterial cause, 78% of lobar pneumonia), _Haemophilus influenzae_ type b (Hib) (second most common), _Bordetella pertussis_, and _Staphylococcus aureus_.
- **Host Factors:**
    - _Age:_ Illness rates are highest in young infants and children. Case fatality rates are highest in young infants and malnourished children.
    - _Gender:_ Under 3 years of age, boys are affected more often and more severely.
- **Environmental & Social Risk Factors:** Overcrowded dwellings, intense indoor smoke pollution (use of solid fuels), poor nutrition, low birth weight, lack of ventilation, and low socio-economic status. Maternal cigarette smoking is linked to increased ARI in the first year of life.

**III. CLINICAL ASSESSMENT OF A CHILD WITH COUGH/DIFFICULT BREATHING (IMNCI GUIDELINES)** During the viva, examiners strictly look for the IMNCI assessment criteria.

- **1. Count the respiratory rate (Fast Breathing cut-offs):**
    - **Less than 2 months:** $\ge$ 60 breaths per minute. _(Note: Always repeat the count if it is $\ge$ 60 because infant breathing can be erratic)._
    - **2 months up to 12 months:** $\ge$ 50 breaths per minute.
    - **12 months up to 5 years:** $\ge$ 40 breaths per minute.
- **2. Look for Chest Indrawing:** Lower chest wall goes IN when the child breathes IN.
- **3. Look and Listen for Stridor or Wheeze:** Stridor is a harsh noise when breathing IN; wheezing is a soft whistling noise when breathing OUT.
- **4. Check for General Danger Signs:** Not able to drink/breastfeed, persistent vomiting, convulsions, abnormally sleepy/lethargic, or severe malnutrition.

**IV. CLASSIFICATION AND MANAGEMENT (REVISED IMNCI GUIDELINES)** The revised WHO/IMNCI guidelines simplified the classification for children aged 2 months to 59 months into two main categories of pneumonia.

- **1. Cough and Cold (No Pneumonia):**
    - _Signs:_ No fast breathing, no chest indrawing, no danger signs.
    - _Treatment:_ Home care advice. NO antibiotics. (Antibiotics are not effective for viral colds and increase resistance).
- **2. Pneumonia:**
    - _Signs:_ Fast breathing AND/OR chest indrawing. _(Note: Previously, chest indrawing was "severe pneumonia", but the revised guidelines merged it here)._
    - _Treatment:_ Oral Amoxicillin (at least 40 mg/kg/dose twice daily for 5 days) + Home care advice.
- **3. Severe Pneumonia or Very Severe Disease:**
    - _Signs:_ General danger signs present.
    - _Treatment:_ First dose of appropriate antibiotic (parenteral ampicillin/penicillin and gentamicin) + URGENT referral to a facility.
- **Young Infants (< 2 months):** Suspected pneumonia always requires hospitalization. Treat with Intramuscular Benzyl Penicillin or Ampicillin + Gentamicin.

**V. PREVENTION STRATEGIES (GAPPD)** The Integrated Global Action Plan for Pneumonia and Diarrhoea (GAPPD) focuses on:

- **Protect:** Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, adequate complementary feeding, Vitamin A supplementation.
- **Prevent:** Immunization (Measles, Hib, Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine [PCV], DPT, Rotavirus), reduction of household air pollution, hand-washing with soap, and safe sanitation.
- **Treat:** Case management at health facilities and community level with appropriate antibiotics and oxygen.

**PROBABLE PSM VIVA QUESTIONS & ANSWERS**

**Q1: What are the IMNCI cut-offs for diagnosing "fast breathing" in children?** _Answer:_ The cut-offs depend on age. For children less than 2 months, it is 60 or more breaths/min. For 2 months to 12 months, it is 50 or more breaths/min. For 12 months to 5 years, it is 40 or more breaths/min.

**Q2: What is the significance of "chest indrawing" in a child with cough, and how does the revised IMNCI guideline classify it?** _Answer:_ Chest indrawing occurs when the lower chest wall goes in during inspiration, indicating increased respiratory effort. In the revised IMNCI guidelines, chest indrawing without general danger signs is classified simply as "Pneumonia" and can be treated with oral Amoxicillin on an outpatient basis. Previously, it was classified as "Severe Pneumonia" requiring injectable antibiotics.

**Q3: Which vaccines in the National Immunization Schedule directly or indirectly prevent pneumonia?** _Answer:_

1. _Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine (PCV):_ Directly prevents S. pneumoniae infections.
2. _Pentavalent Vaccine (contains Hib & DPT):_ Prevents Haemophilus influenzae type b pneumonia, pertussis, and diphtheria.
3. _Measles/MR Vaccine:_ Prevents measles, for which pneumonia is a highly fatal complication.

**Q4: A mother brings a 3-year-old child with a cough and running nose. The respiratory rate is 35/min. There is no chest indrawing or danger signs. How will you manage?** _Answer:_ This is classified as "Cough and Cold (No Pneumonia)". I will not prescribe antibiotics as it is likely viral. I will advise the mother on symptomatic home care, keeping the child warm, continuing feeding, and tell her the warning signs to return immediately if the child's breathing becomes fast or difficult.

**Q5: What are the General Danger Signs you must assess in every sick child under IMNCI?** _Answer:_ I must check if the child is able to drink or breastfeed, if the child vomits everything, if the child has had convulsions, and if the child is lethargic or unconscious.

**Q6: What is the drug of choice for treating Pneumonia under the revised guidelines at the sub-centre/PHC level?** _Answer:_ Oral Amoxicillin in dispersible tablet form. The dose is at least 40 mg/kg/dose twice daily for five days.

**Q7: How does indoor air pollution contribute to ARI burden?** _Answer:_ Exposure to indoor smoke from inefficient solid fuels almost doubles the risk for childhood pneumonia. Over half of the deaths among children under 5 from acute lower respiratory infections are attributable to particulate matter inhaled from indoor air pollution.
