**PROBABLE VIVA QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS FOR A PNC CASE PRESENTATION**

**Q1. What is the definition and what are the primary objectives of Postnatal Care (PNC)?**

- **Definition:** Care of the mother and the newborn after delivery is known as postnatal or postpartal care. Broadly, this care falls into the combined responsibility of the obstetrician and the paediatrician (perinatology).
- **Objectives:**
    1. To prevent complications of the postpartal period.
    2. To provide care for the rapid restoration of the mother to optimum health.
    3. To check the adequacy of breast-feeding.
    4. To provide family planning services.
    5. To provide basic health education to the mother and family.

**Q2. What are the common maternal complications you will look for during the puerperium?**

- **Puerperal Sepsis:** Infection of the genital tract within 3 weeks after delivery. It is accompanied by a rise in temperature and pulse rate, foul-smelling lochia, and pain and tenderness in the lower abdomen.
- **Secondary Haemorrhage:** Bleeding from the vagina any time from 6 hours after delivery to the end of the puerperium (6 weeks). It may be due to retained placenta or membranes.
- **Thrombophlebitis:** Infection of the veins of the leg, rendering the leg tender, pale, and swollen.
- **Others:** Urinary tract infections and mastitis.

**Q3. What is the recommended schedule for postnatal check-ups and what specific physical examinations are conducted?**

- **Schedule:** Health check-ups must be frequent, i.e., twice a day during the first 3 days, and subsequently once a day till the umbilical cord drops off. Further visits should be done once a month during the first 6 months. At the end of 6 weeks, an examination is necessary to check the complete involution of the uterus.
- **Specific Checks:** At each examination, the health worker checks temperature, pulse, respiration, and the breasts. They must check the progress of normal involution of the uterus, look for any abnormality in the lochia, check urine and bowels, and advise on perineal toilet including care of the stitches (if any). Routine haemoglobin examination should also be done to check for anaemia.

**Q4. What is the schedule of home visits by ASHA under the Home Based Newborn Care (HBNC) scheme?** ASHA makes specified visits to all newborns up to 42 days of life.

- **For Institutional Delivery:** 6 visits on Day 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, and 42.
- **For Home Delivery:** 7 visits on Day 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, and 42.
- **For Caesarean Section:** ASHA visits start from Day 7 to Day 42 (when the mother returns home after the institutional stay).

**Q5. What advice will you give to a lactating mother regarding Family Planning?**

- **Timing of Sterilization:** Postpartum sterilization is generally recommended on the 2nd day after delivery.
- **Spacing Methods & Lactation:** While lactation confers some protection against conception, it cannot be depended upon once menstruation returns.
- **Contraindicated Methods:** Combined or sequential oral "pills" should be strictly avoided in a lactating mother as they suppress lactation.

**Q6. What are the essential components of immediate Newborn Care that you must ensure or inquire about?**

- **Clearing the Airway:** Establishing cardio-respiratory functions immediately after birth.
- **Apgar Score:** Taken at 1 minute and 5 minutes after birth to estimate the physical condition of the baby.
- **Care of the Cord:** Cut and tied under aseptic conditions when it has stopped pulsating.
- **Care of the Eyes:** Wiping the lid margins with sterile wet swabs from inner to outer side.
- **Maintenance of Body Temperature:** The baby should be quickly dried, wrapped in clean dry cloth, and given to the mother for skin-to-skin contact to prevent hypothermia.
- **Breast-feeding:** Should be initiated within an hour of birth.

**Q7. What dietary and nutritional advice is given to a postnatal mother?**

- **Caloric & Protein Requirement:** A lactating mother requires an additional 600 kcal daily during the first 6 months and +520 kcal during the next 6 months over her normal requirements. She also requires an additional 17.0 g of protein daily in the first 6 months.
- **IFA Supplementation:** Under the Anaemia Mukt Bharat strategy, 180 Iron and Folic Acid (IFA) Red tablets are provided to lactating women during the postnatal period.
- **General Advice:** A malnourished mother secretes milk at the cost of her own health; therefore, her nutritional needs must be adequately met.

**Q8. What psychological problems can a mother face during the postnatal period?**

- The mother may experience emotional disturbances, fear, timidity, and insecurity regarding the baby.
- She requires the support and companionship of her husband to adapt cheerfully.
- Postpartum psychosis is rare but can be precipitated by birth.

**Q9. What are the Danger Signs in a young infant (up to 2 months) that warrant urgent referral under IMNCI guidelines?** The mother should be counselled to seek care immediately if the newborn shows:

- Not feeding well or inability to feed.
- Convulsions.
- Fast breathing (60 breaths per minute or more) or severe chest indrawing.
- Fever (37.5°C or above) or low body temperature (less than 35.5°C).
- Movement only when stimulated or no movement at all.
- Umbilicus red or draining pus, or skin pustules.
