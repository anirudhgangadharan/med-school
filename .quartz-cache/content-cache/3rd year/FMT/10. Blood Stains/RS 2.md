1. Confirmatory test for blood.
### CONFIRMATORY TESTS FOR BLOOD

While preliminary chemical tests (like Benzidine or Kastle-Meyer) only indicate the _possible_ presence of blood, confirmatory tests are legally and medically required to definitively prove that a stain is blood.

**I. MICROSCOPIC EXAMINATION**

- **Principle:** Direct microscopic visualization of intact red blood cells (RBCs).
- **Morphology:** Human RBCs are identified as circular, biconcave, non-nucleated discs.
- 🚨 **Exam/Clinical Limitation:** This method is strictly useful only when the stains are fresh or when a wet blood clot is available; it is completely useless for old, dried, or disintegrated stains.

**II. MICROCHEMICAL CRYSTAL TESTS (🥇 High-Yield)** These tests are based on the chemical property of the haeme (iron) portion of the haemoglobin molecule to form characteristic, coloured crystals when reacted with specific chemical reagents.

- **1. Haemin Crystal Test (Teichmann's Test)**
    
    - **Reagents:** A small crystal of **sodium chloride (NaCl)** + 2 to 3 drops of **glacial acetic acid**.
    - **Procedure:** Placed on a glass slide, covered, and gently heated over a small flame until the acid evaporates.
    - **Positive Result:** The formation of faint **yellowish-red to brownish-black rhombic crystals** of haemin (haematin chloride), arranged singly or in clusters.
    - _Drawbacks (Causes of False Negatives):_ The test frequently fails if the stain is very old, washed, chemically treated, overheated, or if there is excess salt or moisture in the acid.
- **2. Haemochromogen Crystal Test (Takayama Test)**
    
    - **Reagents:** Takayama Reagent (a mixture of **sodium hydroxide, pyridine, and glucose**).
    - **Positive Result:** The formation of **pink, feathery crystals** of haemochromogen (reduced alkaline haematin), characteristically arranged in sheaves or clusters within 1 to 6 minutes.
    - 🥇 **Medicolegal Pearl:** The Takayama test is universally considered **more delicate and reliable** than Teichmann's test because it consistently yields excellent results even with very old blood stains.

**III. SPECTROSCOPIC EXAMINATION (The Ultimate Gold Standard)**

- **Principle:** This utilizes a spectroscope to pass light through a blood extract. The blood pigment absorbs specific rays from the light spectrum, producing characteristic dark absorption bands that vary depending on the exact type of haemoglobin derivative present (e.g., Oxyhaemoglobin, Reduced haemoglobin, Carboxyhaemoglobin).
- **Diagnostic Value:** It is considered the **most delicate and reliable test** for detecting the presence of blood.
- **Sensitivity & Utility:** It is exceptionally highly sensitive, requiring less than **0.1 mg** of blood to yield a positive result. It is especially valuable because it works flawlessly on both recent and very old stains.
1. Takayama test.
### TAKAYAMA TEST (HAEMOCHROMOGEN CRYSTAL TEST)

**I. PURPOSE & PRINCIPLE**

- **Objective:** It is a highly reliable microchemical test used to confirm the presence of **blood** in suspected biological stains.
- **Mechanism:** The test relies on a chemical reaction that converts the blood pigment into characteristic coloured crystals of **haemochromogen** (also known as reduced alkaline haematin).

**II. REAGENTS & PROCEDURE**

- **Takayama Reagent:** A specific mixture consisting of **sodium hydroxide**, **pyridine**, and **glucose**.
- **Methodology:**
    1. A small piece of the suspected stained material is placed directly on a glass slide.
    2. 2 to 3 drops of the Takayama reagent are added to the stain, and it is covered with a coverslip.
    3. _Clinical Pearl:_ Applying slight heat (warming the slide) hastens the chemical reaction.

**III. INTERPRETATION OF RESULTS (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Positive Result:** The formation of **pink, feathery crystals** of haemochromogen.
- **Morphology:** These crystals are characteristically arranged in clusters or sheaves.
- **Timing:** A positive reaction is usually rapid, with crystals appearing in **1 to 6 minutes**.
- **Negative Result:** The test is only considered definitively negative if no crystals are formed after **30 minutes** of observation.

**IV. MEDICOLEGAL ADVANTAGES (🚨 Exam Focus)**

- **Reliability:** It is considered a delicate and **more reliable** test compared to other microchemical alternatives (like the Teichmann's test).
- **Forensic Utility:** Its greatest advantage in criminal investigations is that it yields excellent, positive results even when applied to **very old blood stains**.
1. Blood group systems.
### BLOOD GROUP SYSTEMS AND GENETIC MARKERS

**I. OVERVIEW & BASIC PRINCIPLES**

- **Definitions:** Blood groups are determined by the presence of specific **antigens (agglutinogens)** on the red blood cell membrane, while **antibodies (agglutinins)** are present in the blood serum.
- **Development:** Red cell antigens appear early in foetal life, whereas isoagglutinins appear in the latter half of antenatal life.
- **Classification:** The blood group systems currently utilized in forensic serology and clinical practice include: (1) Red cell antigens, (2) White cell antigens, (3) Serum protein polymorphisms, and (4) Red cell enzyme polymorphisms.

---

**II. 🥇 HIGH-YIELD RED CELL ANTIGEN SYSTEMS**

**1. The ABO System (Discovered by Landsteiner in 1930)**

- **Genetics:** Inherited via three allelomorphic genes (A, B, and O) resulting in six genotypes (AA, AO, BB, BO, AB, OO) and four phenotypes (A, B, AB, O).
- **Dominance:** A and B are dominant over O; A and B are co-dominant with each other.
- **Clinical Pearl:** Group O is the universal donor, and Group AB is the universal recipient.
- 🚨 **Exam Target (Bombay Phenotype):** Rare individuals possess the "hh" genotype; they completely lack A, B, and H antigens on their erythrocytes and in their secretions.

**2. The Rhesus (Rh) System**

- **Antigens:** A complex of six main antigens designated as Cc, Dd, and Ee.
- **Population:** About 90% of Indians possess the Rh factor (Rh-positive).
- **Antibody Production:** Unlike the ABO system, Rh antibodies **do not** occur naturally in human serum; they are produced only following exposure to foreign Rh antigens, such as via mismatched blood transfusion or during pregnancy.

**3. The MNSs and P Systems**

- **MNSs System:** M and N agglutinogens are distinct from ABO, are inherited as Mendelian dominants, and are feebly antigenic. Phenotypes include MS, NS, MNS, MNs, MSs, NSs, and NNSs.
- **P System:** Present in about 75% of persons but is considered weak and medicolegally unimportant.

---

**III. OTHER GENETIC POLYMORPHISM SYSTEMS**

**1. White Cell Antigens (HLA System)**

- **Genetics:** The major human leucocyte antigens (HLA-A, B, C, D, and DR) are controlled by the Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) situated on the short arm of chromosome 6.
- **Forensic Utility:** The HLA system possesses an extremely high degree of polymorphism (over 125 multiple alleles across seven loci), providing an excellent discriminating capacity for resolving disputed parentage.

**2. Serum Protein & Red Cell Enzyme Polymorphisms**

- **Serum Proteins:** Include Gm and Inv types of gamma globulins (useful for resolving mix-ups of babies in hospitals because newborn IgG is maternally transferred), haptoglobins (Hp), transferrins, and group-specific components (Gc).
- **Red Cell Enzymes:** Include Phosphoglucomutase (PGM), Adenylate kinase (AK), and Adenosine deaminase (ADA), which are of established value in paternity testing but do not persist for more than one month in dried blood stains.

---

**IV. 🚨 THE "SECRETOR" STATUS (Highly Tested Concept)**

- **Definition:** In about 80% of individuals, the ABO agglutinogens appear in a **water-soluble form** and can be demonstrated in all body fluids (e.g., semen, saliva, sweat, tears, vaginal secretions) **except** the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).
- **Genetics:** Secreting capacity is controlled by dominant genes (_SeSe_ or _Sese_). Non-secretors (_sese_) possess these antigens only in a lipoidal form confined to red cells and tissues.
- **Medicolegal Application:** Allows forensic experts to determine the blood group of a suspect from seminal stains left in rape cases, or salivary stains found on cigarette butts and bite marks.

---

**V. MEDICOLEGAL IMPORTANCE OF BLOOD GROUPS**

- **Disputed Paternity:** Blood grouping strictly follows Mendelian inheritance. An antigen cannot appear in a child unless it is present in at least one parent. It acts as a **rule of exclusion**:
    - _First-Order Exclusion:_ The child has a gene absent in both parents.
    - _Second-Order Exclusion:_ A parent is homozygous for a trait, but that trait is absent in the child.
    - _Note:_ Blood grouping can only indicate probabilities or exclude a man; it cannot absolutely prove paternity (unlike DNA fingerprinting).
- **Criminal Investigation:** Blood stains found on a suspect's clothing, weapons, or under fingernails can be matched to the victim, establishing an association. It is also critical in hit-and-run accidents to link the vehicle to the victim.
1. Paternity tests.
### PATERNITY TESTS (DISPUTED PATERNITY)

**I. MEDICOLEGAL INDICATIONS** Paternity tests are frequently ordered by courts to resolve civil and criminal disputes involving the biological fatherhood of a child. High-yield scenarios include:

- **Legitimacy Contests:** A husband denying fatherhood of a child born during lawful wedlock.
- **Affiliation Cases:** An unmarried mother accusing a specific man to claim financial maintenance for the child.
- **Supposititious (Fictitious) Children:** A woman falsely pretending pregnancy and delivery, producing a child as her own to illegally claim inheritance.
- **Posthumous Births:** Claims of inheritance for a child born after the death of the alleged father.
- **Mix-ups:** Accidental interchange of newborn babies in maternity hospitals.

---

**II. TESTING MODALITIES (🥇 HIGH-YIELD FOR EXAMS)**

**1. DNA Fingerprinting / Profiling (The Gold Standard)**

- **Principle:** A child inherits exactly 50% of its DNA from the mother and 50% from the biological father.
- **Methodology:** The DNA bar codes (bands) of the child are first matched with the mother's DNA pattern. All remaining unmatched bands in the child's profile _must_ perfectly match the patterns of the putative (alleged) father.
- 🚨 **Medicolegal Pearl:** DNA analysis is the _only_ test that can positively **fix (establish)** or **exclude** paternity with **100% certainty**.

**2. Serological Tests (Blood Grouping)**

- **Principle:** Blood groups (ABO, MNS, Rh, Kell, Duffy, Kidd) are inherited according to strict Mendelian principles. A specific blood group antigen cannot appear in a child unless it was present in one or both parents.
- **Diagnostic Limitation:** Blood grouping is strictly an **exclusionary test**. It can completely exonerate a falsely accused man, but it **cannot definitely establish** paternity (it only indicates possibility/probability).
- **Rules of Exclusion:**
    - _First-Order Exclusion:_ The child possesses a blood group gene that is absent in _both_ the mother and the putative father.
    - _Second-Order Exclusion:_ The putative father is homozygous for a blood group gene, but that specific gene is completely absent in the child.
- _Exclusion Rates:_ Relying on the ABO system alone excludes paternity in only 18% of cases. Adding MNSs raises it to 60%, and incorporating red cell enzymes (e.g., PGM, AK) and serum proteins (e.g., Haptoglobins, Gm types) pushes the exclusion rate to 94%.

**3. HLA (Human Leukocyte Antigen) Typing**

- **Principle:** The HLA system (white cell antigens) is determined by the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) and possesses a very high degree of polymorphism.
- **Efficacy:** Because of its multiple alleles, it provides an excellent discriminating capacity, resolving disputes with a 98.5% exclusion rate.

**4. Physical & Morphological Traits (Weak Corroborative Evidence)**

- **Parental Likeness & Atavism:** Atavism occurs when a child does not physically resemble its parents but strongly resembles its grandparents due to the inheritance of recessive genes.
- **Developmental Defects:** Tracing the transmission of congenital deformities or hereditary diseases from parent to child.

---

**III. 🚨 CLINICAL & PROCEDURAL PITFALLS**

- **Blood Transfusion Rule:** Neither the parents nor the child should have received a blood transfusion within the **three months** prior to taking the sample, as this invalidates both serological and DNA results.
- **Infant Age:** The child should preferably be at least 6 months of age, and strictly not less than 2 months old before serological testing is performed.
- **Standardization:** To ensure legal admissibility, the testing of the mother, child, and putative father must be conducted in the same laboratory, on the exact same day, by the same person, using the same batch of reagents.
- **DNA Mutations:** Natural gene mutations in a child (non-inherited germline mutations) can occasionally yield false-negative results in DNA paternity testing.
1. HLA typing.
### HLA (HUMAN LEUCOCYTE ANTIGEN) TYPING

**I. DEFINITION & GENETICS**

- **Concept:** The HLA system consists of protein substances (antigens) located on the surface of white blood cells, platelets, tumours, and a wide variety of human tissues and organs.
- **Genetic Locus (🥇 High-Yield):** The major antigens of this system (HLA-A, B, C, D, and DR) are determined by a single chromosomal segment called the **Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC)**, which is situated on the **short arm of chromosome 6**.
- **Inheritance:** Every child inherits exactly one of the two chromosomes containing the HLA region from each parent.

**II. CHARACTERISTICS OF THE HLA SYSTEM**

- **Extreme Polymorphism:** The HLA system possesses a very high degree of polymorphism. There are 68 antigenic factors present on white cells and seven known multiple loci with approximately 125 multiple alleles.
- Because of this massive diversity, the system provides an extremely high discriminating capacity between individuals.

**III. MEDICOLEGAL & CLINICAL APPLICATIONS (🚨 Exam Focus)** **1. Disputed Paternity (Parentage Testing)**

- Because of its immense polymorphism, HLA typing is a powerful tool in affiliation cases and resolving disputed paternity.
- Testing a number of HLA systems yields a definitive **paternity exclusion rate of 95% to 98.5%**.
- _Clinical Pearl:_ It is mandatory that **only fresh blood samples** are examined when solving parentage problems using this method.

**2. Tissue and Organ Transplantation**

- HLA typing is clinically vital for homografts and live organ donations. The closer the HLA match between the donor and the recipient, the significantly higher the chances of graft survival and tolerance.

**IV. LABORATORY DETECTION**

- The presence of these antigens is detected by mixing the patient's white blood cells with a suitable antiserum.
- If the specific antigen is present, the antiserum will either agglutinate the white cells or damage them so that they readily absorb specific stains. Other components can be detected by observing characteristic swelling reactions in lymphocytes.
1. DNA fingerprinting / DNA typing (applications and limitations).
### DNA FINGERPRINTING (DNA TYPING / PROFILING)

**I. OVERVIEW & PRINCIPLE**

- **Discovery:** Developed by Dr. Alec Jeffreys in 1985.
- **Scientific Basis:** While 5% of human DNA codes for proteins, the remaining 95% is "junk DNA" containing short, repeating sequences of bases. These hypervariable regions, known as **Variable Number Tandem Repeats (VNTRs)** or minisatellites, are completely unique to each individual (except identical twins).
- **Uniqueness:** The chance of two people having the exact same DNA sequence is approximately 1 in a million billion.

---

**II. MEDICOLEGAL APPLICATIONS (🥇 High-Yield for Exams)** DNA fingerprinting is the modern gold standard for biological identification and is utilized in the following forensic scenarios:

- **1. Disputed Paternity and Maternity:** The most common application, accounting for about 50% of DNA typing cases in India. A child inherits exactly 50% of its DNA from the mother and 50% from the father.
- **2. Sexual Offences:** Seminal DNA recovered from vaginal swabs, skin, or clothing of the victim is directly matched with the DNA profile of the suspect. It is also highly effective in linking a serial rapist to multiple crime scenes.
- **3. Identification of Unknown Remains:** Crucial for identifying mutilated, decomposed, skeletonised, or burnt bodies in mass disasters (e.g., bomb blasts, air crashes). Extracted DNA is compared against previous records or profiles of close blood relatives.
- **4. Child Swapping:** Resolving cases of accidental or intentional exchange of newborn babies in hospitals.
- **5. Extortion & Trace Evidence:** Analyzing DNA from microscopic trace evidence such as saliva on envelopes, face masks, or cigarette butts.
- **6. Hit-and-Run Accidents:** Matching the victim's blood or tissue found on the suspect's vehicle.
- **7. Sex Determination:** Testing the amelogenin locus can determine the sex of the specimen source (shows one band in females, two bands in males).
- **8. Exoneration:** Definitively clearing falsely accused individuals of crimes.

---

**III. LIMITATIONS & PROBLEMS (🚨 Exam Pitfalls)** Despite its high accuracy, DNA typing has distinct limitations that are frequently tested:

- **Monozygotic Twins:** Routine DNA profiling **cannot** discriminate between identical (uniovular) twins, as they share the exact same genetic code. Differentiating them requires highly specialized sequence analysis of bone marrow-derived B lymphocytes.
- **Sample Degradation & Contamination:** Improper preservation or storage can cause the DNA molecule to fragment. Furthermore, contamination of the biological sample with bacteria rapidly degrades the DNA.
- **Genetic Mutations:** Spontaneous gene mutations (non-inherited germline mutations) in a child can occasionally alter the band pattern, potentially yielding false exclusions in paternity testing.
- **Subjectivity & Interpretation:** The band patterns produced on an autoradiograph can sometimes be faint or have poor resolution. Unlike traditional fingerprints which can be visually enlarged and compared by a judge, DNA profiles are microscopic and complex; thus, the court must rely entirely on the "trust and faith" of the expert witness's subjective interpretation.
- **Lack of Standardization:** There is a lack of universal international guidelines, meaning different laboratories may use different control measures and standardization methods, which occasionally leads to mistakes.
