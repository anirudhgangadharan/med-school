### EXTERNAL POSTMORTEM FINDINGS IN BURNS

**1. General Appearance & Posture (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Pugilistic Attitude (Boxer's/Fencing Posture):** The body assumes a universal flexion posture—hips, knees, elbows, and wrists are flexed, and fingers are hooked like claws.
    - _Pathophysiology:_ Caused by the heat-induced coagulation, desiccation, and contraction of muscle proteins. Because flexor muscles are bulkier than extensors, they contract more forcefully.
    - 🚨 _Exam Pearl:_ This is a postmortem physical effect of heat and occurs whether the person was alive or dead at the time of burning; it does **not** indicate a vital reaction.
- **Facial Features:** The face is swollen, bloated, and distorted. The tongue frequently protrudes due to tissue contraction of the neck and face. Blood-tinged froth may exude from the mouth and nose (indicating pulmonary oedema from inhaling hot, irritating fumes).

**2. Skin & Soft Tissue Changes**

- **Heat Ruptures:** Severe heat causes the skin and soft tissues to contract and split, producing linear tears over fleshy areas (calves, thighs, head).
    - _Differential Diagnosis:_ They mimic traumatic lacerated or incised wounds, but can be distinguished by the absence of bleeding, intact blood vessels and nerves bridging the floor, and lack of vital reaction at the margins.
- **Blisters (Vesication):**
    - _Antemortem:_ Raised domes with a red, hyperaemic base, containing serous fluid rich in proteins (albumin), chlorides, and cells,.
    - _Postmortem:_ Pale-yellow blisters containing mostly air and thin clear fluid, with a hard, dry, yellow base.
- **Line of Redness (Vital Reaction):** In antemortem burns, a 5-20 mm zone of hyperaemia/erythema surrounds the necrotic burnt area.
- **Glove-like Desquamation:** The skin of the hands may detach like a glove due to fluid deposition between skin layers.

**3. Hair & Clothing**

- **Hair:** Singed, burnt, curled, or twisted,. In lesser degrees of heat, the keratin melts and cools, producing a clubbed or bulbous appearance at the tips,.
- **Clothing:** Synthetic fabrics (nylon, polyester) melt and stick tightly to the skin. Unburnt portions should be preserved to check for the characteristic odour of accelerants (kerosene, petrol).

---

### INTERNAL POSTMORTEM FINDINGS IN BURNS

Because internal tissues are poor conductors of heat, the viscera are frequently well-preserved even in severely charred bodies.

**1. Cranial Cavity & Brain (🚨 Exam Target)**

- **Heat Haematoma (Extradural):** A soft, friable, light-chocolate or pink (if COHb is present) blood clot found in the extradural space, typically over the parietotemporal region.
    - _Distinguishing Feature:_ It has a characteristic **honeycomb appearance** due to steam bubbles boiling from the diploic space. It lacks associated blunt trauma signs.
- **Thermal Skull Fractures:** Intense heat causes the outer table of the skull to crack in a radiating, stellate, or criss-cross pattern,,. Unlike traumatic fractures, thermal fractures typically do not cross un-united suture lines.
- **Brain:** Often shrunken, firm, and yellow to light-brown due to a "cooking" or heat-coagulation effect.

**2. Respiratory System (The Gold Standard for Antemortem Proof)**

- **Soot Particles:** The presence of black carbon (soot) particles in the nose, mouth, larynx, trachea, and extending down into the terminal bronchioles is **absolute proof** that the victim was actively breathing during the fire.
- **Airways:** The mucosa is thickened, congested, and covered with frothy mucus; acute oedema of the glottis and larynx is common from inhaling superheated air.
- **Lungs:** Heavy, airless, consolidated, and markedly oedematous.

**3. Cardiovascular System & Blood**

- **Cherry-Red Blood:** The blood, tissues, and internal organs show a characteristic cherry-red discolouration if the Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb) level exceeds 10%,,.
- **Heart:** Usually filled with clotted blood. Petechial haemorrhages are frequently found in the pleurae, pericardium, and endocardium.

**4. Gastrointestinal Tract & Kidneys**

- **Curling's Ulcers:** Acute stress ulcers (sharply punched-out mucosal defects) frequently develop in the duodenum or stomach (typically the first part of the duodenum).
- **Kidneys:** Show cloudy swelling, capillary thrombosis, and the presence of heme casts in the medullary tubules (especially if >30% of the body surface is burnt).

### FILIGREE BURNS (LICHTENBERG'S FLOWERS)

**I. Definition & Appearance**

- **Synonyms:** Arborescent burns or Lichtenberg's figures/flowers.
- **Morphology:** Superficial, thin, irregular, and tortuous erythematous skin markings that characteristically resemble the branching pattern of a fern leaf or a tree,.
- **Location:** Most frequently found over the shoulders or flanks.

**II. Clinical Context & Pathophysiology**

- **Aetiology:** They are the pathognomonic external sign of a **lightning strike**,.
- **Mechanism:** The exact pathogenesis is debated, but they are believed to be caused by either:
    1. Direct electron showers induced by the lightning.
    2. Staining of tissues by haemoglobin from lysed RBCs along the current's path.
    3. Rupture of small superficial blood vessels producing patterned ecchymoses.
    4. Boiling of intercellular fluid along the fascial planes.

**III. Clinical Course & Medicolegal Pitfall**

- **Transient Nature:** These marks appear rapidly (within minutes to an hour) but are highly transient. If the victim survives, they fade and completely disappear within **1 to 2 days**,.
- 🚨 **Differential Diagnosis (High-Yield):** Filigree burns are frequently confused with postmortem **marbling** (a putrefactive change).
    - _Differentiation:_ Filigree burns are **brownish-black** and occur immediately. Marbling is **bluish-green** (due to sulphhaemoglobin) and occurs 36-48 hours after death,,.

### I. RULE OF WALLACE ("RULE OF NINES")

**I. DEFINITION & CLINICAL PURPOSE**

- **Concept:** Wallace's "Rule of Nines" is a standardized, rapid method used to calculate the percentage of **Total Body Surface Area (TBSA)** involved in burn injuries.
- **Clinical Utility:** It is essential for assessing the severity and prognosis of the burn victim, and acts as the foundation for calculating intravenous fluid replacement requirements.

**II. ANATOMICAL BREAKDOWN (🥇 Exam Target)** The body surface is divided into 11 regions, each representing **9%** (or a multiple of 9%) of the total body surface area, with the final **1%** assigned to the external genitalia.

- **Head and Face/Neck:** 9% _(Head 7%, Neck 2%)_.
- **Right Upper Limb:** 9% _(Arm 4%, Forearm 3%, Hand 2%)_.
- **Left Upper Limb:** 9%.
- **Front of Trunk:** 18% _(Front of Chest 9% + Front of Abdomen 9%)_.
- **Back of Trunk:** 18% _(Back of Chest 9% + Back of Abdomen 9%)_.
- **Right Lower Limb:** 18% _(Front 9% + Back 9%)_.
- **Left Lower Limb:** 18% _(Front 9% + Back 9%)_.
- **External Genitalia / Perineum:** 1%.
- **TOTAL:** 100%.

**III. 🚨 MEDICOLEGAL & PROGNOSTIC FATALITY RULE**

- Involvement of **50% or more** of the total body surface area is almost invariably fatal, even if the burns are only superficial (first-degree).
- Involvement of **30%** of the body surface area is generally sufficient to cause death, usually due to complications like shock or sepsis.
- _Alternative for scattered burns:_ The **Rule of Palm** estimates that the palmar surface of the patient's own hand is equivalent to approximately **1%** of their TBSA.

---

### II. CAUSES OF DEATH IN BURNS CASES

For exam purposes, causes of death in burns are strictly classified chronologically into Immediate/Early, Delayed, and Remote causes.

**A. IMMEDIATE & EARLY CAUSES (Within 0 to 48 Hours)**

- **1. Secondary (Hypovolaemic) Shock (🥇 High-Yield):** This is the leading cause of death within the first 48 hours, accounting for more than half of early fatalities. It results from a massive transudation and loss of fluid/plasma from the burned surfaces into the tissues. Circulatory collapse can occur with burns involving as little as 15% of the TBSA.
- **2. Asphyxia and Smoke Inhalation:** Death can occur rapidly from suffocation due to the inhalation of carbon monoxide (CO), carbon dioxide, cyanide, and non-specific toxic free radicals (which inactivate pulmonary surfactants).
- **3. Primary (Neurogenic) Shock:** Occurs rapidly within the first few hours due to intense pain, fear, and extreme fright.
- **4. Airway Obstruction:** Acute oedema of the glottis and massive pulmonary oedema can result from inhaling superheated air or toxic fumes.
- **5. Accidental Traumatic Injuries:** Sustained while attempting to escape the fire, such as injuries from falling masonry, timber, or jumping from heights.

**B. DELAYED CAUSES (Days to Weeks)**

- **1. Sepsis and Septicaemia:** This is the **most important and common cause of death** occurring 4 to 5 days or longer after the burn incident. Prolonged exposure of the denuded skin surface leads to fatal systemic infections such as bronchopneumonia, meningitis, and pericarditis. Secondary infections like pyaemia, gangrene, and tetanus may also supervene.
- **2. Acute Renal Failure:** Hypoperfusion from secondary shock leads to acute tubular necrosis (lower nephron nephrosis), which typically presents around the 3rd or 4th day.
- **3. Toxaemia:** Occurs due to the systemic absorption of toxic metabolites and breakdown products from the dead, burnt tissues (peaks around 3 to 4 days).
- **4. Gastrointestinal Disturbances (🚨 Exam Target - Curling's Ulcers):** Severe stress from prolonged bed rest and extensive burns frequently induces acute peptic ulceration in the duodenum (**Curling's ulcers**), leading to fatal gastrointestinal haemorrhage or stomach dilation.
- **5. Respiratory Complications:** Patients may develop Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) secondary to inhalation injury, or from restriction of respiratory excursions by a tight, leathery eschar.
- **6. Biochemical Disturbances:** Severe fluid shifts and tissue destruction can cause lethal electrolyte imbalances, particularly hypokalaemia.
- **7. Pulmonary Embolism:** Due to deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in the leg veins resulting from tissue damage and prolonged immobilisation.

**C. REMOTE CAUSES (Months to Years Later)**

- **1. Marjolin's Ulcer (🥇 Clinical Pearl):** Death can occur many years after recovery due to the malignant transformation of a chronic, unhealed burn scar into a squamous cell carcinoma.

### PUGILISTIC ATTITUDE (BOXER'S POSTURE)

**I. DEFINITION & SYNONYMS**

- **Synonyms:** Boxer's attitude, fencing attitude, defence attitude, or universal flexion.
- **Concept:** A characteristic posture naturally assumed by a body that has been exposed to prolonged, sustained, and intense heat or fire.

**II. CLASSIC PRESENTATION (MORPHOLOGY)** The body simulates the defensive stance of a boxer due to the universal flexion of the joints:

- **Upper Limbs:** Arms are flexed at the elbows and wrists, and held out in front of the body.
- **Lower Limbs:** Legs are flexed at the hips and the knees.
- **Hands:** All fingers are characteristically hooked like claws.
- **Spine & Head:** The head is slightly extended, and contraction of the paraspinal muscles frequently causes a marked backward arching of the spine (opisthotonus).

**III. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (MECHANISM) (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Protein Coagulation:** Exposure to extreme heat causes the tissues to desiccate (dehydrate) and carbonize on the surface. Simultaneously, the muscle proteins undergo heat-induced denaturation and coagulation.
- **Differential Muscle Bulk:** This protein coagulation forces the muscle fibers to severely contract and shorten. Because the flexor muscles of the body are much bulkier and stronger than the extensor muscles, they contract with significantly greater force, resulting in the universal flexion of all large joints.
- **Severity:** The degree and severity of these contractures directly depend on the intensity of the heat and the duration for which the heat was applied.

**IV. 🚨 MEDICOLEGAL PEARLS (Exam Pitfalls)**

- **Not a Vital Sign:** The pugilistic attitude is strictly a physical effect of heat and is **NOT** an antemortem sign of burns.
- **Alive vs. Dead:** This stiffening and posturing will occur completely regardless of whether the person was **alive or already dead** at the time they were exposed to the fire.
- **Misinterpretation:** It must not be mistakenly interpreted by investigators or courts as evidence that the victim was actively fighting an assailant or struggling to defend themselves at the moment of death.

### DIFFERENTIATING ANTEMORTEM VS. POSTMORTEM BURNS

Differentiating whether a victim was alive or dead when the fire started is a critical medicolegal challenge. The distinction relies on identifying signs of a **vital reaction** (tissue response to injury during life) and systemic evidence of smoke inhalation.

**I. LOCAL SKIN CHANGES (🥇 High-Yield Comparison)**

|Feature|Antemortem (AM) Burns|Postmortem (PM) Burns|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Line of Redness / Erythema**|**Present.** A 5-20 mm zone of hyperaemia (vital reaction) surrounds the burn. It involves the whole thickness of the true skin and persists after death.|**Absent.** The skin merely shows dried scorching resembling burnt paper.|
|**Blister Formation (Vesication)**|Raised dome containing **serous fluid rich in proteins (albumin) and chlorides**. The base is red, hyperaemic, inflamed, and shows swollen papillae.|Pale-yellow blisters containing mostly **gas/air and a thin, clear fluid** with only trace albumin. The base is dry, hard, and yellow.|
|**Histopathology (Vital Reaction)**|Marked cellular exudation, polymorphonuclear leucocyte infiltration, and reactive tissue changes.|No intravital reaction, no cellular exudation.|
|**Enzyme Histochemistry**|The peripheral zone shows an **increase in enzyme reactions** (e.g., esterases, ATP, leucine aminopeptidase, acid/alkaline phosphatase).|No increase in enzyme activity in the peripheral zone.|

**II. SYSTEMIC FINDINGS (🚨 The Gold Standards for Proof of Life)**

To definitively prove the victim was alive and actively breathing during the fire, examiners look for two absolute systemic proofs:

- **1. Soot in the Respiratory Tract:**
    - _Antemortem:_ The presence of black carbon (soot) particles embedded in frothy mucus in the larynx, trachea, and extending down into the **terminal bronchioles** is an absolute proof of life during the fire.
    - _Postmortem:_ Soot is absent deep in the airway; while passive percolation may allow soot into the mouth or back of the pharynx, it will **never** pass beyond the vocal cords.
- **2. Elevated Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb):**
    - _Antemortem:_ Elevated COHb levels (usually **>10%**, and up to 70-80% depending on the fire) impart a characteristic **cherry-red colour** to the blood, tissues, and internal organs. This indicates active inhalation of carbon monoxide produced by incomplete combustion.
    - _Postmortem:_ CO is not absorbed by a body after death, so COHb levels will not be significantly elevated purely from fire exposure.
    - _Note:_ The presence of other toxic gases in the blood, such as hydrogen cyanide (HCN), ammonia, and hydrogen sulphide, also strongly supports antemortem inhalation.

**III. EXAM PITFALLS (Common Distractors)**

- **Pugilistic Attitude (Boxer's Posture):** This universal flexion of the joints is caused by the heat-induced coagulation, dehydration, and contraction of muscle proteins. It is **not** a vital sign and occurs regardless of whether the person was alive or dead at the time of burning.
- **Heat Ruptures:** Severe heat causes the skin and soft tissues to contract and split, simulating incised or lacerated wounds (often over fleshy areas like calves and thighs). These are postmortem thermal artifacts and show intact blood vessels, irregular margins, and no vital reaction.

### DIFFERENTIATION: DRY HEAT BURNS, MOIST HEAT BURNS (SCALDS), AND CHEMICAL BURNS

**I. COMPARATIVE PATHOLOGY & MORPHOLOGY (🥇 High-Yield for Exams)**

|Feature|Dry Heat Burns|Moist Heat (Scalds)|Chemical Burns|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Causative Agent**|Flame, heated solid bodies, or X-rays.|Steam or hot liquids typically above 60°C.|Corrosive chemicals (strong acids or alkalis).|
|**Site of Injury**|Usually at and **above** the site of contact (due to flames and heat rising).|At and **below** the site of contact (due to gravity pulling liquid down).|At and **below** the site of contact.|
|**Splash / Trickle Marks**|**Absent**.|**Present** (classic lines of downward trickling).|**Present**.|
|**Skin Appearance**|Dry, wrinkled, or completely charred.|Sodden, bleached, and macerated.|Tissue is often destroyed; shows distinctive discolouration based on the chemical.|
|**Singeing of Hair**|**Present** (pathognomonic).|**Absent**.|**Absent**.|
|**Charring (Blackening)**|**Present**.|**Absent**.|May be present (e.g., from concentrated sulphuric acid).|
|**Vesicles (Blisters)**|Present, usually restricted to the **circumference/periphery** of the burnt area.|Distributed abundantly **over the entire** burnt area.|**Very rare**.|
|**Red Line of Demarcation**|**Present**.|**Present**.|**Absent**.|
|**Ulceration**|Absent initially.|Absent.|**Present** (corrosion and tissue sloughing).|
|**Scar Formation**|Thick and contracted (highly disfiguring).|Thin and much less contracted.|Thick and severely contracted.|

**II. 🚨 CLINICAL & MEDICOLEGAL PEARLS (Exam Targets)**

- **The "Singeing" Rule:** The single most reliable physical finding to differentiate a dry flame burn from a severe scald is the presence of singed body hair and carbon charring, which are completely absent in scald injuries.
- **The Clothing Paradox in Scalds:** While clothing is destroyed by dry heat, in scald injuries, intact clothing actually _worsens_ the tissue damage by trapping the hot liquid against the skin, prolonging the duration of contact, and preventing the natural cooling effect of evaporation.
- **Chemical Eschar Differentiation:**
    - **Acids:** Precipitate tissue proteins, producing a **coagulation necrosis** that presents as a clearly demarcated, dry, hard eschar.
    - **Alkalis:** Dissolve proteins and saponify fats, producing a **liquefaction necrosis** that permits deeper invasion, presenting as a soft, oedematous, mucilaginous, soapy slough.
1. Electrocution.
### ELECTROCUTION (ELECTRICAL INJURIES)

**I. PHYSICAL FACTORS & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY**

- **Nature of Current:** Alternating current (AC) is **4 to 5 times more dangerous** than an equal voltage of direct current (DC) because AC induces tetanic muscle spasms, preventing the victim from releasing the conductor.
- **Voltage & Amperage:** Domestic supply in India is typically 220–240 volts AC (50 cycles/sec). Currents over 60 mA are dangerous, and **100 mA is fatal**.
- **Resistance:** Dry skin offers high resistance. Moisture or sweat dramatically lowers skin resistance, allowing current to flow easily into the body.

**II. MECHANISM OF DEATH (🥇 High-Yield for Exams)** The cause of death strictly depends on the **path of the current** through the body:

- **1. Ventricular Fibrillation:** Occurs when the current passes through the heart (e.g., arm-to-arm, or left arm-to-leg circuit). This is the most common mechanism of fatal electrocution.
- **2. Respiratory Paralysis:** Occurs when the current involves the brainstem and upper cervical cord (e.g., head-to-limb circuit), causing central paralysis of the medullary respiratory centres.
- **3. Asphyxia:** Prolonged contact with low tension current may cause continuous tetanic spasm of the respiratory muscles (tetanization).

**III. EXTERNAL MORPHOLOGY (🚨 Exam Target)**

- **1. The Joule Burn (Electric Mark):**
    - The pathognomonic **entry wound** of electricity.
    - Appears as a round or oval shallow crater (1 to 3 cm) with a pale, depressed, **hard** floor.
    - Surrounded by an elevated ridge with a hyperaemic border, and a characteristic **areola of blanched (pale) skin** that survives death.
    - **Metallisation:** The transfer of volatilised metal ions from the conductor. Copper/brass conductors leave a **bright green** imprint.
- **2. Exit Wounds:** Often appear as irregular splits or tears in the skin where the current leaves the body. They lack the characteristic hardness, blanching, and metallisation of the entry mark.
- **3. Flash / Spark Burns:** High-tension currents may arc (jump) to the body without direct contact, producing discrete pitted burns or extensive thermal burns over large areas known as **"crocodile flash burns"**.
- **4. Absence of Marks:** Electrical burns may be completely absent if the contact area was broad, the voltage was low, or the skin was heavily moistened (e.g., electrocution in a bathtub).

**IV. HISTOPATHOLOGY OF JOULE BURN (🥇 Classic Exam Pearl)**

- **"Streaming of the Nuclei":** Epidermal cells undergo coagulation necrosis; their nuclei become fusiform, stretched, and hyperchromatic. They align in parallel rows at an acute or right angle to the dermis, producing a characteristic palisade appearance.
- **Microblisters:** Found in the horny layer, representing pathways where boiling tissue fluid (steam) exploded out of the skin.

**V. INTERNAL POSTMORTEM FINDINGS**

- **Cardiovascular:** Multiple **subepicardial and subendocardial petechial haemorrhages** are typically seen, being most prominent on the left ventricle.
- **Skeletal Muscle:** Muscles in the direct path of the current may show **Zenker's degeneration** (waxy hyaline necrosis).
- **Bone & Current Pearls:** Small balls of molten metal from the electrode (**"current pearls"**) may be driven deep into tissues. Heat generated by the current may melt calcium phosphate in bones, creating radiopaque droplets known as **"bone pearls"** or wax drippings.
- **General:** Signs of asphyxia, visceral congestion, and pulmonary oedema are common.

**VI. MEDICOLEGAL ASPECTS**

- **Accidental:** The vast majority of cases are accidental, resulting from defective domestic appliances, worn cables, or industrial mishaps.
- **Suicide & Homicide:** Extremely rare.
- **Judicial Electrocution:** Used in some US states. The condemned person is subjected to 2,000 volts and 7 amperes for one minute, causing instantaneous unconsciousness due to brain heating (up to 60°C) and tetanic spasm.
1. Heat exhaustion.
### HEAT EXHAUSTION (HEAT PROSTRATION)

**I. DEFINITION & SYNONYMS**

- **Synonyms:** Also referred to as heat prostration, heat syncope, or heat collapse.
- **Concept:** A systemic condition characterised by extreme exhaustion and peripheral vascular collapse following prolonged exposure to excessive heat.

**II. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY & AETIOLOGY (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Mechanism:** It develops due to the inadequate replacement of water and salts that are lost through profuse perspiration during thermal stress.
- **Precipitating Factors:** It typically occurs after several days of exposure to high temperatures and is frequently precipitated by heavy muscular work and the wearing of unsuitable clothing.
- **Haemodynamics:** The profound fluid and electrolyte loss leads directly to peripheral vascular collapse.

**III. CLINICAL FEATURES (🚨 Exam Target)**

- **Body Temperature:** The core body temperature is usually **subnormal**, or at most, it **does not exceed 102°F**. _Clinical Pearl: This distinguishes it from heat stroke, which presents with hyperpyrexia._
- **Skin & Appearance:** The face is characteristically pale, and the skin is **cold** with active sweating (unlike the hot, dry skin seen in heat stroke).
- **Neurological/Systemic:** The patient complains of feeling suddenly weak, giddy, and sick. Additional symptoms include marked tiredness, increased thirst, headache, and irritability.
- **Cardiopulmonary:** The pulse is small and thready, respirations are of a sighing nature, and the pupils are dilated.
- **Motor:** The patient may stagger, faint, or completely collapse.

**IV. PROGNOSIS & COMPLICATIONS**

- **Recovery:** The condition is generally reversible, and the patient usually recovers rapidly if placed at rest.
- **Fatality:** Although relatively rare, death may take place due to subsequent heart failure.

### INJURIES DUE TO EXPOSURE TO COLD

Thermal injuries from cold result from either systemic or localised exposure to low temperatures. For clinical and medicolegal examinations, they are broadly divided into **Generalized (Systemic) Effects** and **Localized Effects**.

---

#### I. GENERALISED EFFECT: HYPOTHERMIA

**1. Definition & Pathophysiology (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Definition:** Hypothermia is defined as a core (oral or axillary) body temperature falling below **35°C**.
- **Thermoregulatory Failure:** The ability of the hypothalamus to regulate body temperature is completely lost when the core temperature drops below **30°C**.
- **Mechanism of Death:** As body temperature drops, the dissociation of oxyhaemoglobin progressively decreases. This depresses the oxidative processes in tissues, leading to blood stagnation, severe tissue hypoxia (stagnant anoxia), and ultimate death from circulatory collapse and failure of vital centres.

**2. Clinical Phases of Hypothermia**

- **Phase I (Mild):** Rectal temperature between 90° and 98.4°F. Characterised by a feeling of cold and active shivering.
- **Phase II (Moderate):** Rectal temperature between 75° and 90°F. 🚨 _Exam Pearl:_ **Shivering completely stops** at or below 32°C (approx. 85°F). The victim becomes severely depressed, lethargic, drowsy, and the muscles stiffen, making gait resemble alcoholic intoxication.
- **Phase III (Severe):** Rectal temperature below 75°F (approx. 24°-27°C). The patient passes into stupor and coma; survival is rare, and death inevitably results from anoxia if maintained for 24 hours.

**3. Autopsy Findings in Hypothermia (🚨 Medicolegal Targets)**

- **Postmortem Lividity:** Appears **bright pink** due to the retention of oxygen by haemoglobin at low temperatures.
- **Skin:** Pink or brown-pink patches with blurred margins, specifically over large joints (knees, elbows, hips) and the face (cheeks, chin, nose). Extremities are often cyanosed with marked oedema.
- **Blood & Vessels:** Blood is bright red and fluid; ice crystals may be found inside the blood vessels, heart, and interstitial spaces.
- **Gastrointestinal (🥇 Pathognomonic Sign):** The stomach mucosa is studded with numerous **brown-black acute erosions, ulcerations, and haemorrhages** (representing severe pre-death stress).
- **Pancreas:** Focal **fat necrosis** of the pancreas and adjacent omentum/mesentery is seen in about 50% of cases.

**4. Unique Behavioural Syndromes in Hypothermia**

- **Paradoxical (Reciprocal) Undressing:** Seen in advanced hypothermia, especially in the elderly. Prolonged cold paralyses the vasomotor centre, causing sudden peripheral vasodilation. The victim feels an exaggerated, hallucinatory sensation of extreme heat and strips off their clothes before dying. _Forensic Pitfall:_ Frequently mimics a sexual assault scene.
- **Hide-and-Die Syndrome (Terminal Burrowing):** The dying, confused victim attempts to protect themselves by burrowing under beds, inside cupboards, or deep into the snow. _Forensic Pitfall:_ The resultant disarray of furniture is often misdiagnosed as the scene of a violent robbery or homicide.

**5. Neonatal Cold Injury**

- Occurs in the first week of life due to failure of infant metabolism in unsuitable cold environments (rectal temp < 32°C).
- **Presentation:** Profound lethargy, pitting oedema (hands, feet, eyelids), and a strikingly **red** discolouration of the face, hands, and feet.
- **Medicolegal Note:** It is a recognized method of infanticide for unwanted/illegitimate infants.

---

#### II. LOCALISED EFFECTS OF COLD

The local application of severe cold progresses through three pathophysiological stages: (1) Blanching and pallor due to profound vascular spasm; (2) Erythema, oedema, and swelling from vascular paralysis and increased capillary permeability; (3) Blister formation progressing to necrosis and gangrene due to vascular occlusion and obliterating endarteritis.

**1. Frostbite**

- **Aetiology:** Caused by exposure to **freezing temperatures** (–2.5°C to –8°C).
- **Pathology:** Results in immediate vasospasm and infarction of peripheral tissues, leading to blistering and necrosis beyond a clear line of inflammatory demarcation.
- **Distribution:** Usually affects the most exposed digits and appendages (ears, nose, fingers, toes).

**2. Trench Foot & Immersion Foot (🚨 Differentiating Feature)**

- **Aetiology:** Unlike frostbite, these conditions are caused by prolonged exposure to **non-freezing cold** (5° to 8°C) coupled with severe **dampness or moisture**.
- **Clinical Scenario:** Classically seen in soldiers standing in damp winter trenches (Trench foot) or shipwreck survivors immersed in cold seawater (Immersion foot).
- **Pathology:** Prolonged damp cold causes blister formation, ulceration, and eventually localized **dry gangrene** of the extremities.
1. Frost bite.
### FROSTBITE

**I. DEFINITION & CLASSIFICATION**

- **Category:** It is classified as a localised thermal injury resulting from the physical effects of extreme cold.
- **Concept:** Frostbite is the infarction and localised avascular necrosis of peripheral tissues beyond a line of inflammatory demarcation.

**II. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY & AETIOLOGY (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Temperature Profile:** It uniquely occurs due to exposure to **freezing temperatures**, typically ranging from **$-2$°C to $-8$°C**.
- **Mechanism:** Exposure to extreme cold causes sudden, profound vasospasm and ischaemia, leading to impaired local circulation. This results in the formation of ice crystals inside the tissues due to osmotic forces, which mechanically damages the cells and leads to necrosis.
- **Target Areas:** It most frequently affects the exposed parts of the body, notably the digits (toes and fingers), ears, face, and nose.

**III. CLINICAL STAGES & MORPHOLOGY (🚨 Exam Target)**

- **1. Frostnip:** The earliest, mildest stage characterized by a numb or irritant feeling and skin colour changes, but with no permanent tissue damage.
- **2. Superficial Frostbite:** The affected skin feels warm, appears swollen, and develops blisters. Signs of a vital reaction—such as erythema, swelling, and induration—can be seen around the involved surface.
- **3. Deep Frostbite:** The tissue undergoes frank necrosis and turns blue-black, accompanied by a complete loss of function. The dead skin typically becomes hard and black in about **two weeks**, ultimately progressing to gangrene.

**IV. CLINICAL PEARL: DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS**

- **Frostbite vs. Trench/Immersion Foot:** Examiners frequently test the pathophysiological difference. Frostbite strictly requires **freezing** temperatures ($-2$°C to $-8$°C) and causes immediate freezing of tissue fluids. In contrast, Trench foot and Immersion foot result from prolonged exposure to moisture and damp, **non-freezing** cold temperatures (5° to 8°C).
1. Wilson's classification of burns.
### WILSON'S CLASSIFICATION OF BURNS

**I. BACKGROUND & PRINCIPLES**

- **Historical Context:** Historically, Dupuytren classified thermal burns into six distinct degrees. For practical clinical and medicolegal utility, these were merged into **three main degrees** by Wilson.
- **Basis:** The classification is based entirely on the **depth of tissue destruction**.

**II. WILSON'S THREE DEGREES (🥇 High-Yield Exam Target)**

**1. Epidermal Burns (First Degree)**

- **Depth:** Destruction is strictly limited to the **superficial epidermis**.
- **Clinical Features:** Presents with erythema (redness), capillary dilatation, and transudation of fluid causing swelling. A split at the epidermal-dermal junction leads to the formation of **blisters** (vesicles or bullae) which contain protein-rich fluid and gas. When the epidermis is lost, the underlying dermis becomes reddened, inflamed, and exudes plasma.
- **Sensory:** These burns are **very painful**.
- **Healing & Prognosis:** Complete repair occurs **without scar formation**.

**2. Dermo-Epidermal Burns (Second Degree)**

- **Equivalence:** This stage merges Dupuytren's former third and fourth degrees.
- **Depth:** Destroys the **whole thickness of the skin** (epidermis and dermis), importantly including the destruction of dermal appendages such as hair follicles, sweat glands, and sebaceous glands.
- **Clinical Features:** Appears as shrivelled, depressed areas of coagulated necrotic tissue (eschar), which is bordered by reddish blistered skin. The lesions may be brown or black due to charring.
- **Sensory:** The pain and systemic shock are **greater** than in first-degree burns.
- **Healing & Prognosis:** The necrotic tissue separates in about a week, leaving an ulcer that strictly heals with **permanent scar formation**. 🚨 _Clinical Pearl:_ Contraction of this scar tissue frequently produces severe disfigurement and impaired anatomical function.

**3. Deep Burns (Third Degree)**

- **Depth:** Involves gross destruction extending beyond the skin layers into the **subcutaneous tissue, underlying muscle, and even bone**.
- **Clinical Features:** The appearances are similar to second-degree burns but in a much more severe form. The affected part is completely **charred**.
- **Sensory (🚨 Exam Trap):** Unlike the more superficial degrees, deep burns are **relatively painless** because the sensory nerve endings are completely destroyed by the extreme heat.

**III. MODERN CLINICAL CORRELATION**

- In modern surgical and casualty practice, these degrees are often broadly simplified into two management categories: **Superficial Burns** (involving less than the full thickness of the skin) and **Deep Burns** (involving the full thickness of the skin and deeper tissues).
1. Antemortem burns and postmortem burns.
### DIFFERENTIATION BETWEEN ANTEMORTEM AND POSTMORTEM BURNS

Differentiating whether a burn was sustained during life (antemortem) or after death (postmortem) is one of the most critical medicolegal assessments in fire-related fatalities.

**I. LOCAL CUTANEOUS FINDINGS (🥇 HIGH-YIELD DIFFERENTIATION)**

- **1. Line of Redness (Vital Reaction):**
    - **Antemortem (AM):** A 5 to 20 mm zone of hyperaemia (erythema) is present at the edge of the burnt area on the intact skin. This vital reaction is permanent and persists after death.
    - **Postmortem (PM):** This red line of demarcation is absent. The skin merely shows dried scorching, resembling burnt paper.
    - _🚨 Exam Pitfall:_ Heat applied up to one hour after death can occasionally produce a red line of erythema, but true vital cellular reaction will be absent.
- **2. Blister Formation (Vesication):**
    - **AM Burns:** Blisters appear as raised domes containing serous fluid rich in proteins (albumin) and chlorides. The base and periphery of the blister are red, inflamed, and show swollen papillae.
    - **PM Burns:** Blisters are pale-yellow and contain mostly gas/air and a thin, clear fluid with only traces of albumin and chlorides. The base of the postmortem blister is dry, hard, and yellow.
- **3. Histopathology (Intravital Reaction):**
    - **AM Burns:** Microscopic examination reveals marked cellular exudation, polymorphonuclear leucocytic infiltration, and reactive changes in the tissue cells. Staining for DNA and RNA is noted within 6 hours.
    - **PM Burns:** There is no intravital reaction, no cellular exudation, and no congestion. _Clinical limitation:_ Burns produced immediately before or immediately after death cannot be distinguished by naked eye or microscopic examination.
- **4. Enzyme Histochemistry:**
    - **AM Burns:** The peripheral zone of the burn shows an early, marked increase in enzyme reactions. Non-specific esterases increase within 45 minutes, adenosine triphosphate (ATP) in 1 to 2 hours, leucine aminopeptidase in 2 hours, acid phosphatases in 4 to 6 hours, and alkaline phosphatases in 3 to 12 hours.
    - **PM Burns:** The peripheral zone does not show any increase in enzyme activity.

**II. SYSTEMIC FINDINGS (THE "ABSOLUTE PROOFS" OF LIFE) 🚨**

The presence of internal systemic changes provides the most definitive medicolegal proof that the victim was alive and actively breathing during the fire.

- **1. Respiratory Tract Changes:**
    - **AM Burns:** The inhalation of hot fumes causes acute laryngeal oedema, which can result in sudden asphyxiation. The presence of smoke and black soot (carbon) particles in the respiratory passages, firmly adherent to the oedematous mucosa and extending down into the terminal bronchioles, is **absolute proof** of life during the fire.
    - **PM Burns:** Soot is not actively inhaled. While passive percolation of soot may enter the mouth or back of the pharynx, it does not reach the deep respiratory tree.
- **2. Toxic Gases in Blood (Carboxyhaemoglobin):**
    - **AM Burns:** Incomplete combustion produces carbon monoxide (CO), which is actively inhaled and binds to haemoglobin. A carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb) saturation of **more than 10%** indicates the victim was alive and inhaling CO. Elevated levels of other toxic gases like hydrogen cyanide (HCN), ammonia, nitrogen oxides, and hydrogen sulphide may also be present in the blood.
    - **PM Burns:** CO is not absorbed by a body after death. COHb will not be elevated unless the person was exposed to CO before the fire started.
1. Heat hematoma.
### HEAT HAEMATOMA (THERMAL EXTRADURAL HAEMATOMA)

**I. DEFINITION & CLINICAL CONTEXT**

- **Concept:** A heat haematoma is a postmortem thermal artefact that occurs when the head has been exposed to intense heat sufficient to cause severe charring of the skull.
- **Significance:** It closely simulates a traumatic extradural (epidural) haemorrhage, making it a critical medicolegal differential diagnosis in bodies recovered from fires.

**II. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (MECHANISM)** The exact mechanism of its development is debated, but the accepted theories include:

1. Blood is actively boiled from the diploic layer of the skull bone through the emissary veins and venous sinuses which then rupture into the extradural space.
2. The extreme heat causes severe shrinkage of the meninges and the brain, which creates a vacuum effect that aspirates blood from the venous sinuses.

**III. GROSS MORPHOLOGY (🥇 High-Yield for Exams)**

- **Appearance:** It presents as a thin, granular, soft, and friable blood clot.
- **Colour:** Classically light chocolate-coloured, but it may appear pink if the blood contains Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb).
- **🚨 Pathognomonic Feature:** The clot characteristically exhibits a **"honeycomb" appearance** due to bubbles of steam produced by the intense heat boiling the tissue fluids.
- **Size & Distribution:** The volume can reach up to 120 ml, with a thickness varying from 1.5 to 15 mm. It is usually diffuse and its distribution closely follows the distribution of the charring on the outer table of the skull.
- **Location:** The parietotemporal region is the most common site, though it can sometimes be found in the frontal or occipital regions.
- **Underlying Structures:** The dura mater underneath the haematoma is greatly shrunken, compressing the brain. Under severe tension, the dura may split, allowing brain tissue to ooze out as a mass of frothy paste. The adjacent brain tissue shows hardening and discolouration due to a "cooking" effect.

**IV. 🚨 EXAM TRAP: HEAT HAEMATOMA VS. TRAUMATIC EXTRADURAL HAEMORRHAGE (EDH)** Examiners frequently test the ability to differentiate a heat-induced haematoma from an antemortem traumatic EDH in a burnt body.

|Feature|Heat Haematoma (Thermal Artifact)|Traumatic Extradural Haemorrhage (EDH)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Aetiology**|Intense heat causing charring of the skull.|Blunt force trauma to the head.|
|**Clot Characteristics**|Thin, granular, soft, friable, **honeycomb** appearance, light chocolate or pink.|Rubbery consistency, discoid shape, reddish-purple.|
|**Distribution**|Diffuse (often bilateral); follows the pattern of external bone charring.|Localised; usually adjacent to the Sylvian fissure or site of impact.|
|**Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb)**|**Present.** The COHb level in the haematoma will match the peripheral blood.|**Absent.** If the traumatic bleeding occurred _before_ the fire started, the enclosed clot will not contain COHb.|
|**Underlying Brain (CNS)**|No traumatic injury; brain is hardened/cooked.|Frequently injured (e.g., contusions, lacerations).|
|**Skull Fractures**|May have thermal fractures (radiating/criss-cross).|Associated with traumatic fractures in the temporal area.|
1. How do you diagnose that death is due to burns in a charred body?
### DIAGNOSIS OF DEATH DUE TO BURNS IN A CHARRED BODY

Diagnosing the exact cause of death in a severely charred body is a major medicolegal challenge because extensive tissue destruction often obliterates external signs of trauma or vital reactions. To definitively prove that the victim was alive during the fire and died as a result of it, the examiner must rely heavily on internal, systemic findings.

**I. THE GOLD STANDARDS: ABSOLUTE PROOFS OF LIFE DURING FIRE (🥇 High-Yield)** In a charred body where external skin is destroyed, the respiratory and cardiovascular systems provide the most definitive evidence of antemortem burning.

- **1. Soot in the Respiratory Tract:** The presence of aspirated black carbon (soot) particles embedded in the frothy mucus of the nose, mouth, larynx, trachea, and extending deep into the **terminal bronchioles** is an absolute proof that the victim was actively breathing during the fire. While passive percolation can sometimes allow soot into the upper pharynx of a dead body, it will never pass beyond the vocal cords.
- **2. Elevated Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb):** Active inhalation of carbon monoxide produced by incomplete combustion leads to elevated COHb levels in the blood, typically **>10%**, and often reaching 70% to 80%. This imparts a characteristic **cherry-red colour** to the blood, tissues, and internal organs. CO is not absorbed by a body after death.
- _Exam Pearl:_ The combination of **soot in the deep airways plus an elevated COHb saturation** constitutes the absolute, irrefutable proof that the victim was alive when the fire occurred.

**II. INTERNAL PATHOLOGICAL FINDINGS (🚨 Exam Targets)** Because internal tissues are poor conductors of heat, viscera are frequently well-preserved even in severely charred bodies.

- **1. Heat Haematoma (Extradural):** Intense heat causes blood to boil from the diploic space of the skull into the extradural space, forming a soft, friable, light-chocolate or pink clot.
    - _Diagnostic feature:_ It has a characteristic **"honeycomb" appearance** due to steam bubbles, which differentiates it from a traumatic epidural haemorrhage. Furthermore, a heat haematoma will contain COHb (if inhaled during life), whereas a pre-existing traumatic clot will not.
- **2. Pulmonary Changes:** The inhalation of superheated air and toxic fumes causes acute irritation, leading to severe laryngeal oedema, marked pulmonary oedema, and congestion. **Blood-tinged froth** exuding from the mouth and nose is a strong indicator that the deceased was actively breathing and developed pulmonary oedema while the fire was in progress.
- **3. Toxic Gases:** Depending on the materials burning (e.g., plastics, synthetic fabrics), elevated blood levels of other inhaled toxic gases like hydrogen cyanide (HCN), oxides of nitrogen, and hydrogen sulphide may also be present, confirming active antemortem respiration.

**III. LOCAL VITAL REACTIONS (If unburnt skin survives)** If any patchy areas of unburnt or partially burnt skin remain, the following signs confirm an antemortem vital reaction:

- **Line of Redness (Erythema):** A 5-20 mm zone of hyperaemia surrounding the burn, persisting after death.
- **Antemortem Blisters:** Raised domes containing serous fluid rich in **proteins (albumin) and chlorides**, with a red, inflamed base. Postmortem blisters, conversely, contain mostly gas/air and thin fluid with trace albumin.
- **Enzyme Histochemistry:** The peripheral zone of an antemortem burn shows an increase in enzymatic activity, specifically non-specific esterases, ATP, leucine aminopeptidase, and acid/alkaline phosphatase within 1 to 12 hours of the injury.

**IV. MEDICOLEGAL PITFALLS TO AVOID IN CHARRED BODIES** When examining a charred body, examiners must carefully differentiate thermal artifacts from homicidal trauma:

- **Pugilistic Attitude:** The boxer's posture (universal flexion) is caused by heat-induced coagulation and contraction of muscle proteins. It is **not** an antemortem sign of struggle and occurs even if a dead body is thrown into a fire.
- **Heat Ruptures:** Skin and soft tissues may contract and split, producing linear tears over fleshy areas (calves, thighs) that mimic incised or lacerated wounds. These are postmortem artifacts and show intact blood vessels bridging the floor, no bleeding, and no vital reaction.
- **Thermal Skull Fractures:** Heat can cause the outer table of the skull to crack (radiating or stellate lines), which must not be confused with blunt force trauma. Thermal fractures often have a circular pattern with sharp edges or #crazy-paving# appearance, and typically do not cross un-united suture lines.
- **Concealment of Homicide:** A complete autopsy must rule out prior fatal injuries (e.g., bullets, stab wounds, mechanical strangulation) or poisoning, as perpetrators often burn a body to conceal a primary homicide.
