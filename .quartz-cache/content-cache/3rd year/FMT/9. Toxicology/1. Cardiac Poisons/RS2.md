
1. Cardiac poisons. 
### CARDIAC POISONS

**OVERVIEW & CLASSIFICATION** Cardiac poisons are a group of toxic substances that primarily and directly affect the cardiovascular system, leading to potentially fatal arrhythmias or cardiac arrest. The high-yield medicolegal cardiac poisons include:

1. **Digitalis purpurea** (Foxglove)
2. **Oleanders:** _Nerium odorum_ (White Oleander), _Cerbera thevetia_ (Yellow Oleander), and _Cerbera odallam_ (Pilikirbir)
3. **Aconite** (Monk's hood / Sweet poison)
4. **Nicotiana tabacum** (Tobacco / Nicotine)

---

#### 1. DIGITALIS PURPUREA

- **Active Principles:** Contains over 30 cardiac and steroidal glycosides, primarily **digitoxin, digoxin, and digitalin** (highest concentration in leaves and seeds).
- **Clinical Features:**
    - _GIT:_ Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea.
    - _Cardiac (Hallmark):_ Extrasystoles, atrial flutter/fibrillation, AV block, ventricular tachycardia, and ventricular fibrillation.
    - _Visual:_ Photophobia, transient amblyopia, diplopia, and classic **halos or color aberrations (xanthopsia)**.
- **Fatal Dose:** Digoxin 10 mg; Digitoxin 4 mg; Digitalin 15–30 mg; Leaf 2 g.
- **Specific Treatment:** **Digoxin-specific antibody fragments (Fab)** (10 to 20 vials IV). Phenytoin or Lignocaine are used for ventricular irritability.

#### 2. THE OLEANDERS (Plant Glycosides)

These plants contain potent cardiac glycosides with a mechanism of action nearly identical to Digitalis.

- **A. Nerium odorum (White Oleander / Kaner):**
    - _Active Principles:_ Oleandrin, nerin (rosagenin and folinerin).
    - _Features:_ Dysphagia, frothy salivation, tetanic spasms, lock-jaw, heart block, and respiratory paralysis.
- **B. Cerbera thevetia (Yellow Oleander / Pila Kaner):**
    - _Active Principles:_ Thevetin, nerifolin (most potent), peruvoside, cerberin.
    - _Features:_ Numbness of the tongue, purging, low blood pressure, heart block, and peripheral circulatory failure.
- **C. Cerbera odallam (Pilikirbir):**
    - _Active Principles:_ Cerberin, odollin.
    - _Features:_ Sinus bradycardia, SA block, arrhythmias, and **hyperkalaemia** (highly tested biochemical change).
- **🥇 High-Yield Management for all Oleanders:** **Digoxin immune Fab (ovine)** is the specific antidote for all oleander poisonings. Atropine is given for bradycardia.

#### 3. ACONITE (Monk's Hood / Devil's Helmet / Bish)

- **Active Principles:** Aconitine, pseudo-aconitine, bikhaconitine (alkaloids concentrated in the conical/tapering roots).
- **Action:** Initially stimulates sensory nerves, then paralyses the motor ganglia of the heart and respiratory centre.
- **Clinical Pearls:**
    - **Tingling & Numbness:** Contact with mouth/skin causes immediate local tingling and numbness.
    - **Hippus (Exam Pearl):** Alternate spasmodic contraction and dilation of the pupils.
    - _Cardiac:_ Initial tachycardia followed by severe bradycardia (AV block) and hypotension.
- **Medicolegal Importance:** Known as an **"ideal homicidal poison"** because it is difficult to detect by chemical analysis, and the symptoms perfectly mimic a natural disease (cardiac arrest). It is completely destroyed by putrefaction.

#### 4. NICOTIANA TABACUM (Tobacco)

- **Active Principles:** Nicotine and anabasine (alkaloids).
- **Action:** First stimulates, then depresses and blocks the autonomic ganglia and neuromuscular junctions.
- **Clinical Features:**
    - _Early:_ Tachycardia, hypertension, tachypnoea, miosis, agitation, sweating.
    - _Late:_ Bradycardia, hypotension, respiratory depression, mydriasis, convulsions, coma, and cardiac arrhythmias.
- **Fatal Dose:** 50 to 100 mg of nicotine (rivals cyanide in causing rapid death within 5-15 mins).
- **Specific Treatment:** **Mecamylamine** (Inversine) is a specific antidote. Atropine and hexamethonium are used to counteract peripheral autonomic disturbances.

---

### 🚨 GENERAL AUTOPSY FINDINGS IN CARDIAC POISONS

While gross postmortem findings for cardiac poisons are largely non-specific (usually presenting as generalized asphyxia and visceral congestion), one classic sign must be remembered:

- **Heart:** The hallmark internal finding across major cardiac poisons (especially Aconite and Digitalis) is the presence of **subepicardial petechiae and subendocardial petechio-ecchymotic haemorrhages**, which are most prominent on the **left ventricle**.
1. Aconite poisoning. 
### ACONITE POISONING

**I. OVERVIEW & IDENTIFICATION**

- **Botanical Name:** _Aconitum napellus_ and _Aconitum ferox_.
- **Common Names:** Monk's hood, blue rocket, devil's helmet, meetha zeher, bish, or bikh.
- **Active Principle:** **Aconitine**, a highly toxic alkaloid that initially stimulates and then completely depresses the central nervous system. All parts of the plant are poisonous, but the root is the most potent.
- 🥇 **Exam Pearl (Root Differentiation):** Accidental poisoning often occurs when the aconite root is mistaken for horse-radish root.
    - _Aconite root:_ Conical/tapering, dark-brown externally. When freshly cut, it is white internally but **turns pink on exposure to air**.
    - _Horse-radish root:_ Cylindrical, yellowish-white. It does **not** change color on exposure to air and has a pungent taste.

---

**II. MECHANISM OF ACTION**

- Aconitine first stimulates sensory nerves, leading to characteristic local paresthesias.
- It then paralyzes the motor ganglia of the heart and slows the respiratory center.

---

**III. HIGH-YIELD CLINICAL FEATURES** Symptoms appear almost immediately or within a few minutes.

- **Neurological:** **Tingling and numbness** in the mouth, tongue, and pharynx, which later spreads all over the body. The patient typically experiences muscular weakness, ataxia, and slurred speech, but the **mind remains clear until death**.
- **Ocular (🥇 Highly Tested):** **Hippus**—a condition where the pupils alternately contract and dilate (spasmodic pupil), before remaining dilated in the later stages. Dim vision and diplopia are common.
- **Cardiovascular:** Initially presents as tachycardia, which progresses to bradycardia (due to AV block), hypotension, cardiac arrhythmias, and eventual cardiac arrest.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Burning sensation from mouth to stomach, severe salivation, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea.

---

**IV. TOXICITY PARAMETERS**

- **Fatal Dose:** 1 gram of the root; 2 to 5 mg of aconitine.
- **Fatal Period:** 2 to 6 hours.

---

**V. MANAGEMENT**

- **Gastric Lavage:** Use warm water with a weak solution of **iodine in potassium iodide** or **tannic acid** to precipitate the alkaloid. Animal charcoal (activated charcoal) is also useful.
- **Antidote / Symptomatic:** **Atropine** (0.5 to 1 mg) is highly useful to counteract bradycardia and cardiac depression.

---

**VI. POSTMORTEM FINDINGS**

- Findings are generally non-specific and mimic asphyxia (lungs, kidneys, and brain congestion).
- **Cardiac Pathology (Exam Pearl):** **Subepicardial petechial-ecchymotic hemorrhages** on the left ventricle are frequently observed.
- **Toxicology Warning:** Aconitine is extremely unstable and is **rapidly destroyed by putrefaction**.

---

**VII. MEDICOLEGAL IMPORTANCE**

- **Ideal Homicidal Poison:** Aconite is favored by murderers because it perfectly mimics a natural disease (sudden cardiac arrest), and the alkaloid is readily destroyed by putrefaction, making chemical detection very difficult. Criminals often administer it wrapped in a betel leaf to conceal its sweet-then-acrid taste.
- **Substance Abuse:** It is sometimes added to liquor or toddy to illegally enhance the intoxicating "kick".
- **Other Uses:** Utilized as an abortifacient, cattle poison, and arrow poison.
