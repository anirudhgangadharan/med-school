### DEFINITION AND AMENDMENTS OF RAPE (S. 375 IPC)

Rape is committed if a man performs certain actions under specific circumstances without valid consent.

**I. Prohibited Acts Constituting Rape:** A man commits "rape" if he performs any of the following acts:

- **Penetration:** Penetrates his penis, to **any extent**, into the vagina, mouth, urethra, or anus of a woman; or makes her do so with him or any other person.
- **Insertion:** Inserts, to **any extent**, any object or a part of the body (not being the penis) into the vagina, urethra, or anus of a woman; or makes her do so with him or any other person.
- **Manipulation:** Manipulates any part of the body of a woman so as to cause penetration into the vagina, urethra, anus, or any part of the body of such woman.
- **Oral Contact:** Applies his mouth to the vagina, anus, or urethra of a woman.

**II. Circumstances Vitiating Consent:** The above acts constitute rape when committed under any of the following circumstances:

1. **Against her will**.
2. **Without her consent**.
3. Consent obtained by putting her or a person interested in her in **fear of death or hurt**.
4. Consent given based on the belief that he is her lawfully married husband (when he knows he is not).
5. Consent given when, due to **unsoundness of mind, intoxication**, or administration of a stupefying/unwholesome substance, she is **unable to understand the nature and consequences** of that consent.
6. With or without her consent, when she is under **eighteen years of age** ($\rightarrow$ Statutory Rape).
7. When she is **unable to communicate consent**.

**III. Key Amendments and Definitions (Recent)**

- **Definition of Vagina:** The term "vagina" shall also include the **labia majora**.
- **Definition of Consent:** Consent means an **unequivocal voluntary agreement** when the woman communicates willingness (by words, gestures, or any form of verbal or non-verbal communication) to participate in the specific sexual act.
- **Lack of Resistance:** Lack of **physical resistance** alone is **not** to be regarded as consent to the sexual activity.
- **Valid Age of Consent:** A woman of **eighteen years and above** can give valid consent for sexual intercourse.

**IV. Exceptions to Rape:**

- A medical procedure or intervention shall **not** constitute rape.
- Sexual intercourse or sexual acts by a man with his **own wife** (the wife **not being under fifteen years of age**) is **not** rape.
    - _Note:_ Sexual intercourse by a man with his own wife who is **living separately** (under separation decree or otherwise) **without her consent** is punishable under S. 376B IPC.

### IV. SIGNS OF RAPE IN A 16-YEAR-OLD VIRGIN VICTIM

A 16-year-old victim is legally considered incapable of giving consent, meaning the act constitutes **Statutory Rape**. The medical findings focus on signs of penetration combined with forceful trauma:

1. **Hymenal Status:** Hymen is ruptured. The tearing usually occurs **posteriorly** (5 and 7 o’clock position) and extends to the margin. Margins are typically **sharp and red** and bleed easily (if recent).
2. **Genital Trauma:** Bruising, swelling, and inflammation of the **labia majora, labia minora, and vaginal introitus**. Tears in the **fourchette** and **posterior commissure** are expected due to first penetration.
3. **Vaginal Findings:** Subdermal bruising may be present, particularly on the anterior wall. Seminal fluid/stains may be present in the vagina and posterior fornix (Proof of sexual connection).
4. **Non-Genital Trauma (Struggle):**
    - Bruising/scratches on **inner thighs** (forcible separation of legs).
    - Marks of resistance (bruises, abrasions, bite marks) on the **wrists, arms, and face/mouth**.
    - Tears or damage to **clothing**.

### RAPE DEFINITION (Section 375 IPC)

Rape is committed if a man performs certain specific acts against a woman under circumstances that negate consent.

#### I. PROHIBITED ACTS CONSTITUTING RAPE:

A man commits "rape" if he performs any of the following acts, to **any extent**:

1. **Penetration:** Penetrates his penis into the vagina, mouth, urethra, or anus of a woman.
2. **Insertion:** Inserts any object or a part of his body (not the penis) into the vagina, the urethra, or anus.
3. **Manipulation:** Manipulates any part of the body of a woman so as to cause penetration into the vagina, urethra, anus, or any part of body.
4. **Oral Contact:** Applies his mouth to the vagina, anus, or urethra of a woman.

> **KEY LEGAL CLARIFICATION:** For this section, the term “vagina” also includes the **labia majora**.

#### II. CIRCUMSTANCES NEGATING CONSENT:

These acts constitute rape when committed:

1. **Against her will** or **without her consent**.
2. Consent obtained by putting her or someone she is interested in, in **fear of death or hurt**.
3. Consent given based on the belief that he is her lawfully married husband (when he knows he is not).
4. Consent given when she is unable to understand the nature and consequences of the act due to **unsoundness of mind, intoxication**, or administration of a stupefying substance.
5. **With or without her consent**, when she is **under eighteen years of age** (Statutory Rape).
6. When she is **unable to communicate consent**.

> **CONSENT DEFINED:** Consent means an **unequivocal voluntary agreement** where the woman communicates willingness (by words, gestures, or non-verbal means) to participate. The lack of **physical resistance alone** is expressly _not_ regarded as consent.

### FINDINGS IN RAPE OF A DEFLORATE WOMAN (30 years old)

Since the victim is an adult accustomed to intercourse, genital injuries are **less likely** to be found due to tissue elasticity. However, forensic assessment focuses on identifying:

1. **Absence of Injury is Non-Exclusionary:** Complete penetration can occur **without any evidence of injury**. The lack of physical injuries, due to the elasticity of the genitalia, **does not exclude rape**. Injuries typically disappear or become obscure in _3 to 4 days_ in sexually experienced women.
2. **Proof of Penetration (Semen):** The most critical (and sometimes the _only_) evidence of penetration is the **presence of spermatozoa** in the vagina.
3. **Evidence of Force/Struggle:** Rape usually involves **greater violence** than consensual intercourse, thus injuries must be meticulously sought.
    - If the woman offered resistance, local injury **will be present**.
    - The vagina may show **deep injury, laceration, or bruising** with subsequent effusion and swelling, even if external marks are absent.
    - Look for non-genital trauma (bruises/scratches/bite marks) on areas indicative of restraint or struggle, such as the **wrists, inner thighs, face, or arms**.

### EXAMINATION OF THE ACCUSED IN RAPE CASES

The examination of an accused in a case of rape serves to establish the individual's physical capacity to commit the act and to collect trace evidence linking him to the victim or the crime scene.

---

### I. LEGAL MANDATE & PREREQUISITES (S. 53(A) Cr.P.C.)

- **Authorization:** Examination is conducted upon **requisition from the Investigating Police Officer (I.O.)** or Magistrate.
- **Consent:** Written consent is necessary, but the accused **can be examined using reasonable force** if arrested on a rape charge and examination is requested by a police officer **not below the rank of sub-inspector** (S. 53(A) Cr.P.C.).
- **Timing:** Examination must be performed **without delay** as physical signs disappear rapidly.
- **Documentation:** A comprehensive report must be prepared detailing the findings, including the specific time of commencement and completion (S. 53(A) Cr.P.C.).
- **Medical Objectivity:** The physician must avoid obtaining any history of the specific crime; if the accused volunteers self-incriminating statements, they should be neither recorded nor reported in the medical record.
- **Correlation:** Ideally, the examination is conducted by the same physician who examined the victim, facilitating better correlation between injuries found on both parties.

---

### II. GENERAL EXAMINATION & HISTORY

- **Identification:** Accused must be identified by the escorting police officer. Note name, age, and identification marks.
- **Capacity Assessment:** Determine the **physical power** and development of genital organs to assess the possibility of overcoming the victim.
- **Mental State/Toxicology:** Note the general mental state and check for the **influence of alcohol or drugs**. Collect blood and urine samples for screening if intoxication is suspected.
- **Pre-existing Activity:** Record the accused’s explanation for any injuries found, their last bath, and any recent consensual intercourse.

---

### III. TRACE EVIDENCE & STRUGGLE MARKS

|Area of Examination|Key Findings to Document (Evidence of Struggle/Contact)|
|:--|:--|
|**Clothing**|Examine for **tears, victim’s hair/fibers, blood stains, seminal stains,** or **foreign matter** (e.g., mud, grease). Stains are often found on the inside of the trouser fly and underwear.|
|**Body Surface**|Search for resistance injuries (bruises, abrasions, lacerations, or **bite marks**) inflicted by the victim, commonly found on the **face, neck, hands, arms, buttocks, and genitals**.|
|**Nail Scrapings**|Collect scrapings/clippings from all fingernails (for **victim’s epithelial cells, blood, or fibers**).|
|**Genitals (Local Injuries)**|Note abrasions or bruising on the prepuce, glans penis, or tears of the **frenulum**, especially if the victim was a young child or a virgin (disparity in size).|
|**Smegma Status**|If uncircumcised, assess the presence of smegma. Its **absence** may indicate recent intercourse or washing.|
|**Vaginal Cell Transfer**|Swab the penile shaft and glans for **victim's epithelial cells** (vaginal cells). Glycogen in these cells produces a positive **Lugol’s iodine test** (positive up to 4 days).|

---

### IV. LABORATORY SPECIMENS (S. 53(A) Cr.P.C.)

Samples collected must be dried, sealed, labeled, and sent to the Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL):

1. **Swabs:** From the coronal sulcus, prepuce, penile shaft, and urethral orifice.
2. **Hair:** Pubic hair combings, avulsed pubic hair, and head hair samples (for DNA comparison with victim’s trace evidence).
3. **Blood:** For DNA profile, grouping, and toxicology (alcohol/drug screening).
4. **Urine:** For drug and alcohol analysis.
5. **Nail Scrapings:** From beneath all fingernails.

---

### V. OPINION FRAMEWORK

The doctor **must never make a diagnosis of rape**, as it is a legal determination. The opinion focuses on medical findings and correlation:

- **Potency/Capacity:** State if there are findings suggesting **impotence** or **sterility**.
- **Sexual Contact:** Conclude if there is evidence suggestive of **recent sexual intercourse** or contact, and whether injuries are **consistent with the struggle/resistance** described by the victim.
- **Legal Note:** The opinion is presented in a double negative form: e.g., "There is **nothing to suggest** that the individual is incapable of performing normal sexual intercourse".

### UNNATURAL SEXUAL OFFENCES (S. 377 IPC)

**Definition:**

- Voluntary sexual intercourse against the **order of nature** with any man, woman, or animal.
- **Key Legal Point:** Penetration (to any extent) is **sufficient** to constitute the offence.
- **Punishment:** Imprisonment for life or up to **ten years**, and liable to fine.

|Type|Definition|Clinical/Forensic Relevance (High-Yield)|
|:--|:--|:--|

### SPECIFIC FINDINGS IN SODOMY

#### 1. Examination of Passive Agent (Victim)

- **Anal Status (Recent Act):** Anal muscle often **oedematous, abraded, and bruised**. Oozing/congestion present for 2-3 days.
- **Injury Type:** Linear abrasions and **fissures** (longitudinal tears) are common (often posterior quadrant). Tearing of sphincter ani is rare in adults.
- **Sequelae:** Pain during walking and defecation.
- **Trace Evidence (Crucial):** Swabs must be taken from anal verge and **lower rectum**. Look for **seminal fluid** and foreign hair/fibers.

#### 2. Examination of Active Agent (Accused)

- **Genital Trauma:** Abrasions on glans/prepuce or tearing of the **frenulum** may be seen.
- **Trace Evidence:** Faecal matter, lubricants, or foreign pubic hairs may be found on the penis or clothing.
- **Smegma Status:** If not circumcised, the **absence of smegma** is a strong suggestive finding, as smegma gets rubbed off during intercourse.
- **Wounds:** Scratches/bruises on hands/face may indicate struggle.

#### Note on Habituation

- In habitual passive agents, the anal margin is **smooth and thickened**, the sphincter tone is lost, and the anus may be patulous and dilated (funnel-shaped depression).
- **Crucial Caveat:** Opinion as to the cause of anal dilatation must be guarded, as it can be congenital or due to other diseases/practices.

### SODOMY AND FINDINGS IN A HABITUAL PASSIVE AGENT

**I. DEFINITION & MEDICOLEGAL CONCEPTS (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Definition:** Sodomy (also known as buggery) is defined as anal intercourse between two males, or between a male and a female.
- **Legal Classification:** It falls under unnatural sexual offences and is punishable under **Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)** (Voluntary carnal intercourse against the order of nature).
- **Terminology:** The inserting partner is the _active agent_, while the receiving partner is the _passive agent_. A habitual passive agent is formally called a **catamite**. In India, _Hijras_ (castrated males) and _Zenanas_ (male transvestites) frequently act as habitual passive agents.

**II. FINDINGS IN A HABITUAL PASSIVE AGENT (🚨 Exam Target)** In stark contrast to a victim unaccustomed to sodomy (who presents with acute pain, fresh fissures, bruising, and spasm), the habitual passive agent exhibits chronic adaptive and anatomical changes.

**1. Anal Sphincter & Tone**

- The anal sphincter loses its tone, becoming permanently dilated, lax, and patulous.
- The sphincter muscle fails to contract reflexly when the skin around it is pinched.
- **Digital Examination:** The anus easily and painlessly admits **3 to 4 fingers**, indicating chronic dilatation.

**2. Skin and Mucosal Changes**

- **Perianal Skin:** The skin around the anus becomes smooth, thickened, and keratinised due to constant friction, extending up into the anal canal to the mucocutaneous junction.
- **Mucosa:** There is an epithelisation of the mucosa and a distinct **loss of the normal fine wrinkles (rugosity)**.
- **Rectum:** May show prolapsed mucosa with thickening and disappearance of the normal radial folds.

**3. Associated Scars and Lesions**

- Presence of old and fresh anal fissures, healed scars, and sinuses (tunneling) are hallmark findings.
- Piles (hemorrhoids) and fissures are highly common in older habitual sodomites.

**4. Buttock Anatomy**

- There is a slight depression of the buttocks towards the anus due to the absorption of regional fat.
- A **funnel-shaped anus** may be seen, though this can sometimes be a normal anatomical variant.

**5. Hygiene and Trace Evidence**

- Perianal hair is frequently found shaved and local hygiene is scrupulously maintained.
- Unlike acute assault, blood stains are usually **absent**. Lubricants and loose foreign pubic hair may or may not be present.

**6. Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs)**

- There is a high incidence of associated STDs; look for condylomata (genital warts), syphilitic chancre, or gonorrhoeal discharge around the anal region.
