1. Lab diagnosis of leprosy
	_**Laboratory Diagnosis of Leprosy**_
	
	**I. General Principles**
	
	- **Clinical Diagnosis is Primary:** Due to characteristic clinical manifestations (e.g., anesthetic skin lesions, bony deformities), clinical suspicion guides initial evaluation and management. Laboratory tests serve primarily for **confirmation and classification**.
	- **Non-Cultivable Organism:** _Mycobacterium leprae_ is **not cultivable** on artificial laboratory media or in tissue culture.
	
	**II. Specimen Collection**
	
	- **Slit-Skin Smears:** Collected from active lesions, especially from **ear lobes, nose, forehead, and buttock**.
	- **Nasal Specimens:** (e.g., nasal blow or scraping) are particularly useful for **multibacillary (LL) patients**, as they shed a large number of bacilli.
	- **Biopsy:** From thickened nerves or nodular lesions may be necessary in some cases.
	
	**III. Direct Smear Microscopy (Gold Standard Method)**
	
	- **Staining:** Smears are stained by **Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) technique**, using **5% sulfuric acid** for decolorization, as _M. leprae_ is **less acid-fast** compared to _M. tuberculosis_.
	- **Key Morphological Findings:**
	    - **Thick, stubby, boxcar-shaped, gram-positive bacilli** (though Gram stain is not typically used for _M. leprae_, this morphology is mentioned for _C. perfringens_; for _M. leprae_, ZN stain is key). _Note: The provided source describes M. leprae as "long slender, straight or slightly curved and beaded, red colored acid-fast bacillus" in the context of ZN staining. The "thick, stubby, boxcar-shaped" description is specific to Clostridium perfringens, and should not be applied to M. leprae. I will use the correct description for M. leprae._
	    - **Red, long slender, beaded acid-fast bacilli (AFB)**, arranged **singly or in characteristic groups (cigar-like bundles)**.
	    - Bacilli are often bound together by a lipid-like substance (glia) to form **globi**.
	    - Found **inside foamy macrophages**, known as **Virchow’s lepra cells**.
	    - **Live bacilli** appear **uniformly stained** with parallel sides and rounded ends. **Dead bacilli** are less uniformly stained, fragmented, and granular.
	- **Grading of Smears:** Based on the number of bacilli per oil immersion field (OIF) (1+ to 6+).
	    - **Bacteriological Index (BI):** Represents the total number of bacilli (live and dead) and is calculated by averaging the pluses scored in all smears.
	    - **Morphological Index (MI):** Expressed as the **percentage of uniformly stained bacilli**. This is a **better marker to monitor treatment response**.
	    - **SFG (Solid, Fragmented Granular rod) percentage:** Provides a more sensitive indicator for **monitoring treatment response** by recording percentages of solid, fragmented, and granular rods separately.
	- **Clinical Relevance:** Microscopy provides a **presumptive diagnosis** and is crucial for **classifying cases** into Paucibacillary (smear negative) or Multibacillary (smear positive), which guides treatment regimens.
	
	**IV. Culture**
	
	- _M. leprae_ is **NOT cultivable** on artificial laboratory media or in tissue culture. It **does not follow Koch's postulates**.
	- **Mouse Footpad Cultivation:** This is the **only certain way** to cultivate _M. leprae_.
	    - Specimens are inoculated into the footpads of mice and incubated at **20°C for 6–9 months**. Nine-banded armadillos (_Dasypus novemcinctus_) can also be used.
	    - **Advantages:** **10 times more sensitive** than microscopy, useful for detecting **drug resistance** and evaluating **bacillary viability**.
	    - **Disadvantages:** Time-consuming (6–9 months) and involves ethical issues regarding animal use.
	
	**V. Antibody Detection (Serology)**
	
	- **FLA-ABS (Fluorescent Leprosy Antibody Absorption Test):** Used to identify **subclinical cases**.
	- **ELISA for IgM antibodies to PGL-1 (Phenolic Glycolipid-1) antigen:**
	    - Positive in **95% of patients with untreated lepromatous leprosy (LL)**, and the titre **decreases with effective therapy**.
	    - However, its sensitivity is **low (60%) in tuberculoid (TT) patients**.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Antibodies play a **minor role in disease control** as _M. leprae_ is an intracellular pathogen.
	
	**VI. Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI) Tests**
	
	- **Lepromin Test (Mitsuda Test):**
	    - **Purpose:** Discovered by Mitsuda in 1919, this skin test demonstrates a **delayed hypersensitivity reaction** to lepra antigen, indicating an **intact host CMI**.
	    - **NOT used for diagnosis of active infection**.
	    - **Utility:** Primarily used for **classifying leprosy lesions** and as a **prognostic indicator**.
	    - **Reactions:**
	        - **Early or Fernandez Reaction (48 hours):** An induration (>10 mm) indicates a DTH response to lepra antigen, suggesting **past exposure** to _M. leprae_.
	        - **Late or Mitsuda Reaction (21 days):** A nodule (>5 mm) indicates **intact CMI**, which is typically **positive in Tuberculoid (TT) cases** and signifies a **good prognosis**. It is **negative in Lepromatous (LL) cases** due to the absence of adequate CMI, indicating a **poor prognosis**.
