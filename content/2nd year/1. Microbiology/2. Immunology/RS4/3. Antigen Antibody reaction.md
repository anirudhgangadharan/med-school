1. ELISA-principle, types and **applications**
	Here's a concise, high-yield, note-style answer on Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA), optimized for medical school exams:
	
	---
	
	### Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)
	
	**ELISA** is an immunoassay that detects either **antigen (Ag)** or **antibodies (Ab)** in a specimen using an **enzyme-substrate-chromogen system** for detection.
	
	**I. Principle of ELISA**
	
	- **Immunosorbent Component**: An absorbing material (e.g., polystyrene or polyvinyl) is used to specifically absorb the antigen or antibody present in the serum.
	- **Enzyme Labeling**: An enzyme is used to label one of the components of the immunoassay (either antigen or antibody).
	- **Substrate-Chromogen System**:
	    - A substrate-chromogen system is added as the final step.
	    - The enzyme reacts with its **substrate**, which then activates a **chromogen** to produce a **color**.
	    - **Classical Example**: Horseradish peroxidase (enzyme) reacts with hydrogen peroxide (substrate), which activates tetramethyl benzidine (chromogen) to produce a color.
	    - The **color change is detected by spectrophotometry** in an ELISA reader [452, 452f].
	    - **Intensity of color is directly proportional** to the amount of the detection molecule (Ag or Ab) present in the test serum.
	
	**II. General Procedure of ELISA**
	
	- Performed on a **microtiter plate** (96 wells), made of polystyrene, polyvinyl, or polycarbonate material.
	- Involves a series of sequential steps: reagent addition, incubation, and washing (manually or by automated ELISA washer) [453, 456f].
	
	**III. Types of ELISA**
	
	1. **Direct ELISA** [454, 463f]
	    
	    - **Purpose**: Primarily used for **antigen detection** in a test serum.
	    - **Mechanism**: The **primary antibody** (targeted against the serum antigen) is **labeled with the enzyme**.
	    - Steps: Wells are coated with test antigen (from sample) → enzyme-labeled primary antibody is added → substrate-chromogen system added → color develops.
	2. **Indirect ELISA** [455, 463f]
	    
	    - **Purpose**: Commonly used for **antibody detection**, or less commonly, antigen detection.
	    - **Mechanism**: The **secondary antibody** (an anti-species antibody, e.g., anti-human Ig) is **labeled with the enzyme**.
	    - Steps for Antibody Detection: Wells are precoated with antigen → test serum (containing primary antibody) is added → enzyme-labeled secondary antibody (anti-human immunoglobulin) is added → substrate-chromogen system added → color develops.
	3. **Sandwich ELISA** [459, 463f]
	    
	    - **Purpose**: Detects **antigen** in the test serum.
	    - **Mechanism**: The antigen is "sandwiched" between a **capture antibody** and a **detector antibody**.
	    - Steps: Microtiter well is precoated with a **capture antibody** → test serum (containing antigen) is added → enzyme-labeled primary **"detector antibody"** specific for the antigen is added → substrate-chromogen system added → color develops.
	4. **IgM Antibody Capture (MAC) ELISA** [460, 463f]
	    
	    - **Purpose**: Widely used for detecting **IgM antibodies** in various infections.
	    - **Mechanism**: Based on capturing primary IgM antibody (in test serum) on a microtiter plate pre-coated with **anti-human-IgM antibody**, followed by addition of recombinant antigen.
	    - **Signal Amplification**: Often uses an **avidin-biotin system** to amplify the signal, increasing sensitivity.
	    - **Applications**: Used for dengue, Japanese encephalitis, West Nile virus, scrub typhus, leptospirosis, toxoplasmosis. Kits for dengue and Japanese encephalitis are supplied by NIV, Pune.
	5. **Competitive ELISA** [461, 469f]
	    
	    - **Purpose**: Can detect either antigen or antibody.
	    - **Mechanism**: Antigen in the test serum **competes** with another antigen of the same type coated on the well to bind to the primary antibody.
	    - **Interpretation**: Intensity of color is **inversely proportional** to the amount of antigen present in the test serum.
	6. **IgG Avidity ELISA**
	    
	    - **Purpose**: Useful for **differentiating recent from past infection**.
	    - **Principle**: Avidity reflects the maturity of antibodies and generally **increases with time**.
	    - **Interpretation**: Detection of **low avidity IgG indicates recent infection**, while high avidity IgG indicates past infection.
	    - **Applications**: Available for rubella, CMV, VZV, toxoplasmosis, EBV, HIV, viral hepatitis, and West Nile virus infection.
	
	**IV. Advantages of ELISA**
	
	- **Economical** for large numbers of samples.
	- **High sensitivity**, commonly used for **screening tests** in blood banks and tertiary care sites.
	- Improved specificity with purified recombinant/synthetic antigens and monoclonal antibodies.
	
	**V. Disadvantages of ELISA**
	
	- Less preferred for small sample loads compared to rapid tests.
	- **Longer turnaround time** (2–3 hours) compared to rapid tests (10–20 minutes).
	- Requires **expensive equipment** (ELISA washer and reader).
	- Can have **cross-reactivity** with other related organisms (e.g., dengue IgM ELISA with other flaviviruses).
	
	**VI. Applications of ELISA (Clinical Relevance)** ELISA is widely used for both antigen and antibody detection in various infectious diseases and other conditions:
	
	- **Viral Infections**:
	    
	    - **Hepatitis B**: Detection of HBsAg (Hepatitis B surface antigen) and HBeAg (precore antigen), and antibodies (Anti-HBc, Anti-HBs, Anti-HBe).
	    - **Hepatitis C**: HCV antibody detection (3rd generation ELISA). HCV antigen detection also available as an alternative to RNA testing for active infection and monitoring treatment response.
	    - **HIV**: Detection of anti-HIV antibodies (3rd generation ELISA), and p24 antigen (4th generation ELISA, which also detects antibodies). 4th generation ELISA reduces the window period.
	    - **Dengue**: NS1 antigen detection (from day 1 of fever) and MAC-ELISA for IgM antibodies (recommended in India).
	    - **Japanese Encephalitis**: IgM capture antibody (MAC) ELISA using JERA antigen.
	    - **Rickettsial Infections**: ELISA for specific antibody detection. ELISA using 56-kDa recombinant major surface protein antigens for scrub typhus.
	    - **COVID-19**: IgG antibody detection for serosurveillance (not clinical diagnosis).
	    - **CMV**: Antibody detection using various antigens. IgG avidity ELISA to differentiate recent from past infection.
	    - **EBV**: Specific antibody detection (VCA-IgM/IgG, EA).
	    - **Influenza**: Antibody detection by HAI test, neutralization test, and ELISA.
	    - **Mumps**: Antibody detection using whole-virus antigen, nucleoprotein, and hemagglutinin antigens.
	    - **Rubella**: Preferred method for diagnosis.
	- **Bacterial Infections**:
	    
	    - **Brucellosis**: Highly sensitive for IgM, IgG, and IgA antibodies (using cytoplasmic proteins or LPS antigens). Also used to confirm chronic brucellosis.
	    - **Leptospirosis**: IgM and IgG detection. Lepto dipstick assay detects IgM.
	    - **Syphilis**: Treponemal tests (e.g., using _T. pallidum_ specific antigen) and non-treponemal tests (e.g., RPR, VDRL).
	    - **Chlamydial Infections**: Antigen detection (LPS) using monoclonal antibodies. Antibody detection (MIF test) for species and serovar-specific MOMP antigen.
	    - **Q Fever (_Coxiella burnetii_)**: IFA (Indirect Immunofluorescence Assay) is available. Phase II antibody titer for acute, Phase I for chronic.
	    - **Typhoid Fever (_Salmonella Typhi_)**: Typhidot test detects IgM and IgG using 50 kDa OMP antigen. Antigen detection also available.
	    - **Pertussis**: Serum antibodies detection by enzyme immunoassay.
	    - **Legionnaires' Disease**: Urinary antigen test for _L. pneumophila_ serogroup 1. Antibody detection for epidemiological purpose.
	    - **H. pylori**: IgG antibody detection for screening before endoscopy and seroepidemiological studies. Fecal antigen assay also available.
	- **Parasitic Infections**:
	    
	    - **Malaria**: Detection of malarial antigens (pLDH, aldolase, HRP-II) in Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs).
	    - **Leishmaniasis (Visceral)**: Detection of antibodies to rK-39 antigen by ICT. ELISA also available.
	    - **Toxoplasmosis**: Detection of specific IgM or IgA or IgG antibodies. IgG avidity ELISA to differentiate recent from past infection.
	    - **Cysticercosis**: Antibody detection using crude extract of cysticerci or purified glycoprotein antigens. Antigen detection in CSF or serum using monoclonal _T. solium_ antibodies.
	    - **Hydatid Disease**: Antibody detection (using B2t antigen) and DIGFA (dot immunogold filtration assay).
	    - **Lymphatic Filariasis**: ELISA and ICT for filarial antigens (Og4C3 and AD12). Flow-through assay for antibodies.
	    - **Intestinal Amoebiasis**: Antigen detection (coproantigen) in stool using 170 kDa lectin antigen. Antibody detection (IgG) for epidemiological purposes.
	    - **Giardiasis**: Antigen detection in stool (coproantigen) using labeled monoclonal antibodies against cyst wall protein antigens.
	    - **Cryptosporidiosis**: Coproantigen detection using oocyst antigen.
	- **Fungal Infections**:
	    
	    - **Candidiasis**: Antibody detection against cell wall mannan antigen. Antigen detection of cell wall mannan and cytoplasmic antigens.
	    - **Aspergillosis**: Galactomannan antigen detection. β-d-Glucan assay as a marker of invasive fungal infections.
	- **Other Applications**:
	    
	    - **Monitoring of proteins and drug levels** in serum.
	    - **Biomarkers**: Procalcitonin.
	    - **Hormones, tumor markers, cardiac markers**.
	    - **Screening for allergy**.
	    - **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM)** (e.g. anti-malarial drug susceptibility testing by ELISA for HRP-II or pLDH).
	    - **Cancer diagnosis and immunotherapy** (e.g., antibodies like Trastuzumab, Rituximab, Tositumomab, Bevacizumab target cancer cells).
2. Define agglutination and mention the agglutinaion tests
	
	Here's a concise, high-yield, note-style answer on Agglutination Tests, optimized for medical school exams:
	
	---
	
	### Agglutination Tests
	
	**Agglutination** is an immunoassay that detects either **antigens (Ag)** or **antibodies (Ab)**, resulting in the **clumping of particulate or insoluble antigens** when mixed with their corresponding antibodies in the presence of suitable electrolytes, temperature, and pH.
	
	**I. Principle**
	
	- Involves the visible clumping of particles when particulate antigen (e.g., bacteria, RBCs, latex particles coated with Ag) or antibody (e.g., latex particles coated with Ab) reacts with its specific counterpart.
	- Agglutination tests are generally **more sensitive** than precipitation tests.
	
	**II. Types of Agglutination Tests**
	
	1. **Direct Agglutination Tests**
	    
	    - **Principle**: The antigen is naturally particulate (e.g., bacterial cells, RBCs) and directly agglutinates with the antibody.
	    - **Slide Agglutination Test**:
	        - **Purpose**: Primarily used for **bacterial identification** and **serotyping** of bacterial colonies grown in culture. Also used for **blood grouping and cross-matching**.
	        - **Procedure**: Bacterial colony is mixed with saline, then specific antiserum is added. Positive result shows visible clumping.
	        - **Clinical Relevance**: Confirming bacterial isolates (e.g., _Salmonella_ serotyping).
	    - **Tube Agglutination Test**:
	        - **Purpose**: **Quantitative** estimation of antibody titer in serum (highest dilution showing agglutination).
	        - **Applications**:
	            - **Widal Test**: For **typhoid fever**, detects anti-TO, TH, AH, BH antibodies against _Salmonella Typhi_ and _Paratyphi_ A/B. O agglutination forms granular clumps, H agglutination forms fluffy clumps. O antibodies appear early, H antibodies appear late.
	            - **Standard Agglutination Test (SAT)**: For **brucellosis**, detects antibodies against LPS antigen of _Brucella abortus_. Considered **gold standard** for brucellosis serology. A titer ≥1:160 (non-endemic) or ≥1:320 (endemic/occupational exposure) is significant.
	            - **2-Mercaptoethanol (2ME) SAT**: Differentiates IgM (acute) from IgG (chronic) brucellosis, as 2ME destroys IgM.
	    - **Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT)**:
	        - **Purpose**: High-resolution agglutination, read microscopically.
	        - **Applications**: **Leptospirosis** – **gold standard** for diagnosis, detects antibodies against specific _Leptospira interrogans_ serovars.
	2. **Passive (Indirect) Agglutination Tests (for Antibody Detection)**
	    
	    - **Principle**: Soluble antigen is coated onto a carrier particle (e.g., RBCs, latex beads) to make it particulate. Antibody in the sample then causes agglutination of these coated particles.
	    - **Indirect Hemagglutination (IHA) Test**:
	        - **Principle**: Uses RBCs as carrier particles.
	        - **Clinical Relevance**: Less popular currently.
	    - **Latex Agglutination Test (LAT)**:
	        - **Principle**: Polystyrene latex particles are coated with antigen.
	        - **Applications**: Rapid and simple, widely used.
	            - **ASO (Antistreptolysin O) Antibodies**: Detection of antibodies to streptolysin O, indicating recent streptococcal infection.
	            - **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) Factor**: Detection of IgM autoantibody against Fc portion of IgG.
	3. **Reverse Passive Agglutination Tests (for Antigen Detection)**
	    
	    - **Principle**: Antibody is coated onto a carrier particle. Antigen in the sample then causes agglutination of these antibody-coated particles.
	    - **Latex Agglutination Test (LAT)**:
	        - **Principle**: Polystyrene latex particles are coated with specific antibodies.
	        - **Applications**: Widely used for antigen detection.
	            - **C-Reactive Protein (CRP)**: General marker of inflammation.
	            - **Capsular Antigens**: Detection of capsular antigens in CSF (e.g., **pneumococcus, meningococcus, _Cryptococcus_**) for rapid diagnosis of meningitis.
	            - **Streptococcal Grouping**: Identification of _Streptococcus_ groups based on carbohydrate antigens.
	    - **Coagglutination Test**:
	        - **Principle**: _Staphylococcus aureus_ (Protein A) acts as a carrier molecule for antibodies.
	        - **Clinical Relevance**: Formerly used for antigen detection, now largely **obsolete**.
	4. **Hemagglutination Tests**
	    
	    - **Principle**: Involves the agglutination of RBCs. Can be direct antigen-antibody reactions or non-immunological (viral).
	    - **Direct Hemagglutination Test**:
	        - **Principle**: Serum antibodies directly agglutinate with surface antigens of RBCs.
	        - **Applications**:
	            - **Paul-Bunnell Test**: For **infectious mononucleosis** (Epstein-Barr virus), detects heterophile antibodies against sheep RBC antigens.
	            - **Cold Agglutination Test**: For **Mycoplasma pneumonia**, detects antibodies that agglutinate human O blood group RBCs at cold temperatures.
	            - **Blood Grouping (ABO and Rh)**: Determines blood type compatibility.
	            - **Coombs Test (Antiglobulin Test)**: Diagnoses Rh incompatibility by detecting incomplete Rh antibodies on fetal RBCs (direct Coombs) or in maternal serum (indirect Coombs).
	    - **Viral Hemagglutination Test**:
	        - **Principle**: Not an Ag-Ab reaction. Hemagglutinin antigens on certain viruses (e.g., **influenza virus**) bind to receptors on RBCs, causing agglutination.
	    - **Hemagglutination Inhibition (HAI) Test**:
	        - **Principle**: Antibodies in patient's serum inhibit the viral hemagglutination.
	        - **Clinical Relevance**: Formerly used for diagnosis of **influenza** and other viral diseases.
	
	**III. Technical Issues**
	
	- **Prozone Phenomenon**: Occurs when there is **excess antibody** in the serum, leading to false-negative results due to inhibition of lattice formation. Can be overcome by testing serial dilutions of the serum.
	- **Blocking Antibodies**: Incomplete IgG antibodies that bind to antigens but do not cause visible agglutination, preventing other antibodies from binding, thus leading to false-negative results.
3. Tri-dot ELISA applications
	### Tri-Dot ELISA Applications
	
	The **Tri-dot test** is a type of **dot blot assay** or **flow-through assay**, which are rapid/simple diagnostic methods. It is a commercially available test format for **antibody detection**.
	
	**Key Application:**
	
	- **HIV TRI-DOT test:** This is a classical example of a flow-through assay.
	    - **Purpose:** It is used to **detect antibodies to HIV-1 and HIV-2 separately** in a patient's serum.
	    - **Principle:**
	        - The test system is typically in a cassette format with a **nitrocellulose membrane (NCM)**.
	        - The NCM is coated at specific regions with **HIV-1 and HIV-2 antigens**, along with a **control region coated with anti-human Ig**.
	        - When a patient's serum (sample) is added, if **HIV antibodies are present, they bind to the immobilized antigens**.
	        - A **Protein-A conjugate** (present in the buffer) then binds to the **Fc portion of these HIV antibodies**, resulting in **distinct pinkish-purple dots** for HIV-1 and/or HIV-2.
	        - A control dot also forms, confirming the test validity.
	    - **Clinical Relevance:** Provides a rapid and distinct qualitative diagnosis for HIV infection by differentiating between HIV-1 and HIV-2 antibodies.
