### Immunity & Active Immunity

**I. Definition of Immunity**

- **Immunity** is the **resistance offered by the host against microorganism(s) or any foreign substance(s)**.
- It is broadly classified into two main types:
    - **Innate Immunity**: Present from birth.
    - **Acquired/Adaptive Immunity**: Acquired during an individual's lifetime.

**II. Active Immunity: Detailed Description** Active immunity is a type of acquired immunity where the host's immune system is **actively involved in responding to an antigenic stimulus**. This active involvement leads to the production of **immunologically active T cells, B cells, and specific antibodies**.

**A. Key Characteristics of Active Immunity:**

- **Host Involvement**: The host's immune system directly participates in generating the immune response.
- **Mediators**: Primarily mediated by **T cells and B cells**. Other mediators include the Classical complement pathway and cytokines (IL-2, IL-4, IL-5).
- **Time to Develop (Lag Period)**: The immune response takes **several days to weeks to develop** following microbial entry. This delay is known as a **lag period**.
- **Diversity**: Active immunity, as a form of acquired immunity, is effective against a **wide range of antigens**.
- **Immunological Memory**: A crucial feature of active immunity is the development of **immunological memory**.
    - Upon subsequent exposure to the same antigen, a **prompt, powerful, and prolonged secondary immune response** is elicited.
    - This secondary response is **faster** and produces **higher titres of antibodies** (typically IgG), compared to the primary response which produces lower titres of IgM.
- **Duration**: Generally **long-lasting**, though the duration varies depending on the pathogen.
    - It can be **lifelong** for certain viral infections (e.g., chickenpox, measles, smallpox, mumps, rubella).
    - It may be shorter (e.g., influenza virus infection).
    - In some cases (e.g., spirochetes, _Plasmodium_), it may last only as long as the microbe is present, a phenomenon called premunition or concomitant immunity.
- **Effectiveness in Immunocompromised Hosts**: Active immunity **may fail to develop adequately** in immunocompromised hosts due to their impaired immune system.

**B. Types of Active Immunity Induction:** Active immunity can be induced in two ways:

1. **Natural Active Immunity**: Occurs following **exposure to a microbial infection**.
    - _Clinical Example_: **Measles virus infection** naturally induces active immunity.
2. **Artificial Active Immunity**: Develops following exposure to an **immunogen by vaccination**.
    - _Clinical Example_: **Measles vaccine** induces artificial active immunity.

**C. Factors Influencing Immunogenicity (Relevance to Active Immunity):** The ability of an antigen to induce active immunity (immunogenicity) is influenced by several factors:

- **Size of the Antigen**: Larger molecules (e.g., >10,000 Dalton molecular weight) generally induce a stronger immune response.
- **Chemical Nature**: **Proteins are stronger immunogens** than carbohydrates, lipids, and nucleic acids.
- **Structural Complexity**: More complex molecules are typically more immunogenic.
- **Foreignness to the Host**: The greater the phylogenetic distance between the antigen and the host, the stronger the immunogenicity. **Self-antigens are generally not immunogenic** due to immunological tolerance.
- **Dose and Route of Administration**: Optimal antigen doses induce better responses. Parenteral routes generally induce better immunity, though mucosal routes can induce specific IgA responses.
- **Repeated Doses**: Sometimes, repeated doses are needed to generate an adequate and sustained immune response.

**D. Differentiation from Passive Immunity:** The table below highlights the key differences between active and passive immunity:

|Property|Active Immunity|Passive Immunity|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Production**|Actively by host immune system|Immunoglobulins received passively|
|**Induction**|Infection (natural) or Vaccination (artificial)|Mother to fetus IgG transfer (natural) or Readymade antibody transfer (artificial)|
|**Duration**|Long-lasting|Lasts for short time|
|**Lag Period**|Present|No Lag period|
|**Memory**|Present|No memory|
|**Booster Doses**|Useful|Subsequent doses are less effective|
|**Immunodeficient**|Not useful in immunodeficient individuals|Useful in immunodeficient individuals|
