1. Enumerate the immunodeficiency disorders
	Immunodeficiency is a condition where the **body's defence mechanisms are impaired**, leading to an **enhanced susceptibility to microbial infections** and certain forms of cancer.
	
	### Classification & Enumerate Immunodeficiency Diseases
	
	Immunodeficiency diseases are broadly classified into two main categories:
	
	**I. Primary Immunodeficiency Disorders (Genetic/Congenital)** These are typically due to **inherited defects** in the immune system.
	
	2. **B Cell Defects** (Predispose to infections with **encapsulated bacteria**, viruses cleared by neutralising antibodies like enteroviruses, and parasites resisted by secretory IgA like _Giardia lamblia_):
	    
	    - **X-linked Agammaglobulinaemia (Bruton's)**: Total absence of B cells and plasma cells, leading to depressed serum levels of all immunoglobulin classes. Pre-B cells are present in normal numbers in bone marrow.
	    - **Common Variable Immunodeficiency (CVID)**: Heterogeneous group with **hypogammaglobulinaemia** and increased susceptibility to infection, autoimmune disorders, and lymphoid tumours.
	    - **Selective IgA Deficiency**: Most common primary immunodeficiency. Affected individuals are usually asymptomatic or experience recurrent sinopulmonary and gastrointestinal infections. Autoimmune diseases are also common.
	    - **Hyper-IgM Syndrome**: Characterised by normal or increased IgM levels but **deficiency in IgG, IgA, and IgE**. Many cases are X-linked, caused by defects in **CD40L** (CD40 ligand) on T cells, impairing T-cell help to B cells. Patients are susceptible to _Pneumocystis jirovecii_ infection due to impaired macrophage activation and delayed hypersensitivity response.
	3. **Combined Immunodeficiencies (B and T Cell Defects)** (Predispose to severe recurrent infections by a wide array of pathogens including **Candida**, _Pneumocystis jirovecii_, **Cytomegalovirus**, and _Pseudomonas_):
	    
	    - **Severe Combined Immunodeficiencies (SCID)**: Characterised by profound defects in both humoral and cell-mediated immunity. Common underlying mechanisms include cytokine receptor mutations, adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency, and Jak3 mutation.
	    - **Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome (WAS)**: X-linked disorder with **thrombocytopenia**, eczema, and immunodeficiency. Patients have low IgM and normal/elevated IgA/IgE levels and are prone to develop non-Hodgkin B cell lymphomas.
	    - **Ataxia Telangiectasia**: Autosomal recessive disorder with progressive neurological dysfunction (ataxia), telangiectasia (dilated blood vessels) of the conjunctiva, and immunodeficiency (low IgA and IgE).
	    - **Nezelof Syndrome**: Characterised by thymic hypoplasia leading to T cell deficiency.
	4. **Disorders of Phagocytosis** (Predispose to infections with **staphylococci, _Pseudomonas_**, and _Nocardia_):
	    
	    - **Chronic Granulomatous Disease (CGD)**: Defect in **NADPH oxidase**, leading to impaired killing of catalase-positive bacteria and fungi.
	    - **Myeloperoxidase Deficiency**: Impaired killing of microbes due to a defect in myeloperoxidase enzyme.
	    - **Chediak-Higashi Syndrome**: Defect in lysosomal trafficking, affecting phagocyte function.
	    - **Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency (LAD)**: Defect in integrin expression, impairing leukocyte adhesion and migration.
	    - **Lazy Leukocyte Syndrome**: Impaired chemotaxis of neutrophils.
	    - **Job's Syndrome (Hyper-IgE Syndrome)**: High IgE levels, recurrent skin abscesses, and impaired neutrophil chemotaxis.
	5. **Disorders of Complement** (Detailed in Chapter 13):
	    
	    - **Early Complement Component Deficiencies (C1, C2, C3, C4)**: Predispose to **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)**, glomerulonephritis, and pyogenic infections.
	    - **Membrane Attack Complex (MAC) Deficiencies (C5-C9)**: Predispose to **disseminated _Neisseria_ infection**.
	    - **Regulatory Protein Deficiencies (e.g., C1 esterase inhibitor, Properdin, Factor D)**:
	        - C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency leads to **Hereditary Angioneurotic Oedema**.
	        - Properdin and Factor D deficiencies predispose to _Neisseria_ and pyogenic infections.
	
	**II. Secondary Immunodeficiency Disorders (Acquired)** These are more common than primary immunodeficiencies.
	
	6. **Infections**:
	    
	    - **HIV/AIDS**: The most widespread and important secondary immunodeficiency, targeting CD4 T lymphocytes and macrophages. Leads to severe infections, including _Pneumocystis jirovecii_ pneumonia.
	    - **Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)**: Invades lymphocytes and can cause infectious mononucleosis and various malignancies.
	    - **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**: Invades monocytes, polymorphonuclear cells, and lymphocytes, causing hepatitis and congenital infections.
	7. **Malignancies**: Such as multiple myeloma, chronic lymphocytic leukaemia, acute myeloid or lymphocytic leukaemia, and Hodgkin's lymphoma. These lead to abnormalities in immune cell function (e.g., hypergammaglobulinaemia, hypogammaglobulinaemia, granulocytopenia, abnormal T cell function).
	    
	8. **Malnutrition**.
	    
	9. **Protein-losing states** (e.g., underlying proteinuric renal diseases) leading to loss of immunoglobulins.
	    
	10. **Immunosuppressive medications** or **chemotherapy** (e.g., for malignancy or organ transplantation).
