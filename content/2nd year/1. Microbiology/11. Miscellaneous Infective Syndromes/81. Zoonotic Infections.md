1. Plague-clinical types
	**Plague**
	
	- **Aetiology:** Caused by _**Yersinia pestis**_.
	    
	- **Clinical Types / Manifestations:**
	    
	    - **Bubonic Plague**: Characterised by a **swollen inguinal lymph node (bubo)**. This is depicted in the source material as a "Plague manifestation".
	    - **Black Death**: A manifestation of Plague. Depicted in the source material as **gangrene of the toes**, turning the dead digits black. (Note: "Black Death" often refers to severe, often septicemic, forms historically, and the source specifically illustrates gangrene as a manifestation).
	- **Clinical Relevance:** Understanding the clinical manifestations is crucial for early diagnosis and treatment of this potentially life-threatening infection, as highlighted by the mention of clinical types being an expected question topic. _Y. pestis_ can be visualized with bipolar or safety pin appearance using Wayson or methylene blue stain. Laboratory handling requires biosafety level III precautions.
2. Pulmonary plague-pathogenesis
	Pulmonary plague, caused by _Yersinia pestis_, manifests in two primary ways:
	
	**1. Primary Pneumonic Plague:**
	
	- **Transmission:** Inhalation of aerosolized _Y. pestis_ droplets from a person or animal with pneumonic plague.
	- **Pathogenesis:**
	    - The bacteria directly infect the lungs, bypassing the lymph nodes.
	    - _Y. pestis_ multiplies rapidly within the alveoli and interstitial spaces.
	    - This leads to severe inflammation, characterized by:
	        - **Hemorrhagic pneumonia:** Bleeding into the lung tissue.
	        - **Alveolar edema:** Fluid accumulation in the air sacs, impairing gas exchange.
	        - **Necrotizing pneumonia:** Death of lung tissue due to bacterial toxins and inflammation.
	    - The rapid and overwhelming bacterial proliferation and inflammation cause:
	        - **Respiratory failure:** The lungs cannot effectively oxygenate the blood.
	        - **Septic shock:** Systemic inflammatory response leading to dangerously low blood pressure and organ damage as the bacteria enter the bloodstream.
	
	**2. Secondary Pneumonic Plague:**
	
	- **Transmission:** Occurs as a complication of bubonic or septicemic plague.
	- **Pathogenesis:**
	    - _Y. pestis_ bacteria disseminate from the primary site of infection (lymph nodes or bloodstream) to the lungs via the bloodstream.
	    - Once in the lungs, the pathogenesis is similar to primary pneumonic plague, leading to pneumonia, respiratory failure, and potential septic shock.
	
	**Key Virulence Factors Contributing to Pulmonary Plague Pathogenesis (as per general understanding of _Y. pestis_ virulence):**
	
	While the Apurba textbook would provide specific details, generally, the following virulence factors play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of plague, including the pulmonary form:
	
	- **Type III Secretion System (T3SS):** A needle-like apparatus used by _Y. pestis_ to inject effector proteins (Yops - _Yersinia_ outer proteins) into host cells. These Yops interfere with host immune responses, including phagocytosis by macrophages and neutrophil function, and disrupt inflammatory signaling.
	- **Capsular F1 Antigen:** A surface protein that is produced at body temperature and helps the bacteria resist phagocytosis by immune cells.
	- **Plasminogen Activator (Pla):** A protease that can degrade blood clots, potentially aiding in the dissemination of the bacteria and contributing to the hemorrhagic aspects of the disease. It may also interfere with complement activation.
	- **Yersinia murine toxin (Ymt):** While primarily known for its role in flea survival, it might have some impact on mammalian infection.
	- **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS):** A component of the bacterial outer membrane that can trigger a strong inflammatory response in the host. Modifications to the LPS of _Y. pestis_ can help evade early immune detection.
	- **Biofilm formation:** _Y. pestis_ can form biofilms, particularly in the flea, which is crucial for transmission. The role of biofilm formation in the mammalian host during pulmonary plague is still being investigated.
	
	**Progression and Clinical Features:**
	
	Pulmonary plague is the most virulent form of plague due to the direct involvement of the respiratory system and the potential for rapid person-to-person transmission in primary cases.
	
	- **Rapid onset:** Symptoms typically appear within 1-3 days of exposure.
	- **Early symptoms:** Fever, headache, weakness, and rapidly developing pneumonia.
	- **Respiratory symptoms:** Shortness of breath, chest pain, cough (initially dry, then may produce bloody or watery sputum).
	- **Rapid progression:** Without prompt antibiotic treatment (within 24 hours of symptom onset), respiratory failure and death are almost inevitable.
