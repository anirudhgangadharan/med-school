1. Modes of transmission of infection
	Understanding the modes of transmission of infection is **critical for effective diagnosis, treatment, and public health interventions**. Microorganisms gain access to the human body through various routes.
	
	### I. Direct Transmission
	
	Involves **immediate physical contact** between an infected individual/animal and a susceptible host.
	
	- **Person-to-Person Contact:**
	    - **Sexual Contact:**
	        - **HIV:** The **most common mode of transmission**, accounting for 75% of global cases. Non-ulcerative gonococcal infections can enhance HIV transmission.
	        - **_Neisseria gonorrhoeae_ (Gonorrhea):** Transmitted sexually.
	        - **_Chlamydia trachomatis_:** Causes genital chlamydiasis, typically sexually transmitted.
	        - **_Treponema pallidum_ (Syphilis):** Acquired through **sexual contact**; rapidly penetrates minute skin/mucosal abrasions.
	        - **_Haemophilus ducreyi_ (Chancroid):** Transmitted sexually.
	        - **_Klebsiella granulomatis_ (Donovanosis):** Causes genital ulcers, indicating direct contact transmission.
	        - **Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV):** Transmitted via direct contact with lesions or secretions.
	    - **Non-Sexual Physical Contact:**
	        - **Multidrug-Resistant Organisms (e.g., MRSA, MDR _E. coli_, _Klebsiella_):** Spread by **contact** in hospitals, often through contaminated hands of healthcare professionals, or direct contact with other patients or the environment.
	        - **_Bacillus anthracis_ (Cutaneous Anthrax):** Spread by **contact**.
	        - **Scabies (_Sarcoptes scabiei_):** Ectoparasites penetrate the host skin via direct contact.
	        - **Common Cold (Rhinoviruses):** Spread through direct contact.
	        - **_Erysipelothrix rhusiopathiae_ (Erysipeloid):** Transmitted by **direct inoculation** (e.g., via cuts/abrasions).
	        - **Actinomycosis (_Actinomyces_ species):** Primarily **endogenous** (from commensal flora), but infection can result from **trauma** (e.g., dental extraction) allowing spread.
	        - **Bite Wound Infections:** Result from **bites and scratches** from animals or humans.
	        - **_Pasteurella multocida_:** Mainly transmitted by **animal bites**.
	        - **Leprosy (_Mycobacterium leprae_):** Primarily through **nasal droplet infection (aerosols)**, but direct contact transmission (skin) is also mentioned, though it's not highly communicable and requires prolonged contact.
	
	### II. Indirect Transmission (Vehicle-borne and Fomite)
	
	Pathogen is transmitted via an **intermediate inanimate object (fomite) or contaminated inanimate vehicle (food, water, blood)**.
	
	- **Fomite Transmission:**
	    - **COVID-19:** Transmitted via **contact with contaminated surfaces** or objects used by infected individuals (e.g., stethoscopes, thermometers, clothes).
	    - **Mumps Virus:** Can be transmitted via **fomites**.
	- **Food/Water-borne Transmission (Ingestion):**
	    - **_Salmonella_ and _Shigella_:** Transmitted by **ingestion** of contaminated food or water.
	    - **_Vibrio cholerae_:** Transmitted by **ingestion** of contaminated food and water.
	    - **Diarrheagenic _Escherichia coli_:** Transmitted by **ingestion**.
	    - **Agents of Food Poisoning:** Transmitted by **ingestion**.
	    - **_Campylobacter_ species:** Transmitted by ingestion of contaminated food (e.g., poultry) or water.
	    - **_Entamoeba histolytica_:** Acquired by **ingestion of food and water contaminated with mature cysts**.
	    - **_Giardia lamblia_:** Acquired by **ingestion of food and water contaminated with mature cysts**.
	    - **Toxoplasmosis (_Toxoplasma gondii_):** Most common route is **ingestion of contaminated undercooked meat** or sporulated oocysts from contaminated soil/food/water.
	    - **Listeriosis (_Listeria_ species):** Most commonly through **contaminated food** sources (e.g., coleslaw, milk, soft cheeses, ready-to-eat foods).
	    - **Q Fever (_Coxiella burnetii_):** Can be prevented by **pasteurization of milk**.
	    - **Intestinal Helminths (e.g., _Ascaris lumbricoides_, Hookworm):** Often **soil-transmitted helminths** (via contaminated soil or produce). Hookworm larvae can also penetrate skin upon contact with contaminated soil.
	    - **Typhoid Fever (_Salmonella Typhi_):** Linked to carriers excreting bacteria in feces or urine, which can contaminate food/water.
	    - **Cysticercosis:** Acquired by **ingestion of contaminated food/water**.
	- **Blood/Body Fluid Transmission (via contaminated blood products):**
	    - **HIV:** Spreads through **blood transfusion** (risk >90%) and **needle-stick exposure** (risk 0.3%).
	    - **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV):** Common via **blood and blood product transfusion** and **needle prick injuries**. Also through inoculation during surgical/dental procedures or shared razors/toothbrushes.
	    - **Hepatitis C Virus (HCV):** Screened for in blood donations.
	    - **Malaria:** Can be transmitted by blood transfusion.
	    - **Syphilis (_T. pallidum_):** Can be transmitted by **blood transfusion**.
	    - **Transfusion-Transmitted Infections (TTIs):** Various blood-borne pathogens can be transmitted through **blood transfusions** (e.g., HIV, HBV, HCV, Syphilis, Malaria, _Trypanosoma cruzi_, _Babesia_, _Toxoplasma gondii_, _Leishmania donovani_, Prion disease).
	
	### III. Droplet vs. Airborne Transmission
	
	**Key Distinction for Respiratory Pathogens:** Differentiated by particle size and potential travel distance.
	
	- **Droplet Transmission (Large droplets, typically travel <1 meter):**
	    - **Meningococcus (_Neisseria meningitidis_):** Transmitted by **droplet inhalation**.
	    - **_Corynebacterium diphtheriae_:** Transmitted by **droplet inhalation**.
	    - **Pneumococcus (_Streptococcus pneumoniae_):** Transmitted by **droplets**.
	    - **Influenza Virus:** Primarily transmitted via **respiratory droplets**.
	    - **Mumps Virus:** Transmitted through the **respiratory route via droplets**.
	    - **Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV):** Transmitted by **droplets**.
	    - **COVID-19:** Primarily transmitted via **respiratory droplets** and contact routes.
	    - **Whooping Cough (_Bordetella pertussis_):** Highly contagious, spread via droplets.
	    - **Measles:** Transmitted by **droplets**.
	    - **_Mycoplasma pneumoniae_:** Transmitted by **respiratory droplets**.
	    - **_Chlamydophila pneumoniae_:** Transmitted **person to person by inhalational route**.
	- **Airborne Transmission (Small aerosolised particles, can travel long distances):**
	    - **_Mycobacterium tuberculosis_:** Mainly transmitted by **inhalation of droplet nuclei (aerosols)** generated during coughing/sneezing, which can remain suspended for hours.
	    - **COVID-19:** **Airborne precautions** are necessary during **aerosol-generating procedures (AGPs)**.
	    - **Legionnaires' Disease (_Legionella_ species):** Transmitted by **inhalation of aerosols** (e.g., from contaminated water systems like cooling towers or humidifiers).
	    - **Chickenpox (Varicella-Zoster Virus - VZV):** Commonly spread through airborne transmission.
	    - **Plague (_Yersinia pestis_):** Pneumonic plague can be transmitted by **droplet inhalation (man-to-man)**.
	
	### IV. Vector-borne Transmission
	
	Pathogen transmitted by a **living organism (vector)**, typically an arthropod.
	
	- **Mosquitoes (_Anopheles_, _Aedes_, _Culex_, _Mansonia_):**
	    - **Malaria (_Plasmodium_ species):** Transmitted by **Anopheles** mosquitoes.
	    - **Dengue:** Transmitted by **Aedes aegypti** and _Aedes albopictus_ mosquitoes.
	    - **Lymphatic Filariasis (_Wuchereria bancrofti_, _Brugia malayi_):** Transmitted by **mosquitoes** (e.g., _Mansonia_, _Anopheles_, _Aedes_).
	    - **Japanese Encephalitis:** Transmitted by **Culex** mosquitoes.
	- **Ticks:**
	    - **Rickettsial Infections (e.g., _Rickettsia rickettsii_, _R. conorii_):** Transmitted by **ticks**.
	    - **Lyme Disease (_Borrelia burgdorferi_):** Transmitted by **hard ticks** (_Ixodes scapularis_).
	    - **Endemic Relapsing Fever (_Borrelia duttonii_, _B. hermsii_):** Transmitted by **soft ticks** (_Ornithodoros_ species).
	    - **Babesiosis:** Transmitted by **ticks**.
	    - **Tularemia (_Francisella tularensis_):** Transmitted by **biting insects** (especially ticks and tabanid flies).
	- **Lice (_Pediculus humanus corporis_):**
	    - **Epidemic Typhus (_Rickettsia prowazekii_):** Transmitted by **human body louse**.
	    - **Epidemic Relapsing Fever (_Borrelia recurrentis_):** Transmitted by **louse**.
	- **Fleas (e.g., Rat Flea _Xenopsylla cheopis_):**
	    - **Plague (_Yersinia pestis_):** Transmitted by **rat fleas**.
	- **Sandflies:**
	    - **Leishmaniasis (_Leishmania_ species):** Transmitted by **sandflies**.
	- **Tsetse Fly:**
	    - **African Trypanosomiasis (_Trypanosoma brucei_):** Transmitted by **Tsetse fly**.
	- **Reduviid Bugs:**
	    - **Chagas' Disease (American Trypanosomiasis) (_Trypanosoma cruzi_):** Transmitted by **reduviid bugs**.
	- **Other Animals (Zoonotic - often indirect via contact with excretions/secretions):**
	    - **Leptospirosis (_Leptospira interrogans_):** Contact with **rodent urine**.
	    - **Brucellosis (_Brucella_ species):** Primarily a **zoonotic disease** affecting domestic animals (sheep, goat, cattle), transmitted to humans via contact with infected animals or their products.
	
	### V. Vertical Transmission (Mother-to-Child Transmission - MTCT)
	
	Transmission from **mother to baby**, occurring **transplacentally** (congenital), **during delivery** (intrapartum), or **after delivery** (postnatal, e.g., via breast milk).
	
	- **Transplacental (Congenital Infections):**
	    - **HIV:** Parent to child transmission risk 13–40%.
	    - **Syphilis (_T. pallidum_):** **Transplacental** transmission.
	    - **Toxoplasmosis (_T. gondii_):** **Mother-to-fetus** transmission.
	    - **Cytomegalovirus (CMV):** A common congenital infection (part of TORCH).
	    - **Rubella:** Causes congenital rubella syndrome.
	    - **Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV):** Causes congenital varicella.
	    - **Zika Virus:** Associated with congenital Zika syndrome.
	- **During Birth (Birth Canal):**
	    - **_Listeria_ species:** Vertical transmission from mother to fetus during birth.
	    - **_Streptococcus agalactiae_ (Group B Streptococcus):** Acquired through the infected **birth canal**, common cause of neonatal meningitis.
	    - **Neonatal Herpes (HSV):** Acquired during birth.
	    - **Gonococcal Infection:** Through the infected birth canal.
	    - **_Chlamydia trachomatis_:** Causes inclusion conjunctivitis and infant pneumonia in neonates.
	- **Breast Milk:**
	    - **HTLV-I:** From **mother to child especially via breast milk** (most common route of HTLV-I transmission).
	
	### VI. Iatrogenic/Healthcare-Associated Transmission (HAI)
	
	Infections acquired in a healthcare facility, typically **48 hours after admission**, that were neither present nor incubating at admission.
	
	- **Central Lines/Devices:**
	    - **Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infection (CRBSI):** A major healthcare-associated infection resulting from mishandling during central line insertion or maintenance. Common agents include coagulase-negative staphylococci (CoNS), _S. aureus_, Gram-negative organisms (e.g., _Acinetobacter_, _Pseudomonas_, _Klebsiella_, _E. coli_, _Serratia_), and _Candida_ species.
	- **Surgical Procedures/Trauma:**
	    - **Brain Abscess:** Can occur via **direct inoculation** as a result of trauma or surgery.
	    - **Shunt Infections:** Normal skin flora (CoNS, _S. aureus_, _Propionibacterium acnes_) introduced during surgery; Gram-negative organisms and _Candida_ species are also found.
	    - **Surgical Site Wound Infections:** Common portal of entry for bacteremia.
	    - **Healthcare-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP):** The normal respiratory flora of hospitalized patients is often replaced by **multidrug-resistant (MDR) Gram-negative organisms** from the hospital environment (e.g., _Pseudomonas_, _Acinetobacter_). Endotracheal intubation can damage respiratory epithelium, allowing direct access to the lower respiratory tract.
	    - **Post-Trauma/Injection Abscess:** Caused by **Rapid Growers NTM** like _M. fortuitum_ and _M. chelonae_.
	- **Invasive Procedures:**
	    - **General Bacteremia:** Invasive procedures or extensive surgeries allow bacteria to access the bloodstream.
	- **Suppression of Normal Flora:**
	    - **_Clostridioides difficile_ Infection (CDI):** **Prolonged antimicrobial use** disrupts normal colonic flora, enhancing susceptibility. Spores are widely found in hospitals.
	- **Compromised Host:**
	    - **Fungal Bloodstream Infections (_Candida_ species):** Account for 8-10% of all nosocomial bloodstream infections. Immunosuppression and prolonged survival of debilitated patients increase risk.
	
	### VII. Environmental Exposure
	
	Exposure to pathogens from the **natural environment** (soil, water, air, animal excreta).
	
	- **Soil/Water:**
	    - **_Nocardia_ species:** Environmental saprophytes found in **soil and vegetations**.
	    - **_Burkholderia pseudomallei_ (Melioidosis):** A saprophyte of **soil and water**.
	    - **Free-living Amoebae (_Naegleria fowleri_, _Acanthamoeba_ species):** Widely distributed in **soil and water**.
	    - **_Coccidioides_ species:** A dimorphic **soil-dwelling fungus**.
	- **Animal Excreta:**
	    - **Q Fever (_Coxiella burnetii_):** Can be prevented by proper disposal of **animal excreta**.
	- **Unknown Portal of Entry:**
	    - In up to **25% of cases, the portal of entry for bacteremia remains uncertain**.
	
	**High-Yield Clinical Pearls for Exams:**
	
	- **Droplet vs. Airborne:** **Particle size and travel distance are key.** Droplets are larger and fall rapidly (e.g., Flu, Meningitis), while aerosols are smaller and remain suspended, traveling further (e.g., TB, Legionellosis).
	- **Antimicrobial Misuse:** A major driver for the emergence and spread of **acquired resistance**, notably in MDR and MRSA strains, creating selective pressure for resistant bacteria to thrive.
	- **Hospital vs. Community:** Recognise that many pathogens have **distinct epidemiology** depending on whether they are acquired in healthcare settings (HAIs) or the community. HAIs often involve more resistant organisms due to selective pressure.
	- **Infective Dose:** Varies significantly by pathogen (e.g., _Shigella_ requires a low dose of 10-100 bacilli, while _E. coli_ and _Vibrio cholerae_ require much higher doses of 10^6-10^8 bacilli).
	- **Host Factors:** Host immune status, age extremes, and underlying conditions (e.g., diabetes, splenectomy) significantly influence susceptibility and disease presentation.
