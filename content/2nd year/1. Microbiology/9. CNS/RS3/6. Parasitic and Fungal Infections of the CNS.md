1. Cryptococcus neoformans/cryptococosis (cryptococcal/fungal meningitis) morphology cultural characters and laboratory diagnosis
	**Cryptococcus neoformans / Cryptococcosis (Cryptococcal Meningitis)**
	
	- **Causative Agent:** _**Cryptococcus neoformans**_, a **capsulated yeast**.
	- **Clinical Relevance:** Primary CNS pathogen. Causes potentially **fatal meningitis** in **HIV-infected individuals**, especially with **CD4 T cell counts less than 200/µL**. Presents as **chronic meningitis**. _C. gattii_ can cause infection in immunocompetent individuals.
	
	**Morphology**
	
	- Seen as **round budding yeast cells**.
	- Possesses a prominent **polysaccharide capsule**.
	- Capsule is visualised as a clear, **refractile delineated space** surrounding the yeast cell in negative stains like **India ink**.
	- Capsule can be twice as thick as the diameter of the yeast cell.
	- Appears as gram-positive round budding yeast cells on Gram staining.
	
	**Cultural Characters**
	
	- Grows on standard media: **SDA (without antibiotics)**, blood agar, or chocolate agar.
	- Incubation temperature: **37°C**.
	- Colony appearance: **Mucoid creamy white** and yeast-like colonies. The mucoid appearance is due to the capsule.
	- Specific media for identification:
	    - **Niger seed agar** or **bird seed agar** used to demonstrate **melanin production** (colonies turn brown/black).
	- Biochemical tests: **Urease positive**. Assimilates inositol and nitrate.
	
	**Laboratory Diagnosis**
	
	- **Specimens:** Primarily **CSF**. Also blood, skin scrapings.
	- **Direct Detection:**
	    - **CSF Microscopy:**
	        - **India ink stain (or Nigrosin):** Used for **negative staining** to visualise the capsule as a halo around the yeast cells. **Sensitivity is 60-70%**.
	        - Gram stain: May show gram-positive round budding yeast cells.
	        - Other stains: **Mucicarmine stain** (stains cell wall), **Masson-Fontana stain** (demonstrates melanin).
	    - **Antigen Detection:**
	        - Detection of **capsular antigens** in CSF or serum using the **Latex Agglutination Test**.
	        - This is a **rapid, highly sensitive (95%), and specific** method.
	- **Culture:**
	    - Inoculate CSF onto SDA (without antibiotics), blood agar, or chocolate agar and incubate at 37°C.
	    - Blood culture uses biphasic bottles.
	    - Colony morphology aids identification (mucoid, creamy white).
	    - Culture allows for confirmation and further identification tests.
	- **Identification:**
	    - Based on colonial morphology, microscopic appearance, and biochemical tests.
	    - Key tests: **Melanin production** on Niger seed/bird seed agar, **positive urease test**, growth at 37°C.
	    - Automated systems (MALDI-TOF) can also be used.
