1. Healthcare associated infections
	### **Healthcare-Associated Infections (HAIs)**
	
	**I. Definition & Terminology**
	
	- **Healthcare-Associated Infection (HAI)** refers to a **new infection acquired by a patient in a healthcare facility (HCF) 48 hours or more after admission**, and which was **neither present nor incubating at the time of admission**.
	- The relevance of traditional terms like "hospital-associated" or "nosocomial" has diminished as care shifts to ambulatory settings.
	
	**II. Clinical Significance & Epidemiology**
	
	- HAIs are among the **most common infections globally**.
	- They constitute **major healthcare challenges**, impacting patient outcomes and healthcare costs.
	- **Device-related infections** such as Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infections (CRBSI) are a significant part of HAIs.
	- Bloodstream infections, including HAIs, can have serious immediate consequences such as shock, multiple organ failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulopathies (DIC).
	
	**III. Risk Factors for HAIs**
	
	- **Immunosuppression**.
	- Use of **broad-spectrum antimicrobial agents** can suppress normal flora, leading to resistant strain emergence.
	- **Invasive procedures or extensive surgeries** allowing bacterial access to blood.
	- **Prolonged survival** of debilitated patients.
	- **Indwelling urinary catheters** are the single most important risk factor for CAUTI.
	- **Hospitalized patients** are at increased risk of pneumonia, often due to ventilators (VAP).
	- **Poor hygienic conditions**, close contact, contaminated materials, and individuals with damaged skin contribute to MRSA outbreaks.
	
	**IV. Major Types of HAIs (High-Yield Examples)**
	
	1. **Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infections (CRBSI)**
	    
	    - Occur due to mishandling during central line insertion or maintenance.
	    - A major healthcare-associated infection.
	    - **Common organisms**: Coagulase-negative staphylococci (CoNS) and _Staphylococcus aureus_. Gram-negative organisms (_Acinetobacter, Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, E. coli, Serratia_) and _Candida_ species have also been recovered.
	    - Automated blood culture systems like BacT/ALERT can help in diagnosis by determining differential time to positivity.
	2. **Surgical Site Infections (SSI)**
	    
	    - Among the **most common HAIs**, occurring after nearly 3% of all surgical procedures.
	    - Source often includes the **patient's own flora** (_S. aureus_ being most common), or environmental sources (e.g., _Pseudomonas_, _Acinetobacter_, _E. coli_, _Klebsiella_).
	    - Burn wound infections are a specific type of SSI.
	3. **Healthcare-Associated Pneumonia (HAP)** & **Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP)**
	    
	    - Majority of HAP cases are VAP.
	    - Normal respiratory flora of hospitalized patients is quickly replaced by **multidrug-resistant (MDR) Gram-negative organisms** such as _Pseudomonas_, _Acinetobacter_, _E. coli_, _Klebsiella pneumoniae_, _Enterobacter species_, and _Serratia marcescens_.
	    - **MRSA** is also a significant cause.
	    - Endotracheal intubation damages respiratory epithelium, allowing oropharyngeal bacteria access to the lower respiratory tract.
	4. **Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infections (CAUTI)**
	    
	    - The **most common type of HAI**.
	    - MDR Gram-negative bacilli are important causes.
	    - _Enterococci_ are major healthcare-associated pathogens causing UTIs, especially _E. faecium_ due to its drug resistance.
	5. **_Clostridioides difficile_ Infection (CDI)**
	    
	    - A major cause of healthcare-associated infection, linked to **prolonged hospital stay** and **prolonged antimicrobial use** which disrupts normal colonic flora.
	    - Hypervirulent strains (e.g., BI/NAP1/027 ribotype) produce higher levels of toxins.
	6. **Multidrug-Resistant Organisms (MDROs)**
	    
	    - **MRSA** (_Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus_): Resistance mediated by _mecA_ gene, altering penicillin-binding protein (PBP). Leading cause of HAIs.
	    - **Vancomycin-resistant _Staphylococcus aureus_ (VRSA)** and **Vancomycin-intermediate _Staphylococcus aureus_ (VISA)**: VRSA is mediated by the _vanA_ gene, often acquired from _Enterococcus faecalis_.
	    - **Carbapenem-resistant _Enterobacteriaceae_ (CRE)**: E.g., _E. coli_.
	    - **Drug-resistant _Pseudomonas aeruginosa_** and _**Acinetobacter**_: Notorious for widespread resistance and biofilm formation.
	
	**V. Prevention & Control Measures**
	
	- **Standard Precautions (SPs)** are the minimum infection prevention practices for all patient care.
	    - **Hand Hygiene**: **Most effective method** for preventing transmission; follow WHO's 'My Five Moments'.
	    - **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)**: Includes gloves, gowns, medical masks, N95 respirators, and face shields. Proper **donning and doffing** are essential.
	    - **Environmental Cleaning & Disinfection**: Routine cleaning of patient-care environments and frequent disinfection of high-touch surfaces (e.g., 70% alcohol). **Terminal disinfection** after patient discharge/transfer is important.
	    - **Safe Specimen Handling**: Collect cultures **before antibiotic therapy**. Use sterile, tightly sealed, labelled containers. Immediate transport and processing for precious specimens like CSF and blood cultures.
	    - **Biomedical Waste Management**: Proper segregation, collection, storage, transport, and disposal.
	    - **Needle Stick Injury Prevention & Management**: Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is crucial for HCW safety.
	- **Antimicrobial Stewardship**: Optimizing antibiotic use to combat resistance. **De-escalation approach** is usually followed for bloodstream infections.
	- **Infection Control Programs**: Comprehensive programs are needed, especially in transplant patients.
	- **Screening**: Screening of MRSA carriers among hospital staff during outbreaks.
	
	**VI. Clinical Relevance**
	
	- Understanding HAIs is fundamental to **preventing their spread**, protecting vulnerable patients (e.g., immunocompromised), and ensuring a safe healthcare environment.
	- Timely detection of the causative agent is crucial for bloodstream infections to prevent shock and organ failure.
