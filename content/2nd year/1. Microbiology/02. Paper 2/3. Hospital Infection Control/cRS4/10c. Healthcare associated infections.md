1. Standard precuations for healthcare delivery
	### **Standard Precautions in Healthcare Delivery**
	
	Standard Precautions (SPs) are the **minimum infection prevention practices** that apply to **all patient care**, regardless of suspected or confirmed infection status, in any healthcare setting. Their primary goal is to protect both healthcare workers (HCWs) and patients from the spread of infections.
	
	**Key Components (High-Yield for Exams):**
	
	2. **Hand Hygiene**
	    
	    - **Most effective method for preventing transmission** of infections, particularly crucial during outbreaks like COVID-19.
	    - Perform when **opportunity arises** (e.g., before/after patient contact, after body fluid exposure) following **WHO's 'My Five Moments for Hand Hygiene'**.
	    - Use correct technique and **appropriate duration** (e.g., 20-40 seconds for alcohol-based hand rub).
	3. **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)**
	    
	    - Used to **prevent exposure** to blood, body fluids, secretions, excretions, and contaminated items.
	    - **Gloves, gowns, medical masks (3-ply), N95 respirators, and face shields/goggles** are examples.
	    - **HCWs caring for COVID-19 suspects** require a medical mask, gloves, gown, and face shield; an **N95 respirator** is used for aerosol-generating procedures (AGPs).
	    - **Universal masking** (e.g., cloth/fabric mask) is recommended for anyone entering a healthcare facility, and **targeted continuous medical mask use** for HCWs in non-COVID areas.
	    - Masks should be changed if soiled, wet, or damaged, or at the end of the shift; **avoid touching the front of the mask** or hanging it around the neck.
	    - Proper **donning and doffing** procedures are essential.
	4. **Safe Specimen Handling & Collection**
	    
	    - **Always collect specimens before starting antimicrobial therapy** if possible. If antibiotics are ongoing, collect just before the next dose.
	    - Collect blood cultures in **pairs from two separate venipuncture sites** with **two separate skin decontamination processes**.
	    - **Skin preparation** should be in a **circular motion (5 cm diameter), from center to periphery**, and allowed to **air dry** before venipuncture.
	    - **Adequate blood volume** is critical (e.g., 8-10 mL per bottle for adults).
	    - **CSF** and other sterile body fluids, ocular, tissue, and bone specimens require **immediate transport** (<15 minutes) and processing; blood culture bottles should be **immediately incubated**.
	    - Use **sterile, tightly sealed, leak-proof, wide-mouth, screw-capped containers**.
	    - **Proper labelling** with patient name, age, gender, treating physician, diagnosis, antibiotic history, specimen type, and desired investigation is **paramount**.
	    - **Specimen rejection criteria** apply for improperly labelled, contaminated, or sub-optimal samples (e.g., insufficient quantity, delayed transit, formalin contamination).
	5. **Environmental Cleaning & Disinfection**
	    
	    - **Routine cleaning and disinfection of patient-care environment** is crucial.
	    - **High-touch surfaces** (e.g., lift buttons, door handles) should be **frequently disinfected with 70% alcohol**.
	    - Equipment and patient care items (e.g., stethoscopes, BP apparatus) should be cleaned with 70% alcohol.
	    - **Terminal disinfection** is performed in patient rooms after discharge/transfer.
	6. **Biomedical Waste Management**
	    
	    - Proper segregation, collection, storage, transport, and disposal of medical waste to prevent environmental contamination and potential health hazards.
	7. **Needle Stick Injury Prevention & Management**
	    
	    - Minimizing sharp injuries is essential for HCW safety.
	    - **Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)** is required after occupational exposures (e.g., needle stick or sharp injury) to reduce transmission risk.
	    - Hospitals should have a **nodal center for PEP management** and provide it free of cost.
	    - AETCOM highlights **confidentiality** and **counselling** related to patient HIV/HBV status following such injuries.
	
	**Clinical Relevance:** These precautions are fundamental to **preventing Healthcare-Associated Infections (HAIs)**, protecting both patients and healthcare personnel from infectious agents, and maintaining a safe clinical environment. They are especially critical in managing communicable diseases and protecting vulnerable populations (e.g., immunocompromised patients).
