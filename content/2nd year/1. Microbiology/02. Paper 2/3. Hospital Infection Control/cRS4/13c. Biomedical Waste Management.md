1. Management and counselling of needle stick injury
	### **Management & Counselling of Needle Stick Injury (NSI)**
	NSI is a significant **occupational hazard** for healthcare workers (HCWs) and a major concern for **Healthcare-Associated Infection (HAI) prevention**. Effective management and counselling are crucial for HCW safety and preventing pathogen transmission.
	
	**I. Immediate Actions Post-Exposure:**
	
	2. **Local Wound Care:**
	    - **Physical Cleansing:** Thoroughly **wash all bite wounds and scratches with soap and water for 15 minutes**.
	    - **Chemical Inactivation:** Use **antiseptics** such as **povidone iodine or alcohol** to inactivate residual viruses.
	    - **Avoid Suturing (if possible):** Suturing can help spread viruses by causing local tissue damage. If deep wounds _must_ be sutured, do so **loosely and only after Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) is infiltrated into the wound**.
	    - **Debridement:** Remove devitalised tissues.
	    - **General Measures:** Ensure **tetanus prophylaxis** and **antibiotic treatment** to prevent secondary bacterial infection. **Do not touch the wound with bare hands** or apply irritants (e.g., soil, oil, lime, herbs, chalk, betel leaves).
	
	**II. Risk Assessment:**
	
	- Assess the **source patient's infection status** (e.g., HIV, HBV, HCV, Rabies).
	- Consider the **type of exposure** (e.g., percutaneous injury, mucocutaneous exposure, licks on broken skin, transdermal bites with oozing blood).
	
	**III. Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP):**
	
	- **General Principles:**
	    
	    - PEP is required to **reduce the risk of transmission** after occupational exposures.
	    - Every hospital should have a **nodal centre for PEP management** and **must provide PEP free of cost to the employee**.
	    - Timely initiation of PEP is critical.
	- **Pathogen-Specific PEP:**
	    
	    1. **HIV PEP:**
	        - Administer **antiretroviral drugs**.
	        - The NACO (National AIDS Control Organization, India) recommended first-line ART regimen for adults includes a combination of **tenofovir, lamivudine, and efavirenz (TLE regimen)**.
	        - **Counselling** is an integral part of HIV PEP.
	    2. **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) PEP:**
	        - Management depends on the source patient's HBV status and the HCW's vaccination/immunity status [Information from other sources, not directly in provided text].
	    3. **Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) PEP:**
	        - No established PEP regimen, but monitoring and early treatment are crucial [Information from other sources, not directly in provided text].
	    4. **Rabies PEP:**
	        - **Category III (Major Risk) Exposures:** Require **local wound care, rabies vaccine, and rabies immunoglobulin (RIG)**.
	        - **Rabies Vaccine:**
	            - **Type:** Cell line derived **non-neural vaccines** are recommended (e.g., Purified Chick Embryo Cell (PCEC) vaccine, Purified Vero Cell (PVC) vaccine, Human Diploid Cell (HDC) vaccine).
	            - **Site:** Preferred site is the **deltoid area for adults** and the **anterolateral area of the thigh for children (<2 years)**; **never administered in the gluteal region**.
	            - **Dose:** One intradermal (ID) dose is 0.1 mL; one intramuscular (IM) dose is an entire vial, irrespective of size.
	            - **ID PEP Regimen (2-2-2): 2-site ID vaccine given on days 0, 3, and 7**. This is cost-effective and time-saving.
	            - **IM PEP Regimens:** Total four doses. One common schedule is 1-site IM vaccine given on days 0, 3, 7, and the fourth dose between days 14-28.
	        - **Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG):**
	            - Available as human RIG (hRIG) and equine RIG (eRIG), both with similar clinical outcomes.
	            - **Maximum dose is 20 IU (hRIG) or 40 IU (eRIG) per kg body weight**; no minimum dose.
	            - **Administration:** **Infiltrated as much as possible into the wound**.
	            - **Action:** If correctly administered, RIG neutralises the virus at the wound site within a few hours.
	
	**IV. Counselling & Ethical Considerations (AETCOM Principles):**
	
	- **Confidentiality:**
	    - **Patient's personal and health information (including laboratory results)**, particularly for sensitive conditions like HIV, **should not be shared with others without the patient's explicit consent**, even close relatives or employers.
	    - **Exceptions (Privileged Communication):** A doctor may, after attempting to convince the patient to disclose, directly inform individuals at risk (e.g., spouse, HCWs involved in patient care) or an authority, when there are corresponding legal, social, or moral obligations.
	- **Informed Consent:** **Written permission** should be obtained from the patient for HIV testing after explaining the potential implications, risks, and advantages of the possible test results.
	- **Counselling:**
	    - **Confidential communication** between the HCW and a counsellor is essential to understand the risk, cope with stress, and make informed personal decisions related to HIV/AIDS.
	    - Counsellors should provide information and support to help the individual cope with the diagnosis and induce behavioural changes.
	    - **Sensitivity:** When breaking results to HCWs, **sympathy and proper counselling** are paramount, maintaining confidentiality regarding occupational hazards.
	- **ICTC (Integrated Counselling and Testing Center) Method (for HIV):**
	    - Ensures privacy during interaction with the patient.
	    - **Pre-test counselling and written informed consent** are taken before sample collection.
	    - Reports are generated **without the patient's name**, using identification marks (e.g., moles, scars), age, and gender.
	    - **Post-test counselling** is provided, and the report is handed directly to the patient, ensuring confidentiality throughout the process.

	- **Standard Precautions:** Always adhere to standard precautions for collecting and handling all specimens.
	- **Safe Handling of Sharps:** Proper handling and disposal of needles and sharps is key to prevention.
