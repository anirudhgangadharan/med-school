1. Lab diagnosis of Pertussis
	1. **Laboratory Diagnosis of Pertussis**
		- **Causative Agent:** _Bordetella pertussis_, a gram-negative coccobacillus.
	- **Specimen Collection:**
	    - **Nasopharyngeal secretions** are the best specimens.
	    - Obtained by: Nasopharyngeal aspiration (best method) or pernasal swab. Throat and sputum specimens are unacceptable.
	    - **Swabs:** Alginate or dacron swabs for culture are best. Dacron or rayon swabs are recommended for PCR. Cotton swabs are not satisfactory for culture, though charcoal-impregnated cotton swabs may be useful.
	    - Collect **six swabs** at 1-2 day intervals for maximum yield (for culture).
	    - **Timing:** Culture is positive mainly in the first **three weeks** (catarrhal stage). PCR remains positive in the first **four weeks**.
	    - **Transport:** Process immediately. If delay is expected, use suitable **charcoal-based medium** (Amies, Regan-Lowe, Jones-Kendrick).
	- **Direct Smear Microscopy:**
	    - Specimens: Nasopharyngeal secretions.
	    - Gram stain may show gram-negative coccobacilli and pus cells.
	    - **Direct Immunofluorescence Test (DIF):** Uses fluorescein labelled antibodies to detect _B. pertussis_ directly. Poor sensitivity (18-75%) and specificity (65-99%), therefore **not widely used**.
	- **Culture Methods:**
	    - Considered the **gold standard method** of diagnosis.
	    - _B. pertussis_ is **fastidious** and a **strict aerobe**.
	    - Requires special complex media: **Regan-Lowe medium** or **Bordet-Gengou glycerine-potato-blood agar**.
	    - Incubation: 35–37°C.
	    - Growth: Colonies appear after 3–5 days.
	    - Colony Morphology: Greyish white, convex with a shiny surface, described as **mercury drops** or **bisected pearls appearance**.
	    - Culture Smear: Gram-staining shows small, ovoid coccobacilli in loose clumps, giving a **thumb print appearance**.
	    - Timing: Culture is typically positive only during the first **three weeks** of infection (catarrhal stage). Becomes negative within 5 days of starting antibiotics.
	    - Sensitivity: More sensitive than microscopy (DIF).
	- **Molecular Methods (PCR):**
	    - Increasingly used, replacing culture, due to increased **sensitivity, specificity, and quicker results**.
	    - PCR remains positive in the first **four weeks** of symptom onset.
	    - Targets: Most common targeted genes are **IS481** and the **pertussis toxin promoter region genes**.
	    - Formats: Real-time PCR is more sensitive, specific, and can be quantitative. Multiplex PCRs (e.g., BioFire FilmArray respiratory panel) can detect multiple respiratory pathogens simultaneously.
	- **Antibody Detection (Serology):**
	    - Method of Choice: Enzyme immunoassays (EIAs) using purified antigens like pertussis toxin, FHA, and pertactin.
	    - Diagnosis: Demonstration of a rise of IgG antibodies in paired sera or detection of IgA or IgM antibodies provides definite diagnosis.
	    - Limitation: Antibodies are also elevated in immunized people.
	- **Identification from Culture:**
	    - Can be done by automated methods such as **MALDI-TOF** or **VITEK**.
	    - Typing: Serotyping and genotyping can be used for epidemiological studies.
