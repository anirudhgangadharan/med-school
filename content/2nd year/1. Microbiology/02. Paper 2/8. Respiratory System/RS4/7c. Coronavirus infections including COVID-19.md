The resident doctor who was also not wearing the mask, took the history and examined the patient. His throat swab was sent for COVID-19 testing, which turned out to be positive. 	**Case Scenario Analysis: Suspected COVID-19 Transmission in Hospital**
	
	**a. Four Respiratory Viruses** Common microbial pathogens implicated in respiratory tract infections (RTIs) include viruses. Four respiratory viruses mentioned in the sources are:
	
	- Influenza viruses
	- Coronaviruses (including the virus causing COVID-19)
	- Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV)
	- Adenoviruses
	
	**b. Laboratory Diagnosis of this Disease (COVID-19)** The disease described is COVID-19, caused by SARS-CoV-2. Laboratory diagnosis involves several methods:
	
	- **Specimens:** Preferred specimens are throat (oropharyngeal) and nasal swabs. Nasopharyngeal swab, bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL), or tracheal aspirate are alternative specimens. Specimens should be dipped in viral transport media (VTM).
	- **NAAT (Nucleic Acid Amplification Testing):** This is a primary method.
	    - **Formats:** Real time RT-PCR, automated formats like CBNAAT and Truenat.
	    - **Gene Targets:** Screening targets include E, N, M genes. Confirmatory targets include RdRp, N2 genes. CBNAAT uses E gene (screening) and N2 gene (confirmation). Truenat uses E gene (screening).
	    - Real-time RT-PCR is considered the **gold standard** diagnostic method for detecting agents of viral pneumonia, including SARS-CoV-2. Interpretation involves observing amplification curves.
	- **Antigen Detection Assay:** Point-of-care tests detect nucleocapsid protein antigen in nasopharyngeal swabs.
	- **Antibody (IgG) Detection Assay:** Used for serosurveillance and surveys in high-risk groups, **not for clinical diagnosis**. Antibodies typically appear later in the disease course.
	- **Sequencing:** Used to determine mutations in the viral genome.
	- **Viral Culture:** Used for research purposes.
	- **Non-Specific Tests:**
	    - Radiology (chest CT scan): May show **ground-glass appearance** and/or consolidation. Ground-glass opacity is a feature of interstitial pneumonia.
	    - Biomarkers: Elevated IL-6 (**cytokine storm**), elevated D-dimer (coagulopathy), elevated serum ferritin (inflammation), severe lymphopenia, and elevated C-reactive protein (acute inflammation) can be seen, particularly in the setting of ARDS.
	
	**c. Infection Control Breaches Identified** The case describes several breaches in infection control protocols:
	
	- The patient, presenting with symptoms like dry cough, sore throat, and fever, was **not wearing a mask** upon entering the hospital. Anyone entering a healthcare facility, regardless of activity, must wear a face mask (e.g., cloth/fabric mask). This is referred to as **'universal masking'**.
	- The security guard guided the symptomatic patient without wearing a mask. This violates the requirement for individuals in a healthcare facility to wear masks.
	- The resident doctor took history and examined the symptomatic patient without wearing a mask. Healthcare workers (HCWs) giving care to COVID-19 suspects should wear a medical (3-ply) mask, a pair of gloves, gown, and a face shield. For AGPs, an N95 respirator is recommended. Examining a patient involves close contact, necessitating PPE, including a mask.
	
	**d. Infection Control Measures and Note on Vaccines** Prevention of transmission for this condition (COVID-19) involves several measures:
	
	- **Hand Hygiene:** Strict hand hygiene must be followed, using the correct technique for an appropriate duration (20-40 seconds for hand rub).
	- **Isolation:** Patients should be kept in an isolation room or cohorting should be followed.
	- **Containment of Coughs and Sneezes:** This includes respiratory hygiene and cough etiquette, such as wearing a medical mask by all symptomatic individuals, and handwashing immediately after sneezing or coughing.
	- **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** HCWs caring for suspects/patients require a medical mask (or N95 for AGPs), gloves, gown, and face shield. Patients should also wear a mask. HCWs in non-COVID areas should wear a medical mask (targeted continuous medical mask use).
	- **Work Restriction:** People with influenza-like illness should stay home until at least 24 hours after being fever-free without medication.
	- **Environmental Cleaning:** Surfaces and floors can harbour SARS-CoV-2. Cleaning with detergent followed by disinfection with 0.5% sodium hypochlorite is recommended. Equipment and high touch surfaces should be disinfected with 70% alcohol. Terminal disinfection is needed in patient rooms after discharge.
	- **Lockdown:** Limiting population movement was adopted as a preventive measure. One objective was immobilizing unexposed individuals to prevent contact with potentially infectious persons.

2. During the current pandemic, a 32-year-old male who travelled from Britain developed fever, sore throat and loss of smell. On the 6th day he developed difficulty in breathing and drop-in oxygen saturation. CT thorax showed patches in lungs:
	a. Mention the etiological diagnosis
	b. Discuss in detail the sample collection and laboratory diagnosis of this patient.
	c. What precautions are to be followed by health care workers?
	d. How to segregate and handle the biomedical waste while caring for this patient?
	**Case Scenario Analysis: Suspected COVID-19 Progression**
	
	**a. Etiological Diagnosis** Based on the clinical presentation (fever, sore throat, loss of smell, progressive dyspnea, dropped oxygen saturation, and lung patches on CT thorax) in the context of the current pandemic, the probable etiological diagnosis is **Coronavirus Disease (COVID-19)**. This disease is caused by the virus **SARS-CoV-2**, which is a type of Coronavirus. Coronaviruses cause respiratory tract infections, ranging from mild common cold to severe pneumonia.
	
	**b. Sample Collection and Laboratory Diagnosis** The laboratory diagnosis of COVID-19 involves several methods using appropriate specimens:
	
	- **Specimen Collection:**
	    - **Preferred specimens:** **Throat (oropharyngeal) and nasal swabs**. Dacron or polyester **flocked swabs** are used.
	    - After collection, swabs should be dipped in **viral transport media (VTM)**.
	    - **Alternative specimens:** **Nasopharyngeal swab**, **bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL)**, or **endotracheal aspirate** (in ventilated patients). Nasopharyngeal swabs can be collected by inserting a flexible swab through the nose into the posterior nasopharynx.
	    - Appropriate **PPE** (gloves, gown, N95 respirator, face shield) must be used for specimen collection.
	- **Laboratory Diagnosis Methods:**
	    - **NAAT (Nucleic Acid Amplification Testing):** This is a primary and often **gold standard** method.
	        - **Formats:** Real time RT-PCR, automated formats like **CBNAAT** and **Truenat**.
	        - **Gene Targets:** Screening targets include **E, N, M genes**. Confirmatory targets include **RdRp, N2 genes**. Automated systems like CBNAAT use E gene (screening) and N2 gene (confirmation), while Truenat uses the E gene (screening). Real time RT-PCR is highly sensitive, specific, and quantitative. Molecular diagnosis is the **gold standard** method for detection of respiratory viruses like coronaviruses.
	    - **Antigen Detection Assay:** Point-of-care tests detect **nucleocapsid protein antigen** in nasopharyngeal swabs.
	    - **Antibody (IgG) Detection Assay:** Used for serosurveillance and surveys, **not for clinical diagnosis**. Antibodies typically appear at a later stage.
	    - **Non-Specific Findings/Tests:**
	        - **CT scan of lungs:** May show **ground glass appearance** and/or consolidation. Ground-glass opacity can be a feature of interstitial pneumonia.
	        - **Biomarkers (in severe disease/ARDS):** Elevated **IL-6** (indicating cytokine storm), elevated **D-dimer** (suggesting coagulopathy), elevated **serum ferritin** (indicating inflammation), severe **lymphopenia**, and elevated **C-reactive protein** (marker of acute inflammation).
	
	**c. Precautions for Healthcare Workers (HCWs)** Precautions for HCWs caring for suspected or confirmed COVID-19 patients involve **droplet precautions** and specific PPE use:
	
	- **Hand Hygiene:** Strict and frequent hand hygiene.
	- **PPE Use:** HCWs should wear a **medical (3-ply) mask**, a pair of **gloves**, **gown**, and a **face shield** or goggles.
	- For **aerosol-generating procedures (AGPs)** (like endotracheal intubation), an **N95 respirator** is recommended.
	- HCWs should follow **respiratory hygiene and cough etiquette**.
	- Patients should ideally be kept in an **isolation room**.
	- **Work Restriction:** HCWs with influenza-like illness should stay home until fever-free for at least 24 hours without medication.
	
	**d. Biomedical Waste Handling** Biomedical waste generated while caring for a COVID-19 patient should be handled according to specific guidelines:
	
	- Use of **double bag** for waste collection.
	- Use of **dedicated trolley and collection bins**.
	- Waste bags/bins should be labelled as **"COVID-19 waste"**.
	- The **outer bag** should be **disinfected with hypochlorite** before handing over for disposal.
	- All linens used for COVID-19 patients should be washed at **60-90°C with laundry detergent** followed by soaking in **0.1% sodium hypochlorite**.
3. Covid 19-pathogenesis, laboratory diagnosis and prophylaxis
	**COVID-19: Pathogenesis, Laboratory Diagnosis, and Prophylaxis**
	
	**a. Pathogenesis**
	
	- COVID-19 is caused by the **SARS-CoV-2 virus**, a type of Coronavirus.
	- Viruses infect respiratory tract epithelial cells.
	- The virus is primarily transmitted via **respiratory droplets and contact routes**.
	- **Aerosol transmission** (spread >1 metre) is not fully documented but may occur during **aerosol-generating procedures (AGPs)** like intubation.
	- **Pre-symptomatic transmission** occurs 1-3 days before symptom onset, with highest viral loads around the day of onset.
	- After initial replication in the upper respiratory tract, the virus may descend to the lower respiratory tract.
	- In severe cases, immune response leads to deposition of inflammatory cells in the lungs, causing pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs).
	- This often appears as **'ground-glass' opacities** on chest imaging, a feature of interstitial pneumonia. Consolidation can also be seen on CT scan.
	- In later stages, fibrosis of the lungs can occur, leading to respiratory failure.
	- Severe disease can progress to **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)**.
	- ARDS is associated with a **cytokine storm** (elevated IL-6) and other biomarkers like elevated D-dimer (coagulopathy), elevated serum ferritin (inflammation), severe lymphopenia, and elevated C-reactive protein.
	
	**b. Laboratory Diagnosis**
	
	- Laboratory diagnosis is necessary in specific indications like **influenza-like illness (ILI)** or **severe acute respiratory infection (SARI)**, and for high-risk contacts.
	- **Specimens:**
	    - Preferred: **Throat (oropharyngeal) and nasal swabs**.
	    - Alternative: Nasopharyngeal swab, bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL), or tracheal aspirate [from prior conversation].
	    - Swabs (flocked swabs are preferable) should be dipped in **viral transport media (VTM)** [from prior conversation].
	    - Specimen collection requires appropriate **PPE** [from prior conversation].
	- **Nucleic Acid Amplification Testing (NAAT):** This is the primary method and the **gold standard** for diagnosis.
	    - **Formats:** Real time RT-PCR, automated formats like **CBNAAT** and **Truenat**.
	    - **Gene Targets:** Screening targets include E, N, M genes. Confirmatory targets include **RdRp**, N2 genes. CBNAAT uses E and N2. Truenat uses E.
	    - Real-time RT-PCR is quantitative. A sample is positive if both screening and confirmatory genes are detected with a cycle threshold (Ct) ≤ 40 cycles.
	    - NAAT can detect the virus from day 1 of symptom onset (usually after 5 days of infection) and becomes undetectable later.
	    - NAAT requires a **Biosafety Level-2 facility**.
	- **Antigen Detection Assay:** Point-of-care tests detect **nucleocapsid protein antigen** in nasopharyngeal swabs. Can be performed without a Biosafety Cabinet.
	- **Antibody (IgG) Detection Assay:** Used for serosurveillance and surveys, **not for clinical diagnosis** as antibodies appear late. ELISA is primarily used, confirmed by IFA and microneutralization assays.
	- **Sequencing:** Used to determine mutations.
	- **Viral Culture:** Used for research purposes and requires a **Biosafety Level-3 facility**.
	- **Non-Specific Tests:**
	    - **Radiology (chest CT scan):** May show **ground-glass appearance** and/or consolidation.
	    - **Biomarkers (in ARDS):** Elevated IL-6, elevated D-dimer, elevated serum ferritin, severe lymphopenia, elevated C-reactive protein.
	
	**c. Prophylaxis (Infection Prevention and Control)**
	
	- **Infection prevention and control (IPC)** is the most effective preventive method.
	- **Hand Hygiene:** Absolute and frequent hand hygiene is crucial, using correct technique for 20-40 seconds (hand rub).
	- **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** HCWs caring for suspects/patients should wear a **medical (3-ply) mask**, **gloves**, **gown**, and **face shield**. An **N95 respirator** is recommended for **aerosol-generating procedures (AGPs)**.
	- **Masking:** All individuals entering a healthcare facility must wear a face mask (**universal masking**) [from prior conversation]. Symptomatic individuals should wear a mask [from prior conversation]. General public should wear masks [implied by frequent hand wash etc for public].
	- **Isolation:** Patients should be kept in an isolation room [from prior conversation]. Isolation is part of outbreak management.
	- **Containment:** Strategies like containing coughs and sneezes (respiratory hygiene/cough etiquette) [from prior conversation] and identifying containment zones are used. Limiting population movement (lockdown) was adopted [from prior conversation].
	- **Environmental Cleaning:** Disinfection of surfaces is important [implied by contact transmission]. Outer biomedical waste bags should be disinfected with hypochlorite. Linens should be washed at high temperatures (60-90°C) and soaked in 0.1% sodium hypochlorite.
	- **Biomedical Waste Management:** Specific guidelines include using double bags, dedicated bins, labelling "COVID-19 waste", and disinfecting the outer bag.
	- **Vaccines:** Several COVID-19 vaccines are available after proving safe and effective in clinical trials. They are based on principles like inactivated/weakened virus, protein-based platforms, viral vectors (e.g., Adenovirus), or RNA/DNA technology. Vaccination is part of pandemic management strategies.
