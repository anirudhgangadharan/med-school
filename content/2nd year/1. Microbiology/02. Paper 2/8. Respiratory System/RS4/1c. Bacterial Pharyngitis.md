1. Lab diagnosis of faucial diphtheria.  
	**Lab Diagnosis of Faucial Diphtheria**
	
	- **Specimen Collection**
	    
	    - Ideal specimen: **Throat (oropharyngeal) swab** containing fibrinous exudates.
	    - Alternatively: A portion of the **pseudomembrane**.
	    - Collection: **Vigorous rubbing** over posterior pharynx and both tonsillar pillars.
	    - Two swabs recommended: One for smear, one for culture.
	- **Direct Smear Microscopy**
	    
	    - **Gram Stain**:
	        - Appearance: **Irregularly stained club-shaped gram-positive bacilli**, 3–6 µm.
	        - Arrangement: Typically in a **Chinese letter or cuneiform pattern** (V- or L-shaped).
	        - Limitation: Difficult to differentiate from commensal coryneforms.
	    - **Albert's Stain**:
	        - Specificity: **More specific for _C. diphtheriae_**.
	        - Appearance: **Green bacilli with bluish black metachromatic granules** at the poles (Volutin granules). Granules well-developed on enriched media like Loeffler's serum slope.
	- **Culture**
	    
	    - Organism: Fastidious, aerobic and facultative anaerobic. Does not grow on ordinary media.
	    - Enriched Media:
	        - **Blood agar**, **Chocolate agar**, **Loeffler's serum slope**. Incubate aerobically at 37°C.
	        - Loeffler's: Colonies are small, circular, glistening, white with a yellow tinge. **Advantages:** Growth detectable in 6–8 hours, best for metachromatic granule production.
	    - Selective Media:
	        - **Potassium tellurite agar**, **Tinsdale medium**.
	        - Potassium tellurite agar: **Inhibits throat commensals**. Colonies appear **black** after 48 hours. Different biotypes show distinct morphology.
	- **Identification**
	    
	    - **Biochemical Tests**: Sugar fermentation (Hiss’s serum sugar media). **Pyrazinamidase and Urease tests are negative**.
	    - Automated systems: **MALDI-TOF or VITEK**.
	- **Toxin Demonstration**
	    
	    - Essential because pathogenesis is toxin-mediated. Isolation alone is not sufficient.
	    - **In vitro tests**:
	        - **Elek's gel precipitation test**: Demonstrates toxigenicity by precipitation lines.
	        - **Detection of _tox_ gene by PCR**.
	        - Detection of diphtheria toxin by ELISA or ICT.
	        - Cytotoxicity on cell lines.
	    - In vivo tests: Guinea pig inoculation (subcutaneous, intracutaneous).
	- **Clinical Context**
	    
	    - Laboratory diagnosis provides confirmation, aids control measures, and is for epidemiological purposes.
	    - **Specific treatment should be started immediately based on clinical suspicion** without waiting for laboratory results due to risk of respiratory obstruction.
2. Diptheria, Pertussin and Tetanus toxoid (DPT)  vaccine and dosage schedule
	 Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus Toxoid (DPT) Vaccine 
	
	- **Type:** **Combined vaccine** used for active immunization against Diphtheria, Pertussis (whooping cough), and Tetanus.
	- **Composition:**
	    - Contains **Diphtheria Toxoid (DT)**, **Tetanus Toxoid (TT)**, and **Pertussis component**.
	    - Pertussis component can be **whole cell killed preparation** (in DPT) or **acellular pertussis (aP)** (in DaPT).
	    - Pertussis component acts as an **adjuvant**, increasing the immunogenicity of DT and TT.
	    - DT is prepared by incubating toxin with formalin to become toxoid, adsorbed onto alum (adjuvant).
	    - **Adult dose (Td)** contains a lower dose of diphtheria toxoid (2 Lf) compared to the usual children's dose (25 Lf units).
	- **Dosage Schedule (National Immunization Schedule of India 2020):**
	    - **Children:** Total **seven doses**:
	        - Primary series: **Three doses** of **pentavalent vaccine** (DPT + Hepatitis B + _H. influenzae_ type b) given at **6, 10, and 14 weeks** of birth.
	        - Booster 1: **DPT** at **16–24 months**.
	        - Booster 2: **DPT** at **5 years**.
	        - Additional Boosters: **Td** at **10 years** and **16 years**. (Introduced to reduce adult diphtheria resurgence).
	    - **Pregnant women:** **Two doses** of **Td** at a **one-month interval**. (Also helps reduce adult diphtheria resurgence).
	    - **Adults >18 years (if primary vaccination not completed):** **3 doses of Td** given at 0, 1 month, and 1 year.
	    - **Adults >18 years (completed primary vaccination):** **Td booster every 10 years** till 65 years.
	- **Administration:**
	    - Route: **Deep intramuscular (IM)**.
	    - Site: **Anterolateral aspect of thigh** (children), **deltoid** (adults), not gluteal region (fat may inhibit absorption).
	- **Storage:** Keep at **2–8°C**; discard if accidentally frozen.
	- **Protective Titer:** An antitoxin titer of **≥ 0.01 IU/mL** is considered protective.
	- **Adverse Reactions:**
	    - **Mild (common):** Fever and local reaction (swelling and indurations).
	    - **Severe (associated with whole-cell pertussis vaccine):** **Neurological complications** (encephalitis, prolonged convulsion) and hypotonic hyporesponsive syndrome. More common in children > 7 years. **Acellular pertussis (DaPT) is not associated with these neurological complications**.
	- **Contraindications (Absolute for DPT):**
	    - Hypersensitivity to a previous dose.
	    - Progressive neurological disorder.
	    - DaPT is recommended for children >7 years who haven't received primary vaccination due to lower risk of neurological complications.
	- **Note:** Diphtheria toxoid vaccine is not effective for preventing cutaneous diphtheria or eliminating the carrier state. Td boosters for older children, adolescents, and pregnant women are an attempt to reduce diphtheria resurgence in adults.
