1. Blood borne viral infections
	### **Bloodborne Viral Infections: High-Yield Notes for Medical School Exams**
	
	**Bloodborne viral infections** refer to diseases caused by viruses primarily transmitted through contact with infected blood or other body fluids, often via parenteral routes (e.g., blood transfusions, needle-sharing) or percutaneously. These infections can lead to serious systemic and organ-specific pathology, including chronic disease, immunosuppression, and cancer.
	
	---
	
	**I. Key Bloodborne Viral Agents (Most Common & Clinically Significant)**
	
	2. **Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)**
	    
	    - **Etiology**: Retrovirus (RNA virus). Contains reverse transcriptase, protease, and integrase enzymes.
	    - **Transmission**: Most commonly **sexual route**, but also significantly via **parenteral route (blood transfusions)**, **percutaneous (needle prick injury)**, and **vertical (mother to fetus)**. Often listed as a transfusion-transmitted infection.
	    - **Pathogenesis**: Attacks **CD4 T lymphocytes and macrophages**. Initial **primary viremia** (acute mononucleosis-like syndrome) with flu-like illness 3-6 weeks post-infection. Viral genome integrates with host cell chromosome, leading to **long period of clinical latency**.
	    - **Clinical Manifestations**:
	        - **Acute HIV Syndrome**: Flu-like illness (fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, pharyngitis).
	        - **AIDS**: Defined by severe **immunosuppression** (CD4 T cell count), leading to opportunistic infections (e.g., _Pneumocystis jirovecii_ pneumonia, _Cryptosporidium parvum_ diarrhea, _Toxoplasma_ encephalitis, CMV retinitis, herpes simplex lesions) and malignancies.
	        - **Clinical Relevance**: A predisposing condition for Infective Endocarditis (IE).
	    - **Laboratory Diagnosis**:
	        - **Antibody Detection**: Mainstay of diagnosis (Rapid tests, ELISA).
	        - **Antigen Detection**: **p24 antigen** by ELISA (useful during window period).
	        - **Molecular Methods**: **RT-PCR** for **viral RNA** (most appropriate tool for **viral load monitoring** and early diagnosis during window period). DNA PCR useful for pediatric HIV diagnosis.
	        - **CD4 T cell count**: Used for assessing risk of opportunistic infections and monitoring ART response.
	    - **Treatment**: **Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)** – combination of at least three drugs to maximally suppress the virus. "TREAT ALL" irrespective of CD4 count or clinical stage.
	3. **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV)**
	    
	    - **Etiology**: DNA virus, family Hepadnaviridae.
	    - **Transmission**: Primarily via **parenteral route** (blood/blood products transfusion, needle prick injuries, surgical/dental procedures, shared razors/toothbrushes). **Perinatal transmission** (mother to newborn) is maximum during delivery. Also sexual transmission. It is a common transfusion-transmitted infection.
	    - **Pathogenesis**: Causes **hepatitis** and **jaundice**. Can lead to chronic infection, cirrhosis, and **hepatocellular carcinoma**. Replication involves reverse transcription.
	    - **Clinical Relevance**: Patients are infectious as long as HBsAg is present in blood.
	    - **Laboratory Diagnosis**:
	        - **Serological Markers**: **HBsAg** (indicates infection/infectivity), Anti-HBs (indicates immunity/recovery), HBeAg (active viral replication), Anti-HBc (past/present infection).
	        - **Molecular Methods**: **HBV DNA detection** (indicates active viral replication, high infectivity, and used for monitoring treatment response).
	    - **Treatment**: Antiviral drugs such as **telbivudine and tenofovir**.
	    - **Prevention**: **Vaccine** (recombinant HBsAg vaccine) and prevention of transmission routes.
	4. **Hepatitis C Virus (HCV)**
	    
	    - **Etiology**: RNA virus, family Flaviviridae, genus Hepacivirus.
	    - **Transmission**: Explicitly listed as a transfusion-transmitted infection. Known to be primarily bloodborne.
	    - **Pathogenesis**: Can lead to **chronic liver cell injury**, cirrhosis, and **liver cancer**.
	    - **Laboratory Diagnosis**:
	        - **Antibody Detection**: Anti-Hepatitis C antibodies in serum.
	        - **Molecular Methods**: **NAAT** (nucleic acid amplification test) is highly sensitive. **RT-PCR** used for detection and quantification of viral RNA.
	    - **Treatment**: **Direct-acting antiviral agents (DAAs)** are the treatment of choice, producing high sustained viral response rates.
	
	---
	
	**II. Other Important Bloodborne Viral Agents (Including Transfusion-Transmitted Infections - TTIs)**
	
	- **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**:
	    
	    - **Transmission**: Can be transmitted via **blood transfusion**, and causes infections in immunocompromised/transplant recipients. Also congenital/perinatal transmission.
	    - **Clinical**: Can cause mononucleosis-like syndrome post-transfusion. Associated with "owl's eye appearance" inclusion bodies.
	    - **Diagnosis**: PCR for viral DNA, pp65 antigen detection, antibody detection by ELISA.
	    - **Treatment**: Ganciclovir, valganciclovir, foscarnet, cidofovir.
	- **Human T-lymphotropic Viruses (HTLV)**:
	    
	    - **Transmission**: Listed as a transfusion-transmitted infection. Oncogenic retrovirus.
	- **Parvovirus B19**:
	    
	    - **Transmission**: Listed as a transfusion-transmitted infection.
	    - **Clinical**: Causes erythema infectiosum (fifth disease) in children.
	    - **Diagnosis**: PCR for viral DNA is most sensitive, also antibody detection by ELISA.
	- **Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)**:
	    
	    - **Transmission**: Primarily by oropharyngeal contact (salivary secretions). However, it can also be a rare transfusion-transmitted infection.
	    - **Clinical**: Infectious mononucleosis, various malignancies (e.g., Burkitt's lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, Hodgkin's/non-Hodgkin's lymphoma).
	- **Viral Hemorrhagic Fevers (VHF)**:
	    
	    - **Filoviruses (Ebola, Marburg viruses)**: Cause severe hemorrhagic fever. Transmission includes **contact with blood and body fluids** of infected individuals. Laboratory handling requires Biosafety Level-4 facilities due to high risk of laboratory spread.
	    - **Hantaviruses**: Rodent-borne viruses that cause VHF. Can lead to viremia. Diagnosis by RT-PCR.
	    - **Arboviruses (e.g., Dengue, Chikungunya, Yellow Fever, West Nile virus)**: Primarily **transmitted by arthropod vectors** (e.g., _Aedes aegypti_ mosquito for Dengue, Chikungunya, Yellow Fever). While they cause viremia, **transfusion-transmission is rare**. Yellow Fever causes hepatitis. Diagnosis often involves NS1 antigen detection, IgM antibody, and RT-PCR.
	
	---
	
	**III. Clinical Relevance & High-Yield Considerations for Bloodborne Viruses**
	
	- **Diagnosis Importance**: **Timely detection of the causative agent** is paramount in bloodstream infections due to the risk of shock, multi-organ failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulopathies (DIC).
	- **Blood Cultures**: For suspected bloodstream infections, **isolation of the causative microorganism from blood cultures is critical** for diagnosis and guiding treatment. Two sets >12h apart, or three sets over one hour.
	- **Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs)**: Increasingly used for screening transfusion-transmitted infections due to **higher sensitivity** and ability to detect infections during the **window period** (early infectivity when immunologic tests are non-reactive).
	- **Safety Precautions**: For highly infectious bloodborne pathogens (e.g., Ebola), laboratory procedures require high biosafety levels (e.g., Biosafety Level-4).
2. parasitic agents causing anemia and the types of anemia they induce, optimized for medical school exams:
	### **Parasitic Agents Causing Anemia & Anemia Types**
		
	Anemia, characterized by a decrease in red blood cells (RBCs) or hemoglobin, or a reduced oxygen-carrying capacity of blood, can result from various etiologies, including parasitic infections.
	
	**I. Types of Anemia & Associated Parasitic Agents**
	
	1. **Iron Deficiency Anemia**
	    
	    - **Mechanism**: Occurs due to **blood loss** as a result of infection. This leads to **microcytic hypochromic anemia**.
	    - **Common Parasitic Agents**:
	        - **Hookworm** (_Necator americanus_ and _Ancylostoma duodenale_): These are intestinal nematodes that cause blood loss.
	        - _Trichuris trichiura_ (Whipworm): A large intestinal nematode that can cause dysentery and blood loss.
	        - _Schistosoma species_: Blood flukes that can reside in the venous plexus of the bladder or gastrointestinal tract, leading to blood loss.
	2. **Hemolytic Anemia**
	    
	    - **Mechanism**: Occurs due to the **destruction of RBCs**.
	    - **Common Parasitic Agents**:
	        - **Malaria** (_Plasmodium falciparum_): _Plasmodium_ parasites infect and destroy RBCs during their erythrocytic life cycle. This can lead to severe normochromic, normocytic anemia.
	        - _Babesia microti_: Similar to _Plasmodium_, _Babesia_ parasites infect RBCs.
	3. **Megaloblastic Anemia**
	    
	    - **Mechanism**: Caused by the parasite _Diphyllobothrium latum_ (fish tapeworm). It leads to a **decrease in absorption of vitamin B12** in the ileum by causing dissociation of the vitamin B12-intrinsic factor complex within the gut lumen.
	    - **Clinical Relevance**: This effect has been noted particularly in Scandinavians, with up to 2% of infected elderly patients developing megaloblastic anemia, potentially due to genetic predisposition. Neurologic sequelae like paresthesia may also be exhibited.
	
	---
	
	**II. Clinical & Diagnostic Considerations**
	
	- **Microscopy**: Direct blood microscopy is useful for detecting malaria parasites (ring forms and gametocytes), _Leishmania donovani_ (LD bodies or amastigote forms), and trypanosomes (trypomastigote forms).
	- **Stool Examination**: For intestinal parasites like hookworm, _Trichuris trichiura_, and _Diphyllobothrium latum_, stool examination for eggs or adult parasitic forms is crucial for diagnosis.
	- **Bone Marrow Examination**: In cases of disseminated parasitic infections, such as those caused by _Leishmania donovani_, amastigote forms can be visualized in smears or sections made from bone marrow material.
