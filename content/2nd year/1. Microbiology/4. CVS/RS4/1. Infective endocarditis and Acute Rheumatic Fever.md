1. Bacteria causing infective endocarditis
	### **Bacterial Agents Causing Infective Endocarditis (IE): High-Yield Notes**
	
	**Infective Endocarditis (IE)** refers to microbial invasion of heart valves or mural endocardium, leading to the formation of bulky, friable vegetations. It is a serious condition with varying etiologies and clinical presentations.
	
	---
	
	**I. Overall Most Common Etiological Agents**
	
	- **Staphylococcus aureus** is the most common cause of IE overall.
	- **Coagulase-negative staphylococci (CoNS)**, particularly _Staphylococcus epidermidis_, are the most common agents in prosthetic valve endocarditis.
	- **Viridans streptococci** are the most common cause of subacute bacterial endocarditis.
	
	---
	
	**II. Specific Etiological Agents & Clinical Contexts (High-Yield Subtypes)**
	
	1. **Staphylococci:**
	    
	    - _**Staphylococcus aureus**_ **(highly virulent)**:
	        - Typically causes **acute endocarditis**, often on **previously normal cardiac valves**.
	        - Most common agent in **healthcare-associated native valve endocarditis**.
	        - The **most common agent in endocarditis in IV drug abusers**, predominantly affecting the **right-sided (tricuspid) valve**.
	        - A significant majority of _S. aureus_ causing IE are **Methicillin-Resistant S. aureus (MRSA)**.
	        - Often associated with **larger vegetations (>10 mm)** and features of **septic embolization** (e.g., subungual hemorrhage, Osler's nodes). Embolization risk is higher for mitral valve IE.
	    - **Coagulase-negative staphylococci (CoNS)** (e.g., _Staphylococcus epidermidis_):
	        - The **most common agents in prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE)**, both early (within 12 months of replacement) and overall.
	        - The majority of CoNS causing PVE are **methicillin-resistant**.
	2. **Streptococci:**
	    
	    - _**Viridans streptococci**_ **(low virulence)**:
	        - The **most common cause of subacute bacterial endocarditis (SABE)**, typically affecting **previously damaged heart valves** (e.g., scarred or deformed valves).
	        - Common cause of **community-acquired native valve endocarditis**.
	        - Primary portal of entry: **Oral cavity** (e.g., after chewing, tooth brushing, dental procedures).
	    - _**Streptococcus gallolyticus**_ (formerly _S. bovis_):
	        - A group D _Streptococcus_ found as a commensal in the **intestine**.
	        - Can cause **subacute endocarditis** and is notably **associated with colorectal cancer or polyps**. Portal of entry: Gastrointestinal tract.
	    - **Nutritionally variant streptococci** (_Granulicatella_ and _Abiotrophia_ species):
	        - Normal inhabitants of the oral cavity, can cause endocarditis.
	        - Important cause of **culture-negative endocarditis**.
	3. **Enterococci**:
	    
	    - Commonly involved in **left-sided endocarditis (mitral and aortic valves)**, especially in **intravenous drug abusers**.
	    - Portal of entry: **Gastrointestinal tract**.
	4. **HACEK Group**:
	    
	    - Highly fastidious, slow-growing, gram-negative bacteria: _Haemophilus species_, _Aggregatibacter species_ (_A. actinomycetemcomitans_ is the most common HACEK member to cause IE), _Cardiobacterium hominis_, _Eikenella corrodens_, _Kingella kingae_.
	    - Normally reside in the **oral cavity**.
	    - Account for ~3% of all IE cases.
	    - Typically has a **subacute course**, often in patients with **pre-existing valvular defects or those undergoing dental procedures**.
	5. **Other Important Agents:**
	    
	    - _**Pneumococci**_.
	    - _**Enterobacteriaceae**_.
	    - _**Pseudomonas species**_: Usually in **IV drug users**, can cause left-sided and native valve endocarditis.
	    - _**Candida species**_: Can cause IE, especially in **IV drug abusers**.
	    - _**Diphtheroids**_.
	    - _**Elizabethkingia meningosepticum**_: Can cause **nosocomial endocarditis**.
	6. **Culture-Negative Endocarditis**:
	    
	    - Accounts for 5-10% of cases.
	    - Major reasons: **Prior antibiotic exposure** or **fastidious organisms**.
	    - Key fastidious agents (beyond nutritionally variant streptococci and HACEK):
	        - _**Coxiella burnetii**_ (agent of Q fever, known for chronic Q fever manifesting as endocarditis).
	        - _**Bartonella species**_.
	        - _**Brucella species**_.
	        - _**Tropheryma whipplei**_ (causes an indolent, afebrile form).
	
	---
	
	**III. Clinical Relevance & High-Yield Considerations:**
	
	- **Risk Factors:** Underlying cardiac defects (e.g., congenital valvular disease, prosthetic valves), use of intravenous catheters, and IV drug abuse are major predisposing factors for IE.
	- **Pathogenesis:** Endothelial injury, often due to turbulent blood flow or IV catheter trauma, leads to nonbacterial thrombotic endocarditis (NBTE). This thrombus is then colonized by bacteria during transient bacteremia, forming protective vegetations that can seed bacteria to distant sites (metastasis).
	- **Diagnosis:**
	    - **Blood Cultures:** **Critical** for diagnosis and guiding treatment. Two sets collected >12h apart, or three sets over one hour.
	    - **Modified Duke Criteria:** A highly sensitive and specific schema based on clinical, laboratory, and echocardiographic findings to diagnose IE.
	    - **Echocardiography:** Allows anatomical confirmation, vegetation sizing, and detection of intracardiac complications.
	- **Embolization:** _S. aureus_ IE, especially of the mitral valve, is frequently associated with septic embolization to extracardiac sites.
