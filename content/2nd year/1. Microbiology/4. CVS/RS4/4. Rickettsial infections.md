	1. ### **Weil-Felix Test: Uses & Interpretations (High-Yield Notes)**
		
		The Weil-Felix test is a **serological test** that has historically been used for the diagnosis of **rickettsial infections**. It is classified as a **non-specific heterophile agglutination test**.
		
		**I. Principle**
		
		- The test is based on the **antigenic cross-reactivity** between **LPS (lipopolysaccharide) antigens** found in some rickettsiae and certain strains of _Proteus_.
		- It detects **rickettsial antibodies** in patient serum by using these **non-specific, cross-reacting _Proteus_ antigens**.
		
		**II. Clinical Utility & Uses**
		
		- Primarily used for the **serodiagnosis of rickettsial diseases**.
		
		**III. Limitations & Disadvantages**
		
		- **Non-specific**: As a heterophile agglutination test, it is considered a non-specific method for rickettsial disease diagnosis.
				- **Lack of High Sensitivity & Specificity**: The test generally **lacks high sensitivity and specificity**. This means it can produce false-positive or false-negative results, limiting its diagnostic reliability.
