1. Opportunistic infections and predisposing factors involved in opportunistic disease
	**Opportunistic Infections**
	
	- Infections that occur more often or are more severe in people with **weakened immune systems** compared to those with healthy immune systems.
	
	**Key Predisposing Factors & Associated Infections**
	
	- **AIDS (HIV infection)**: The **most important clinical condition** prone to opportunistic infections.
	    
	    - Increased risk for various invasive diarrheal illnesses, such as **salmonellosis** and **cryptosporidiosis**.
	    - Toxoplasmosis is one of the common opportunistic parasitic infections in patients with AIDS (15–40%).
	        - HLA DQ3 is associated with **encephalitis** in AIDS patients infected with _Toxoplasma_.
	    - Patients with advanced HIV infection (CD4 T cell counts < 200/µL) are at high-risk of acquiring **Cryptococcus neoformans** infection. _C. gattii_ is typically _not_ associated with HIV.
	    - Microsporidia primarily cause opportunistic infections in HIV-infected patients, particularly when CD4 T cell count falls below 100 µL.
	    - Increased risk of **EBV-associated malignancies** and **HPV-associated malignancies**.
	    - High frequency of tuberculosis, accounting for up to two-thirds of all cases. Disseminated TB may be indicated for blood culture in HIV-coinfected patients.
	    - Nonulcerative gonorrhea enhances the transmission of HIV.
	    - Risk factor for severe cholera disease.
	    - Increased risk for pneumococcal pneumonia.
	    - Kaposi's sarcoma is associated with AIDS.
	- **Organ Transplantation**: Characterized by profound immunosuppression, leading to increased risk of various opportunistic infections.
	    
	    - Major infections of concern include **CMV**, **molds**, and **respiratory viruses**.
	    - Risk is higher if the donor is unrelated.
	    - Sources include donor-derived, recipient-derived (activation of latent infection), endemic community-derived, and healthcare-associated pathogens.
	    - Strongyloidiasis hyperinfection syndrome risk factor.
	    - Increased risk for pneumococcal pneumonia.
	    - Increased risk of **EBV-associated malignancies** (post-transplant lymphomas).
	- **Immunosuppression (General)**: Includes conditions like chronic renal failure, congenital or acquired immunodeficiencies, malignancy, and solid organ transplant.
	    
	    - Higher risk for diarrhea.
	    - Risk factor for severe cholera disease.
	    - Risk factor for invasive candidiasis.
	    - Predisposes to Pseudomonas invasion and pathogenesis in hospitalized patients.
	    - Risk factor for Mucormycosis.
	    - Risk factor for _C. difficile_ infection.
	    - Strongyloidiasis hyperinfection syndrome risk factor.
	    - Increased risk of EBV-associated malignancies.
	- **Prolonged Antimicrobial Use**:
	    
	    - Disruption of normal colonic flora enhances susceptibility to **Clostridioides difficile infection**.
	- **Steroid Therapy (Prolonged Systemic Corticosteroid)**:
	    
	    - Prone to develop various opportunistic infections, including **aspergillosis**.
	    - Main risk factor for Strongyloidiasis hyperinfection syndrome.
	    - Risk factor for invasive candidiasis.
	    - Risk factor for Mucormycosis.
	- **Malignancies**: Patients with cancers are at increased risk for various opportunistic infections.
	    
	    - Hematologic malignancies (e.g., Hodgkin's lymphoma, leukemia) with abnormal T cell function increase risk for **intracellular pathogens** (_M. tuberculosis_, _Listeria_, _Salmonella_, _Cryptococcus_, herpesviruses).
	    - Acute myeloid or lymphocytic leukemia with granulocytopenia and skin/mucous membrane lesions increase risk for **extracellular gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria** and **fungi**.
	    - Multiple myeloma (hypergammaglobulinemia) and Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (hypogammaglobulinemia) increase risk for _S. pneumoniae_, _H. influenzae_, and _N. meningitidis_.
	    - Strongyloidiasis hyperinfection syndrome risk factor.
	    - Increased risk for severe COVID-19.
	    - Risk factor for _C. difficile_ infection.
	- **Splenectomy / Hyposplenism / Splenic Dysfunction**:
	    
	    - Increase the risk of **pneumococcal meningitis**.
	    - Increase the risk for invasive infections by encapsulated bacteria like _S. pneumoniae_, _H. influenzae_, and _N. meningitidis_.
	    - Risk factor for Capnocytophaga infection.
	- **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)**: The **most important risk factor** for invasive **mucormycosis**. Acidosis releases iron, a growth factor for the fungi.
	    
	- **Hospitalization**:
	    
	    - Patients get colonized with organisms from the hospital environment or staff.
	    - Predisposed to infection in the presence of underlying risk factors (burn wounds, immunosuppression, post surgeries).
	    - Risk factors for Pseudomonas invasion and pathogenesis.
	    - Pseudomonas is notorious for causing hospital infections, including ventilator-associated pneumonia, central line-associated bloodstream infection, post-neurosurgical meningitis, catheter-associated UTI, wound/soft tissue infections, and infections in burn patients.
	    - _C. difficile_ spores are found widely in hospitals.
	    - UTI is the leading cause of **gram-negative sepsis (urosepsis)**, especially in hospitalized patients; urinary catheters are the origin of nearly 50% of **nosocomial UTIs**.
	- **Other Factors**:
	    
	    - **Hypogammaglobulinemia**: Higher risk for _C. difficile_ colitis and giardiasis. Also seen in CLL and MM.
	    - **Age > 65 years**: Major risk factor for severe COVID-19. Risk factor for _C. difficile_ infection. Common age for cholangiocarcinoma related to _Clonorchis/Opisthorchis_.
	    - **Malnutrition**: Risk factor for severe cholera disease.
	    - **Chronic Renal Failure**: Increased risk for invasive candidiasis. Increased risk for pneumococcal pneumonia. Major risk factor for severe COVID-19.
	    - **Underlying Liver Disease / Iron Overload**: Risk factors for _Vibrio vulnificus_ sepsis. Increased iron load is a risk factor for Mucormycosis.
	    - **Genetic Factors**: Genetically determined receptors on uroepithelial cells may help bacterial attachment in UTI. HLA DQ3 associated with Toxoplasma encephalitis in AIDS patients and hydrocephalus in fetuses. Activation of H-ras and inactivation of p53/retinoblastoma genes associated with bladder cancer from _Schistosoma haematobium_.
	    - **Urinary Catheters / Functional Obstruction (e.g., neurogenic bladder) / Vesicoureteral reflux**: Risk factors for UTI.
	    - **Burn Wounds**: Risk factor for Pseudomonas infections.
	    - **Critically-ill patients**: Higher risk for invasive candidiasis.
	    - **Low birth weight infants**: Higher risk for invasive candidiasis.
	    - **Terminal complement deficiencies (C5–C9) / Eculizumab or ravulizumab therapy**: Risk factors for meningococcal disease.
