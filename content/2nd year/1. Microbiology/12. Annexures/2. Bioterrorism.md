1. Name three biological agents causing bioterrorism
	**Bioterrorism Agents**
	
	- **Definition:** Intentional and deliberate release of biological agents (bacteria, viruses, fungi, or their toxins) to cause mass illness or death in people, animals, or plants.
	- **Classification:** Bioweapons are classified into categories based on risk and impact. **Category A agents are the highest priority** and pose the greatest risk to national security. They are easily disseminated, result in high mortality, and have the potential for major public health impact, causing public panic and social disruption.
	
	**Three Key Biological Agents (Category A):**
	
	- **Anthrax:** Caused by _Bacillus anthracis_. Known clinical relevance includes cutaneous, inhalation (wool sorter's disease), gastrointestinal, and injection forms. High mortality, especially for inhalation anthrax. Has been used historically and in modern bioterrorism events (e.g., 2001 US anthrax attacks).
	- **Plague:** Caused by _Yersinia pestis_. Transmitted naturally via the rat flea (propagative transmission). Clinical forms include bubonic (most common), pneumonic, and septicemic. Pneumonic plague is highly infectious by aerosol and has a high case-fatality ratio. Historically known as "black death".
	- **Tularemia:** Caused by _Francisella tularensis_. Highly infectious agent. Can be transmitted through insect bites (especially ticks and tabanid flies), interaction with infected animals, or inhalation. Poses a severe threat due to its infectious nature and potential for aerosol transmission.
