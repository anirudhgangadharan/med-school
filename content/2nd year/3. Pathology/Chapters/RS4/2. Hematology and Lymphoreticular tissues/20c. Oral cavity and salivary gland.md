1. Pleomorphic adenoma-etiology, pathogenesis, pathology (morphology) and complications, gross microscopy, clinical features
	**Pleomorphic Adenoma (Mixed Salivary Tumour)**
	## 2. **Etiology**

	### **A. Risk/Predisposing Factors**
	
	- **Age:** Most in 30–50 years.
	    
	- **Sex:** Slight female predominance.
	    
	- **Site:** Parotid gland most common; submandibular less common; minor salivary glands (~10%).

	## B.Molecular/Genetic Changes** _(Robbins, molecular pathology section)_
	
	- **PLAG1 activation** (Pleomorphic Adenoma Gene 1)
	    
	    - Chromosomal translocations (e.g., t(3;8)(p21;q12)) → juxtaposition of **PLAG1** with strong promoters → overexpression.
	        
	    - PLAG1 is a transcription factor that upregulates growth factor genes → tumor cell proliferation.
	        
	- **HMGA2 rearrangements**
	    
	    - High-mobility group protein → alters chromatin structure → dysregulated cell cycle genes.
	        
	- Both PLAG1 & HMGA2 changes are present in **majority of cases**.
	    
	
	---
	
	## 3. **Pathogenesis**
	
	1. **Initiating Event**:
	    
	    - Chromosomal rearrangements activate oncogenes (PLAG1, HMGA2) in ductal or myoepithelial cells.
	        
	2. **Biphasic Tumor Growth**:
	    
	    - **Epithelial component**: Forms ducts, tubules, or solid nests.
	        
	    - **Myoepithelial component**: Produces extracellular matrix (chondromyxoid or myxoid stroma).
	        
	3. **Mesenchymal-like Stroma Formation**:
	    
	    - Myoepithelial cells undergo **mesenchymal metaplasia** → secretion of cartilage-like matrix, collagen, myxoid material.
	        
	4. **Slow, Painless Expansion**:
	    
	    - Encapsulated but **capsule often incomplete** → finger-like protrusions into adjacent gland parenchyma.
	        
	5. **Malignant Transformation Risk**:
	    
	    - Long-standing tumors (esp. >10 years) may develop **carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma** (aggressive adenocarcinoma within).
	- **Definition:** Benign tumour of salivary glands characterised by a **mixed epithelial and stromal (mesenchymal)** composition. It is an example of a tumour with mixed patterns.
	- **Etiology & Pathogenesis:** 
	- **Pathology (Morphology):** Diagnosis relies on gross and microscopic features.
	    - **Gross Appearance (G/A):**
	    - ![[Pasted image 20250815132041.png]]
	        - Commonest tumour in the parotid gland. Also occurs in salivary glands generally.
	        - Usually a **circumscribed**, often **pseudoencapsulated** (sometimes described as encapsulated) mass.
	        - Typically **round or oval with a smooth surface**.
	        - May be **multilobulated**.
	        - Size is usually 2-5 cm in diameter.
	        - **Cut surface:** Grey-white and bluish, **variegated**, with soft to mucoid consistency. May show a semitranslucent parenchyma.
	    - **Microscopic Appearance (M/E):**
	    - ![[Pasted image 20250815131151.png]]
	        - Characterised by **epithelial elements** within a **stromal matrix**.
	        - **Epithelial Component:**
	            - Forms various patterns: **ducts, acini, tubules, sheets, and strands** of cells.
	            - Cells are of ductal or myoepithelial origin.
	            - Ductal cells are cuboidal or columnar.
	            - Myoepithelial cells may be polygonal or spindle-shaped.
	            - Lumina of duct-like structures may contain PAS-positive epithelial mucin.
	            - Focal areas of squamous metaplasia and keratinisation may be present.
	            - Tumour cells are immunoreactive for epithelial (cytokeratin, EMA, CEA) and myoepithelial (actin, vimentin, S-100) antibodies.
	        - **Mesenchymal Component (Stroma):**
	            - Composed of **mucoid, myxoid, and chondroid tissue**.
	            - Often described as **pseudocartilage**.
	            - Randomly admixed with the epithelial elements.
	- **Clinical Features ## **3. Clinical Features**
		
		- **Site:**
		    
		    - **Parotid gland** (~60% cases; superficial lobe most common)
		        
		    - Submandibular gland (~10%)
		        
		    - Minor salivary glands (~10%, esp. palate)
		        
		- **Symptoms:**
		    
		    - **Painless**, slow-growing, mobile, firm mass.
		        
		    - Usually unilateral; overlying skin and facial nerve function preserved unless malignant transformation.
		        
		- **Course:**
		    
		    - Tumor enlarges gradually over years.
		        
		    - No ulceration or fixation unless malignant.
			    
	- **Complications:**
	    
	    - Risk of **malignant transformation** into carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma.
	    - Malignant areas show typical features of carcinoma: anaplasia, nuclear hyperchromatism, large nucleolisation, mitoses, and invasive growth.
	    - Any type of usual salivary gland carcinoma can develop within a pleomorphic adenoma.

	**Warthin's Tumour (Papillary Cystadenoma Lymphomatosum / Adenolymphoma)**
	
	- **Definition/Classification:**
	    
	    - A **benign** tumour of salivary glands.
	    - Classified as a **monomorphic adenoma**.
	    - Characterised by **cystic spaces lined by papillary arrangement lined by epithelial cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm and lymphoid stroma**.
	- **Etiology & Pathogenesis:**
	## **2. Etiology**
	- **Almost exclusively in parotid gland** (tail region).
	- **Strong association with smoking** – ~8× increased risk.
	- Occurs mainly in **5th–7th decades**, slight male predominance (historically; now more equal as women smoke more).
    ## **3. Pathogenesis**
	
	1. **Developmental Basis**:
	    
	    - During embryogenesis, salivary gland tissue can be trapped in lymph nodes within parotid.
	        
	2. **Neoplastic Change**:
	    
	    - Smoking and possibly other irritants → epithelial proliferation with oncocytic change (mitochondria-rich cells).
	        
	3. **Histologic Components**:
	    
	    - **Epithelial**: Double layer – inner columnar oncocytic cells + outer cuboidal/basal cells, forming papillary infoldings into cystic spaces.
	        
	    - **Lymphoid stroma**: Dense lymphoid tissue with germinal centers.
	- Pathogenesis is thought to arise from **heterotopic salivary gland tissue in intraparotid lymph nodes**.

	    - **Gross Appearance (G/A):**

	        - Cut surface shows characteristic **slit-like or cystic spaces**, containing **milky fluid** and having **papillary projections**.
	    - **Microscopic Appearance (M/E):**
	        - Shows **two components: epithelial parenchyma and lymphoid stroma**.
	        - **Epithelial Parenchyma:**
	            - Composed of **glandular and cystic structures** having **papillary arrangement**.
	            - Lined by characteristic **eosinophilic epithelium**.
	            - Variants may include mucous goblet cells and sebaceous differentiation.
	        - **Lymphoid Stroma:**
	            - Present **under the epithelium**.
	            - Forms **prominent lymphoid tissue**, often with **germinal centres**.
	        - Shows **cystic spaces lined by papillary arrangement**.
	- **Complications:**
	    
	    - **Recurrence**: Rare after complete excision.
		    
		- **Malignant transformation**: Extremely rare.
		    
		- Cosmetic deformity if large.
	    - ## **4. Clinical Features**
			
			- **Site**: Parotid gland (almost always).
			    
			- **Laterality**: 10–15% bilateral; sometimes multifocal.
			    
			- **Presentation**:
			    
			    - Painless, slow-growing, fluctuant or firm mass.
			        
			    - Often mobile; overlying skin normal.
			        
			    - No facial nerve involvement (benign).
			        
			- May be discovered incidentally.
