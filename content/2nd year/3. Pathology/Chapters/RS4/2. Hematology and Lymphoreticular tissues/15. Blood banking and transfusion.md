1. Blood transfusion reactions-Hazards of blood transfusion reactions
	Based on the provided sources, here is a summary of blood transfusion reactions and their hazards in a note-style format relevant for medical school exams:
	
	**Blood Transfusion Reactions - Hazards of Blood Transfusion**
	
	- Blood transfusion is generally safe, but **untoward complications** occur in 5-6% of transfusions. These complications can be minor, while others are more **serious and at times fatal**.
	- Transfusion reactions are generally classified into 2 types: **immune** and **non-immune**.
	
	**A. Immunologic Transfusion Reactions**
	
	1. **Haemolytic Transfusion Reactions**
	    
	    - Can be **immediate or delayed**, **intravascular or extravascular**.
	    - **Very rapid cell destruction** associated with **intravascular haemolysis** is usually due to **ABO incompatibility**.
	        - Mechanism: **Naturally-occurring antibodies** (anti-A and anti-B) are capable of **fixing complement**. Complement activation can lead to the formation of the **membrane attack complex (MAC)** (C5-9), disrupting membrane integrity and causing cell lysis. Antibodies binding to antigens can also promote phagocytosis (opsonization).
	        - **Clinical Features (Immediate):** Restlessness, anxiety, flushing, chest or lumbar pain, tachypnoea, tachycardia, and nausea. Followed by **shock and renal failure**.
	    - Examples related to antibody reactions against red cells include:
	        - Transfusion reactions due to incompatible or mismatched blood transfusion.
	        - **Haemolytic disease of the newborn (erythroblastosis foetalis)**: Foetal red cells destroyed by maternal isoantibodies crossing the placenta. Sensitisation of the mother may result from previous blood transfusion.
	2. **Other Allergic Reactions**
	    
	    - **Febrile reaction:** Usually attributed to immunologic reaction against **white blood cells, platelets, or IgA class immunoglobulins**.
	    - **Anaphylactic shock:** Patients with **antibodies against IgA molecule** may develop this.
	    - Urticaria.
	    - **Transfusion-related graft-versus-host disease (GVHD):** Mediated by **donor T lymphocytes**. Requires an immunocompetent graft (containing T-cells), HLA mismatch (minor or major), and an immunosuppressed recipient. GVHD can be fatal. Target organs include skin, gastrointestinal (GI) tract, and liver. The risk can be reduced by depletion of T cells from the graft.
	
	**B. Nonimmune Transfusion Reactions**
	
	3. **Circulatory Overload:** The **most important and most common complication** that may result in death.
	    
	    - Mechanism: Results in **pulmonary congestion and acute heart failure**.
	    - Risk: Particularly high in patients with **chronic anaemia, infants, and the elderly**.
	    - Onset: May be immediate, or delayed up to 24 hours.
	4. **Massive Transfusion:** Occurs when the volume of stored blood transfused exceeds the patient's normal blood volume.
	    
	    - Results in: **Dilutional thrombocytopenia** and dilution of coagulation factors.
	5. **Transmission of Infection:** Many diseases can be transmitted by transfusion of infected blood.
	    
	    - Examples: **Hepatitis (HBV, HCV), CMV infection, syphilis, malaria, toxoplasmosis, infectious mononucleosis, brucellosis and AIDS (HIV infection)**.
	    - Risk: Increases in patients who receive **multiple transfusions** (e.g., cases of haemophilia, thalassaemia major, acute leukaemias, acute severe haemorrhage).
	    - Prevention: **Mandatory screening** of every unit of blood for HIV, HBV, HCV, syphilis and malarial parasite.
	6. **Air Embolism:** Unlikely with modern plastic bags. Risk depends on volume and patient health.
	    
	7. **Thrombophlebitis:** More commonly associated with venesection for blood transfusion. Risk is enhanced if transfusion is continued longer than 12 hours at a single site.
2. Mandatory serological test in screening of blood donors.
	Based on the sources provided, the mandatory serological tests for screening blood donors are:
	
	**Mandatory Serological Tests in Blood Donor Screening**
	
	- Prior to any human transfusion, every unit of blood **must be screened**.
	    
	- This screening is mandatory for the detection of infectious organisms.
	    
	- The required serological testing includes:
	    
	    - **HIV (AIDS)**
	    - **HBV (Hepatitis B Virus)**
	    - **HCV (Hepatitis C Virus)**
	    - **Syphilis**
	    - Presence of **Malarial parasite**
	- Donor testing also includes **ABO-Rh grouping**.

	**Investigation of Suspected Adverse Transfusion Reaction**
	
	- **Immediate Recognition & Action:** Reactions are observed during supervised transfusion. Symptoms of immediate haemolytic reaction include restlessness, anxiety, flushing, pain (chest/lumbar), tachypnoea, tachycardia, and nausea, followed by shock and renal failure. _Immediate steps (not detailed in sources) are critical clinician actions._
	- **Laboratory Testing:**
	    - Checking for **incompatibility**: ABO incompatibility is a cause of rapid, severe haemolytic reactions. Blood grouping of recipient and donor is part of the standard process.
	    - Testing for red cell destruction: Investigation for haemolytic anaemia includes tests of increased red cell breakdown (e.g., serum unconjugated bilirubin, urine urobilinogen, serum haptoglobin, plasma LDH, haemoglobinaemia, haemoglobinuria, haemosiderinuria).
	    - Antibody detection: The Indirect Antiglobulin Test (IAT) is used in the investigation of haemolytic transfusion reactions to detect incomplete antibodies (IgG) in the patient's serum.
	    - Re-checking compatibility: Pre-transfusion compatibility testing includes cross-matching patient's serum against donor red cells.
	    - Testing for transmitted infections: While screened _before_ transfusion, investigation might include serology for HBV, HCV, HIV, and syphilis, and screening for malarial parasite, as these are known transfusion-transmitted diseases.

4. Blood transfusion reactions (immediate complications
	 **Blood Transfusion Reactions - Immediate Complications**
	1. **Immediate Haemolytic Transfusion Reaction (IHTR)**:
	    
	    - Caused by **very rapid red cell destruction** associated with **intravascular haemolysis**.
	    - Usually due to **ABO incompatibility**.
	    - Mechanism: Recipient's preformed **naturally-occurring antibodies** (anti-A, anti-B) bind to donor red cells. These antibodies are capable of **fixing complement**. Complement activation can lead to the formation of the **membrane attack complex (MAC)** (C5-9), causing direct red cell lysis (intravascular haemolysis). Antibodies also promote phagocytosis (opsonization).
	    - Clinical Features (Rapid onset during or shortly after transfusion):
	        - **Restlessness, anxiety, flushing**.
	        - **Chest or lumbar pain**.
	        - **Tachypnoea, tachycardia**.
	        - **Nausea**.
	        - Followed by **shock and renal failure**.
	    - Considered a severe form of **Type II hypersensitivity reaction**.
	2. **Immunologic Reactions (Other than Haemolytic)**:
	    
	    - **Allergic Reactions (Urticaria, Febrile)**: Usually due to immunologic reactions against **white blood cells, platelets, or IgA class immunoglobulins** in the donor blood. Can occur during or shortly after transfusion.
	    - **Anaphylactic Shock**: A severe, systemic form of immediate hypersensitivity. Patients with **antibodies against the IgA molecule** are at risk. Can occur rapidly. Manifestations can include symptoms like shock. Considered a severe **Type I hypersensitivity reaction** or linked to immune reactions against immunoglobulins.
	3. **Circulatory Overload**:
	    
	    - The **most important and most common complication that may result in death**.
	    - Mechanism: Results in **pulmonary congestion and acute heart failure** due to excessive volume.
	    - Onset: May be **immediate**, or delayed up to 24 hours.
	    - High risk in patients with **chronic anaemia, infants, and the elderly**.
	
	While **Air Embolism** is listed as a potential non-immune complication, it is noted as unlikely with modern plastic bags.
