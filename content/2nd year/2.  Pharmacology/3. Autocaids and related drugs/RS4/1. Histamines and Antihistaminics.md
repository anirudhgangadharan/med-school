	Nonsedative Antihistamines (H1 Blockers) – Newer Second-Generation**
	
	These drugs, marketed after 1980, are characterised by **reduced central nervous system (CNS) effects** and **higher H1 receptor selectivity**.
	
	**1. Mention Four Examples:**
	
	- **Fexofenadine**
	- **Loratadine**
	- **Desloratadine**
	- **Cetirizine**
	
	**2. Advantages over First-Generation Antihistamines:**
	
	- **Minimal/Absent CNS Depression**:
	    - **Practically non-sedating** and do not cause significant psychomotor impairment. Patients are **not cautioned against operating motor vehicles or machinery**.
	    - They **do not potentiate the effects of alcohol or benzodiazepines**.
	    - This lack of CNS effect makes them **preferred for children** as they do not interfere with learning and academic tasks.
	- **Lack of Anticholinergic Side Effects**:
	    - Due to their **higher H1 selectivity**, they produce **no/minimal anticholinergic side effects** such as dry mouth, altered bowel movements, urinary hesitancy, or blurring of vision.
	- **Improved Cardiac Safety Profile**:
	    - They generally have a **low propensity to block cardiac ion channels** (e.g., delayed rectifier K-channels), thus **minimising the risk of cardiac arrhythmias** (e.g., Torsades de pointes), unlike older withdrawn second-generation drugs like terfenadine and astemizole.
	    - Many have **minimal drug interactions** related to metabolism (e.g., fexofenadine is minimally metabolised, loratadine/cetirizine have not shown interactions with macrolides or antifungals).
	- **Additional Antiallergic Mechanisms (beyond H1 blockade)**:
	    - Some second-generation antihistamines possess **additional antiallergic properties**, such as inhibiting the **late phase allergic reaction** by modifying the release/action of mediators like leukotrienes (LTs), platelet-activating factor (PAF), and cytokines. For example, cetirizine inhibits histamine release, cytotoxic mediators from platelets, and eosinophil chemotaxis.
	
	**Clinical Relevance:** The superior safety and tolerability profiles of second-generation antihistamines, particularly their minimal CNS and anticholinergic effects, make them the **first-line choice** for chronic allergic conditions such as allergic rhinitis and urticaria. Their selective action means they are **less effective** as antipruritics, antiemetics, or antitussives compared to first-generation agents, whose broader effects are often linked to their non-H1 receptor activity.
