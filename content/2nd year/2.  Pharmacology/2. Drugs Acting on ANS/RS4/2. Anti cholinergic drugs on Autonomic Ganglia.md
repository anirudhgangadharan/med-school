1. Atropine substitutes and uses, clinical effects and contraindications
	### **Atropine Substitutes: Uses, Clinical Effects, & Contraindications**
	
	**I. Rationale for Substitutes:**
	
	- Atropine substitutes are **semisynthetic derivatives** of belladonna alkaloids or **synthetic compounds**.
	- Developed to achieve **more selective actions** on specific functions, minimizing widespread atropinic side effects.
	
	**II. Key Atropine Substitutes & Their Specific Uses:**
	
	- **Hyoscine Butyl Bromide:**
	    - Less potent, longer acting than atropine.
	    - Used for **esophageal and gastrointestinal spastic conditions**.
	- **Atropine Methonitrate:**
	    - For **abdominal colics** and **hyperacidity**.
	- **Ipratropium Bromide:**
	    - **Selective bronchodilator** acting on bronchial muscle **without affecting respiratory secretions or mucociliary clearance**.
	    - Acts mainly on **larger central airways**.
	    - **More effective in COPD than bronchial asthma** (COPD has major reversible parasympathetic component).
	    - Used for **regular prophylactic use** in COPD, not acute relief.
	    - Also controls **rhinorrhoea** in perennial rhinitis/common cold.
	    - **Tiotropium bromide:** A **longer-acting alternative** for once-daily maintenance in **COPD**, reducing exacerbations.
	- **Valethamate:**
	    - Used to **hasten cervical dilation in delayed labour**.
	    - Visceral **antispasmodic** for urinary, biliary, intestinal colic.
	- **Pirenzepine:**
	    - **Selectively blocks M1 muscarinic receptors**.
	    - **Inhibits gastric secretion** with fewer typical atropinic side effects (which are M2/M3 mediated).
	    - Primarily acts on gastric intramural plexuses.
	    - Previously used for peptic ulcer, but overshadowed by H2 blockers/PPIs.
	- **Oxybutynin:**
	    - High affinity for **urinary bladder and salivary gland receptors**.
	    - Relatively selective for **M3 and M1 subtypes**.
	    - Used for **detrusor instability** (urinary frequency, urge incontinence), neurogenic bladder, enuresis.
	    - Intravesical instillation can minimize systemic side effects.
	- **Homatropine:**
	    - 10 times less potent than atropine.
	    - Used as a **mydriatic** (pupil dilation) for refraction, but cycloplegia (paralysis of accommodation) may be unsatisfactory in children.
	- **Tropicamide:**
	    - Preferred mydriatic for **refraction testing** due to **briefer action**.
	    - Also used for **fundoscopy** where only mydriasis is needed.
	    - Often combined with phenylephrine for faster, stronger mydriatic effect.
	
	**III. General Clinical Effects (Atropine-like Actions):**
	
	- **Antisecretory:** Marked decrease in **sweat, salivary, tracheobronchial, and lacrimal secretions** due to M3 blockade. Historically used preanaesthetically for irritant general anesthetics.
	- **Cardiac:**
	    - Increases **heart rate** by reducing vagal tone (M2 blockade).
	    - Facilitates **A-V conduction**, abbreviating P-R interval.
	    - Used for **sinus bradycardia** and partial **heart block** when due to increased vagal tone.
	- **Ocular:**
	    - Causes **mydriasis** (pupil dilation) and **cycloplegia** (loss of accommodation).
	    - Can increase intraocular tension, especially in narrow-angle glaucoma.
	    - Therapeutic use in **iritis, iridocyclitis, keratitis** to rest intraocular muscles and prevent adhesions.
	- **Smooth Muscle Relaxation:**
	    - **Bronchial:** Causes bronchodilation and reduces airway resistance (e.g., in COPD).
	    - **Gastrointestinal:** Decreases motility and tone.
	    - **Urinary Tract:** Relaxes ureter and bladder (detrusor muscle), which can lead to **urinary retention**, particularly in older males with prostatic hypertrophy. Can be beneficial for **neurogenic bladder** and **enuresis** by increasing bladder capacity.
	- **Central Nervous System (CNS):**
	    - **Atropine** at high doses can cause **excitement, mental confusion, disorientation, tremors, convulsions, delirium, and hallucinations**.
	    - **Hyoscine** at low doses causes **depression, amnesia, fatigue, and drowsiness**.
	    - Central anticholinergics are used for **Parkinsonism** and **drug-induced extrapyramidal syndromes**.
	
	**IV. Contraindications & Precautions:**
	
	- **Narrow iridocorneal angle / Acute congestive glaucoma:** Absolute contraindication, as it can precipitate or aggravate acute angle-closure glaucoma.
	- **Prostatic hypertrophy:** Caution advised due to risk of **urinary retention**.
	- **Febrile patients:** Caution advised as they can inhibit sweating and stimulate the hypothalamic temperature-regulating center, leading to **atropine fever**.
	- **Infants and the elderly:** More susceptible to adverse effects.
	- **Interactions:** Can slow the absorption of other drugs.
	
	**V. General Side Effects (Exacerbated with Overdose):**
	
	- Dry mouth, hot/flushed skin, fever, difficulty in micturition, decreased bowel sounds, blurred vision, photophobia, palpitation, excitement, psychotic behaviour, ataxia, delirium, and visual hallucinations. Severe overdose can lead to hypotension, shock, convulsions, and coma.
