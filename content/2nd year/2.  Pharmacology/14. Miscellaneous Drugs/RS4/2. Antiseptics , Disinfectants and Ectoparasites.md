	**Antiseptics: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. Definition & Characteristics**
	
	- **Antiseptic**: An agent used to **eliminate microorganisms on living tissues**.
	- **Germicide**: An agent used to kill microorganisms but not spores, encompassing both disinfectants and antiseptics.
	- **Ideal Antiseptic Qualities**:
	    - **Effective against all pathogens**.
	    - **Effective in the presence of organic matter** (e.g., blood, pus, excreta).
	    - **Stable**.
	    - **Should not cause irritation** on topical application.
	
	**II. Classification & Uses**
	
	**A. Phenols and Related Agents**
	
	- **Phenol (Carbolic acid)**:
	    - **Uses**: Rarely used as antiseptic (corrosive, penetrates intact skin). Can disinfect utensils, excreta, infected glassware.
	- **Cresol (Methylphenol)**:
	    - **Uses**: Disinfect utensils, excreta, infected glassware. More active and safer than phenol.
	- **Chloroxylenol**:
	    - **Uses**: **Active ingredient of Dettol**. Disinfect surgical instruments, used as **antiseptic for skin before surgery**.
	- **Resorcinol**:
	    - **Uses**: Used in **eczema, ringworm, seborrhoeic dermatitis** (keratolytic and antipruritic properties).
	- **Hexachlorophene**:
	    - **Uses**: Used as an **antiseptic for skin before surgery, furunculosis, seborrhoeic dermatitis**.
	
	**B. Chlorhexidine**
	
	- **Uses**:
	    - **Mouthwash**.
	    - **Antiseptic for skin prior to surgery**.
	    - **Antiplaque and antigingivitis agent** (as mouthwash).
	
	**C. Alcohols**
	
	- **Mechanism**: Denature and precipitate bacterial proteins.
	- **Ethyl alcohol (70%)**:
	    - **Uses**: **Antiseptic on skin before injections and surgical procedures**.
	    - _Note_: Efficacy decreases above 90%. Not for open wounds, mucosa, ulcers (irritant), or disinfecting instruments (promotes rusting).
	- **Isopropyl alcohol (68%–72%)**:
	    - **Uses**: Antiseptic (more potent than ethyl alcohol). Can **disinfect clinical thermometers**.
	
	**D. Aldehydes**
	
	- **Mechanism**: Denature proteins (protoplasmic poisons).
	- **Formaldehyde (40% solution = Formalin)**:
	    - **Uses**: Removal of warts (palms/soles), disinfection of sputum, preservation of anatomical/pathological specimens. Gas for **fumigation of wards/operation theatres**.
	- **Glutaraldehyde (2% solution)**:
	    - **Uses**: **Sterilize surgical instruments, plastic endotracheal tubes, endoscopes**. Treat hyperhidrosis of palms/soles.
	
	**E. Oxidizing Agents**
	
	- **Mechanism**: Release nascent oxygen, which oxidizes bacterial protoplasm.
	- **Hydrogen peroxide**:
	    - **Uses**: **Cleaning wounds and abscess cavities**, removal of slough/ear wax. Disinfect contact lenses, plastic implants.
	- **Potassium permanganate**:
	    - **Uses**: **Gargling** (1:4000–1:10,000 solution, Condy's lotion). **Styptic** (5% solution). Fungal infections (e.g., athlete's foot, 1% solution). **Stomach wash in alkaloid poisoning**. Purification of well water.
	
	**F. Halogens**
	
	- **Mechanism**: Oxidizing agents.
	- **Chlorine**:
	    - **Uses**: Disinfection of water.
	    - **Chloramines**: Mouthwash, dressing of wounds.
	    - **Chlorinated lime (Bleaching powder)**: Disinfect drinking water, toilets.
	    - **Sodium hypochlorite**: Root canal disinfectant.
	- **Iodine**:
	    - **Uses**:
	        - **Tincture iodine (2% in alcohol)**: Antiseptic on skin for wounds, prior to surgery.
	        - **Mandl's paint**: Topical use in tonsillitis and pharyngitis.
	        - **Lugol's iodine**: Used in thyrotoxicosis.
	- **Iodophores (e.g., Povidone iodine)**:
	    - **Uses**: Nonirritant, used in **burns, boils, prior to surgery**.
	
	**G. Acids**
	
	- **Boric acid and sodium borate (Borax)**:
	    - **Uses**: Fungistatic and bacteriostatic. Component of prickly heat powder.
	- **Benzoic acid**:
	    - **Uses**: Antibacterial and antifungal. **Whitfield’s ointment** (6% benzoic acid + 3% salicylic acid) for ringworm infections.
	
	**H. Metallic Salts**
	
	- **Zinc sulphate**:
	    - **Uses**: Antiseptic and astringent. Decreases sweating (component in deodorants). Component in calamine lotion for urticaria and eczema.
	- **Silver nitrate**:
	    - **Uses**: Astringent and antiseptic. Can be used on **oral ulcers**.
	
	**I. Dyes**
	
	- **Uses**: Topical antiseptics that stain the skin.
	    - **Gentian violet and Brilliant green**: Used in **gingivitis, oral thrush, bed sores, chronic ulcers, burns**.
	
	**J. Surface Active Agents (Surfactants)**
	
	- **Mechanism**: Lower surface tension of solutions.
	- **Cationic surfactants**:
	    - **Benzalkonium chloride**: Antiseptic on skin prior to surgery, to store sterilized instruments.
	    - **Cetrimide**: Component of **Savlon (with chlorhexidine)** used to disinfect thermometers.
	    - **Cetylpyridinium chloride**.
	
	**K. Gases**
	
	- **Ethylene oxide**:
	    - **Uses**: **Sterilization of heat-sensitive equipment** like heart-lung machines, plastic equipment, sutures, dental equipment, cardiac catheters.
	    - _Note_: Highly inflammable and explosive, not for fumigation.
	
	**L. Miscellaneous**
	
	- **Nitrofurazone**: Mentioned as miscellaneous, but specific uses as an antiseptic are not detailed in the provided text.
2. Drug treatment of scabies-enlist the drugs
	**Drug Treatment of Scabies: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. General Principles / Clinical Relevance**
	
	- Scabies is a contagious skin infestation caused by _Sarcoptes scabiei_ (itch mite).
	- **All family members should be treated simultaneously** to prevent re-infestation.
	
	**II. Enlisted Drugs & Key Features**
	
	1. **Permethrin**
	    
	    - **Formulation**: 5% cream/lotion/gel/soap.
	    - **Application**: Applied to the skin from **neck to toes**, washed off after **10–12 hours**.
	    - **Efficacy**: **Most efficacious and most frequently used drug** for scabies.
	    - **Side Effects**: Skin rashes, redness, itching, burning.
	2. **Gamma Benzene Hexachloride (Lindane)**
	    - **Formulation**: 1% emulsion/lotion/cream/ointment/soap.
	    - **Application**: Applied to the skin from **neck to toes**, washed off after **12 hours**.
	    - **Contraindications**: **Contraindicated in children, epileptics, and pregnant women** due to potential neurological side effects.
	    - **Side Effects**: Skin rashes, headache, restlessness, convulsions, cardiac arrhythmias.
	3. **Crotamiton**
	    
	    - **Formulation**: 10% lotion/cream.
	    - **Application**: Applied to the skin **below the chin twice at 24-hour intervals** after a bath.
	    - **Efficacy**: **Less efficacious**, often requires repeated administration.
	    - **Use in Children**: Can be used in children.
	    - **Side Effects**: Skin rashes, itching, dermatitis.
	4. **Benzyl Benzoate**
	    
	    - **Formulation**: 25% emulsion/lotion.
	    - **Application**: Applied to the skin from **neck to toes twice at 12-hour intervals** after a bath; washed off 24 hours after the second application.
	    - **Role**: Considered a **second-line drug** for scabies.
	    - **Side Effects**: Skin rashes, dermatitis.
	5. **Ivermectin**
	    
	    - **Formulation**: Oral tablet (0.2 mg/kg).
	    - **Efficacy**: **Single oral dose produces almost complete cure**.
	    - **Contraindications**: **Contraindicated in children, pregnant, and lactating women**.
