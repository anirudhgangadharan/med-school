1. Name the live attenuated vaccines
	**Live Attenuated Vaccines: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. Definition & Key Characteristics**
	
	- Consist of **living organisms** (bacteria or viruses) where virulence has been **reduced** (attenuated).
	- They elicit a strong immune response.
	- Produce **long-lasting immunity**.
	- Are **more efficacious** compared to inactivated (killed) vaccines.
	- Are **less stable at room temperature** and require proper storage.
	
	**II. Examples (for Medical School Exams)**
	
	- **Bacterial:**
	    - **Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG)**
	        - Administered as a single dose **intradermally** to the upper arm, usually at birth or within 6 weeks of age.
	        - May cause ulceration and lymphadenitis.
	- **Viral:**
	    - **Oral Polio Vaccine (Sabin)**
	        - Prepared from **live attenuated virus**.
	        - Less expensive and suitable for controlling epidemics.
	        - Requires storage in a freezer.
	    - **Measles, Mumps, and Rubella (MMR)**
	        - A **combination vaccine** providing protection against Measles, Mumps, and Rubella.
	        - Administered intramuscularly.
	        - May cause pain, swelling, redness, irritability, and fever.
