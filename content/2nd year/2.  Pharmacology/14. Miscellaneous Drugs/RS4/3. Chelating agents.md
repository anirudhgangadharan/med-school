1. Dimercaprol/ British Anti Lewisite-uses , indications and contraindications
	**Dimercaprol (British Anti-Lewisite, BAL): High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. General Information**
	
	- Developed as an antidote for **arsenic-containing war gases** (e.g., lewisite) during World War II.
	
	**II. Mechanism of Action (MOA)**
	
	- Acts as a **chelating agent**.
	- The **sulphydryl (SH) groups of dimercaprol react with metals to form a complex**.
	- This complex is **water-soluble** and **rapidly excreted from the body**.
	
	**III. Route of Administration (ROA)**
	
	- Administered **intramuscularly (i.m.)**.
	
	**IV. Therapeutic Uses / Indications**
	
	- Primarily used in the treatment of poisoning by:
	    - **Arsenic (As)**
	    - **Mercury (Hg)**
	    - **Gold (Au)**
	    - **Bismuth (Bi)**
	- Also used as an **adjuvant in copper (Cu) and lead (Pb) poisoning**.
	
	**V. Contraindications**
	
	- **Iron poisoning**
	- **Cadmium poisoning**
	
	**VI. Adverse Effects**
	
	- **Nausea**
	- **Vomiting**
	- **Headache**
	- **Fever**
	- **Salivation**
	- **Rise in Blood Pressure (BP)**
	- **Tachycardia**
	- **Pain at the site of injection**
2. D-pencilamine-uses
	**D-Penicillamine: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. General Information**
	
	- **Class/Origin**: It is a degradation product of penicillin.
	- **Clinical Relevance**: May have **cross-reactivity with penicillins**.
	
	**II. Therapeutic Uses**
	
	- **Heavy Metal Poisoning**: Effective in treating **copper, mercury, zinc, and lead poisoning**.
	- **Wilson's Disease**:
	    - **Mechanism**: Used to **chelate copper** and **promote its excretion** from the body.
	    - **Clinical Note**: Wilson's disease is characterised by copper accumulation in tissues due to decreased serum ceruloplasmin.
	    - **Duration**: **Life-long therapy is required** for Wilson's disease.
	- **Other Conditions**:
	    - **Scleroderma**
	    - **Cystinuria**
	    - **Rheumatoid Arthritis**
3. Drug treatment for acute iron poisoning-mention antidote and give MOA
	**Acute Iron Poisoning: High-Yield Notes**
	
	**I. Clinical Presentation (Relevant)**
	
	- Commonly seen in **young children**.
	- Manifestations include **nausea, vomiting, epigastric pain, bloody diarrhoea, dehydration, cyanosis, drowsiness, hyperventilation, metabolic acidosis, convulsions, coma, and can be fatal**.
	
	**II. General Management**
	
	- **Hospitalisation** and careful observation are essential.
	- **Supportive Measures**: Maintain **airway, breathing, circulation**, and correct **fluid and electrolyte imbalance**.
	- **Gastrointestinal Decontamination**:
	    - **Gastric lavage** with **sodium bicarbonate** to precipitate iron and reduce its absorption. This should ideally be performed within 2–3 hours of poisoning. Endotracheal intubation is required if the patient is unconscious.
	    - Vomiting can also be induced to remove ingested iron.
	- **Symptomatic Control**: **Intravenous diazepam** can be administered slowly to control convulsions.
	
	**III. Specific Therapy: Antidotes**
	
	**A. Desferrioxamine (Deferoxamine)**
	
	- **Antidote of Choice**: This is the **drug of choice for acute iron poisoning**.
	- **Class**: A potent **iron-chelating agent**.
	- **Route of Administration (ROA)**: Administered **intravenously (i.v.) infusion or intramuscularly (i.m.)**, depending on the severity of poisoning. It is **not effective orally** due to poor absorption.
	- **Mechanism of Action (MOA)**:
	    - Desferrioxamine acts as a **chelating agent** by forming **water-soluble complexes** with metals.
	    - These water-soluble complexes are **rapidly excreted from the body**.
	    - Specifically, it **chelates iron from haemosiderin and ferritin** (iron storage proteins). However, it **does not affect iron bound in haemoglobin or cytochrome**.
	    - It exhibits a **low affinity for calcium**.
	- **Adverse Effects (Key for Exams)**: Allergic reactions (e.g., skin rashes, itching, flushing, anaphylaxis), diarrhoea, dyspnoea, dysuria, hypotension, and tachycardia. Long-term use may lead to neurotoxicity.
	- **Contraindications**: **Pregnancy** and **renal insufficiency**.
	
	**B. Deferiprone**
	
	- **Role**: An **oral iron chelator**.
	- **Efficacy**: It is **less effective than desferrioxamine** in acute iron poisoning.
	- **Other Use**: Also used in transfusion siderosis in thalassaemia.
	- **Adverse Effects**: Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, joint pain, and rarely agranulocytosis.
