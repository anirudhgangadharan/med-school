1. Metronidazole-mechanism of action, uses and side effects
	### **Metronidazole: Mechanism of Action, Uses, and Side Effects**
	
	**Key Concept:** Metronidazole is a **prodrug** selectively active against **anaerobic bacteria** and **protozoa**.
	
	#### **Mechanism of Action**
	
	- **Selective Toxicity:** Metronidazole itself is inactive. It is selectively toxic to anaerobic and microaerophilic microorganisms because of unique redox proteins found in these organisms.
	- **Reductive Activation:** After entering the microbial cell by diffusion, its **nitro group (-NO2)** is _reductively activated_ by specific redox proteins (like ferredoxins) that operate only in anaerobic environments.
	- **Reactive Nitro Radical Formation:** This reduction generates a **highly reactive nitro radical**.
	- **DNA Damage & Energy Disruption:** This nitro radical acts as an **electron sink**, competing with the biological electron acceptors in the pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase (PFOR) enzyme pathway.
	    - This **disrupts the energy metabolism** of anaerobes (which lack mitochondria).
	    - Crucially, the reactive radical **damages microbial DNA**, leading to the **death of the organism**.
	- **Bactericidal/Cidal Effect:** The combination of metabolic disruption and DNA damage results in a **cidal effect** (killing).
	- **Aerobic Inactivity:** It is ineffective against aerobic bacteria because oxygen competes with the nitro radical for electrons, inhibiting its reductive activation.
	- **Resistance:** Can develop if anaerobes become deficient in the mechanism generating the nitro radical or have lower PFOR levels.
	
	#### **Clinical Uses**
	
	Metronidazole exhibits **broad-spectrum cidal activity** against a range of protozoa and anaerobic bacteria.
	
	- **Protozoal Infections:**
	    
	    - **Amoebiasis:**
	        - **First-line drug** for **all forms of amoebic infection**, including invasive dysentery and liver abscess.
	        - **Dosage:** 800 mg TDS orally for 7-10 days for invasive dysentery/liver abscess; 500 mg i.v. every 6-8 hours in severe cases.
	        - _Clinical Point:_ Less effective at eradicating cysts from the colon; _always combine or follow with a luminal amoebicide_.
	    - **Giardiasis:** Highly effective, 400 mg TDS for 7 days or 2g daily for 3 days.
	    - **Trichomonas Vaginitis:** Drug of choice, typically a 2.0g single oral dose.
	    - **Guinea Worm Extraction:** Used to facilitate extraction of _Dracunculus medinensis_.
	- **Anaerobic Bacterial Infections:**
	    
	    - **General Anaerobic Infections:** Effective against _Bacteroides fragilis_, _Clostridium perfringens_, _Clostridium difficile_, _Fusobacterium_, peptococci, spirochetes, and anaerobic streptococci. Used in pelvic inflammatory disease, lung abscess, intra-abdominal infections, etc..
	    - **Brain Abscess:** Often used in **combination with cephalosporins or gentamicin** due to mixed aerobic/anaerobic etiology.
	    - **Pseudomembranous Enterocolitis (PMC):** Caused by _Clostridium difficile_. **Oral metronidazole (400-800 mg BD-TDS for 10-14 days) is preferred** over vancomycin (cheaper, less toxic, more convenient).
	    - **Acute Necrotizing Ulcerative Gingivitis (ANUG) / Trench Mouth:** Drug of choice for mixed anaerobic flora. Often combined with amoxicillin/tetracycline/erythromycin.
	    - **_Helicobacter pylori_ Eradication:** Part of **triple drug regimens** (e.g., with amoxicillin/clarithromycin and a proton pump inhibitor).
	    - **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Recommended in high-risk procedures like colorectal and biliary surgery.
	
	#### **Side Effects**
	
	Generally frequent and unpleasant, but mostly non-serious.
	
	- **Gastrointestinal (Common):**
	    - **Metallic taste** (very characteristic).
	    - Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, dry mouth, glossitis, stomatitis, furry tongue, diarrhoea.
	- **Neurological:**
	    - Headache, dizziness, vertigo, ataxia, incoordination.
	    - Drowsiness, insomnia, paresthesias.
	    - Peripheral neuropathy (with prolonged high doses), rarely seizures or encephalopathy.
	    - _Clinical Point:_ **Contraindicated in neurological disease**.
	- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Rashes, urticaria, itching.
	- **Disulfiram-like Reaction:** Can occur with **alcohol consumption** (abdominal cramps, nausea, vomiting, flushing, headache). Patients should be warned to avoid alcohol during treatment.
	- **Urine Discoloration:** Harmless **dark reddish-brown urine**.
	- **Haematological:** Rarely, bone marrow depression (leucopenia, neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia).
	- **Local:** Thrombophlebitis of the injected vein if solution is not well diluted.
	- **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated in the first trimester** due to its mutagenic potential, despite no proven teratogenic effects in humans.
	
	**Drug Interactions (Clinical Relevance):**
	
	- **Warfarin:** Metronidazole **enhances warfarin's anticoagulant effect** by inhibiting its metabolism, leading to increased bleeding risk. Warfarin dose may need reduction.
	- **Lithium:** Decreases renal elimination of lithium, potentially precipitating lithium toxicity.
	- **Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, Phenobarbitone):** Can reduce metronidazole's therapeutic effect by hastening its metabolism.
	- **Cimetidine:** Can reduce metronidazole metabolism.
2. Metronidazole is not affective in aerobic infections, explain
	### **Metronidazole: Ineffectiveness in Aerobic Infections**
	
	**Key Concept:** Metronidazole is a **prodrug** that requires an **anaerobic or microaerophilic environment for activation**, rendering it ineffective against aerobic organisms.
	
	#### **Mechanism of Ineffectiveness (Why Aerobes are Spared)**
	
	- **Selective Toxicity & Reductive Activation:**
	    - Metronidazole itself is **inactive**.
	    - It is **selectively toxic** to anaerobic and microaerophilic microorganisms.
	    - Its **nitro group (-NO2)** must undergo **reductive activation** to form a **highly reactive nitro radical**.
	- **Anaerobic-Specific Enzymes:**
	    - This crucial reduction step is carried out by **unique redox proteins** (e.g., ferredoxins) found **only in anaerobic environments**.
	    - These enzymes are part of pathways like the pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase (PFOR) pathway, which is essential for the energy metabolism of anaerobes (which lack mitochondria).
	- **Oxygen's Role (Competitive Inhibition):**
	    - In the presence of oxygen (aerobic conditions), **O2 competes with the nitro radical** of metronidazole for the free electrons generated during the anaerobes' energy metabolism.
	    - This competition **inhibits the reductive activation** of metronidazole.
	- **No Toxic Radical Formation & DNA Damage:**
	    - Without this reductive activation, the toxic nitro radical is **not formed**.
	    - Consequently, metronidazole cannot act as an "electron sink" to disrupt anaerobic energy metabolism or damage microbial DNA, which are its primary cidal mechanisms.
	- **Aerobic Organism Characteristics:**
	    - Aerobic bacteria **do not possess the specific redox proteins** or metabolic pathways required to activate metronidazole.
	
	#### **Clinical Relevance**
	
	- This selective mechanism dictates Metronidazole's narrow spectrum, limiting its use to **anaerobic bacterial infections** (e.g., _Bacteroides fragilis_, _Clostridium difficile_) and **protozoal infections** (e.g., _Entamoeba histolytica_, _Giardia lamblia_, _Trichomonas vaginalis_).
	- For **mixed aerobic-anaerobic infections** (e.g., brain abscesses, intra-abdominal infections), Metronidazole is often **combined with other antibiotics** (e.g., gentamicin or cephalosporins) to ensure coverage of the aerobic pathogens.
