1.  Classify antitubercular drugs. Explain the mechanism of action and adverse effects of any one first line anti-tubercular drug. Mention two drugs useful in treating multidrug resistant tuberculosis
	**Antitubercular Drugs**
	
	**1. Classification**
	
	Antitubercular (Anti-TB) drugs are classified based on clinical utility and priority in use:
	
	- **First Line Oral Drugs (Group I):** Most potent, best tolerated, used routinely.
	    - Isoniazid (H)
	    - Rifampin (R)
	    - Pyrazinamide (Z)
	    - Ethambutol (E)
	- **Injectable Drugs (Group II):** Potent and bactericidal, but require injection.
	    - Streptomycin (S) - _Historically first-line, now often grouped here or considered second-line due to resistance/toxicity profile compared to oral first-line_.
	    - Kanamycin (Km)
	    - Amikacin (Am)
	    - Capreomycin (Cm)
	- **Fluoroquinolones (FQs) (Group III):** Well-tolerated, bactericidal oral drugs; crucial for drug-resistant TB.
	    - Ofloxacin (Ofx)
	    - Levofloxacin (Lfx)
	    - Moxifloxacin (Mfx)
	    - Ciprofloxacin (Cfx) - _Less active against M. tuberculosis than other FQs, not typically used for TB now_.
	- **Second Line Oral Drugs (Group IV):** Less effective, bacteriostatic, or more toxic drugs for resistant TB.
	    - Ethionamide (Eto)
	    - Prothionamide
	    - Cycloserine (Cs)
	    - Terizidone
	    - Paraaminosalicylic acid (PAS)
	    - Rifabutin
	    - Rifapentine
	- **Drugs with Unclear Efficacy (Group V):** Not recommended for MDR-TB, may be used as reserve drugs or in extensively resistant TB (XDR-TB).
	    - Bedaquiline (BDQ) - _Note: Recently approved, novel mechanism, high potency against resistant strains, but clinical use guidelines cautious_.
	    - Clarithromycin
	    - Clofazimine
	    - Linezolid
	    - Coamoxiclav
	    - Imipenem/cilastatin
	
	**2. Mechanism of Action and Adverse Effects of a First Line Drug: Rifampin (R)**
	
	- **Mechanism of Action:**
	    - **Bactericidal** to _M. tuberculosis_ and many other bacteria.
	    - Interrupts **RNA synthesis** by binding to the **β-subunit of mycobacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase**.
	    - This binding **blocks the enzyme's polymerizing function**.
	    - Basis of **selective toxicity**: Mammalian RNA polymerase **does not avidly bind rifampin**.
	    - Highly effective against **all subpopulations** of TB bacilli, especially slowly or intermittently dividing ones ("spurters").
	    - Affects both **extra- and intracellular** bacilli.
	    - Has good **sterilizing** and **resistance preventing** actions when used in combination.
	- **Adverse Effects:**
	    - Incidence similar to Isoniazid.
	    - Causes **red-orange discolouration** of urine, sweat, tears, saliva, and contact lenses.
	    - **Hepatotoxicity** is the most serious adverse effect, often seen as asymptomatic rise in transaminases, but can rarely cause hepatitis.
	    - Flu-like syndrome (fever, chills, myalgia) can occur with intermittent dosing, but is rare with daily dosing.
	    - Cutaneous reactions (rashes).
	    - Gastrointestinal disturbances (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea).
	    - Rarely: thrombocytopenia, haemolytic anaemia, renal failure.
	    - **Potent microsomal enzyme inducer** (CYP450 system, including CYP3A4, CYP2D6, etc.). This leads to **significant drug interactions**, increasing metabolism and reducing levels of many co-administered drugs (e.g., warfarin, oral contraceptives, corticosteroids, HIV protease inhibitors, NNRTIs, theophylline). **Contraceptive failures** can occur.
	
	**3. Drugs Useful in Treating Multidrug-Resistant Tuberculosis (MDR-TB)**
	
	MDR-TB is defined as resistance to both Rifampin and Isoniazid, with or without resistance to other first-line drugs. Treatment requires complex, longer, more expensive, and often more toxic regimens.
	
	Two drugs useful in treating MDR-TB, as indicated in the standard RNTCP regimen, are:
	
	- **Levofloxacin (Lfx):** A Fluoroquinolone (Group III). It is a potent oral bactericidal drug and a **key component** of regimens for drug-resistant TB. It kills mycobacteria inside macrophages.
	- **Kanamycin (Km):** An injectable Aminoglycoside antibiotic (Group II). It is often included in the intensive phase of MDR-TB regimens. Kanamycin is similar to Streptomycin in anti-TB activity and adverse effects.
2. Enumerate the first line and the second line drugs for treatment of tuberculosis. (Classify antitubercular drugs with examples.) Write the mechanism of action, therapeutic uses and adverse effects of rifampicin. Write the short course regimen for TB.
	**Tuberculosis (TB) Treatment**
	
	TB is a major health problem, particularly in developing countries. Treatment often requires a combination of drugs over a prolonged period.
	
	**Classification of Antitubercular Drugs**
	
	Antitubercular drugs are classified based on their efficacy and toxicity.
	
	1. **First Line Drugs:**
	    
	    - High antitubercular efficacy and low toxicity.
	    - Used routinely.
	    - **Examples:**
	        - **Isoniazid (H)**
	        - **Rifampin (R)**
	        - **Pyrazinamide (Z)**
	        - **Ethambutol (E)**
	        - Streptomycin (S) (often considered a supplemental 1st line drug, restricted use due to toxicity/injection requirement)
	    - Group I of the Alternative Grouping includes the oral first-line drugs (H, R, Z, E).
	    - Group II includes injectable drugs like Streptomycin, Kanamycin, Amikacin, Capreomycin.
	2. **Second Line Drugs:**
	    
	    - Either lower antitubercular efficacy or higher toxicity, or both.
	    - Used when first-line drugs cannot be used, or to supplement them, particularly in drug-resistant TB.
	    - **Examples:**
	        - Fluoroquinolones (FQs) (Ofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin, Ciprofloxacin) - Group III
	        - Ethionamide - Group IV
	        - Prothionamide - Group IV
	        - Cycloserine - Group IV
	        - Paraamino salicylic acid (PAS) - Group IV
	        - Rifabutin - Group IV
	        - Rifapentine - Group IV
	        - Bedaquiline - Group V (unclear efficacy, reserve)
	        - Clarithromycin - Group V
	        - Clofazimine - Group V
	        - Linezolid - Group V
	        - Coamoxiclav (Amoxicillin/clavulanate) - Group V
	        - Imipenem/cilastatin - Group V
	
	**Rifampin (Rifampicin, R)**
	
	- **Mechanism of Action:**
	    
	    - Interrupts **RNA synthesis**.
	    - Binds to the **β subunit of mycobacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase** (encoded by the _rpoB_ gene), blocking its polymerizing function.
	    - Selective toxicity because mammalian RNA polymerase does not avidly bind rifampin.
	    - Rapid development of resistance via _rpoB_ gene mutation reduces affinity for the drug.
	    - No cross-resistance with other anti-TB drugs, except rifampin congeners.
	- **Therapeutic Uses:**
	    
	    - **Primary use in Tuberculosis:**
	        - An **essential component of all antitubercular regimens**, unless the patient is intolerant or bacilli are resistant.
	        - As efficacious as INH against TB, and considered better than all other drugs.
	        - **Bactericidal** to _M tuberculosis_, killing all subpopulations, including slowly or intermittently dividing bacilli ('spurters').
	        - Affects both extra- and intracellular bacilli.
	        - Good sterilizing and resistance preventing actions.
	        - Combination with INH is synergistic, shortening treatment duration. Inclusion in short course regimens has enabled reduction of treatment duration to 6 months.
	    - **Other Uses:**
	        - Leprosy (most potent cidal drug for _M. leprae_).
	        - Prophylaxis of Meningococcal and _H. influenzae_ meningitis and carrier state.
	        - Second/third choice drug for MRSA, diphtheroids, and _Legionella_ infections.
	        - Combination with doxycycline is first-line therapy for brucellosis.
	- **Adverse Effects:**
	    
	    - Similar incidence to INH.
	    - Common: Abdominal cramps, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea.
	    - Causes urine and secretions to become **orange-red** (harmless).
	    - Rare but serious reactions: Purpura, haemolysis, shock, and renal failure.
	    - Avoid in hepatic or renal dysfunction.
	    - **Significant enzyme inducer** (microsomal enzymes like CYP450s). This enhances its own metabolism and that of many other drugs (e.g., warfarin, oral contraceptives, HIV protease inhibitors, NNRTIs, theophylline). Contraceptive failures can occur; higher dose OCP or alternative contraception advised.
	
	**Short Course Regimen for Drug-Sensitive TB**
	
	Based on the RNTCP guidelines (2016) for new patients presumed to have drug-sensitive TB:
	
	- **Total Duration:** **6 months**.
	- **Intensive Phase (IP):**
	    - Duration: **2 months**.
	    - Drugs: **4 drugs** - **Isoniazid (H) + Rifampin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E)** (i.e., **2HRZE**).
	    - Dosing Frequency: **Daily** administration is now preferred. Fixed Dose Combinations (FDCs) are advised for compliance.
	    - Purpose: Rapidly kill bacilli, reduce bacillary load, achieve sputum conversion, provide fast symptomatic relief, and reduce risk of resistance. Z is particularly effective in this phase.
	- **Continuation Phase (CP):**
	    - Duration: **4 months**.
	    - Drugs: **3 drugs** - **Isoniazid (H) + Rifampin (R) + Ethambutol (E)** (i.e., **4HRE**). (Note: Z is stopped after the intensive phase).
	    - Dosing Frequency: **Daily**. FDCs used.
	    - Purpose: Eliminate remaining bacilli to prevent relapse. Inclusion of E in this phase (in the 2016 guidelines) is relevant in areas of high primary H resistance.
	
	Patients with previously treated TB or suspected drug resistance (like MDR-TB, RR-TB) require different, more complex, longer regimens often including second-line drugs. XDR-TB requires even more complex regimens.

	**Clinical Scenario: Newly Diagnosed Pulmonary TB**
	
	A 40-year-old male presents with evening rise of temperature, cough, and weight loss, consistent with pulmonary tuberculosis. Timely and appropriate treatment is crucial.
	
	**a. Treatment Regimen for Newly Diagnosed Pulmonary TB**
	
	For new patients presumed to have drug-sensitive pulmonary TB, the standard regimen based on the RNTCP guidelines (2016) is **short course chemotherapy**.
	
	- **Total Duration:** **6 months**.
	- **Phases:**
	    - **Intensive Phase (IP):**
	        - Duration: **2 months**.
	        - Drugs: **4 drugs** administered daily - **Isoniazid (H) + Rifampin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E)** (Regimen code: **2HRZE**).
	        - Purpose: To rapidly kill bacilli, reduce bacillary load, and achieve sputum conversion. Z is highly effective against intracellular and inflamed site bacilli, contributing significantly to sterilizing activity in this phase. Daily administration is preferred over thrice-weekly dosing. Fixed Dose Combinations (FDCs) are advised for compliance.
	    - **Continuation Phase (CP):**
	        - Duration: **4 months**.
	        - Drugs: **3 drugs** administered daily - **Isoniazid (H) + Rifampin (R) + Ethambutol (E)** (Regimen code: **4HRE**). (Z is stopped after the IP).
	        - Purpose: To eliminate remaining bacilli and prevent relapse. Inclusion of E in this phase in the 2016 guidelines is relevant in areas with high primary H resistance.
	
	**b. Adverse Effects and Uses of Rifampin (Rifampicin, R)**
	
	- **Uses:**
	    - **Essential Component of TB Regimens:** An **essential drug** in all antitubercular regimens unless contraindicated or resistance is present.
	    - **High Efficacy:** As efficacious as INH against TB and better than other first-line drugs.
	    - **Bactericidal:** Kills _M. tuberculosis_.
	    - **Activity Profile:** Acts on all subpopulations of TB bacilli, particularly slowly or intermittently dividing ones ('spurters'). Affects both extracellular and intracellular bacilli. Has good sterilizing and resistance preventing actions when used in combination.
	    - **Combination Synergy:** Combination with INH is synergistic, shortening treatment duration. Inclusion in short course regimens allows 6-month treatment.
	    - **Other Uses:** Treatment of Leprosy (most potent cidal drug for _M. leprae_), prophylaxis for Meningococcal and _H. influenzae_ meningitis, second/third choice for MRSA, diphtheroids, _Legionella_, first-line for brucellosis with doxycycline.
	- **Adverse Effects:**
	    - Causes **orange-red discolouration** of urine, sweat, tears, saliva (harmless).
	    - **Hepatotoxicity:** Most serious effect, can cause asymptomatic transaminase elevation or rarely hepatitis. Avoid in hepatic dysfunction.
	    - **Flu-like syndrome:** Occasional with intermittent dosing.
	    - Cutaneous reactions (rashes).
	    - Gastrointestinal disturbances (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal cramps).
	    - Rarely: purpura, haemolysis, shock, renal failure. Avoid in renal dysfunction.
	    - **Potent Microsomal Enzyme Inducer:** **Significant drug interactions** by inducing CYP450 isoenzymes. Increases metabolism/reduces levels of many drugs (e.g., warfarin, oral contraceptives, corticosteroids, HIV protease inhibitors, NNRTIs, theophylline). **Contraceptive failures** can occur; higher dose OCP or alternative contraception recommended.
	
	**c. Four Drugs Used in Multidrug-Resistant Tuberculosis (MDR-TB)**
	
	MDR-TB is defined as resistance to both Rifampin and Isoniazid, with or without resistance to other first-line drugs. Treatment requires complex, longer, often more toxic regimens, usually including second-line drugs.
	
	Four drugs useful in treating MDR-TB, as per sources, include:
	
	1. **Kanamycin (Km):** An injectable aminoglycoside (Group II). Included in the intensive phase of the RNTCP standardized regimen for MDR-TB. Effective against many Streptomycin-resistant and MDR strains. Can cause vestibular toxicity (less than hearing loss) and nephrotoxicity.
	2. **Levofloxacin (Lfx):** A fluoroquinolone (Group III). A key component of all regimens for MDR-TB, unless resistance is present. Included in the RNTCP standardized regimen for MDR-TB. High activity against _M. tuberculosis_.
	3. **Ethionamide (Eto):** An oral second-line drug (Group IV) with moderate efficacy. Mechanism similar to INH, interfering with mycolic acid synthesis. Included in the intensive and continuation phases of the RNTCP standardized regimen for MDR-TB. Can cause gastrointestinal intolerance, hepatotoxicity.
	4. **Cycloserine (Cs):** An oral second-line drug (Group IV). Inhibits cell wall synthesis. Used in MDR-TB cases, included in the RNTCP standardized regimen. Can cause dose-related CNS toxicity (psychoses, seizures). Pyridoxine is given to prevent neurotoxicity of anti-TB drugs like Cycloserine and INH.
4. Short course chemotherapy regimen for a newly diagnosed drug sensitive pulmonary tuberculosis
	**Short Course Chemotherapy Regimen: Newly Diagnosed Drug-Sensitive Pulmonary TB**
	
	This regimen is based on the Revised National Tuberculosis Control Programme (RNTCP) guidelines, 2016, for new patients presumed to have drug-sensitive TB.
	
	- **Total Duration:** **6 months**.
	- **Phases:** The treatment is divided into two phases:
	    - **Intensive Phase (IP):**
	        - Duration: **2 months**.
	        - Drugs: **4 drugs** administered **daily**.
	            - **Isoniazid (H)**
	            - **Rifampin (R)**
	            - **Pyrazinamide (Z)**
	            - **Ethambutol (E)**
	        - Regimen Code: **2HRZE**.
	        - Purpose: Rapidly kill bacilli, significantly reduce the bacillary load, achieve sputum conversion quickly, provide fast symptomatic relief, and reduce the risk of selecting resistant bacilli. Z is particularly effective against intracellular bacilli and those at inflamed, acidic sites, providing good sterilizing activity. The four-drug combination in the IP reduces the risk of resistance, especially given increasing primary H resistance. Daily administration is considered optimal and is the preferred frequency in the 2016 guidelines. Fixed Dose Combinations (FDCs) are advised for compliance.
	    - **Continuation Phase (CP):**
	        - Duration: **4 months**.
	        - Drugs: **3 drugs** administered **daily**.
	            - **Isoniazid (H)**
	            - **Rifampin (R)**
	            - **Ethambutol (E)**
	        - Regimen Code: **4HRE**. (Note: Z is stopped after the IP).
	        - Purpose: Eliminate remaining bacilli ('persisters') to ensure cure and prevent relapse. Inclusion of E in this phase (a change in the 2016 guidelines) is based on WHO suggestions for areas with high primary H resistance. Daily administration is preferred. FDCs are used.
	
	**Note:** This regimen is for _new_ patients who have not previously received anti-TB drugs for more than one month and are _presumed_ to have drug-sensitive TB. Patients with previously treated TB or suspected drug resistance require different, more complex regimens.
