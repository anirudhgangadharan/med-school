1. Corticosteroids (glucocorticoids)-pharmacological actions, therapeutic uses, adverse effects of prolonged therapy, contraindications
	Corticosteroids (Glucocorticoids) are a class of powerful hormones that exert widespread effects on metabolism, inflammation, and immunity. They are vital for maintaining homeostasis and responding to stress.
	
	### **1. Pharmacological Actions**
	
	Glucocorticoids influence various bodily systems through direct and permissive actions, meaning they can directly cause effects or facilitate other hormones in doing so.
	
	- **Metabolic Effects**:
	    - **Carbohydrate & Protein Metabolism**: Promote **glycogen deposition in the liver** and **gluconeogenesis**, while inhibiting glucose utilization by peripheral tissues. This leads to **hyperglycaemia, insulin resistance, and a diabetes-like state**. They are **catabolic**, causing protein breakdown and amino acid mobilization from peripheral tissues, leading to muscle wasting, lympholysis, and thinning of skin. Uric acid excretion is increased.
	    - **Fat Metabolism**: Primarily permissive, they promote lipolysis due to glucagon, growth hormone, adrenaline, and thyroxine. They cause a **redistribution of body fat**, leading to features like 'moon face', 'buffalo hump', and 'fish mouth', while extremities may thin.
	    - **Calcium Metabolism**: **Inhibit intestinal absorption** and **enhance renal excretion of Ca$^{2+}$**. This results in a negative calcium balance and loss of osteoid from bone, with spongy bones being more sensitive.
	- **Water Excretion**: Maintain normal glomerular filtration rate (GFR) and enhance secretory activity of renal tubules. In adrenal insufficiency, the capacity to excrete a water load is significantly reduced.
	- **Cardiovascular System (CVS)**: Restrict capillary permeability, maintain arteriolar tone, and myocardial contractility. They play a permissive role in the pressor action of adrenaline and angiotensin, as well as in the development of hypertension. Adrenal insufficiency can lead to cardiovascular collapse due to these changes along with hypovolaemia.
	- **Skeletal Muscles**: Optimal corticosteroid levels are necessary for normal muscle activity. Both hypocorticism (diminished work capacity) and hypercorticism (hypokalaemia, muscle wasting) can lead to weakness.
	- **Central Nervous System (CNS)**: Mild **euphoria** is common with pharmacological doses and may progress to increased motor activity, insomnia, hypomania, or depression. They maintain sensory perception and neuronal excitability, but **high doses can lower the seizure threshold**.
	- **Gastrointestinal (GIT)**: **Increase gastric acid and pepsin secretion**, which **may aggravate peptic ulcers**.
	- **Foetal Lungs**: Promote structural and functional maturation of foetal lungs near term and stimulate pulmonary surfactant production, essential for inflation at birth.
	- **Lymphoid Tissue and Blood Cells**: Enhance destruction of lymphoid cells (T cells more sensitive than B cells) and have a marked lytic effect on malignant lymphatic cells (e.g., in lymphomas). They increase circulating red blood cells (RBCs), platelets, and neutrophils, while decreasing lymphocytes, eosinophils, and basophils by sequestration in tissues.
	- **Inflammatory Responses**: Suppress inflammation non-specifically, irrespective of the type of injury. This includes attenuating increased capillary permeability, local exudation, cellular infiltration, phagocytic activity, and late responses like capillary proliferation and scar formation. The cardinal signs of inflammation (redness, heat, swelling, pain) are suppressed. The overall mechanism appears to be limiting the recruitment of inflammatory cells and the production of proinflammatory mediators (like PGs, LTs, PAF) by indirectly inhibiting phospholipase A2. These actions are **palliative**, do not remove the cause of inflammation, and **favour the spread of infections** while **interfering with healing and scar formation** (e.g., peptic ulcer perforation).
	- **Immunological and Allergic Responses**: Impair immunological competence and suppress all types of hypersensitivity and allergic phenomena. The clinical effect is mainly due to suppression of leukocyte recruitment at the antigen site and the inflammatory response to immunological injury. They cause greater suppression of Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI), which involves T cells, making them useful in autoimmune diseases and organ transplantation.
	- **Mechanism at Cellular Level**: Corticosteroids penetrate cells and bind to a cytoplasmic receptor. This steroid-receptor complex then migrates into the nucleus and binds to specific DNA sequences (glucocorticoid responsive elements or GREs) on the chromatin, promoting or suppressing the transcription of specific mRNA and thus regulating protein synthesis. This process takes at least 30-60 minutes, so effects are not immediate and persist long after the steroid is cleared from plasma. Some rapid actions, like inhibition of ACTH release, may involve a cell membrane receptor or a different non-protein synthesis mechanism.
	
	### **2. Therapeutic Uses**
	
	Corticosteroids have a wide range of therapeutic uses due to their potent anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive properties, but their use requires careful consideration.
	
	- **Replacement Therapy**:
	    - **Chronic Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease)**: Hydrocortisone, often supplemented with fludrocortisone for salt wasting.
	    - **Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia**: Hydrocortisone, with fludrocortisone if salt wasting persists, to suppress pituitary ACTH and reduce androgen production.
	- **Anti-inflammatory and Immunosuppressive Uses (Non-endocrine diseases)**: These uses are empirical and palliative, potentially life-saving, but general principles for use must be observed (e.g., no abrupt withdrawal after >2-3 weeks, increase dose during stress, use local therapy where possible).
	    - **Arthritides**:
	        - **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Used in severe cases as adjuncts to NSAIDs, to suppress exacerbations, or for systemic manifestations. Low doses are often difficult to withdraw, leading to steroid dependence.
	        - **Osteoarthritis**: Intraarticular injection for acute exacerbations.
	        - **Rheumatic Fever**: Used in severe cases with carditis and congestive heart failure (CHF) for rapid symptom suppression.
	        - **Acute Gouty Arthritis**: Used when NSAIDs or colchicine are not tolerated, or for limited joint involvement (intraarticular injection preferred).
	    - **Collagen Diseases**: Most cases of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), polyarteritis nodosa, dermatomyositis, nephrotic syndrome, and glomerulonephritis may require corticosteroid therapy, often life-saving.
	    - **Severe Allergic Reactions**: Used for short periods in anaphylaxis, angioneurotic oedema, urticaria, and serum sickness (but **not a substitute for adrenaline** in anaphylactic shock). Topical use in allergic conjunctivitis and rhinitis.
	    - **Respiratory Diseases**:
	        - Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) for persistent asthma, to suppress inflammation and reduce exacerbations.
	        - Systemic steroids for **status asthmaticus/acute asthma exacerbations** (intravenous) and severe chronic asthma (oral).
	        - Also used for aspiration pneumonia and pulmonary oedema from drowning.
	        - Administered during late pregnancy to accelerate foetal lung maturation and prevent respiratory distress syndrome.
	    - **Infections (under effective chemotherapeutic cover)**: Severe tuberculosis (miliary, meningeal, renal), severe lepra reactions, bacterial meningitis, and _Pneumocystis carinii_ pneumonia with hypoxia in AIDS patients.
	    - **Ocular Diseases**: Inflammatory conditions of the anterior (e.g., allergic conjunctivitis, iritis, keratitis) and posterior segments (retinitis, optic neuritis, uveitis) of the eye. **Contraindicated in herpes simplex keratitis and ocular injuries**.
	    - **Skin Diseases**: Widely used topically for eczematous conditions. Systemic therapy for severe conditions like pemphigus vulgaris, exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
	    - **Intestinal Diseases**: Acute phases of ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, and coeliac disease (oral or retention enema). May be added to other treatments or used for systemic manifestations.
	    - **Neurological Conditions**: Cerebral oedema (due to tumours, tuberculous meningitis). In neurocysticercosis, they suppress the reaction to dying larvae during anthelmintic treatment. Short course can hasten recovery from Bell's palsy and acute exacerbation of multiple sclerosis.
	    - **Nausea and Vomiting**: Dexamethasone is frequently used to augment the antiemetic effect of other drugs in chemotherapy-induced or general anaesthesia-associated nausea and vomiting.
	    - **Malignancies**: Essential in chemotherapy for acute lymphatic leukaemia, Hodgkin's, and other lymphomas due to their lympholytic action. Can offer symptomatic relief in advanced malignancies by improving appetite and controlling secondary hypercalcaemia (though bisphosphonates are more effective for hypercalcaemia).
	    - **Organ Transplantation**: Used with other immunosuppressants to prevent rejection.
	    - **Septic Shock**: Low-dose hydrocortisone may be used for relative adrenal insufficiency unresponsive to fluids and vasopressors; high-dose therapy is abandoned.
	    - **Thyroid Storm**: Hydrocortisone helps reduce peripheral T4 to T3 conversion and addresses concomitant adrenal insufficiency.
	    - **Diagnostic Use**: Dexamethasone suppression test to assess pituitary-adrenal axis function.
	
	### **3. Adverse Effects of Prolonged Therapy**
	
	These effects are dose and duration-dependent extensions of their pharmacological actions and represent significant limitations to long-term use.
	
	- **Mineralocorticoid Effects**: Sodium and water retention, oedema, hypokalaemic alkalosis, and a progressive rise in blood pressure. These are now less common with highly selective glucocorticoids.
	- **Glucocorticoid Effects**:
	    - **Cushingoid Features**: Characteristic appearance with rounded face, truncal obesity, thin limbs, and supraclavicular hump.
	    - **Skin Changes**: Fragile skin, purple striae, easy bruising, telangiectasia, hirsutism, and acne.
	    - **Metabolic Derangements**: **Hyperglycaemia, glycosuria, and precipitation or worsening of diabetes mellitus**. Muscle weakness and myopathy (primarily proximal muscles).
	    - **Increased Susceptibility to Infection**: Non-specific for all pathogens; can cause flare-ups of latent tuberculosis and opportunistic infections (e.g., _Candida_).
	    - **Impaired Healing**: Delayed healing of wounds and surgical incisions.
	    - **Gastrointestinal**: **Peptic ulceration (risk doubled, with potential for bleeding and silent perforation)**. Dyspeptic symptoms are frequent with high dose therapy.
	    - **Musculoskeletal**: **Osteoporosis (especially vertebral and spongy bones)**, leading to compression fractures. Avascular necrosis of the head of femur, humerus, or knee joint may occur. **Growth retardation in children**.
	    - **Ocular**: Posterior subcapsular cataract (especially in children) and glaucoma (in susceptible individuals after prolonged topical therapy).
	    - **Pregnancy-related Risks**: Increased risk of gestational diabetes, pregnancy-induced hypertension, and preeclampsia.
	    - **Psychiatric Disturbances**: Mild euphoria is common, but can progress to manic psychosis, nervousness, insomnia, mood changes, or depression.
	    - **Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis Suppression**: Occurs depending on dose and duration, leading to adrenal cortex atrophy. Abrupt withdrawal can precipitate a severe withdrawal syndrome or acute adrenal insufficiency under stress. Measures to minimise HPA axis suppression include using shorter-acting steroids at the lowest possible dose for the shortest period, giving the entire daily dose in the morning, switching to alternate-day therapy, and using local preparations with poor systemic availability.
	
	### **4. Contraindications**
	
	Given their broad effects, corticosteroids have several important contraindications. These are generally considered **relative contraindications**, meaning the drugs may be used under compelling circumstances with due precautions.
	
	- **Peptic ulcer**.
	- **Diabetes mellitus**.
	- **Hypertension**.
	- **Viral and fungal infections** (including Herpes simplex keratitis), unless under effective anti-infective cover.
	- **Tuberculosis and other infections**, unless specific chemotherapeutic cover is provided.
	- **Osteoporosis**.
	- **Psychosis**.
	- **Epilepsy** (due to lowering of seizure threshold).
	- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)**.
	- **Renal failure**.
	- **Combined formulation of corticosteroids with any other drug meant for internal use is banned in India**.
