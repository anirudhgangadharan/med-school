	### Adrenaline: Drug of Choice in Anaphylactic Shock
	
	**Adrenaline (Epinephrine)** is the **drug of choice** and the **only life-saving measure** for **anaphylactic shock**.
	
	**Reasoning for its Efficacy:**
	
	Anaphylactic shock is a severe, rapidly developing Type-I hypersensitivity reaction. It involves the **massive release of inflammatory mediators** (e.g., histamine, leukotrienes). These mediators cause widespread vasodilation, increased capillary permeability (leading to angioedema and fluid extravasation), and severe bronchospasm. Adrenaline effectively counteracts these life-threatening effects through its comprehensive pharmacological actions:
	
	1. **Bronchodilation:** Adrenaline, as an adrenergic drug, stimulates **β2 receptors** in the bronchial smooth muscle. This leads to **increased cAMP formation** and **rapid bronchodilation**, alleviating severe bronchospasm and wheezing.
	2. **Cardiac Stimulation:** Adrenaline acts on adrenoceptors in the heart, resulting in **increased cardiac stimulation**. This helps to **maintain cardiac output** and counteracts the syncope and cardiovascular collapse associated with severe shock.
	3. **Vasoconstriction & Blood Pressure Support:** Adrenaline exerts a **vasoconstrictor effect** (primarily via α1 receptors, acting as a physiological antagonist to vasodilators like histamine). This action **increases peripheral vascular resistance** and **raises blood pressure**, counteracting the profound hypotension and vascular collapse seen in anaphylaxis.
	4. **Reduced Vascular Permeability:** By acting on α1 receptors in the vasculature, adrenaline **reduces capillary permeability**, thereby **alleviating angioedema** and preventing further fluid extravasation from the circulation. This helps to restore effective circulating blood volume.
	5. **Suppression of Mediator Release:** While its primary actions are direct physiological antagonism, adrenaline also stabilises mast cells and basophils (via β2 receptors), **reducing further release of histamine and other inflammatory mediators** that perpetuate the anaphylactic reaction.
	
	**Clinical Relevance & Administration:**
	
	- **Route of Choice:** Adrenaline should be administered **intramuscularly (IM)**, typically 0.5 mg for adults (0.5 ml of 1 in 1000 solution).
	- **Repeat Dosing:** Doses can be **repeated every 5-10 minutes** if the patient does not improve or if improvement is transient.
	- **Intravenous (IV) Use:** IV administration of adrenaline is generally **avoided unless shock is immediately life-threatening**, due to the risk of severe cardiac adverse effects. If IV is necessary, it must be **highly diluted (1:10,000 or 1:100,000)** and infused slowly with continuous monitoring.
	- **Adjuvant Therapy:** While adrenaline is life-saving, H1 antihistaminics and intravenous glucocorticoids (e.g., hydrocortisone) may be given as **adjuvant therapy** to help manage symptoms and prevent prolonged reactions, but they are **not substitutes** for adrenaline.
