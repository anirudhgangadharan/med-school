### BURNS: DEFINITION & CLASSIFICATION

**I. DEFINITION**

- **Concept:** A burn is an injury caused by the application of heat (by conduction or radiation, such as a flame or highly heated solid objects) or chemical substances to the external or internal surfaces of the body, resulting in the destruction of tissues.
- **Thermal Threshold:** The minimum temperature required to produce a burn is approximately 44°C for an exposure of 5 to 6 hours, whereas a temperature of 65°C can cause full-thickness destruction within seconds.

**II. CLASSIFICATION (🥇 High-Yield)** Historically categorized into six degrees by Dupuytren, burns are clinically merged into three main degrees by Wilson:

- **1. Epidermal (First Degree) Burns:**
    - Involves only the superficial epidermis.
    - Presents with erythema (redness), capillary dilatation, transudation of fluid causing swelling, and blister (vesicle/bulla) formation.
    - These are very painful but heal completely without leaving a permanent scar.
- **2. Dermo-epidermal (Second Degree) Burns:**
    - Involves the whole thickness of the skin, destroying dermal appendages like hair follicles, sweat glands, and sebaceous glands.
    - _Clinical Pearl:_ These are extremely painful because they directly affect sensory nerve endings, and they heal with permanent scar formation.
- **3. Deep (Third Degree) Burns:**
    - Involves gross destruction of the skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscles, and even bone, often presenting with complete charring.
    - 🚨 _Exam Pitfall:_ Unlike more superficial burns, deep burns are relatively **painless** because the nerve endings are completely destroyed.

### DIFFERENTIATING ANTEMORTEM VS. POSTMORTEM BURNS (🚨 Exam Target)

Differentiating whether a person was alive or dead when the fire started is a critical medicolegal question.

|Feature|Antemortem (AM) Burns|Postmortem (PM) Burns|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Line of Redness / Erythema**|**Present.** A vital reaction (zone of hyperaemia) is seen on the intact skin adjacent to the burn.|**Absent.** Skin shows dried scorching resembling burnt paper.|
|**Blister Formation**|Raised dome containing **serous fluid (rich in proteins/albumin and chlorides)**. The base is red, inflamed, and has swollen papillae.|Pale-yellow blisters containing air and a thin, clear fluid. The base is **dry, hard, and yellow**.|
|**Vital Reaction (Histology)**|Marked cellular exudation, polymorphonuclear leucocyte infiltration, and reactive tissue changes.|No intravital reaction, no cellular exudation.|
|**Enzyme Activity**|The peripheral zone of the burn shows an **increase in enzyme reactions** (e.g., esterases, ATP, acid/alkaline phosphatase).|Peripheral zone does not show any increase in enzyme activity.|
|**Soot in Airway**|**Present.** The presence of carbon/soot particles in the terminal bronchioles is an absolute proof of life during the fire.|**Absent.** Soot is not actively inhaled into the deep respiratory tract.|
|**Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb)**|**Elevated.** COHb levels >10% (often up to 70-80%) indicate the victim was actively breathing CO during the fire.|**Absent.** CO is not absorbed by a body after death.|

### CAUSES OF DEATH DUE TO BURNS

Fatalities from burns are strictly classified based on the time elapsed since the thermal injury occurred:

**I. IMMEDIATE & EARLY CAUSES (0 - 48 Hours)**

- **Primary (Neurogenic) Shock:** Occurs rapidly due to intense pain and fright.
- **Asphyxia / Suffocation:** Results from the inhalation of smoke, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, cyanide, and other irrespirable gases produced by burning materials. Acute oedema of the glottis/larynx from inhaling hot fumes can also rapidly occlude the airway.
- **Secondary (Hypovolaemic) Shock:** This is the cause of more than half of the deaths occurring within the first 48 hours. It results from massive fluid loss (plasma exudation) from the burned surface.
- **Accidental Injuries:** Death may occur from injuries sustained while attempting to escape, or from falling masonry/timber.

**II. DELAYED CAUSES (Days to Weeks)**

- **Toxaemia:** Occurs within 3 to 4 days due to the absorption of toxic metabolites from the necrotic burnt tissues.
- **Sepsis / Infection:** This is the **most important factor** in deaths occurring 4 to 5 days or longer after the burn. Prolonged exposure of the raw skin leads to septicaemia, meningitis, and bronchopneumonia.
- **Acute Renal Failure:** Usually occurs around the third or fourth day due to lower nephron nephrosis, driven by hypoperfusion and haemoglobinuria/myoglobinuria.
- **Gastrointestinal Complications:** Severe stress can induce acute peptic ulceration (**Curling's ulcers**, specifically in the duodenum), acute dilation of the stomach, or fatal GI haemorrhage.
- **Respiratory Complications:** Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) or secondary bronchopneumonia.
- **Pulmonary Embolism:** Resulting from deep vein thrombosis due to prolonged immobilization.
- **Malignancy (Remote Cause):** Death may occur years later due to malignant transformation of a chronic, non-healing burn scar (Squamous cell carcinoma, known as **Marjolin's ulcer**).

### EXTERNAL POSTMORTEM FINDINGS IN BURNS

**1. General Appearance & Posture**

- **Pugilistic Attitude (Boxer/Fencing Posture):** A characteristic posture where the hips, knees, elbows, and wrists are flexed, with fingers hooked like claws, resembling a boxer. 🚨 _Exam Pearl:_ This is caused by the heat-induced coagulation and contraction of muscle proteins; it is **not** an antemortem sign of life and occurs whether the person was alive or dead at the time of burning.
- **Facial Features:** The face is swollen and distorted, and the tongue often protrudes due to tissue contraction. Blood-tinged froth may be present at the mouth and nose due to heat irritation causing pulmonary oedema.

**2. Skin & Soft Tissues**

- **Heat Ruptures:** Severe heat causes the skin and soft tissues to contract and split, producing linear tears that simulate incised or lacerated wounds, commonly over fleshy areas like calves and thighs. 🥇 _Distinguishing feature:_ Unlike traumatic wounds, heat ruptures show no bleeding, intact blood vessels and nerves at the base, and irregular margins without vital reaction.
- **Blisters (Vesication):**
    - _Antemortem Blisters:_ Raised domes with a red, hyperaemic, inflamed base, containing serous fluid rich in albumin, chlorides, and cells.
    - _Postmortem Blisters:_ Pale-yellow with a dry, hard, yellow base, containing mostly air and thin clear fluid with trace albumin.
- **Line of Redness:** In antemortem burns, a 5-20 mm zone of hyperaemia (erythema) surrounds the necrotic area.

**3. Hair & Clothing**

- **Hair:** Singed, curled, twisted, completely burnt, or showing a clubbed/bulbous appearance at the tips due to the melting and cooling of keratin.
- **Clothing:** Synthetic fabrics (nylon/polyester) melt and stick to the skin. Traces and characteristic odours of accelerants (kerosene, petrol) may be present.

### INTERNAL POSTMORTEM FINDINGS IN BURNS

Despite severe external charring, internal organs are frequently well-preserved because body tissues are poor conductors of heat.

**1. Cranial Cavity & Brain**

- **Heat Haematoma:** A soft, friable, chocolate-coloured (or pink if CO is present) blood clot found in the extradural space. 🚨 _High-Yield:_ It has a characteristic **"honeycomb" appearance** due to steam bubbles boiling from the bone, distinguishing it from traumatic epidural haemorrhage.
- **Thermal Skull Fractures:** Intense heat causes the outer table of the skull to crack in a radiating or stellate pattern that crosses suture lines.
- **Brain:** The brain is often shrunken, firm, and yellow to light-brown due to a "cooking" effect.

**2. Respiratory System (The Gold Standard for Antemortem Proof)**

- **Soot Particles:** The presence of black carbon (soot) particles in the larynx, trachea, and extending down into the terminal bronchioles is **absolute proof** that the victim was breathing during the fire.
- **Airways:** The mucosa is thickened, blanched, or reddened with severe acute oedema of the glottis.
- **Lungs:** Congested, heavy, consolidated, and markedly oedematous.

**3. Cardiovascular System & Blood**

- **Cherry-Red Blood:** The blood, tissues, and internal organs show a characteristic cherry-red discolouration if the Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb) level exceeds 10%.

**4. Gastrointestinal Tract & Kidneys**

- **Curling's Ulcers:** Acute stress ulcers (sharply punched-out defects) developing in the duodenum or stomach, typically seen in extensive burns.
- **Kidneys:** Show cloudy swelling, tubular necrosis, capillary thrombosis, and the presence of heme casts in the medullary tubules.

### CAUSES OF DEATH IN BURNS

The causes of death are strictly chronologically classified into immediate, delayed, and remote causes:

**I. Immediate & Early Causes (0 - 48 Hours)**

1. **Neurogenic Shock (Primary Shock):** Immediate death resulting from excruciating pain and extreme fright.
2. **Suffocation / Asphyxia:** Caused by the inhalation of smoke, carbon monoxide (CO), carbon dioxide (CO2), cyanide, and other toxic combustion products.
3. **Hypovolaemic Shock (Secondary Shock):** The most common cause of death within the first 48 hours, resulting from massive transudation and loss of fluid/plasma from the burnt surfaces.
4. **Laryngeal Oedema:** Acute asphyxiation from swelling of the glottis due to the inhalation of superheated air or fumes.
5. **Accidental Traumatic Injuries:** Sustained while attempting to escape, e.g., falling masonry.

**II. Delayed Causes (After 48 hours to Weeks)**

1. **Septicaemia / Sepsis:** The most common cause of death occurring 4 to 5 days or longer after the burn, due to widespread bacterial infection of the raw surfaces.
2. **Toxaemia:** Caused by the systemic absorption of toxic metabolites from necrotic, burnt tissue (peaks at 3-4 days).
3. **Acute Renal Failure:** Due to lower nephron nephrosis (acute tubular necrosis) secondary to prolonged hypoperfusion and shock.
4. **Respiratory Complications:** Development of Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), bronchopneumonia, or pulmonary embolism resulting from prolonged immobilisation and venous thrombosis.
5. **Gastrointestinal Complications:** Fatal haemorrhage or perforation secondary to Curling's ulcers.

**III. Remote Causes (Years Later)**

1. **Marjolin's Ulcer:** Death may occur years later from the malignant transformation of an untreated or chronic non-healing burn scar into a squamous cell carcinoma.

### CLASSIFICATION OF BURNS

**I. HISTORICAL & PATHOLOGICAL CLASSIFICATION (Wilson's Modification)** Historically, Dupuytren classified burns into six degrees, which were later practically merged by Wilson into **three high-yield degrees** based on the depth of tissue destruction.

- **1. First Degree (Epidermal) Burns:**
    
    - **Depth:** Involves only the epidermis.
    - **Clinical Features:** Erythematous (red) with capillary dilatation and swelling due to fluid transudation.
    - **Blistering:** A split may occur at the epidermal-dermal junction to form a blister (vesicle or bulla) containing protein-rich fluid and gas.
    - **Sensory:** **Very painful** due to irritated nerve endings.
    - **Healing:** Complete repair occurs **without scar formation**.
- **2. Second Degree (Dermo-Epidermal) Burns:**
    
    - **Depth:** Destroys the whole thickness of the skin (epidermis and dermis), including dermal appendages (hair follicles, sweat, and sebaceous glands).
    - **Clinical Features:** Shrivelled, depressed areas of coagulated necrotic tissue (eschar) bordered by reddish blistered skin.
    - **Sensory:** Extremely painful; pain and shock are greater than in first-degree burns.
    - **Healing:** Necrotic tissue separates in about a week. It heals with **scar formation** which may lead to severe contractures, disfigurement, and impaired function.
- **3. Third Degree (Deep) Burns:**
    
    - **Depth:** Gross destruction extending beyond the skin into the subcutaneous tissue, underlying muscle, and even bone.
    - **Clinical Features:** The affected part is completely **charred** (brown or black).
    - **Sensory:** 🚨 **High-Yield Exam Pearl:** These burns are **relatively painless** because the sensory nerve endings are completely destroyed.

**II. MODERN CLINICAL CLASSIFICATION** In surgical and clinical practice, burns are broadly classified into two categories to guide management:

- **Superficial Burns:** Involves less than the full thickness of the skin.
- **Deep Burns:** Involves the full thickness of the skin and deeper tissues.

**III. 🥇 CLINICAL RELEVANCE & PROGNOSIS**

- **Severity Factor:** The prognosis and fatality of a burn depend much more on the **extent of the body surface area involved** rather than the depth (degree) of the burn.
- **Fatality Rule:** Involvement of **50% or more** of the body surface area is almost invariably fatal, even if the burns are only first-degree. 30% involvement is sufficient to cause death.

### CAUSES OF DEATH IN BURNS

For maximum exam marks, the causes of death in burn injuries must be classified chronologically into immediate/early, delayed, and remote causes.

**I. IMMEDIATE OR EARLY CAUSES (Within 0 to 48 Hours)**

- **Primary (Neurogenic) Shock:** This occurs within the first few hours and is caused by excruciating pain and neurogenic reflexes from the extensive tissue trauma.
- **Asphyxia and Smoke Inhalation (🚨 Danger Zone):** Inhalation of toxic fumes in a burning environment is highly lethal. Death can result from oxygen deprivation and the inhalation of carbon monoxide (CO), carbon dioxide, cyanide, and non-specific toxic free radicals. The free radicals in smoke can inactivate pulmonary surfactants, preventing oxygen from crossing the alveoli. Furthermore, inhalation of superheated air leads to acute oedema of the glottis and massive pulmonary oedema, causing mechanical airway obstruction.
- **Secondary (Hypovolaemic) Shock (🥇 High-Yield):** This is the leading cause of death within the first 48 hours, accounting for more than half of early burn fatalities. It results from a massive transudation of fluid and plasma from the burned surfaces into the tissues. Circulatory collapse can occur with burns involving as little as 15% of the total body surface area.
- **Accidental Trauma:** Victims may die from secondary mechanical injuries sustained while attempting to escape, such as jumping from windows or being crushed by falling masonry.

**II. DELAYED CAUSES (3 to 5 Days and Beyond)**

- **Sepsis and Septicaemia:** This is the most important and common cause of death occurring four to five days or longer after the burn incident. Prolonged exposure of the denuded skin surface allows microorganisms to invade, leading to fatal systemic infections such as bronchopneumonia, meningitis, and pericarditis. Secondary infections like pyaemia, gangrene, and tetanus may also supervene.
- **Acute Renal Failure:** Due to severe hypoperfusion and shock, the patient may develop acute tubular necrosis (lower nephron nephrosis). This typically causes death around the third or fourth day.
- **Toxaemia:** The systemic absorption of toxic metabolites and breakdown products from the necrotic, burnt tissues produces toxaemia, which typically peaks and persists for 3 to 4 days.
- **Gastrointestinal Complications:** Severe stress can induce acute peptic ulceration, classically known as **Curling's ulcers** (which commonly affect the duodenum), as well as acute dilation of the stomach and fatal gastrointestinal haemorrhage.
- **Respiratory Complications:** The patient may develop Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS). Additionally, the presence of a tight, leathery eschar over the thorax can physically inhibit respiratory excursions, leading to respiratory distress.
- **Biochemical Disturbances:** Massive cellular destruction and fluid shifts cause lethal electrolyte imbalances, particularly hypokalaemia.
- **Pulmonary Embolism:** Prolonged immobilisation and severe tissue damage predispose the patient to deep vein thrombosis in the legs, which can shower fatal pulmonary emboli.
- **Fat Embolism:** Though rare, this can occur secondary to the destruction of subcutaneous adipose tissue.
- **Multisystem Organ Failure:** A frequent terminal event resulting from the combination of shock, sepsis, and toxaemia.

**III. REMOTE OR LATE CAUSES (Years Later)**

- **Marjolin's Ulcer (🥇 Clinical Pearl):** Death can occur many years after the initial recovery due to the malignant transformation of a chronic, non-healing burn scar into a squamous cell carcinoma.
1. Filigree burns.
### FILIGREE BURNS (LICHTENBERG'S FLOWERS)

**I. DEFINITION & SYNONYMS**

- **Synonyms:** Also known as **Arborescent burns** or **Lichtenberg's figures/flowers**.
- **Appearance:** These are superficial, thin, irregular, and tortuous erythematous markings on the skin that characteristically resemble the branching pattern of a tree or a fern leaf.

**II. CLINICAL CONTEXT & TIMING (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Etiology:** They are the classic, pathognomonic external sign of a **lightning strike**.
- **Location:** Usually found over the shoulders or the flanks.
- **Timing:** They appear very rapidly, within a few minutes to one hour of the lightning strike.
- _Clinical Pearl:_ These marks are transient; if the victim survives the lightning strike, the filigree burns typically disappear completely within **1 to 2 days**.

**III. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY / MECHANISM** The exact mechanism is not definitively known, but several accepted theories exist:

1. **Electron Showers:** Caused directly by electron showers induced by the lightning discharging over the skin surface.
2. **Haemolysis & Staining:** Slight staining of the tissues by haemoglobin released from lysed red blood cells along the path of the electric current.
3. **Micro-trauma:** Rupture of smaller blood vessels at several places, giving rise to patterned ecchymoses.
4. **Thermal Effect:** Boiling of the intercellular fluid following the fascial planes due to extreme heat generation.

**IV. 🚨 EXAM TRAP: DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS** Filigree burns are frequently confused with **Marbling**, which is an early postmortem change. Examiners love to test the difference between the two:

- **Filigree Burns (Lightning):** Appear **brownish-black** or erythematous, occur immediately after a lightning strike, and represent a thermal/electrical patterned skin injury.
- **Marbling (Putrefaction):** Appears **bluish-green** or purplish-red. It is a decomposition change occurring **36 to 48 hours** after death, caused by bacteria producing hydrogen sulphide which reacts with blood to form sulfhaemoglobin, staining the superficial venous network.
1. Immersion foot.
### IMMERSION FOOT (AND TRENCH FOOT)

**I. DEFINITION & CLASSIFICATION**

- **Category:** It is classified as a localised thermal injury resulting from the physical effects of cold.
- **Concept:** Immersion foot (which is pathophysiologically grouped with trench foot) is a cold-induced injury specifically affecting the extremities.

**II. ETIOLOGY & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Temperature Profile:** Unlike frostbite (which requires freezing temperatures), immersion foot is caused by prolonged exposure to **non-freezing cold**, specifically temperatures ranging between **5° to 8°C**.
- **Environmental Factor:** It necessitates prolonged exposure to dampness, moisture, or direct immersion in water.

**III. CLINICAL FEATURES & MORPHOLOGY (🚨 Exam Target)**

- **Site:** Primarily involves the exposed extremities, notably the feet.
- **Lesion Progression:** The condition presents with blister formation and subsequent ulceration of the affected skin.
- **Complication:** The prolonged ischemia and tissue damage classically progress to the formation of localised **dry gangrene**.

**IV. CLASSIC CLINICAL SCENARIOS**

- **Immersion Foot:** Typically seen in shipwreck survivors or persons subjected to prolonged immersion or exposure to cold sea water.
- **Trench Foot:** A virtually identical condition classically seen in soldiers participating in winter warfare, specifically from standing in cold, damp trenches for many hours.
1. Heat stroke.
### HEAT STROKE (SUN STROKE)

**I. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Definition:** A life-threatening thermal emergency characterized by a core body temperature exceeding **41°C (106°F)** accompanied by profound neurological disturbances (delirium, stupor, coma, or convulsions).
- **Mechanism:** Complete breakdown of the hypothalamic heat-regulating center due to the failure of cutaneous blood flow and sweating mechanisms.

**II. CLASSIFICATION**

- **1. Classic Heat Stroke:** Typically seen in the elderly during heat waves. Characterized by a **complete absence of sweating**.
- **2. Exertional Heat Stroke:** Occurs in athletes or military personnel working hard in hot environments. _Clinical Pearl:_ Unlike the classic form, persistent sweating is still observed in about 50% of these patients due to increased catecholamine production.

**III. PREDISPOSING FACTORS**

- **Environmental:** High temperature combined with increased humidity (e.g., 100% humidity at 32°C can trigger heat stroke), lack of air movement.
- **Host Factors:** Lack of acclimatization, extreme age (elderly), obesity, alcoholism, and pre-existing cardiovascular disease.
- **Pharmacological:** Use of major tranquilizers (which impair thermoregulation).

**IV. CLINICAL FEATURES (🚨 Exam Target)**

- **Prodrome:** Headache, dizziness, nausea, staggering gait, muscle cramps, and extreme thirst.
- **The Classic Triad (Once established):**
    1. **Hyperpyrexia:** Rectal temperature of 40°C to 43°C or higher.
    2. **Skin Changes:** Flushed, dry, and hot skin with the absence of sweating.
    3. **CNS Dysfunction:** Psychosis, delirium, convulsions, passing rapidly into coma.
- **Systemic Signs:** Pupils are initially contracted, pulse is rapid (>130 bpm) and irregular, breathing is rapid and deep (Kussmaul type).
- **Terminal Events:** When temperature exceeds 42°C, massive vasodilation causes a sharp decrease in effective blood volume, leading to severe hypotension, circulatory collapse, and cardiac failure.

**V. CAUSE OF DEATH**

- Death usually occurs within 5 minutes to 3 days.
- Primary mechanisms include central paralysis of the medullary heat-regulating center, profound circulatory collapse (shock), or **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**.

**VI. POSTMORTEM APPEARANCES (🥇 Medicolegal Pearls)**

- **Postmortem Caloricity:** The body temperature uniquely **remains high after death**.
- **Rigor Mortis & Lividity:** Rigor mortis sets in very early and disappears early. Postmortem lividity is unusually marked.
- **CNS:** The brain is heavy, congested, and markedly oedematous, with flattening of the convolutions and petechial haemorrhages in the white matter.
- **Cardiovascular & Respiratory:** Right auricle dilation, flabby myocardium, and confluent subepicardial/subendocardial haemorrhages. Lungs show severe oedema, congestion, and frothy haemorrhagic fluid in the trachea.
- **Hepatorenal Damage:** Centrilobular necrosis of the liver; the kidneys show severe congestion, acute tubular necrosis, and (if the victim survives longer) haemoglobinuric nephrosis.
- **Systemic:** Widespread petechial haemorrhages across most organs, consistent with DIC.
1. Rule of 9 (nine).
### WALLACE'S "RULE OF NINES"

**I. DEFINITION & PURPOSE (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Concept:** A standardized, rapid method used to estimate the percentage of **Total Body Surface Area (TBSA)** involved in burn injuries.
- **Clinical Utility:** It is essential for assessing the severity/prognosis of the patient and is critical for calculating intravenous fluid replacement requirements.
- **Methodology:** The adult body surface is divided into 11 anatomical regions, each representing **9%** (or a multiple of 9%) of the total body surface area, with the final 1% assigned to the perineum/genitalia.

**II. ANATOMICAL BREAKDOWN FOR ADULTS (🚨 Exam Target)**

- **Head and Face/Neck:** 9%
- **Right Upper Limb:** 9%
- **Left Upper Limb:** 9%
- **Front of Trunk:** 18% _(Front of Chest 9% + Front of Abdomen 9%)_
- **Back of Trunk:** 18% _(Back of Chest 9% + Back of Abdomen 9%)_
- **Right Lower Limb:** 18% _(Front 9% + Back 9%)_
- **Left Lower Limb:** 18% _(Front 9% + Back 9%)_
- **External Genitalia / Perineum:** 1%
- **TOTAL:** 100%

**III. EXCEPTIONS & ALTERNATIVE METHODS**

- **1. Rule of Palm (For Scattered/Patchy Burns):** When burn distribution is irregular or scattered, the surface area of the **patient's own palm** is used as a reference. The palmar surface of the patient's hand is equivalent to approximately **1%** of their total body surface area.
- **2. Pediatric Patients:** The standard "Rule of Nines" is inaccurate for infants and children because their body proportions differ significantly (they have a relatively larger head and smaller lower limbs). Special age-adjusted charts must be used for pediatric burn estimations.

**IV. MEDICOLEGAL & PROGNOSTIC SIGNIFICANCE**

- The overall prognosis of a burn victim depends much more on the _extent_ of the body surface area involved than on the _degree_ (depth) of the burns.
- 🚨 **Lethality Threshold:**
    - Involvement of **50% or more** of the total body surface area is almost invariably fatal, even if the burns are only superficial (first-degree).
    - Involvement of **30%** of the body surface area is generally sufficient to cause death, often due to complications like secondary shock or sepsis.
1. Pugilistic attitude.
### PUGILISTIC ATTITUDE (Boxer's or Fencing Posture)

**I. DEFINITION & APPEARANCE**

- **Concept:** A characteristic posture assumed by a body that has been exposed to prolonged and intense heat or fire.
- **Classic Presentation (Universal Flexion):** The body simulates the stance of a boxer defending themselves.
    - **Limbs:** The arms are flexed at the elbows and wrists and held out in front of the body; the legs are flexed at the hips and knees.
    - **Hands:** All fingers are hooked like claws.
    - **Head & Spine:** The head is slightly extended, and contraction of the paraspinal muscles frequently causes a marked backward arching of the spine (opisthotonus).

**II. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (Mechanism of Action)**

- **Protein Coagulation:** Exposure to extreme heat causes the tissues to desiccate (dehydrate) and carbonize on the surface, while the muscle proteins undergo denaturation and coagulation (similar to cooking meat).
- **Differential Muscle Bulk:** This coagulation forces the muscle fibers to severely contract and shorten. Because the flexor muscles of the body are much bulkier and stronger than the extensor muscles, they contract with greater force, resulting in the universal flexion of all large joints.
- **Severity:** The degree and severity of these contractures directly depend on the intensity of the heat and the duration of exposure.

**III. 🚨 MEDICOLEGAL PEARLS (High-Yield for Exams)**

- **Not a Vital Sign:** The pugilistic attitude is strictly a physical effect of heat and is **NOT** a sign of antemortem burning (vital reaction).
- **Alive vs. Dead:** This stiffening will occur regardless of whether the person was **alive or already dead** at the time they were exposed to the fire.
- **Exam Trap:** Do not mistake this posture as evidence that the victim was actively fighting an assailant or struggling to defend themselves at the moment of death.
1. Scalds.
### SCALDS (MOIST HEAT INJURIES)

**I. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY**

- **Definition:** A thermal injury resulting from the application of **moist heat**—specifically, liquids heated above 60°C or superheated steam.
- **Pathogenesis:** The protection offered by the skin and the typically short duration of contact usually prevent inward heat conduction, limiting destruction primarily to the superficial layers. However, water at 70°C can cause full-thickness scalds in just one second.
- **Latent Heat Factor:** Sticky, viscous liquids (e.g., hot oils, tar, syrups) cause much deeper and more severe scalds than hot water due to their high heat retention capacity (latent heat) and prolonged adherence to the skin.
- **Clothing Effect:** Scalding frequently occurs through intact clothing. Clothing actually _worsens_ the tissue damage by trapping the hot liquid against the skin, prolonging contact, and reducing the natural cooling effect of evaporation.

**II. CLINICAL FEATURES & MORPHOLOGY (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Distribution:** The injury is limited to the area of contact and is most severe at the point of initial impact.
- **Trickle/Splash Marks:** Streaks of liquid running downwards from the main impact area cause characteristic lines of blisters or "splash" patterns.
- **Margins:** A scald usually presents with a sharply demarcated edge corresponding exactly to the limits of the fluid contact.
- **Blistering (Vesication):** Redness appears instantly, followed by blistering within a few minutes. Blisters contain fluid rich in white and red cells, are surrounded by a hyperaemic zone, and show reddened, swollen papillae in the floor.
- **Healing:** Scars formed from scalds are much thinner than those of deep dry burns, causing far less wound contraction and disfigurement.

**III. DEGREES OF SCALDS**

1. **First Degree (Erythema):** Caused by vasoparalysis.
2. **Second Degree (Blistering):** Caused by increased capillary permeability.
3. **Third Degree (Necrosis):** Characterized by necrosis of the dermis.

**IV. SCALDS vs. DRY HEAT BURNS (🚨 Exam Target)** Differentiating these two is a frequent and critical exam question.

- **Singeing of Hair:** Completely **absent** in scalds; Present in dry burns.
- **Charring/Blackening:** **Absent** in scalds; Present in dry burns.
- **Skin Appearance:** Sodden and bleached in scalds; Dry, wrinkled, or charred in dry burns. Superheated steam specifically soddens the skin, turning it a dirty-white colour.
- **Vesicles (Blisters):** Distributed abundantly _over_ the entire scalded area; Usually restricted to the _circumference/periphery_ in dry burns.
- **Red Line of Demarcation:** Present in both.

**V. CLINICAL COMPLICATIONS & MEDICOLEGAL ASPECTS**

- **Causes of Death:** Extensive scalds commonly lead to death via secondary shock, fluid and electrolyte disturbances, and secondary bacterial infections.
- **Respiratory Scalds:** Inhalation of superheated steam or children accidentally sucking the spout of a boiling kettle can cause severe thermal injury to the respiratory tract. This leads to acute laryngeal oedema, thick mucus plugs, airway obstruction, and death by asphyxia.
- **Medicolegal Circumstances:**
    - _Accidental:_ The vast majority of cases (e.g., bursting boilers, kitchen spills, pulling over saucepans, bursting hot water bottles).
    - _Homicide/Child Abuse:_ Deliberate scalding with hot fluids is a well-documented presentation of child abuse. Boiling water may also be thrown maliciously to injure an opponent.
    - _Suicide:_ Extremely rare due to the intense pain and uncertain fatality.
- **Inflammable Fluids:** If fluids like kerosene are splashed onto a recumbent body and ignited, trickling "runs" of burning liquid will be seen running downwards (e.g., sides of the neck, trunk, between thighs).
- **Differential Diagnosis:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) secondary to drugs or disease can cause blistering and a raw, weeping separation of the superficial skin layers that perfectly mimics a scald injury.
1. Joule burns.
### JOULE BURN (ELECTRIC MARK)

**I. DEFINITION & PRINCIPLE**

- **Concept:** A Joule burn (also known as an electric mark) is a specific, endogenous thermal burn found at the **point of entry** of an electric current into the body.
- **Mechanism:** It is produced by the heat generated within the body's tissues due to the resistance offered against the passage of electricity.
- **Diagnostic Value:** It is **pathognomonic (diagnostic)** of contact with electricity and is typically seen in accidents involving high-tension currents.

**II. GROSS APPEARANCE (🥇 High-Yield Morphology)**

- **Shape & Size:** Typically appears as round or oval, shallow craters measuring **1 to 3 cm in diameter**.
- **The Floor:** The base of the crater is pale, flattened, depressed, and noticeably **hard to the touch**.
- **The Margins:** Surrounded by an elevated ridge of skin (1 to 3 mm high) with a hyperaemic (reddened) border.
- **The Areola:** A highly characteristic feature is the presence of an **areola of blanched (pale) skin** at the periphery of the mark, which survives death.
- **Hair Changes:** If hair is present at the point of contact, it will appear burnt and singed.
- **Metallisation (Exam Pearl):** If the electrical conductor contains copper or brass, the electric mark may exhibit a distinct **bright green colour** due to the transfer of metallic ions.
- **Patterning:** The mark may occasionally reproduce the distinctive shape/pattern of the conductor (e.g., a linear wire or specific metal object).

**III. HISTOPATHOLOGY (🚨 Highly Tested Concept)** Microscopic examination reveals classic heat-induced architectural changes:

- **Microblisters:** Develop within the squamous epithelium and horny layer, representing defects through which steam exited the boiling tissue fluids.
- **"Streaming of the Nuclei":** Epidermal cells develop large vacuoles, and their nuclei become fusiform, hyperchromatic, and uniquely distorted (stretched and narrowed). They align in parallel rows at an acute or right angle to the dermis, producing a characteristic palisade-type appearance.

**IV. MEDICOLEGAL & CLINICAL RELEVANCE**

- **Confirmation of Electrocution:** The presence of a Joule burn absolutely confirms the passage of electricity, even if internal findings are non-specific.
- **Differentiating Entry vs. Exit Wounds:** While an electrical exit mark may look somewhat similar (as a split or tear in the skin), it is usually **less hard** and lacks the distinctive blackening, blistering, and hair singeing typically found at the entry (Joule burn) site.
- **Limitations:** A Joule burn may be **absent** if the area of contact was relatively large, if the voltage was low, or if the skin was well-moistened (e.g., wet hands, electrocution in a bathtub), as moisture significantly lowers skin resistance and prevents local heat buildup.
1. Filigree burns.
### FILIGREE BURNS (LICHTENBERG'S FLOWERS)

**I. DEFINITION & CLINICAL APPEARANCE**

- **Concept:** Filigree burns, also known as arborescent burns or Lichtenberg's flowers, are superficial, thin, irregular, and tortuous markings found on the skin.
- **Morphology:** They characteristically exhibit a fern-like or tree-branching pattern of erythema.
- **Location:** These marks are most frequently observed over the shoulders or the flanks.
- **Timing:** They appear very rapidly, usually within a few minutes to one hour following the accident.

**II. AETIOLOGY & MECHANISM (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Primary Cause:** They are pathognomonic of a **lightning strike**.
- **Pathophysiology:** The exact mechanism by which this pattern occurs remains unconfirmed, but several theories are proposed. One theory suggests the pattern is caused by electron showers induced directly by the lightning. Another theory proposes it results from the boiling of intercellular fluid following the fascial planes. Alternatively, they may result from the rupture of smaller blood vessels producing ecchymoses, or the slight staining of tissues by haemoglobin derived from lysed red blood cells along the path of the electric current.

**III. CLINICAL COURSE & PROGNOSIS**

- **Clinical Significance:** These superficial marks visually indicate the exact paths taken by the electrical discharge across the body.
- **Resolution:** If the victim survives the lightning strike, the filigree burns are highly transient and will completely disappear within **one to two days**.

**IV. 🚨 MEDICOLEGAL DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS (Exam Pearl)**

- **Diagnostic Challenge:** Filigree burns are frequently mistaken for the "marbling" appearance typically found on decomposed bodies.
- **Key Distinguishing Feature:** Filigree burns present with a **brownish-black** colour, whereas putrefactive marbling presents as a **bluish-green** discolouration.
- **Diagnostic Pitfall:** Differentiating between the two becomes exceptionally difficult if the body is recovered a few days after the incident when postmortem putrefaction has already set in.
