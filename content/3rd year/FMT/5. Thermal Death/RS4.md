### I. CLASSIFICATION OF THERMAL INJURIES

Thermal injuries are caused by the systemic and/or localized effects of excessive heat or cold. For medicolegal and clinical purposes, they are broadly classified into two main categories:

**A. BASED ON THE CAUSATIVE AGENT (HEAT VS. COLD)**

**1. Effects of Heat**

- **Generalized (Systemic) Effects:**
    - **Heat Cramps:** Painful spasm of voluntary muscles due to loss of water and salt.
    - **Heat Exhaustion / Prostration:** Extreme exhaustion and peripheral vascular collapse due to inadequate water/salt replacement; body temperature does not exceed 102°F.
    - **Heat Syncope (Collapse):** Hypotension and reduced blood supply to the brain due to peripheral pooling of blood.
    - **Heat Stroke (Sun Stroke):** Breakdown of the hypothalamic heat-regulating center. Characterized by absence of sweating, flushed hot skin, and body temperature $\ge$ 106°F.
- **Localized Effects:**
    - **Burns:** Caused by the application of dry heat (e.g., flame, heated solid body, radiant heat, microwave, electricity).
    - **Scalds:** Caused by the application of moist/wet heat (e.g., hot liquids above 60°C or steam).

**2. Effects of Cold**

- **Generalized (Systemic) Effect:**
    - **Hypothermia:** Oral/axillary temperature falls below 35°C, ultimately leading to failure of vital centers due to anoxia and circulatory collapse.
- **Localized Effects:**
    - **Frostbite:** Localized avascular necrosis beyond a line of inflammatory demarcation, occurring at freezing temperatures ($-2$° to $-8$°C). Affects exposed parts (ears, nose, digits).
    - **Trench Foot & Immersion Foot:** Blistering and dry gangrene caused by prolonged exposure to damp, non-freezing cold (5° to 8°C).

**B. CLINICAL CLASSIFICATION OF BURNS (BY DEPTH)**

- **1. Epidermal Burns (First Degree):** Involves only the epidermis. Presents with erythema and blistering. _Exam Pearl:_ Extremely painful but heals rapidly without leaving a permanent scar.
- **2. Dermoepidermal Burns (Second Degree):** Involves the whole thickness of the skin (epidermis + dermis) including hair follicles and sweat glands. _Exam Pearl:_ Extremely painful (sensory nerve endings affected) and heals with scar formation.
- **3. Deep Burns (Third Degree):** Destruction extends into subcutaneous soft tissues, muscles, and bones. _Exam Pearl:_ These burns are completely **painless** because the sensory nerve endings are completely destroyed.

### II. METHOD OF CALCULATION OF PERCENTAGE OF BURN INJURIES

To assess the prognosis and calculate fluid replacement, estimating the total body surface area (TBSA) involved is critical. The prognosis depends much more on the _extent_ of the body surface involved than on the _degree_ of the burns.

🥇 **HIGH-YIELD METHODS FOR EXAMS:**

**1. Wallace's "Rule of Nines" (For Adults)** The body surface is divided into 11 anatomical regions, each representing 9% (or a multiple of 9%) of the total body surface area, plus 1% for the perineum.

- **Head and Face/Neck:** 9%
- **Right Upper Limb:** 9%
- **Left Upper Limb:** 9%
- **Front of Trunk:** 18% _(Chest 9% + Abdomen 9%)_
- **Back of Trunk:** 18% _(Upper back 9% + Lower back 9%)_
- **Right Lower Limb:** 18% _(Front 9% + Back 9%)_
- **Left Lower Limb:** 18% _(Front 9% + Back 9%)_
- **External Genitalia / Perineum:** 1%
- **Total:** 100%

**2. Rule of Palm (For Scattered Burns)** When burns are irregularly distributed or patchy, the surface area of the **patient's own palm** is used as a reference guide. The palmar surface of the patient's hand is equivalent to approximately **1%** of their total body surface area.

**3. Lund and Browder Chart (For Children)** The Rule of Nines is inaccurate for infants and small children because their body proportions differ significantly from adults (they have a relatively larger head and smaller lower limbs).

- _Adjustment:_ The Lund and Browder chart uses age-specific percentages to accurately calculate the burned surface area in pediatric patients.

### 🚨 CLINICAL & MEDICOLEGAL PEARLS

- **Lethality Threshold:** Involvement of **50%** of the body surface area is almost invariably fatal, and even **30%** involvement is sufficient to cause death, particularly if complicated by secondary shock or sepsis.
- **Pugilistic Attitude:** A severely burned body often assumes a "boxer's" posture (flexion of all joints). This is a _postmortem_ artifact caused by heat coagulation and contraction of muscle proteins, and does not indicate that the person died fighting.
- **Vital Sign of Antemortem Burns:** The presence of a red line of erythema (vital reaction) adjacent to the burn, and the finding of **soot particles** in the respiratory tract or elevated **carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb > 10%)** in the blood are absolute proofs that the victim was alive during the fire.

### CAUSES OF DEATH IN BURNS

For medical school exams, the causes of death in burn victims are classically categorized chronologically into **Immediate/Early** and **Delayed/Remote** causes.

**I. IMMEDIATE & EARLY CAUSES (Within 48 Hours)**

- **1. Primary (Neurogenic) Shock:** Occurs rapidly within the first few hours due to intense pain, fear, and fright.
- **2. Secondary (Hypovolaemic) Shock:** 🥇 _High-Yield!_ This accounts for more than half of the deaths within the first 48 hours. It is caused by massive transudation and loss of fluid/plasma from the extensive burned surface.
- **3. Asphyxia & Smoke Inhalation:** Death can occur rapidly from suffocation due to the inhalation of carbon monoxide (CO), carbon dioxide, cyanide, and other toxic combustion products. Inhalation of superheated air also causes acute laryngeal oedema (oedema of the glottis) and pulmonary oedema.
- **4. Accidental Injuries:** Trauma sustained while attempting to escape the fire, such as injuries from falling masonry or jumping from heights.

**II. DELAYED CAUSES (Days to Weeks)**

- **1. Sepsis & Septicaemia:** 🚨 _Clinical Pearl!_ Sepsis is the **most important and common cause of death** occurring 4 to 5 days or longer after the burning. Prolonged exposure of the denuded skin leads to severe infections (e.g., meningitis, bronchopneumonia) and ultimately multisystem failure.
- **2. Toxaemia:** Occurs due to the systemic absorption of various toxic metabolites and breakdown products from the dead, burnt tissues (peaks around 3 to 4 days).
- **3. Acute Renal Failure:** Hypoperfusion from secondary shock leads to acute tubular necrosis (lower nephron nephrosis), which typically presents around the 3rd or 4th day.
- **4. Gastrointestinal Disturbances (Curling's Ulcers):** 🥇 _Exam Target!_ Acute stress ulcers frequently form in the duodenum or stomach following extensive burns, which can lead to fatal gastrointestinal haemorrhage or perforation.
- **5. Biochemical Disturbances:** Severe fluid shifts and tissue destruction can cause lethal electrolyte imbalances, particularly hypokalaemia.
- **6. Respiratory Failure:** Can occur due to Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) secondary to inhalation injury.
- **7. Fat Embolism:** A rare complication following severe tissue destruction.

**III. REMOTE CAUSES (Weeks, Months, or Years Later)**

- **1. Pulmonary Embolism:** Due to deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in the leg veins resulting from tissue damage and prolonged bed-rest immobilisation.
- **2. Specific Infections:** Delayed deaths may occur from secondary infections like tetanus, pyaemia, or gangrene.
- **3. Marjolin's Ulcer:** 🚨 _Long-Term Complication!_ Death can occur years after recovery due to the malignant transformation (squamous cell carcinoma) of a chronic, unhealed burn scar.
