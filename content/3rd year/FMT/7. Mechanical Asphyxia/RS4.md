1. Define hanging. Enumerate the types of hanging. Describe the various causes of death in hanging.
### DEFINITION OF HANGING

Hanging (self-suspension) is a form of violent mechanical asphyxia caused by the suspension of the body by a ligature which encircles the neck, where the constricting force is the weight of the body (or a portion of it).

---

### CLASSIFICATION (TYPES OF HANGING)

**I. Based on the Degree of Suspension**

- **Complete Hanging:** The entire body is completely suspended, and no part of the body touches the ground. The full weight of the body acts as the fatal constricting force.
- **Partial Hanging:** The body is only partly suspended. The toes, feet, or knees may touch the ground, or the individual may be in a sitting, kneeling, prone, or lying posture. The whole weight of the body is not required; the weight of the head (5 to 6 kg), chest, and arms provides sufficient constricting force to produce death.

**II. Based on the Position of the Knot**

- **Typical Hanging:** The knot is situated exactly at the **nape of the neck (occipital region)**. The ligature runs symmetrically upward on both sides of the neck. Death is presumed to be purely due to asphyxia, as the maximum pressure exerted is directly opposite the point of suspension (front of the neck).
- **Atypical Hanging:** The knot is located anywhere _other_ than the occiput (e.g., on the right or left side of the neck, under the chin, or behind the mastoid process). The head is typically tilted toward one side (away from the knot), and compression of vessels or the airway may be asymmetrical.

**III. Medicolegal Classification**

- **Suicidal Hanging:** The absolute most common manner of hanging.
- **Accidental Hanging:** Rare, but seen in children playing, individuals trapped in machinery/cords, or during autoerotic (sexual) asphyxia.
- **Homicidal Hanging:** Extremely rare, as it is difficult for a single assailant to hang a conscious adult. It may involve incapacitated victims (intoxicated, children) or **Lynching** (homicidal hanging by a mob to spread terror).
- **Judicial Hanging:** Execution of a legal death sentence utilizing a long drop (5 to 7 metres) to intentionally break the neck.
- **Postmortem Suspension:** Suspending a dead body after murder (e.g., via throttling) to simulate suicidal hanging and mislead investigators.

---

### CAUSES OF DEATH IN HANGING

**A. Immediate (Primary) Causes**

1. **Combined Asphyxia and Venous Congestion (High-Yield):** This combination is the most common cause of death, accounting for approximately **90% of cases**.
2. **Venous Congestion:** The ligature compresses the **jugular veins**, stopping venous return from the head while arterial supply continues. This leads to a rapid rise in intracranial venous pressure, cerebral engorgement, and unconsciousness.
3. **Asphyxia:** The constricting force pushes the root of the tongue upward and backward against the posterior pharyngeal wall, folds the epiglottis over the larynx, and causes compressive narrowing of the tracheal lumen, physically blocking the airway.
4. **Cerebral Anaemia (Ischaemia):** Complete occlusion of the arterial blood supply to the brain. Pressure on the large arteries produces immediate cerebral anoxia and immediate coma (within 10 to 15 seconds).
5. **Reflex Vagal Inhibition:** Frictional rubbing or direct pressure of the ligature on the **carotid bodies or vagal sheath** triggers a parasympathetic reflex, leading to sudden cardiac arrest. This reflex is heightened by fear, apprehension, and catecholamine release during the struggle.
6. **Fracture-Dislocation of Cervical Vertebrae:** Seen almost exclusively in **judicial hanging** (long drops of 5 to 8 metres). The sudden jerk causes a fracture-dislocation at the **C2–C3 or C3–C4** level (Hangman's fracture), leading to pulping and transection of the upper cervical spinal cord and brainstem. Death is **instantaneous**.

**B. Delayed Causes of Death (Post-Resuscitation)** If a hanging attempt is foiled and the victim is rescued, death may still occur hours or days later due to the secondary effects of profound hypoxia and ischaemia.

- **Respiratory:** Aspiration pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, and severe laryngeal oedema.
- **Neurological:** Hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy, cerebral infarction, brain abscess, and cerebral softening (liquefaction).
- **Infections:** Cervical cellulitis or systemic infections.

> **💡 CLINICAL & FORENSIC PEARL:** The forces required to occlude vital neck structures are surprisingly low:
> 
> - **2 kg** of tension blocks the **jugular veins**.
> - **4 to 5 kg** of tension blocks the **carotid arteries**.
> - **15 kg** of tension blocks the **trachea**.
> - **20 kg** of tension blocks the deeper **vertebral arteries**. This physiological reality explains why partial hanging (where only the weight of the head/chest is suspended) is so consistently and rapidly fatal.

### DIFFERENCE BETWEEN HANGING AND LIGATURE STRANGULATION

**HIGH-YIELD OVERVIEW** Hanging and ligature strangulation are both forms of violent mechanical asphyxia caused by constriction of the neck. In hanging, the constricting force is the victim's own body weight, whereas in ligature strangulation, the force is applied by an external agent (assailant). Distinguishing between the two is a classic, heavily tested medicolegal topic.

The differences are classically divided into three categories: **The Ligature Mark**, **General External Signs**, and **Internal Autopsy Findings**.

---

#### I. THE LIGATURE MARK (Most Critical Diagnostic Feature)

|Feature|Hanging (Self-Suspension)|Ligature Strangulation (Assault)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Level on Neck**|**High up**, typically above the thyroid cartilage, merging with the hairline.|**Low**, at or below the level of the thyroid cartilage.|
|**Direction**|**Obliquely upward** toward the point of suspension (knot).|**Transverse (horizontal)** or horizontally oblique.|
|**Continuity**|**Incomplete/Interrupted**, showing an inverted 'V' gap at the site of the knot.|**Continuous**, completely encircling the neck without discontinuity.|
|**Multiplicity/Knots**|Usually a single loop without multiplicity; an impression of the knot may be present.|Crossing over, twisting, or multiple knots are common; multiple twists may completely encircle the neck.|
|**Base of Mark**|Pale, hard, dry, and **parchment-like**.|Often **soft and reddish**.|
|**Edge Abrasions/Bruising**|Abrasions and ecchymoses about the edges are **rare or uncommon**.|Abrasions and ecchymoses about the edges are **very common**.|

---

#### II. GENERAL EXTERNAL SIGNS

|Feature|Hanging|Ligature Strangulation|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Facial Appearance**|Usually **pale**; petechiae are not common (except in partial hanging).|Intensely **congested and livid**; petechiae are markedly present.|
|**Asphyxial Signs**|External signs are generally less marked.|External signs are well-marked and severe.|
|**Salivary Stains**|**Present**; saliva classically dribbles from the angle of the mouth opposite the knot.|**Absent**.|
|**Bleeding (Nose/Mouth/Ears)**|Not common.|**Common**.|
|**Neck Appearance**|Stretched and elongated.|Not stretched or elongated.|
|**Tongue**|Swelling and protrusion are less marked.|Swelling and protrusion are **more marked**, often bruised and bitten.|
|**Involuntary Discharges**|Discharge of feces and urine is less common.|Discharge of feces and urine is **more common**.|
|**Seminal Fluid**|Emission at the glans is **more common**.|Emission at the glans is less common.|

---

#### III. INTERNAL AUTOPSY FINDINGS (Bloodless Field Dissection)

|Feature|Hanging|Ligature Strangulation|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Subcutaneous Tissues**|White, hard, and glistening (dry) under the mark.|Severe **engorgement and hemorrhage** in and above the compressed area.|
|**Neck Muscle Bruising**|Less common.|Extensive bruising is **more common**.|
|**Carotid Arteries**|**Damage may be seen**; transverse intimal tears (splits) can occur due to stretching/traction.|Damage is **very rare**.|
|**Thyroid Cartilage**|Fracture is less common.|Fracture (especially of the superior cornua) is **more common**.|
|**Larynx & Trachea**|Fracture is rare.|Fracture may be found.|
|**Hyoid Bone**|Fracture may occur (typically an **outward/abduction fracture** due to anteroposterior compression by the ligature).|Fracture is **uncommon**.|
|**Lungs (High-Yield)**|Emphysematous bullae are **not present**.|Emphysematous bullae are **very common** on the surface of the lungs (indicating violent agonal struggle).|

> **💡 MEDICOLEGAL EXAM PEARL** The presence of **saliva dribbling** from the angle of the mouth is a highly reliable sign of antemortem hanging. In contrast, the presence of **emphysematous bullae** on the lungs and **bleeding from the ears/nose** strongly points toward the violent agonal struggle characteristic of ligature strangulation. Remember that putrefaction does _not_ quickly obliterate the ligature mark in strangulation, as the extreme pressure empties the underlying vessels of blood, depriving putrefactive bacteria of their medium.

### HYOID BONE FRACTURES

**I. OVERVIEW** The hyoid bone (situated at the root of the tongue) is highly vulnerable to compression forces acting on the neck. Evaluating its integrity is a critical component of the medicolegal autopsy in suspected violent asphyxial deaths.

---

### II. CLASSIFICATION OF HYOID BRACTURES (HIGH-YIELD)

**1. Inward Compression (Adduction) Fractures**

- **Mechanism:** Caused by the fingers squeezing the greater horns towards each other.
- **Pathology:** The bone is fractured within 1 cm of the tip, and the posterior fragment is displaced **inwards**. The periosteum is torn on the **outer** side of the bone only.
- **Clinical Association:** Classically seen in **throttling (manual strangulation)**.

**2. Anteroposterior Compression (Abduction/Outward) Fractures**

- **Mechanism:** The hyoid bone is forced directly backwards against the vertebral column, increasing the divergence of the greater horns.
- **Pathology:** The posterior fragment is displaced **outwards**. The periosteum is torn on the **inner** side of the fracture only.
- **Clinical Association:** Classically seen in **hanging**. It can also be seen in ligature strangulation, blunt blows to the front of the neck, or run-over accidents.
- _Note:_ In hanging, bilateral fractures may occasionally show one horn fractured outwards and the other inwards due to side-to-side shifting and counter-pressure against the vertebrae.

**3. Avulsion (Tug or Traction) Fractures**

- **Mechanism:** Violent lateral or downward movement of the thyroid cartilage (or pressure between the cartilage and hyoid) exerts severe traction through the **thyrohyoid ligaments**, avulsing the bone.

---

### III. MEDICOLEGAL IMPORTANCE & EXAM PEARLS

**1. Age Dependency (Crucial Concept)**

- Fractures are **rare below 40 years** of age because the cartilage is highly elastic and the joints are mobile.
- Fractures are **common in persons above 40 years** because the hyoid bone undergoes rigid ossification and calcification, losing its flexibility.

**2. Differentiating Forms of Asphyxia**

- **Throttling:** Fracture is relatively **highly common (30 to 50%)** due to direct, localized lateral compression by the assailant's fingers. Its presence is strongly suggestive of manual strangulation.
- **Hanging:** Less frequent (15 to 20%). It merely indicates that external compression was applied over the neck and does not definitively prove hanging on its own.
- **Ligature Strangulation:** Fracture is **uncommon** because the ligature typically lies at or below the thyroid cartilage, well below the anatomical level of the hyoid bone.

**3. Confirming Antemortem Vital Reaction**

- To prove the fracture occurred during life, the forensic pathologist must demonstrate **extravasation of blood (focal hemorrhage)** at the fracture site and into surrounding soft tissues.
- **Diagnostic Tests:**
    - **Histopathology:** Shows red cell extravasation.
    - **Benzidine Test:** To confirm the presence of blood.
    - **Toluidine Blue Test (High-Yield):** Application of 1% aqueous toluidine blue to the suspected area will make fractured sites appear bluish under a stereomicroscope.

**4. Autopsy Artefacts (Diagnostic Pitfall)**

- Hyoid fractures can be accidentally produced _postmortem_ by the autopsy surgeon.
- **Causes of Artefactual Fracture:** Rough handling of the body, hyperextension of the neck, or improper dissection techniques (e.g., removing the neck structures _en masse_ by forceful downward traction).
- **Prevention:** Neck structures must be dissected _in situ_ in a **bloodless field** (after removing the brain and thoracoabdominal organs) to prevent artefactual bleeding and accurately confirm true antemortem fractures. Unilateral bony non-union of hyoid segments should also not be mistaken for a true fracture.
