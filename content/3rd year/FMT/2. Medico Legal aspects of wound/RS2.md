
1. Objectives of a fetal autopsy.
### OBJECTIVES OF A FETAL / INFANT AUTOPSY

**I. CORE MEDICOLEGAL OBJECTIVES (🚨 High-Yield Exam Targets)** In cases involving the death of a fetus or newborn (such as suspected infanticide or concealment of birth), the forensic pathologist conducts an autopsy to systematically answer five primary legal questions:

- **1. Has the fetus attained Viability (Maturity)?**
    - To determine if the fetus had reached a stage of physical development capable of independent extrauterine survival. _Note: Viability is generally considered to be reached after 210 days (7 months) of intrauterine life_.
- **2. Was the child Born Alive or Dead? (🥇 Most Critical Question)**
    - To differentiate the exact nature of the birth:
        - **Livebirth:** The child showed signs of life after being completely delivered out of the mother.
        - **Deadborn:** The child died _in utero_ prior to the onset of labor (evidenced by maceration, mummification, or rigor mortis present at birth).
        - **Stillborn:** The child was alive _in utero_ but died during the process of birth (e.g., due to prolonged labor, anoxia, or birth trauma).
- **3. What was the Duration of Survival?**
    - If the child was born alive, the autopsy must estimate how long the child lived after birth. This is assessed by observing progressive physiological changes (e.g., aeration of lungs, obliteration of fetal circulation, changes in the umbilical cord, passage of meconium, and presence of milk in the stomach).
- **4. What was the exact Cause of Death?**
    - To definitively establish whether the death was:
        - _Natural:_ Due to immaturity, congenital malformations, or neonatal infections.
        - _Accidental:_ Due to birth trauma, cord prolapse, or precipitate labor.
        - _Criminal (Infanticide):_ Due to acts of commission (e.g., smothering, strangulation, blunt head injury) or acts of omission (e.g., deliberate neglect, failure to tie the umbilical cord).
- **5. Establishment of Identity:**
    - To link the infant to the suspected mother via blood grouping, DNA profiling, and documentation of any congenital deformities or racial characteristics.

**II. CLINICAL PEARL 🚨**

- **The Presumption of Law:** The law presumes that _every newborn child found dead was born dead_ until the contrary is scientifically proved.
- **Burden of Proof:** Therefore, the primary objective of the forensic expert is to demonstrate the **absolute signs of livebirth** (such as completely expanded and aerated lungs via the Hydrostatic Test, or the presence of milk in the stomach) to successfully support a charge of infanticide.

### OBJECTIVES OF MEDICOLEGAL AND CLINICAL AUTOPSIES

**I. OBJECTIVES OF MEDICOLEGAL (FORENSIC) AUTOPSY (🚨 Legal & Investigative Focus)**

A medicolegal autopsy is an investigative, scientific dissection of a dead body performed for the state, with the primary orientation of providing factual and objective information to the police and courts.

**🥇 High-Yield Objectives:**

- **Cause & Manner of Death:** To definitively find out the medical cause of death (natural vs. unnatural) and determine the manner of death (accidental, suicidal, or homicidal).
- **Time Since Death (TSD):** To estimate the probable time interval between death and the postmortem examination.
- **Injury Analysis & Reconstruction:** To accurately document injuries, find out how they occurred, determine the probable weapon used, and deduce the relative positions of the victim and the assailant at the time of infliction.
- **Establishment of Identity:** To fix the absolute identity of the individual when it is not known (e.g., in mutilated or decomposed bodies).
- **Collection of Evidence:** To collect physical and trace evidence to identify the object/weapon causing death and to help identify the criminal.
- **Preservation of Tissues:** To retain relevant viscera, organs, and tissues for vital laboratory investigations such as histopathological (HPE) and chemical/toxicological analysis.
- **Infant Deaths:** In cases involving newborn infants, to determine whether the child was a dead-born, stillborn, or live-born, and to assess viability.

---

**II. OBJECTIVES OF CLINICAL (PATHOLOGICAL) AUTOPSY (🔬 Academic & Medical Focus)**

A clinical or pathological autopsy is performed by treating clinicians in association with a clinical pathologist in cases of purely natural death where the cause is already confirmed. It requires the mandatory consent of the legal heirs.

**🥇 High-Yield Objectives:**

- **Morphological Confirmation:** To find morphologic changes that explain the signs and symptoms of the disease process.
- **Extent of Disease:** To determine the exact extent of the natural disease or the combination of comorbidities that ultimately led to death.
- **Treatment Efficacy:** To evaluate and assess the effectiveness of the medical or surgical treatment provided during the patient's life.
- **Occult Pathologies:** To detect any previously unrecognised or occult diseases that were not clinically apparent.
- **Genetic/Inheritable Conditions:** To discover and inform the family of any inheritable or genetic conditions present in the deceased.
- **Academic Advancement:** To acquire scientific knowledge that is academically oriented and will be directly useful for clinicians when treating similar patients in the future.

1. Describe the dissection of heart at autopsy.
### DISSECTION OF THE HEART AT AUTOPSY

**I. IN SITU EXAMINATION & REMOVAL**

- **Pericardial Sac:** Examine the pericardium while the heart is still _in situ_ for adhesions, pericarditis (white/milk spots), effusions, or blood (cardiac tamponade). Open the pericardium using an **inverted T-shaped incision**.
- **Pulmonary Artery Palpation (🚨 Critical Step):** Palpate the pulmonary arteries _before_ cutting them to check for any antemortem thrombus or massive embolus. If a thrombus is felt, the right ventricle and pulmonary trunk must be opened _in situ_ to assess the size and extent of the clot.
- **Removal:** Grasp the heart at the apex, lift it upwards, and sever the pulmonary vessels, superior and inferior vena cavae, and the ascending aorta as far away from the base as possible. The great vessels should be kept attached at least 1 cm beyond the pericardial fold.

**II. EXTERNAL PREPARATION & MEASUREMENTS**

- **Initial Steps:** Wash the surface of the heart thoroughly and weigh it after removing blood clots from the cavities.
- **Measurements:** Measure the height (from the base of the aorta to the apex), width (at the valvular region), and circumference (1 cm below the A-V sulcus).
- **External Pathology:** Inspect the epicardium for subepicardial petechiae (which may indicate asphyxia or electrocution), visible fibrosis, or recent infarcts.

**III. CORONARY ARTERY DISSECTION (🥇 High-Yield Exam Target)**

- **Timing:** The coronary arteries **MUST** be examined before opening the heart chambers. Identify the right and left coronary ostia.
- **Method 1 (Serial Sectioning - Preferred):** Make **serial cross-sections 2 to 3 mm apart** along the entire epicardial course of the major vessels using a scalpel. _Clinical Pearl:_ This is the preferred technique because it accurately demonstrates the percentage of luminal stenosis and detects antemortem thrombi without the danger of dislodging them.
- **Method 2 (Longitudinal Opening):** Passing small curved coronary scissors through the ostia into the lumen. _Contraindication:_ This is generally advised against if a thrombus is suspected, as the crushing and cutting action of the scissors distorts the vessel and obscures or pushes the clot.

**IV. OPENING THE CHAMBERS: THE INFLOW-OUTFLOW METHOD** The heart is systematically opened following the exact **direction of blood flow** using an enterotome (a special autopsy scissors).

- **1. Right Atrium (Inflow):** Cut between the openings of the superior and inferior vena cavae. Extend the cut into the right auricular appendage to search for hidden thrombi.
- **2. Right Ventricle (Inflow):** With the lateral margin of the right ventricle facing the dissector, pass the enterotome through the tricuspid orifice and open the right ventricle downwards along its **lateral margin (right border)**.
- **3. Pulmonary Outflow:** Position the heart so the apex points towards the examiner. Insert the enterotome close to the apex and cut upwards through the conus pulmonalis and pulmonary valve. _🚨 Surgical landmark:_ Stay about **10 mm to the right of, and parallel to, the anterior descending branch indicating the interventricular septum**.
- **4. Left Atrium (Inflow):** Cut between the openings of the pulmonary veins, then extend the incision along its lateral wall into the left auricle.
- **5. Left Ventricle (Inflow):** Extend the atrial incision down through the mitral orifice, passing along the **lateral margin (left border)** of the left ventricle all the way to the apex.
- **6. Aortic Outflow:** From the apex, cut upwards directly along the **interventricular septum** into the aorta, thereby opening the aortic valve.

**V. INTERNAL EVALUATION & SECTIONING (🔬 Pathology Focus)**

- **Valves:** Measure the circumference of all valves (graduated cones can be used for intact valves). Water competency tests can be performed on the pulmonary and aortic valves.
- **Ventricular Thickness:** Measure the thickness of the right and left ventricles. The left ventricle is strictly measured **1 cm below the atrioventricular septum**.
- **Internal Structures:** Examine the interatrial septum (check for patent foramen ovale), chordae tendineae, and papillary muscles for infarction, rupture, or adherence.
- **Myocardial Slicing (🥇 The Final Step):** Incise the thick muscle of both ventricles in a plane **parallel to the epicardial and endocardial surfaces**. This specific slicing technique most clearly reveals deep areas of fresh infarction or old fibrous scarring. Observe the endocardium for any subendocardial haemorrhages.
1. Enumerate the precautions to be taken while handling and performing autopsy examination on an HIV positive dead body.
### PRECAUTIONS IN AUTOPSY OF HIV-INFECTED BODIES (UNIVERSAL WORK PRECAUTIONS)

**I. GENERAL PRINCIPLES & TRANSMISSION RISK (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Transmission Mode:** HIV is transmitted via transdermal inoculation (needle pricks/cuts), mucosal splashes, and skin contact with superficial injuries. It is **not** associated with air-borne transmission.
- **Viability:** HIV in high concentrations can remain viable in tissues for 3 weeks and in liquid blood for up to 2 months after death.
- **Personnel Restriction:** Any worker with exudative lesions, weeping dermatitis, or external skin injuries must **NOT** handle AIDS victims.

**II. PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE) (🚨 Exam Target)** Maximum barrier protection is mandatory for all personnel:

- **Gloves:** Strict **double gloving** (heavy autopsy gloves worn over standard surgical gloves).
- **Body Protection:** Full-sleeved overalls (instead of simple surgical gowns), water-proof plastic apron, and waterproof rubber gumboots of knee length with shoe covers.
- **Head & Face:** Head cap, face mask, and goggles. A **plastic visor** must be worn to protect eyes and mucosal surfaces from splash injuries.
- **Respirator:** A high-efficiency particulate air-filled (HEPA) respirator or powered air-purifying respirator should be utilized.

**III. SAFE AUTOPSY TECHNIQUES**

- **Dissection Method:** **Rokitansky's technique** (in situ dissection combined with _en block_ removal) is the **preferred choice** in patients with highly transmissible diseases like HIV, to minimize exposure. It is preferable to leave some organs _in situ_ rather than eviscerating them en masse.
- **Organ Slicing:** Lungs and other organs should be fixed whole in formalin _before_ slicing to reduce exposure.
- **Aerosol Minimization:** To prevent aerosol splatter when opening the skull, use an electrical oscillating saw attached to a **vacuum dust exhaust and filter**, or a band saw under a transparent anti-splash cover.
- **Sharps Handling:** Extreme caution must be exercised to avoid accidental pricks from needles and scalpels. If a glove is cut or punctured, it must be removed and replaced immediately.

**IV. SPECIMEN HANDLING & DISINFECTION (🥇 High-Yield Chemicals)**

- **Specimens:** Avoid all mucocutaneous contact and aerosol inhalation. Specimens must be properly labelled, filled with **10% formalin** solution, and handled strictly with gloved hands.
- **Instruments:** Dip used instruments in **2% glutaraldehyde (Cidex)** for 30 minutes, wash with soap/detergent and water, dry, rinse in methylated spirit, and air dry.
- **Surfaces & Spills:** Wipe small spills with disposable tissues and discard in biohazard bags. Clean the autopsy table and floors with a **1% bleach solution** (1:10 dilution of common household bleach or freshly prepared sodium hypochlorite), followed by washing with soap and water.
- **Laundry:** Soak aprons and towels in **1% bleach for 30 minutes**, wash with detergent and hot water, and autoclave.

**V. BODY AND WASTE DISPOSAL**

- **Body Preparation:** After the autopsy, all body orifices must be packed. The body must be wrapped and tied tightly in a **double-layer heavy plastic bag** to prevent leakage.
- **Labelling:** A red tag clearly stating **"Biologically Hazardous"** (with name, age, sex, and registration number) must be attached to the body.
- **Final Disposal:** All soiled gauze and cotton must be collected in double plastic biohazard bags for incineration. The body itself should ideally be burnt or incinerated.

**VI. PROTOCOL FOR ACCIDENTAL EXPOSURE (🚨 Clinical Pearl)** If an accidental needle stick or scalpel cut occurs during the autopsy:

1. **Immediate Wash:** Wash the wound thoroughly under running water immediately.
2. **Encourage Bleeding:** Actively encourage the wound to bleed to flush out potential inoculums, then disinfect.
3. **Reporting:** Report the incident to the proper authority immediately to get blood checked for HIV seropositivity.
4. Preservation, packing and dispatch of viscera in a case of strychnine poisoning.
### PRESERVATION, PACKING, AND DISPATCH OF VISCERA IN STRYCHNINE POISONING

Strychnine (derived from _Strychnos nux vomica_) is a powerful alkaloidal **spinal poison**. Because of its specific toxicokinetics and target organs, both routine and special viscera must be meticulously collected and preserved for chemical analysis.

**I. VISCERA TO BE PRESERVED (🥇 High-Yield Exam Target)**

**A. Routine Viscera (The Standard "5 Bottles")**

- **Bottle 1:** The entire stomach and its contents. _(Note: If empty, the stomach wall must still be preserved)_.
- **Bottle 2:** The upper part of the small intestine (about 30 cm length) and its contents.
- **Bottle 3:** Liver (200 to 500 gm) and half of each kidney.
- **Bottle 4:** Blood (30 ml; minimum 10 ml) collected from a peripheral vessel (e.g., femoral vein) to avoid postmortem diffusion contamination.
- **Bottle 5:** Urine (Minimum 30 ml).
- **Bottle 6 (Control):** A 100 ml sample of the exact preservative used, to rule out prior contamination.

**B. Special Viscera for Strychnine Poisoning (🚨 Highly Tested)** Because strychnine specifically targets the central nervous system (specifically the anterior horn cells), additional tissues **MUST** be preserved:

- **Spinal Cord:** The **entire length** must be preserved.
- **Brain:** 100 gm of the cerebrum or cerebellum.
- **Heart:** Strychnine is one of the few poisons where the heart is routinely preserved for chemical analysis.

**II. PRESERVATIVES USED**

- **Solid Organs (Stomach, Liver, Kidneys, CNS, Heart):** Saturated Sodium Chloride (NaCl) solution OR Rectified Spirit (Ethyl Alcohol).
- **Blood:** 10 mg/ml of Sodium/Potassium Fluoride (enzyme inhibitor/preservative) + 3 mg/ml Potassium Oxalate (anticoagulant).
- _Contraindication:_ **Never use Formaldehyde (Formalin)**, as it coagulates proteins and makes the extraction of alkaloid organic poisons like strychnine extremely difficult.

**III. PACKING PROTOCOL (🚨 Clinical Pearls)**

- **Containers:** Use clean, wide-mouthed, 1-litre glass bottles fitted with glass stoppers. Avoid rubber inserts under caps, as they can extract certain poisons.
- **Preparation:** Solid organs (liver, kidney, brain, heart) must be cut into small pieces (0.5 to 1 cm thick) or minced to allow the preservative to penetrate deeply. Hollow organs (stomach, intestines) must be opened before being placed in the bottle.
- **The "Two-Thirds" Rule:** Fill only **two-thirds (2/3)** of the bottle's capacity with the viscera and preservative. The remaining empty space prevents the bottle from bursting if putrefactive gases form.
- **Volume:** The quantity of the preservative should be equal to or greater than the bulk of the viscera.

**IV. SEALING, LABELLING, AND DISPATCH**

- **Sealing:** The glass stoppers must be well-fitted, covered with a piece of cloth, tied firmly with tape or string, and sealed with a personal or departmental wax seal immediately to prevent contamination.
- **Labelling:** Each bottle must be strictly labelled with: Name of the victim, age, sex, autopsy number, crime number, police station, specific organs contained, date/place of autopsy, preservative used, and the medical officer's signature.
- **Dispatch (Chain of Custody):**
    - The sealed bottles are placed in a box, which is then locked and sealed.
    - A copy of the inquest report, postmortem report, and the Magistrate's authorization letter must accompany the box.
    - The key to the box and a sample of the wax seal are placed in a separate sealed envelope.
    - The box is handed over to the designated police constable after obtaining a receipt, who personally delivers it to the Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL).

**V. FORENSIC PEARL: STRYCHNINE & DECOMPOSITION**

- Strychnine strongly **resists putrefaction**. It is highly stable and can be detected in the cadaver for up to **four years** after death, making proper viscera preservation highly rewarding even in exhumed or decomposed bodies.

1. Types of autopsy.

### TYPES OF AUTOPSY

Autopsy (necropsy) is defined as the scientific dissection of a dead body. In medical and forensic practice, autopsies are primarily classified into several types based on their purpose and methodology:

**I. PATHOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (CLINICAL / ACADEMIC AUTOPSY) 🔬**

- **Definition:** An autopsy performed by treating clinicians in association with a hospital or clinical pathologist.
- **Prerequisite (🚨 High-Yield):** The **consent of the legal heir is absolutely mandatory**.
- **Indications:** It is strictly performed in cases of **natural death** where the cause is already clinically confirmed.
- **Objectives:**
    - To find morphological changes that explain the signs and symptoms of a known disease.
    - To assess the effectiveness of medical or surgical treatments.
    - To detect previously unrecognized (occult) diseases or inheritable conditions for the family's knowledge.
    - Academically oriented to acquire knowledge for treating similar patients in the future.

**II. MEDICOLEGAL AUTOPSY (FORENSIC AUTOPSY) ⚖️**

- **Definition:** An investigative, scientific dissection of a dead body performed for the state to aid in the administration of justice.
- **Prerequisite (🚨 Exam Target):** **No consent from relatives is required**. It is conducted under the statutory authorization of the Police (not below Sub-Inspector rank) or an Executive Magistrate, and is performed even if relatives object.
- **Who Performs It:** Can only be performed by a registered allopathic physician (MBBS) in an authorized center.
- **Indications:** Unnatural, suspicious, unexplained, sudden, and medically unattended deaths.
- **Objectives (🥇 Core Forensic Goals):**
    - To definitively establish the cause, manner (suicide, homicide, accident), and mechanism of death.
    - To estimate the Time Since Death (TSD).
    - To meticulously document injuries and reconstruct the sequence of events.
    - To establish identity in unknown bodies.
    - To collect, preserve, and dispatch trace evidence and viscera for chemical or histopathological analysis.

**III. PSYCHOLOGICAL AUTOPSY 🧠**

- **Definition:** A set of postmortem investigative procedures to evaluate the role of physical and psychological factors, primarily used to determine the exact manner of death in **equivocal deaths or suicides**.
- **Methodology:** Involves systematic collection of data through structured interviews with the deceased's family, friends, and employers, evaluating their medical, psychiatric, and financial records.
- **Clinical Relevance:** Helps ascertain the state of mind at the time of death and provides therapeutic bereavement counseling for survivors.

**IV. VERBAL AUTOPSY 🗣️**

- **Definition:** An autopsy method that does not involve dissection.
- **Usage:** It is primarily utilized in **infant deaths** to gather epidemiological data on infant mortality rates.

**V. VIRTUAL AUTOPSY (VIRTOPSY) 💻**

- **Definition:** A highly advanced, **non-invasive** technique of examining dead bodies using imaging technologies.
- **Methodology:** It combines multi-slice computed tomography (MSCT), magnetic resonance (MR) imaging, photogrammetry, and 3D optical measuring techniques.
- **Advantages (🥇 Clinical Pearl):** It does not destroy vital evidence that might be lost in a traditional autopsy. It is particularly excellent for demonstrating soft tissue injuries, air embolism, pneumothorax, hyperbaric trauma, and tracing firearm/bullet tracts in 3D.

2. Aims and objectives of medicolegal autopsy.
### AIMS AND OBJECTIVES OF MEDICOLEGAL AUTOPSY

A medicolegal autopsy (investigative scientific dissection) is performed under statutory authority primarily to aid the police and courts in the administration of justice.

**I. PRIMARY OBJECTIVES (🥇 High-Yield Exam Targets)**

- **Determine the Cause of Death:** To definitively establish the medical cause of death, identifying whether it is purely natural (due to disease) or unnatural (due to trauma, poisoning, etc.).
- **Determine the Manner of Death:** To ascertain the circumstances of an unnatural death, specifically whether it was homicidal, suicidal, or accidental.
- **Estimate Time Since Death (TSD):** To assess the probable time interval between death and the autopsy based on the progression of postmortem changes.

**II. RECONSTRUCTION & DOCUMENTATION (🚨 Core Forensic Goals)**

- **Injury Analysis and Documentation:** To meticulously detect, measure, describe, and document all external and internal injuries.
- **Event Reconstruction:** To evaluate how injuries occurred, assess the mode of infliction, identify the probable weapon or object used, and deduce the relative positions of the victim and assailant at the time of the attack.
- **Establish Identity:** To fix the absolute identity of the individual in cases where the body is unknown, decomposed, burnt, or mutilated.

**III. EVIDENCE COLLECTION & PRESERVATION (🔬 Clinical Relevance)**

- **Trace and Physical Evidence:** To collect and preserve physical evidence from the body and clothing (e.g., bullets, hair, fibers, seminal stains) to identify the weapon causing death and potentially link the criminal to the act.
- **Viscera and Tissue Preservation:** To retain and correctly dispatch relevant organs, tissues, and body fluids for Histopathological Examination (HPE) and chemical/toxicological analysis.

**IV. SPECIAL MEDICOLEGAL CIRCUMSTANCES**

- **Infant Deaths:** In cases of newborn deaths, a primary objective is to determine whether the infant was live-born, stillborn, or dead-born, and to ascertain if the fetus had attained viability.
- **Public Interest and Justice:** To uncover factual, objective, and unbiased scientific information that serves evidentiary and confirmatory value for the police and courts of law.


1. Various methods of removal of organs at autopsy.
### METHODS OF ORGAN REMOVAL AT AUTOPSY

In medicolegal and pathological autopsies, the evisceration (removal of internal organs) is accomplished using one of four primary, standardized techniques. The choice of technique depends on the specific requirements of the case, the suspected pathology, and safety protocols.

**I. VIRCHOW'S TECHNIQUE (Individual Organ Removal)**

- **Method:** Organs are removed **one by one**.
- **Order of Evisceration:** The cranial cavity is exposed first, followed by the thoracic, cervical, and abdominal organs. The spinal cord is removed from the back last.
- **🚨 Medicolegal Disadvantage:** The normal anatomico-pathologic relationships between the organs are completely destroyed and cannot be evaluated later.

**II. ROKITANSKY'S TECHNIQUE (In Situ Dissection)**

- **Method:** This technique involves **_in situ_ (within the body) dissection** in part, combined with _en block_ (partial block) removal.
- **🥇 Clinical Pearl (High-Yield for Exams):** It is the **preferred choice** in autopsies of patients with highly transmissible or infectious diseases, such as **HIV and Hepatitis B**. This method minimizes the handling of organs, reducing the risk of splashing, aerosol generation, and percutaneous injuries to the autopsy staff.

**III. LETTULLE'S TECHNIQUE (En Masse Removal)**

- **Method:** The cervical, thoracic, abdominal, and pelvic organs are removed together **en masse** (as one single massive block) and then dissected outside the body as a single organ block.
- **🥇 Major Advantage:** It has the distinct advantage of leaving **all anatomical attachments and vascular connections perfectly intact**. This makes it the best technique for demonstrating systemic diseases, vascular pathologies, or complex traumatic injuries spanning multiple regions.

**IV. GHON'S TECHNIQUE (En Bloc Removal)**

- **Method:** The organs are removed in functionally or anatomically related groups known as **"organ blocks"**.
- **Block Divisions:** The organs are typically divided into the following separate system blocks: cervical, thoracic, abdominal, and urogenital systems. The neuronal system (brain and spinal cord) is removed as another independent block.
1. Heart dissection procedure at autopsy.
### HEART DISSECTION PROCEDURE AT AUTOPSY

**I. IN SITU EXAMINATION & REMOVAL**

- **Pericardial Sac:** Examine while the heart is still _in situ_ for adhesions, pericarditis, effusions, or blood (cardiac tamponade). Open the pericardium using an **inverted T-shaped incision**.
- **Pulmonary Artery Palpation:** 🚨 _Crucial Step:_ Palpate the pulmonary arteries _before_ cutting them to check for any antemortem thrombus or massive embolus.
- **Removal:** Remove the heart with the great vessels attached, keeping at least 1 cm beyond the pericardial fold.

**II. PRELIMINARY PREPARATION & CORONARY ARTERY DISSECTION (🥇 High-Yield)**

- **Initial Measurements:** Wash the surface, weigh the heart, and measure the height, width, and circumference (1 cm below the A-V sulcus). Check for epicardial petechiae or fibrosis.
- **Coronary Arteries (🚨 Exam Target):** The coronaries must be examined **before** opening the heart chambers.
    - _Preferred Method:_ Make **serial cross-sections 2 to 3 mm apart** along the entire course of the major vessels using a scalpel. This accurately demonstrates the percentage of stenosis and prevents dislodging antemortem thrombi.
    - _Contraindication:_ Avoid passing scissors through the ostia if a thrombus is suspected, as the crushing/cutting action distorts the vessel and pushes the clot. (Though passing a small curved coronary scissors is noted as an alternative method in some practices).

**III. OPENING THE CHAMBERS: THE INFLOW-OUTFLOW METHOD** The heart is systematically opened following the exact **direction of blood flow** using an enterotome.

- **1. Right Atrium (Inflow):** Cut between the openings of the superior and inferior vena cavae. Make a small secondary cut into the right auricular appendage to search for hidden thrombi.
- **2. Right Ventricle (Inflow):** Pass the enterotome through the tricuspid orifice and open the right ventricle downwards along its **lateral margin (right border)**.
- **3. Pulmonary Outflow:** With the apex pointing towards the examiner, insert the enterotome close to the apex. Cut upwards through the conus pulmonalis and pulmonary valve, staying about **10 mm to the right of, and parallel to, the interventricular septum**.
- **4. Left Atrium (Inflow):** Cut between the openings of the pulmonary veins, then extend the cut along its lateral wall extending to the left auricle.
- **5. Left Ventricle (Inflow):** Extend the atrial incision down through the mitral orifice, passing along the **lateral margin (left border)** of the left ventricle down to the apex.
- **6. Aortic Outflow:** From the apex, cut upwards along the **interventricular septum** directly into the aorta, opening the aortic valve.

**IV. INTERNAL EVALUATION & SECTIONING (🔬 Clinical Relevance)**

- **Valves & Structures:** Measure the circumference of the valves (using graduated cones if intact). Examine the chordae tendineae, papillary muscles, and interatrial/interventricular septa (check for patent foramen ovale or ruptures).
- **Ventricular Muscle:** Measure the thickness of the right and left ventricles (left ventricle is measured 1 cm below the atrioventricular septum).
- **Myocardial Slicing:** Incise the muscle of both ventricles in a plane **parallel to the epicardial and endocardial surfaces** to best reveal areas of fresh infarction or old fibrous scarring. Remove postmortem clots and observe the endocardium for subendocardial haemorrhages.


1. Stomach dissection at autopsy.
### STOMACH DISSECTION AT AUTOPSY

**I. ORGAN REMOVAL & PREPARATION**

- **Orientation:** For examining the abdominal organs, they are kept on the dissection board with the anterior surface facing upwards and the liver positioned away from the operator.
- **Isolation (🚨 Exam Target):** The stomach must be isolated by applying **double ligatures** at each end (the cardiac and pyloric ends) before it is removed.
- **Placement:** Once removed, it is placed in a clean dish (preferably a clean porcelain dish) for further examination.

**II. INCISION TECHNIQUE**

- **The Cut:** The stomach is typically opened along its **greater curvature**, extending from the cardiac end to the pyloric end. _(Note: Some authors alternatively describe opening the stomach by passing scissors along the lesser curvature)._
- **Adjacent Structures:** The size of the pyloric ring is noted using a finger, and the duodenum is subsequently opened along its anterior wall.
- **Toxicology Golden Rule (🥇 High-Yield):** A ligatured stomach should **never** be sent for chemical analysis without being opened first. If left unopened, putrefaction can obscure mucosal changes, and the buildup of putrefactive gases can force the lid off the jar during transit.

**III. EXAMINATION OF GASTRIC CONTENTS** The contents must be carefully observed, measured, and documented before any washing occurs.

- **General Parameters:** Note the volume, colour, consistency, odour, and character of the contents.
- **Food & Digestion:** Assess the nature of any food present and its exact state of digestion.
- **Foreign & Toxic Matter:** Meticulously search for distinct visible particles, seeds, leaves, fragments of capsules/tablets, powdered poisons, or foreign bodies like nails, pins, and glass. Any distinct suspicious particles should be isolated and sent separately for chemical analysis.

**IV. CLINICAL PEARLS: APPEARANCE OF GASTRIC CONTENTS 🚨**

- **Yellow/Yellow-green:** Indicates regurgitation of bile from the duodenum.
- **"Coffee-ground" appearance:** Indicates partially digested blood from small haemorrhages.
- **Large, soft clots:** May form a cast of the gastric outline, indicating massive antemortem haemorrhage (blood may also have been swallowed from the lungs, oronasopharynx, or oesophagus).
- **Dirty-green, brown, or black foul-smelling fluid:** Seen in cases of paralytic ileus.
- **Faecal matter:** Indicates a gastrocolic fistula.

**V. EXAMINATION OF THE GASTRIC MUCOSA**

- If the contents are not required for chemical analysis, they are washed out to properly examine the mucous membrane.
- **Toxicology check:** The wall must be examined for fragments of poison adhering to it (e.g., starch from tablets, powdered poisons, cantharides).
- **Pathology check:** The mucosa is evaluated for signs of congestion, haemorrhage, erosions, ulcerations, or other abnormalities. Evidence of severe inflammation, discoloration, and necrosis is especially prominent in ingested corrosive poisoning.


1. Osteometric board.
### OSTEOMETRIC BOARD

**I. DEFINITION & STRUCTURE**

- **Concept:** A specialized forensic and anthropological instrument designed for the highly accurate measurement of bones.
- **Construction:** It consists of a flat rectangular base with a ruler fixed along one of its long sides. It features a **fixed upright** at one end and a **movable upright** that slides along the length of the board.
- **Common Model:** The **Hepburn osteometric board modified by Trevor** is the most frequently used standard model.

**II. MEDICOLEGAL INDICATIONS (🥇 High-Yield Exam Targets)**

- **1. Estimation of Stature:** It is the gold standard device used to measure the maximum length of weight-bearing long bones (e.g., femur, tibia, humerus, radius). Mathematical regression formulae (like Trotter and Gleser's) are then applied to these measurements to calculate the living height of the individual.
    - _🚨 Exam Pearl:_ Measurements of long bones taken using standard measuring tapes or calipers are considered **inaccurate**. An osteometric board must be used.
- **2. Determination of Race (Cephalic Index):** It is used to accurately measure the maximum length of the skull (from the frontal eminence to the occipital protuberance) and the maximum breadth (bi-parietal diameter) to calculate the Cephalic Index.

**III. TECHNIQUE OF USE**

- **Standard Method:** The dry bone is placed flat on the board with one of its extremities resting flush against the fixed upright. The sliding movable upright is then brought up to firmly contact the furthest projecting point of the opposite end of the bone, and the maximum distance is read directly from the ruler.
- **Improvised Alternative:** If an osteometric board is not available, the bone should be measured on a flat bench with its maximum length taken between two parallel, vertical boards placed in firm contact with both bone ends.
1. Second autopsy.
### SECOND AUTOPSY (RE-AUTOPSY)

**I. DEFINITION & CONTEXT**

- **Concept:** A second autopsy is a subsequent medicolegal postmortem examination performed on a previously autopsied body, which is very frequently an exhumed body.
- **Medicolegal Value:** It is possible that highly valuable new results may be obtained. However, even if no new information is gleaned, performing a second autopsy is crucial as it helps in putting an end to rumours or suspicions raised by relatives or the public.

**II. PREREQUISITES & PRE-AUTOPSY PROTOCOL (🥇 High-Yield)** Before commencing a second autopsy, strict protocols must be followed:

- **Document Collection:** The medical officer _must_ obtain all available documents relating to the case. This crucially includes the first autopsy report, photographs of the death scene and body taken during the first autopsy, inquest papers from the first postmortem, hospital records, police investigative reports, and X-rays.
- **Correlation:** If possible, the forensic pathologist who performed the first autopsy should be called to be present, to help correlate all the previous and current findings.
- **Recording:** The entire second autopsy must be **videographed**.

**III. CHALLENGES & INTERPRETATIVE DIFFICULTIES (🚨 Exam Target)** Interpreting the findings of a second autopsy is notoriously difficult. Examiners frequently test the reasons for this difficulty, which include:

1. **Progressive Decomposition:** Natural putrefactive changes severely distort tissues.
2. **Artifacts:** The presence of various artifacts caused by the burial and subsequent exhumation processes.
3. **Missing Evidence:** The previous removal, dissection, and retention of viscera during the first autopsy.
4. **Anatomical Alterations:** Serious structural alterations and tissue disruptions resulting directly from the dissection techniques of the first autopsy.

**IV. SPECIFIC FINDINGS: CONTUSIONS (🥇 Clinical Pearl)**

- **Visibility:** Interestingly, contusions that were missed initially may become visible during a second autopsy because the blood is thoroughly drained from the tissues following the first autopsy.
- **Decomposition Effect:** However, as decomposition progresses, it causes the merger of these contusions, leading to the blurring of their distinct patterns.

**V. DOCUMENTATION PROTOCOL**

- The findings of the second autopsy must be documented in exceptionally great detail.
- The report must explicitly state whether the new findings are **confirmatory** or **contradictory** with respect to the results of the first autopsy report.
