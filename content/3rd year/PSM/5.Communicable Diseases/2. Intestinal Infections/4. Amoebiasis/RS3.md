1. Treatment of amoebiasis 
	**TREATMENT OF AMOEBIASIS**
	
	The medical management of amoebiasis is categorized based on the clinical presentation, specifically whether the patient is symptomatic or an asymptomatic carrier.
	
	**1. SYMPTOMATIC CASES**
	
	- **Drug of Choice:** Metronidazole given orally is highly effective. Tinidazole can also be used as an alternative.
	- **Dosage (Metronidazole):** 30 mg/kg of body weight/day, divided into 3 doses administered after meals, for a duration of 8 to 10 days.
	- **Clinical Marker:** A positive clinical response within 48 hours of starting treatment often confirms the suspected diagnosis of amoebiasis.
	- **Complications:** Any suspected cases of extra-intestinal amoebiasis, such as an amoebic liver abscess, must be immediately referred to the nearest hospital for specialized management.
	
	**2. ASYMPTOMATIC INFECTIONS (CARRIERS)** The treatment strategy for asymptomatic cysts passers depends on the epidemiological setting:
	
	- **In Endemic Areas:** The general consensus is _not_ to treat asymptomatic individuals because the probability of rapid reinfection is very high.
	    - _Exception:_ If the carrier is a food handler, they must be treated to prevent transmission to others.
	- **In Non-Endemic Areas:** Asymptomatic carriers are always likely to be treated to prevent the introduction and spread of the parasite.
	- **Drugs of Choice for Carriers (Luminal Amoebicides):**
	    - **Oral Diiodohydroxyquin:** 650 mg TDS (Three times a day) for adults, or 30–40 mg/kg of body weight/day for children, given for 20 days.
	    - **Oral Diloxanide furoate:** 500 mg TDS for 10 days for adults.
	
	**3. CHEMOPROPHYLAXIS AND MASS TREATMENT**
	
	- **Chemoprophylaxis:** At present, there is no acceptable chemoprophylactic agent available for the prevention of amoebiasis.
	- **Mass Treatment:** Mass examination and blanket treatment of the community are not considered viable or effective solutions for the public health control of amoebiasis.
