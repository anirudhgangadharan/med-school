1. Lymphatic filariasis- **Epidemiology, Prevention, and Control:**  
	**LYMPHATIC FILARIASIS: EPIDEMIOLOGY, PREVENTION, AND CONTROL**
	
	**1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS**
	
	**A. Agent Factors**
	
	- **Agent:** The disease is caused by nematode worms. About 90% of global lymphatic filariasis is caused by _Wuchereria bancrofti_, while the rest are caused by _Brugia malayi_ and _Brugia timori_.
	- **Hosts:** Man is the definitive host (where adult worms live in the lymphatic system) and the mosquito is the intermediate host (where microfilariae develop into infective larvae).
	- **Periodicity:** The microfilariae (Mf) of _W. bancrofti_ and _B. malayi_ in India display nocturnal periodicity, appearing in large numbers in the peripheral blood at night (maximum density between 10 pm and 2 am) to coincide with the biting habits of the vector mosquitoes.
	- **Life Span:** Adult worms can survive for 15 years or more in the human body, while Mf can live up to a year or more.
	
	**B. Vector Factors**
	
	- **Bancroftian Filariasis:** Transmitted primarily by the _Culex quinquefasciatus_ mosquito, which is a domestic species that breeds profusely in dirty, polluted water collections like stagnant drains, cesspools, and septic tanks.
	- **Brugian Filariasis:** Transmitted primarily by _Mansonia_ mosquitoes (_M. annulifera_, _M. uniformis_). These breed in ponds and lakes containing specific aquatic plants (like _Pistia stratiotes_ or water hyacinth), as the larvae attach their siphon tubes to the rootlets of these plants for air.
	
	**C. Host Factors**
	
	- **Age and Sex:** All ages are susceptible, with infection rates rising up to 20–30 years and then leveling off. The Mf rate is generally higher in men.
	- **Immunity:** Man develops resistance only after many years of continuous exposure. A large proportion of infected people remain asymptomatic but carry Mf in their blood, acting as the primary reservoir of infection.
	- **Socio-economic:** Disease is closely associated with illiteracy, poverty, poor sanitation, urbanization, and industrialization.
	
	**D. Environmental Factors**
	
	- **Climate:** Maximum prevalence of the _Culex_ vector occurs at temperatures between 22°C to 38°C and a relative humidity of around 70%.
	- **Sanitation:** Inadequate sewage disposal and bad drainage are the strongest environmental predisposing factors.
	
	**E. Mode of Transmission & Incubation**
	
	- **Transmission:** Occurs via the bite of an infected vector mosquito (cyclo-developmental biological transmission).
	- **Clinical Incubation Period:** The time from the invasion of infective larvae to the development of clinical manifestations is usually 8 to 16 months.
	
	---
	
	**2. PREVENTION AND CONTROL MEASURES**
	
	The current strategy for filariasis control requires an integrated approach, principally relying on chemotherapy, vector control, and morbidity management.
	
	**A. Chemotherapy**
	
	- **1. Mass Drug Administration (MDA) / Preventive Chemotherapy:**
	    - **Strategy:** The administration of a single annual dose of antifilarial medicines to the entire at-risk population for 5 to 6 years to interrupt transmission.
	    - **Regimen:** Co-administration of Diethylcarbamazine (DEC) at 6 mg/kg + Albendazole (400 mg) OR Ivermectin (150-200 mcg/kg) + Albendazole (400 mg). A triple-drug therapy (IDA: Ivermectin + DEC + Albendazole) is also being used in specific regions.
	    - **Exclusions:** Pregnant women, children below 2 years of age, and seriously ill persons must NOT be given these drugs.
	- **2. Selective Treatment:**
	    - DEC is given only to individuals whose blood is positive for Mf. The recommended full treatment dose for Bancroftian filariasis is 6 mg/kg body weight daily for 12 days.
	- **3. DEC-Medicated Salt:**
	    - A special form of mass treatment where 1 to 4 grams of DEC is added per kg of common cooking salt. It is safe, cheap, and effective when used for at least 6 to 9 months.
	
	**B. Vector Control**
	
	- **1. Anti-larval Measures (Crucial for Urban Areas):**
	    - _Source Reduction:_ Elimination of breeding places by minor environmental engineering (filling ditches, draining stagnant water, and proper maintenance of septic tanks).
	    - _Chemical Control:_ Application of organophosphorus larvicides like Temephos (Abate) or Fenthion weekly to breeding sites. Mosquito larvicidal oil and Pyrosene oil-E are also used.
	    - _Plant Removal:_ For _Mansonia_ mosquitoes, breeding is best controlled by destroying or removing aquatic vegetation (e.g., _Pistia_) manually or via herbicides.
	- **2. Anti-adult Measures:**
	    - Due to vector resistance against DDT and HCH, indoor residual spraying is generally not recommended for _Culex_ control. Pyrethrum space sprays (fogging) can be used for temporary reduction.
	- **3. Personal Prophylaxis:**
	    - Reduction of man-mosquito contact using mosquito nets (especially insecticide-treated nets), window screening, and insect repellents.
	
	**C. Morbidity Management and Disability Prevention (MMDP)**
	
	- **Lymphoedema Management:** Home-based foot care is highly effective. It involves washing and drying the affected limb, preventing and treating entry lesions (using antiseptic/antifungal creams), elevating the limb, and exercising to promote lymph drainage. Acute dermato-lymphangio-adenitis (ADLA) attacks are managed with analgesics, antipyretics, and antibiotics (like Amoxicillin or Penicillin).
	- **Hydrocele Management:** Individuals identified with scrotal swelling during community surveys must be referred to health facilities for surgical correction (hydrocelectomy).
	1. . Lymphatic filariasis-**Clinical Manifestation:**.**
	**CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS OF LYMPHATIC FILARIASIS**
	
	Only a small proportion of infected individuals exhibit overt clinical signs. The disease manifestations are broadly divided into two distinct clinical types: (a) typical lymphatic filariasis (caused by the parasite localizing in the lymphatic system) and (b) occult filariasis (caused by immune hyper-responsiveness of the host).
	
	**1. LYMPHATIC FILARIASIS (CLASSICAL)** The natural history of classical lymphatic filariasis is categorized into four distinct stages:
	
	- **Stage 1: Asymptomatic Amicrofilaraemia**
	    - A proportion of the population in endemic areas shows neither clinical manifestations nor detectable microfilariae (Mf) in the blood.
	    - With current diagnostic procedures, it is difficult to determine whether these persons have undetectable infections or are entirely free from infection.
	- **Stage 2: Asymptomatic Microfilaraemia**
	    - Patients remain completely asymptomatic (for months or even years) but have blood positive for circulating microfilariae.
	    - **High-Yield:** These carriers are a highly important source of infection in maintaining community transmission.
	- **Stage 3: Stage of Acute Manifestations**
	    - Occurs during the first months and years of infection, marked by recurrent episodes of acute inflammation of the lymph glands and vessels.
	    - **Clinical signs:** Filarial fever, lymphangitis, lymphadenitis, transient lymphoedema of various body parts, and/or epididymo-orchitis in males.
	- **Stage 4: Stage of Chronic Obstructive Lesions**
	    - Usually develops 10 to 15 years after the onset of the first acute attack.
	    - **Pathology:** Result of permanent structural changes due to fibrosis and obstruction of lymphatic vessels.
	    - **In Bancroftian filariasis (_W. bancrofti_):** The main clinical features are hydrocele, elephantiasis, and chyluria (though chyluria has a very low prevalence). Elephantiasis primarily affects the legs, scrotum, arms, penis, vulva, and breasts (in that order of decreasing frequency).
	    - **In Brugian filariasis (_B. malayi_):** Generally similar to Bancroftian filariasis, but a distinguishing feature is that the genitalia are rarely involved.
	
	**2. OCCULT (CRYPTIC) FILARIASIS**
	
	- **Definition:** Filarial infections where classical clinical manifestations are completely absent and microfilariae (Mf) are _not_ found in the peripheral blood.
	- **Pathogenesis:** It is believed to result from an immune hyper-responsiveness (hypersensitivity reaction) to filarial antigens derived from the microfilariae.
	- **Classical Example:** Tropical pulmonary eosinophilia.

1. Lymphatic filariasis- Night Blood Examination/Rationale:**  
	**LYMPHATIC FILARIASIS: NIGHT BLOOD EXAMINATION AND RATIONALE**
	
	**1. RATIONALE FOR NIGHT BLOOD EXAMINATION**
	
	- **Nocturnal Periodicity:** The microfilariae (Mf) of _Wuchereria bancrofti_ and _Brugia malayi_ occurring in India typically display a nocturnal periodicity.
	- **Timing of Appearance:** They appear in large numbers in the peripheral blood stream during the night and retreat from the circulation during the day. The maximum density of microfilariae in the blood is reported between **10:00 PM and 2:00 AM**.
	- **Biological Adaptation:** This phenomenon is a specific biological adaptation by the parasite to match the nocturnal (night-time) biting habits of the vector mosquitoes (such as _Culex quinquefasciatus_).
	- _High-Yield Note:_ If the sleeping habits of the human host are altered (e.g., a person working night shifts and sleeping during the day), a reversal in this periodicity has been observed.
	
	**2. SIGNIFICANCE AND INDICATIONS**
	
	- **Definitive Diagnosis:** The definitive diagnosis of lymphatic filariasis in a community relies heavily upon the demonstration of living parasites in the human body, which necessitates a night blood survey.
	- **Detection of Carriers:** A significant proportion of infected individuals remain asymptomatic despite having microfilariae circulating in their blood. Night blood examination is crucial to detect these "asymptomatic microfilaraemia" cases, as they serve as the primary reservoir and source of infection in the community.
	
	**3. PROCEDURE (THICK FILM METHOD)**
	
	- **Time of Collection:** Blood is optimally collected between **8:30 PM and 12:00 midnight**.
	- **Technique:** 20 cu. mm of capillary blood is collected by performing a deep finger prick.
	- **Smear Preparation:** A thick smear is prepared on a glass slide, allowed to dry, and serially numbered.
	- **Laboratory Processing:** On the following day, the thick blood films are dehaemoglobinized, stained, dried, and then systematically examined field-by-field under low power for the presence of microfilariae.
	
	**4. ALTERNATIVE: DAYTIME EXAMINATION (DEC PROVOCATION TEST)** In situations where night blood collection is not logistically feasible, a daytime alternative can be used:
	
	- **Mechanism:** Microfilariae can be artificially induced to appear in the peripheral blood during the daytime by administering a provocative dose of the drug Diethylcarbamazine (DEC).
	- **Dosage & Timing:** The patient is given **100 mg of DEC orally**. The microfilariae begin to reach their peak concentration in the blood within 15 minutes. The blood sample is ideally collected and examined **one hour** after the administration of the drug.
	1. **Filarial Indices 
	**FILARIAL INDICES (ASSESSMENT PARAMETERS)**
	
	The effect of a filariasis control programme and the endemicity of the disease in a community are assessed using three main categories of indices or parameters: Clinical, Parasitological, and Entomological.
	
	**1. CLINICAL PARAMETERS** These measure the burden of symptomatic disease in the community.
	
	- **Incidence of Acute Manifestations:** Measures the occurrence of acute episodes such as adeno-lymphangitis, epididymo-orchitis, etc.
	- **Prevalence of Chronic Manifestations:** Measures the burden of long-term sequelae such as lymphoedema, elephantiasis, hydrocele, and chyluria.
	
	**2. PARASITOLOGICAL PARAMETERS (HIGH-YIELD)** These are the core indices derived from blood surveys (usually 20 cu. mm of capillary night blood).
	
	- **Microfilaria Rate:** It is defined as the percentage of persons showing microfilariae (Mf) in their peripheral blood (20 cu. mm) in the sample population, with one slide taken from each person. The species of the parasite should be specified.
	- **Filarial Endemicity Rate:** It is the percentage of persons examined who show microfilariae in their blood, OR exhibit disease manifestations, OR both.
	- **Microfilarial Density:** It is the number of microfilariae per unit volume (20 cu. mm) of blood in samples from individual persons. This index specifically indicates the _intensity_ of the infection.
	- **Average Infestation Rate:** It is the average number of microfilariae per _positive_ slide, with each slide made from 20 cu. mm of blood. This indicates the prevalence of microfilaraemia within the infected population.
	
	**3. ENTOMOLOGICAL PARAMETERS** These indices help measure the progress of the control campaign against the insect vectors.
	
	- **Vector Density:** Measured as the vector density per 10 man-hour catch.
	- **Infection Rate in Mosquitoes:** The percentage of mosquitoes positive for _all_ developmental stages of the parasite.
	- **Infectivity Rate in Mosquitoes:** The percentage of mosquitoes positive specifically for the _infective (stage III)_ larvae.
	- **Annual Biting Rate:** Used for the assessment of disease transmission dynamics.
	- **Breeding Places:** Assessment of the types and extent of larval breeding places.
