1. Tetanus neonatorum—prevention
	
	**PREVENTION OF NEONATAL TETANUS (TETANUS NEONATORUM)**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION & EPIDEMIOLOGY**
	
	- **Definition:** Neonatal tetanus (tetanus neonatorum) is a highly fatal disease occurring in newborns, typically caused by infection of the umbilical stump following birth.
	- **Aetiology:** It occurs when infants are delivered in non-aseptic conditions, particularly when the umbilical cord is cut with unclean instruments or the stump is dressed with contaminated materials like ashes, soil, or cowdung.
	- **Clinical Presentation:** The first symptoms usually appear around the 7th day after birth, which is why it is commonly referred to as the **"8th day disease"**.
	- **Mortality:** In the absence of high-quality medical treatment, it is a killer disease with case-fatality rates reaching 80-90 per cent.
	
	**2. STRATEGIES FOR PREVENTION (HIGH-YIELD)** The prevention of neonatal tetanus relies on a three-pronged approach: active immunization of the mother, clean delivery practices, and passive immunization of at-risk infants.
	
	**A. Active Immunization of Pregnant Women** Tetanus toxoid given to the mother protects both the mother and the child by transferring maternal antibodies across the placenta.
	
	- **Unimmunized Pregnant Women:** Must receive **two doses** of tetanus toxoid.
	    - _1st Dose:_ As early as possible during pregnancy.
	    - _2nd Dose:_ At least one month after the first dose, and at least 3 weeks prior to the expected date of delivery.
	    - _Timing:_ Under the National Immunization Schedule, these doses are typically administered between 16 to 36 weeks of pregnancy.
	- **Previously Immunized Women:** If the woman was immunized in a previous pregnancy, a single **booster dose** of tetanus toxoid is considered sufficient for subsequent pregnancies to avoid hyperimmunization.
	- **Late Presentation ("Golden Rule"):** No pregnant mother should be denied vaccination even if she presents very late in pregnancy; she must be given at least one dose of tetanus toxoid.
	- **High-Risk Areas:** In regions with a high incidence of neonatal tetanus, the primary 2-dose course can be extended to all women of child-bearing age.
	
	**B. Clean Delivery Practices (The "Five Cleans")** In many developing countries, operational research has proven that training traditional birth attendants alone can reduce neonatal tetanus deaths by 90 per cent. Prevention emphasizes the strict observance of the **"Five Cleans"**:
	
	1. **Clean hands:** Thorough washing of hands.
	2. **Clean delivery surface:** Ensuring the surface where the delivery occurs is clean.
	3. **Clean blade:** Using a sterile or clean instrument to cut the umbilical cord.
	4. **Clean cord tie:** Using a clean tie for the umbilical cord.
	5. **Clean cord care:** Leaving the stump dry and strictly avoiding the application of harmful substances like soil or cowdung on the umbilical stump. _Disposable home delivery kits_ are provided to trained birth attendants to facilitate these practices.
	
	**C. Passive Immunization for "At-Risk" Infants**
	
	- **Indication:** Infants born to mothers who have not previously received the required two doses of tetanus toxoid are exposed to a high risk of neonatal tetanus.
	- **Prophylaxis:** These high-risk newborns can be temporarily protected by administering an injection of tetanus antitoxin (heterologous serum, **750 IU**).
	- **Timing:** To be effective, this passive immunization must be administered **within 6 hours of birth**.
	
	**3. MATERNAL AND NEONATAL TETANUS ELIMINATION (MNTE) PROGRAMME**
	
	- **Elimination Target:** MNTE is defined as reducing the incidence to **less than 1 case per 1000 live births** in every district.
	- **India's Achievement:** India was officially validated and certified as having achieved maternal and neonatal tetanus elimination in **May 2015**.
	- **Key Interventions under MNHM:**
	    - Promotion of institutional deliveries through schemes like the **Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)**, a conditional cash transfer scheme.
	    - Engagement of Accredited Social Health Activists (**ASHAs**) to generate demand for health services and track pregnancies.
	    - Strengthening facility-based newborn care by setting up newborn care corners and stabilization units.
1. Epidemiology of STD's
	**EPIDEMIOLOGY OF SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED DISEASES (STDs)**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION & PROBLEM STATEMENT**
	
	- **Definition & Shift:** STDs are a group of communicable diseases transmitted predominantly by sexual contact. The classification has shifted from the 5 classical "venereal diseases" (syphilis, gonorrhoea, chancroid, lymphogranuloma venereum, and donovanosis) to encompass over 20 pathogens, collectively known as sexually transmitted diseases or infections (STIs).
	- **Global Burden:** Over 1 million STIs are acquired every day among individuals aged 15-49 years, the majority being asymptomatic. Annually, there are an estimated 374 million new infections of chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis, and trichomoniasis combined.
	- **Indian Burden:** STDs are a major public health problem in India, though true incidence is obscured by inadequate reporting and social secrecy. In 2022, India reported 38,587 cases of syphilis and 30,250 cases of gonorrhoea. Chancroid is widely prevalent, Lymphogranuloma venereum (LGV) is more common in southern states, and Donovanosis is endemic in coastal areas.
	
	**2. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS**
	
	**A. Agent Factors (Causative Pathogens)** Over 20 distinct pathogens are spread by sexual contact.
	
	- **Bacterial Agents:** _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_, _Treponema pallidum_ (Syphilis), _Haemophilus ducreyi_ (Chancroid), _Chlamydia trachomatis_, and _Calymmatobacterium granulomatis_ (Donovanosis).
	- **Viral Agents:** Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), Hepatitis B virus, Herpes simplex virus (HSV-1 and HSV-2), Human papillomavirus (HPV), and Molluscum contagiosum.
	- **Protozoal Agents:** _Trichomonas vaginalis_, _Entamoeba histolytica_, and _Giardia lamblia_.
	- **Fungal Agents:** _Candida albicans_.
	- **Ectoparasites:** _Phthirus pubis_ (crab louse) and _Sarcoptes scabiei_ (itch mite).
	
	**B. Host Factors**
	
	- **Age:** The highest incidence rates are consistently observed in the 20-24 years age group, followed by the 25-29 and 15-19 years age groups. Additionally, serious morbidity is observed in the foetus and neonate due to vertical transmission.
	- **Sex:** While the overall morbidity rate is higher in men, the severity of the infection and its complications (such as pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility, and ectopic pregnancy) are much more severe in women.
	- **Marital Status:** Infection frequency is higher among single, divorced, and separated individuals compared to married couples.
	- **Socio-economic Status:** Individuals from the lowest socio-economic groups exhibit the highest morbidity rates.
	
	**C. Social and Behavioural Factors (High-Yield)** STDs are deeply rooted in human behaviour and societal structures.
	
	- **Prostitution:** A major driver of STD spread. Prostitutes act as a reservoir of infection, fundamentally supplying a demand created by "prostituants" (clients).
	- **Broken Homes:** Promiscuous individuals are often drawn from broken or unhappy homes (due to death or separation of parents), seeking affection or happiness elsewhere.
	- **Urbanization & Industrialization:** Long working hours, geographical mobility, and relative isolation from the family in urban setups foster casual sexual relationships.
	- **International Travel:** Travellers play a critical role in the rapid global transmission of STDs, exporting and importing infections like HIV and resistant strains of _N. gonorrhoeae_.
	- **Changing Behavioural Patterns:** Modern society has seen a relaxation of traditional moral values, independence, and a break away from traditional lifestyles, particularly among the youth.
	- **Social Stigma:** The intense stigma attached to STDs acts as a major barrier to control. It leads to non-detection, hiding of contacts, dropping out of treatment, and seeking help from quacks or self-treatment.
	- **Poverty & Easy Money:** In developing nations, prostitution is frequently a reflection of poverty and a lack of female employment opportunities, offering a source of "easy money".
	- **Social Disruption:** Wars, disasters, and civil unrest consistently lead to an increased spread of STDs.
	- **Alcoholism:** Operates indirectly by encouraging prostitution and reducing inhibitions.
	1. Syndromic management of vaginal discharge.
	**SYNDROMIC MANAGEMENT OF VAGINAL DISCHARGE**
	
	**1. CLINICAL HISTORY** A detailed history is essential to arrive at a probable diagnosis. The following must be elicited:
	
	- **Menstrual history:** To rule out pregnancy.
	- **Discharge characteristics:** Nature, amount, smell, colour, and consistency.
	- **Associated symptoms:** Genital itching, dysuria (burning while passing urine), increased frequency of micturition, low backache, and presence of any ulcer or swelling in the vulval or inguinal region.
	- **Partner history:** Presence of genital complaints in the sexual partner(s).
	
	**2. CAUSATIVE ORGANISMS** The syndromes are broadly divided based on the anatomical site of infection:
	
	- **Vaginitis:** _Trichomonas vaginalis_ (TV), _Candida albicans_, and organisms causing Bacterial Vaginosis (BV) such as _Gardnerella vaginalis_ and _Mycoplasma_.
	- **Cervicitis:** _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_, _Chlamydia trachomatis_, _Trichomonas vaginalis_, and Herpes simplex virus.
	
	**3. CLINICAL EXAMINATION** A per speculum examination is necessary to differentiate between vaginitis and cervicitis.
	
	- **Vaginitis signs:**
	    - Trichomoniasis: Greenish frothy discharge.
	    - Candidiasis: Curdy white discharge.
	    - Bacterial vaginosis: Adherent discharge.
	    - _Note:_ Mixed infections may present with an atypical discharge.
	- **Cervicitis signs:** Cervical erosion, cervical ulcer, or mucopurulent cervical discharge.
	- **Bimanual pelvic examination:** Done to rule out Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID).
	- _High-Yield Note:_ If a speculum examination is not possible or the client is hesitant, the patient must be treated for BOTH vaginitis and cervicitis.
	
	**4. PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT (TREATMENT PROTOCOL)** Patients must be instructed to avoid douching. If both vaginitis and cervicitis are present, treat for both.
	
	**A. Treatment for Vaginitis (TV + BV + Candida):**
	
	- **For TV & BV:** Tab. Secnidazole 2 gm orally, single dose OR Tab. Tinidazole 500 mg orally, twice daily for 5 days.
	    - Tab. Metoclopramide is taken 30 minutes before Secnidazole to prevent gastric intolerance.
	- **For Candidiasis:** Tab. Fluconazole 150 mg orally single dose OR local Clotrimazole 500 mg vaginal pessaries once.
	
	**B. Treatment for Cervicitis (Gonorrhea + Chlamydia):**
	
	- Tab. Cefixime 400 mg orally, single dose.
	- **PLUS** Tab. Azithromycin 1 gram orally, 1 hour before lunch. If vomiting occurs within 1 hour, give an anti-emetic and repeat the dose.
	
	**5. MANAGEMENT IN PREGNANT WOMEN** Pregnancy must be ruled out during the per speculum examination, as STIs can lead to complications like abortion and premature rupture of membranes.
	
	- **First Trimester:**
	    - Oral Fluconazole is strictly contraindicated in pregnancy.
	    - Use local treatment with Clotrimazole vaginal pessary/cream ONLY for candidiasis.
	    - Metronidazole pessaries or cream can be used intravaginally if trichomoniasis or BV is suspected.
	- **Second and Third Trimester:** Oral metronidazole can be safely given.
	
	**6. LABORATORY INVESTIGATIONS (If available)** Although syndromic management doesn't rely on labs, the following can confirm the diagnosis:
	
	- Wet mount microscopy of the discharge for _Trichomonas vaginalis_ and clue cells.
	- 10% KOH preparation for _Candida albicans_.
	- Gram's stain of vaginal smear to detect clue cells (indicative of Bacterial Vaginosis).
	- Gram's stain of endocervical smear to detect intracellular gonococci.
	
	**7. PARTNER MANAGEMENT & FOLLOW-UP**
	
	- **Partner treatment:** Treat the current partner ONLY if there is no improvement after the initial treatment. If the partner is symptomatic, treat both the client and the partner using the above protocols.
	- **Counselling:** Advise sexual abstinence during the course of the treatment. Provide condoms and educate the patient about their correct and consistent use.
	- **Follow-up:** Schedule a return visit for the patient after 7 days. Pregnancy, diabetes, and HIV should be considered as influencing factors if the patient presents with recurrent infections.
	1. Management of Genital Ulcers
	**SYNDROMIC MANAGEMENT OF GENITAL ULCERS**
	
	**1. CAUSATIVE ORGANISMS (HIGH-YIELD)** Genital ulcers are primarily caused by the following pathogens:
	
	- _Treponema pallidum_ (Syphilis).
	- _Haemophilus ducreyi_ (Chancroid).
	- _Klebsiella granulomatis_ (Granuloma inguinale / Donovanosis).
	- _Chlamydia trachomatis_ (Lymphogranuloma venereum).
	- Herpes simplex virus (Genital herpes).
	
	**2. CLINICAL HISTORY** A thorough history should elicit:
	
	- Burning sensation in the genital region.
	- Sexual exposure of either partner to high-risk practices, including oro-genital sex.
	- History of recurrent lesions.
	- Co-existence of HIV infection.
	
	**3. CLINICAL EXAMINATION & ULCER CHARACTERISTICS** Look for the presence of vesicles, single or multiple ulcers, and associated inguinal lymph node swelling.
	
	- **Herpes Simplex:** Painful vesicles or ulcers, single or multiple.
	- **Syphilis:** Painless ulcer with shotty lymph nodes.
	- **Chancroid:** Painful ulcer (usually single), sometimes associated with a painful bubo.
	- **Granuloma Inguinale & LGV:** Painless ulcer with inguinal lymph nodes.
	
	**4. PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT PROTOCOL (SYNDROMIC APPROACH)** Management is based on the visual characteristics of the lesions:
	
	**A. If Vesicles or Multiple Painful Ulcers are Present (Treat for Herpes):**
	
	- **Tab. Acyclovir:** 400 mg orally, three times a day for 7 days.
	
	**B. If Vesicles are NOT Seen and ONLY Ulcer is Seen (Treat for Syphilis + Chancroid):**
	
	- _Counsel the patient on herpes genitalis_.
	- **To cover Syphilis:** **Inj. Benzathine Penicillin** 2.4 million IU intramuscularly (IM) after a test dose, given in two divided doses (always keep emergency tray ready).
	    - _Alternative (if allergic to penicillin):_ Cap. Doxycycline 100 mg orally, twice daily for 14 days.
	- **To cover Chancroid (Add ONE of the following):**
	    - **Tab. Azithromycin:** 1 g orally, single dose.
	    - **Tab. Ciprofloxacin:** 500 mg orally, twice a day for 3 days.
	- _Note:_ Treatment should be extended beyond 7 days if the ulcers have not epithelialized (formed a new layer of skin).
	
	**5. MANAGEMENT IN PREGNANT WOMEN** Special precautions must be taken during pregnancy:
	
	- **Contraindicated Drugs:** Quinolones (ofloxacin, ciprofloxacin), doxycycline, and sulfonamides are strictly contraindicated in pregnant women.
	- **Syphilis Treatment:**
	    - Pregnant women testing positive for RPR should be considered infected unless adequate prior treatment is documented.
	    - Administer Inj. Benzathine penicillin 2.4 million IU IM (after test dose). A second dose is administered 1 week later for primary, secondary, or early latent syphilis.
	    - If allergic to penicillin: Tab. Erythromycin base or ethyl succinate 500 mg orally four times a day for 15 days. _(Erythromycin estolate is contraindicated due to hepatotoxicity)_. The neonate must be treated for syphilis after delivery.
	- **Herpes Management:**
	    - Carefully examine for herpetic lesions. Oral acyclovir may be given for first-episode or severe recurrent herpes.
	    - Women with genital herpetic lesions at the onset of labour must be delivered by caesarean section to prevent neonatal herpes.
	
	**6. PARTNER MANAGEMENT & FOLLOW-UP**
	
	- **Contact Tracing:** Treat all partners who have been in sexual contact with the patient in the last 3 months for both syphilis and chancroid.
	- **Counselling:** Advise strict sexual abstinence during the course of treatment.
	- **Condom Promotion:** Provide condoms and educate on their correct and consistent use.
	- **Screening:** Refer the patient for voluntary counselling and testing (VCT) for HIV, Syphilis (RPR test), and Hepatitis B.
	- **Follow-up:** Schedule a return visit after 7 days. If not responding to treatment, refer to a higher centre.
1. Prevention of parent to child transmission
	**PREVENTION OF PARENT-TO-CHILD TRANSMISSION (PPTCT) OF HIV**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION**
	
	- **Definition:** PPTCT refers to interventions aimed at preventing the transmission of HIV from an infected mother to her foetus or infant during pregnancy, labour, delivery, or breast-feeding.
	- **Transmission Risk:** In the absence of any intervention, the rate of mother-to-child transmission varies from 20 to 25 per cent.
	- **Goal:** The primary aim of the PPTCT programme is to offer HIV testing to every pregnant woman (universal coverage) to detect all HIV-positive pregnant women and completely eliminate the transmission of HIV from mother to child. The National Strategic Plan (2017-2024) set an objective to eliminate mother-to-child transmission of HIV and syphilis by 2020.
	
	**2. EVOLUTION OF PPTCT STRATEGY IN INDIA**
	
	- **2002:** Programme initiated using single-dose Nevirapine (SD-NVP) prophylaxis for the HIV-positive pregnant woman during labour and for the newborn immediately after birth.
	- **2012:** Transitioned to WHO's "Option B" involving multi-drug ARV prophylaxis.
	- **2013-14 Onwards:** Implementation of **Lifelong ART** (using a triple-drug regimen) for all pregnant and breast-feeding women living with HIV, regardless of their CD4 count or WHO clinical stage.
	
	**3. ESSENTIAL PACKAGE OF PPTCT SERVICES (HIGH-YIELD)** The comprehensive package of PPTCT services provided in India includes the following 11 core components:
	
	- **Routine Screening:** Routine offer of HIV counselling and testing to all pregnant women enrolled into antenatal care, utilizing an "opt-out" approach.
	- **Family-Centric Approach:** Ensuring the active involvement of the spouse and other family members, moving away from a strictly "ANC-Centric" model.
	- **Maternal ART:** Provision of life-long ART (TDF + 3TC + EFV) to all pregnant and breast-feeding HIV-infected women, irrespective of CD4 count and clinical stage of HIV progression.
	- **Safe Delivery:** Promotion of institutional deliveries for all HIV-infected pregnant women. Elective caesarean section before the onset of labour and rupture of membranes can prevent transmission, though it is not always a safe option in resource-poor settings.
	- **Associated Care:** Provision of care for associated conditions such as STI/RTI, Tuberculosis (TB), and other opportunistic infections.
	- **Nutritional & Psychosocial Support:** Provision of adequate nutrition, counselling, and psychosocial support for the HIV-infected pregnant women.
	- **Infant Feeding Practices:** Counselling and support for the initiation of exclusive breast-feeding within one hour of delivery as the preferred option, continued exclusively for 6 months.
	- **Infant ARV Prophylaxis:** Provision of ARV prophylaxis to infants from birth up to a minimum of 6 months.
	- **Routine Healthcare Integration:** Integrating the follow-up of HIV-exposed infants into routine healthcare services, including the national immunization schedule.
	- **Early Infant Diagnosis (EID) & CPT:** Ensuring the prompt initiation of Co-trimoxazole Prophylactic Therapy (CPT) and Early Infant Diagnosis (EID) using HIV-DNA PCR at **6 weeks of age**. Co-trimoxazole prophylaxis should be continued until HIV infection has been definitively excluded after the complete cessation of breast-feeding.
	- **Community Outreach:** Strengthening community follow-up and outreach through local community networks to support HIV-positive pregnant women and their families.
1. Clinical manifestation of HIV infection
	**CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS OF HIV INFECTION**
	
	The clinical features of HIV infection are broadly classified into four categories:
	
	**I. INITIAL INFECTION**
	
	- About 70 per cent of people experience a generally mild illness comprising fever, sore throat, and rash a few weeks after the initial infection with the virus.
	- Most infected people have no further symptoms for the first five years or so, though they are infectious right from the start.
	- **Window Period:** It usually takes between 2 to 12 weeks for HIV antibodies to appear in the bloodstream. During this window period, standard antibody blood tests will be negative, but the person is highly infectious due to a high concentration of the virus in the blood.
	
	**II. ASYMPTOMATIC CARRIER STATE**
	
	- Infected individuals test positive for HIV antibodies but show no overt clinical signs of disease.
	- The only possible clinical finding may be **persistent generalized lymphadenopathy**.
	- This asymptomatic carrier state can last for many years.
	
	**III. AIDS-RELATED COMPLEX (ARC)**
	
	- Patients present with illnesses caused by damage to the immune system, but without the specific opportunistic infections or cancers that define full-blown AIDS.
	- **Clinical signs include:**
	    - Unexplained diarrhoea lasting longer than a month.
	    - Fatigue, malaise, and night sweats.
	    - Loss of more than 10 per cent of body weight.
	    - Prolonged fever.
	    - Milder opportunistic infections such as oral thrush (oropharyngeal candidiasis).
	    - Generalized lymphadenopathy and an enlarged spleen.
	- Diagnosis of ARC is made when patients from high-risk groups have two or more of these manifestations (typically including generalized lymphadenopathy) accompanied by a decreased number of T-helper lymphocytes.
	
	**IV. AIDS (END-STAGE HIV INFECTION)**
	
	- This is the end-stage of HIV infection, characterized by severe opportunistic infections (OIs) and/or malignancies due to uncontrolled, unexplained defects in immunity.
	- **Major Clinical Syndromes & Opportunistic Infections:**
	    - **"Slim Disease" (HIV Wasting Syndrome):** Characterized by chronic diarrhoea and severe weight loss.
	    - **AIDS Encephalopathy (AIDS Dementia):** Caused by HIV crossing the blood-brain barrier, resulting in disabling mental or motor dysfunction resembling senile dementia.
	    - **Tuberculosis:** A highly common OI; HIV-positive individuals are 30-50 times more likely to develop active TB.
	    - **Kaposi Sarcoma:** A malignancy presenting with reddish-brown or purplish plaques or nodules on the skin and mucous membranes, seen relatively early in the disease.
	    - **Fungal Infections:** Oropharyngeal candidiasis (oral thrush), Candida oesophagitis, Cryptococcal meningitis (presenting with fever, headache, vomiting, and neck stiffness).
	    - **Parasitic Infections:** _Pneumocystis carinii_ pneumonia (dry cough, breathing difficulty, fever) and _Toxoplasma gondii_ encephalitis (focal neurological signs like hemiplegia or seizures).
	    - **Viral Infections:** Herpes zoster (shingles), chronic/progressive herpes simplex, and Cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis which can lead to blindness (usually seen when CD4 count is <50).
	    - **Hairy Leukoplakia:** White, corrugated patches on the sides of the tongue.
	
	---
	
	**WHO CLINICAL STAGING OF HIV DISEASE IN ADULTS AND ADOLESCENTS (HIGH-YIELD)**
	
	The WHO clinical staging system is based on clinical criteria and is used to determine prognosis and criteria for starting antiretroviral (ARV) therapy.
	
	- **Clinical Stage 1:**
	    - Asymptomatic.
	    - Persistent generalized lymphadenopathy.
	- **Clinical Stage 2:**
	    - Moderate unexplained weight loss (<10% of body weight).
	    - Recurrent respiratory tract infections (sinusitis, tonsillitis, otitis media, pharyngitis).
	    - Herpes zoster, angular cheilitis, recurrent oral ulcerations.
	    - Papular pruritic eruptions, seborrheic dermatitis, fungal nail infections.
	- **Clinical Stage 3:**
	    - Unexplained severe weight loss (>10% of body weight).
	    - Unexplained chronic diarrhoea for >1 month.
	    - Unexplained persistent fever for >1 month.
	    - Persistent oral candidiasis, oral hairy leukoplakia.
	    - Pulmonary tuberculosis.
	    - Severe bacterial infections (pneumonia, meningitis, empyema, bone/joint infection, severe PID).
	    - Acute necrotizing ulcerative stomatitis, gingivitis, or periodontitis.
	    - Unexplained anaemia (<8 g/dl), neutropenia (<0.5 x 10^9/l), and/or chronic thrombocytopenia (<50 x 10^9/l).
	- **Clinical Stage 4:**
	    - HIV wasting syndrome.
	    - _Pneumocystis jiroveci_ pneumonia, recurrent severe bacterial pneumonia.
	    - Extrapulmonary tuberculosis.
	    - Kaposi sarcoma.
	    - Chronic herpes simplex infection (>1 month duration or visceral).
	    - Oesophageal candidiasis.
	    - Cytomegalovirus disease (retinitis or infection of other organs).
	    - Central nervous system toxoplasmosis, HIV encephalopathy, extrapulmonary cryptococcosis.
	    - Lymphoma (cerebral or B cell non-Hodgkin) and invasive cervical carcinoma.
1. Human AIDS antiretroviral therapy
	**HUMAN AIDS ANTIRETROVIRAL THERAPY (ART)**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION & GOALS OF ART**
	
	- **Definition:** Antiretroviral therapy (ART) involves the use of a combination of at least three ARV drugs from different classes to inhibit HIV replication and reduce viraemia to undetectable levels.
	- **"Treat All" Policy:** WHO recommends lifelong ART for all people living with HIV (PLHIV), including children, adolescents, adults, pregnant, and breast-feeding women, regardless of clinical status or CD4 cell count.
	- **Goals of ART:**
	    - **Clinical:** Increase survival and improve quality of life.
	    - **Virological:** Achieve the greatest possible sustained reduction in viral load.
	    - **Immunological:** Achieve immune reconstitution (both quantitative and qualitative increases in CD4 count).
	    - **Therapeutic:** Rational sequencing of drugs to limit toxicity, maintain future treatment options, and facilitate adherence.
	    - **Preventive:** Reduce HIV transmission by suppressing the viral load.
	
	**2. CLASSIFICATION OF ANTIRETROVIRAL DRUGS** The drugs used for ART are broadly classified into the following classes:
	
	- **Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs):** Zidovudine (AZT), Lamivudine (3TC), Abacavir (ABC), Emtricitabine (FTC).
	- **Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NtRTIs):** Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF).
	- **Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NNRTIs):** Efavirenz (EFV), Nevirapine (NVP), Etravirine (ETV).
	- **Protease Inhibitors (PIs):** Lopinavir/ritonavir (LPV/r), Atazanavir + ritonavir (ATV/r), Darunavir + ritonavir (DRV/r).
	- **Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs):** Dolutegravir (DTG), Raltegravir (RAL).
	- **Fusion Inhibitors:** Enfuvirtide (T-20).
	
	**3. PRINCIPLES OF ART INITIATION**
	
	- **Fixed-Dose Combinations (FDCs):** FDCs are preferred as they are easy to prescribe, reduce pill burden, and facilitate improved treatment adherence.
	- **Rapid ART Initiation:** Same-day or rapid ART initiation (within 7 days of HIV diagnosis) is recommended for all newly diagnosed PLHIV who are adequately prepared and have no clinical contraindications (e.g., active opportunistic infections).
	
	**4. FIRST-LINE ART REGIMENS (NACO GUIDELINES)** The basic principle for first-line ART in treatment-naive patients is to use a triple combination from two different classes (usually an NRTI backbone + one INSTI or NNRTI).
	
	**A. Adults and Adolescents (>10 years and >30 kg)**
	
	- **Preferred Regimen:** **TLD Regimen** – Tenofovir (TDF 300 mg) + Lamivudine (3TC 300 mg) + Dolutegravir (DTG 50 mg).
	    - _Administration:_ Given as a Fixed-Dose Combination (FDC) single pill once a day, preferably at bedtime.
	- **Alternative Regimen:** Tenofovir (300 mg) + Lamivudine (300 mg) + Efavirenz (600 mg) is used if DTG is not opted or suitable.
	
	**B. Paediatric Regimens (Infants and Children)** The choice of drugs strictly depends on the child's age and body weight:
	
	- **< 6 years AND < 20 kg:** Abacavir (ABC) + Lamivudine (3TC) + Lopinavir/ritonavir (LPV/r).
	- **6 to 10 years AND 20-30 kg:** Abacavir (ABC) + Lamivudine (3TC) + Dolutegravir (DTG) (ALD regimen).
	- **> 10 years AND > 30 kg:** Tenofovir (TDF) + Lamivudine (3TC) + Dolutegravir (DTG) (TLD regimen).
	
	**C. Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (PPTCT)**
	
	- **Strategy:** Lifelong ART is initiated for all pregnant and breast-feeding HIV-infected women, regardless of CD4 count and clinical stage.
	- **Regimen:** The recommended triple-drug regimen is Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Efavirenz (TDF + 3TC + EFV).
	
	**5. MONITORING OF PATIENTS ON ART** Follow-up is essential to track clinical progress, monitor well-being, and identify adverse drug reactions and toxicities.
	
	- **Clinical Monitoring:** Conducted at every visit. Includes weight/height measurement, screening for 4-symptom TB, assessing treatment adherence, and clinical T-staging.
	- **CD4 Count:** Must be done every 6 months. It can be discontinued if the CD4 count reaches > 350 cells/mm³ and the viral load is < 1000 copies/ml.
	- **Viral Load Testing:** Conducted at 6 months, 12 months, and then every 12 months thereafter.
	
	**6. OCCUPATIONAL POST-EXPOSURE PROPHYLAXIS (PEP)**
	
	- **Indications:** Recommended for healthcare personnel exposed to potentially infectious body fluids (blood, semen, CSF, synovial, pleural, pericardial, peritoneal, and amniotic fluids) via percutaneous injury (needle stick) or mucous membrane/non-intact skin contact.
	- **Timing:** Must be initiated as soon as possible (preferably within hours). Do not delay while awaiting source patient test results. It is generally less effective if started >72 hours after exposure.
	- **Preferred Regimens (Duration: 4 weeks):**
	    - Raltegravir (400 mg twice daily) + Tenofovir/Emtricitabine (300/200 mg once daily).
	    - **OR** Dolutegravir (50 mg once daily) + Tenofovir/Emtricitabine (300/200 mg once daily).
	    - _(Note: Lamivudine/3TC may be substituted for Emtricitabine)_.
2. Objectives of multidrug therapy in leprosy
	**OBJECTIVES OF MULTIDRUG THERAPY (MDT) IN LEPROSY**
	
	The main objectives of introducing multidrug chemotherapy for the treatment and control of leprosy are:
	
	- **To Interrupt Transmission:** To halt the spread of the infection within the community by sterilizing infectious patients as rapidly as possible using bactericidal drugs.
	- **To Prevent Deformities:** To ensure the early detection and adequate treatment of cases, which is crucial to prevent the development of permanent deformities and disabilities.
	- **To Prevent Drug Resistance:** To prevent the emergence of drug-resistant strains of _M. leprae_ (a significant drawback of the older dapsone monotherapy).
	
	**Additional Programmatic Advantages:** In addition to the primary clinical objectives, the implementation of MDT provides several operational benefits:
	
	- **Curtails Treatment Duration:** It considerably shortens the overall duration of treatment required to cure the disease.
	- **Improves Patient Compliance:** The shorter and more effective therapy leads to better patient acceptance and adherence.
	- **Cost-Effectiveness:** It provides a highly cost-effective approach to leprosy management.
	- **Decreases Workload:** The reduced duration of therapy significantly decreases the long-term workload on healthcare personnel and the health system.
3. Drugs used for paucibacillary and multibacillary leprosy in children.
	
	**DRUGS USED FOR LEPROSY IN CHILDREN (MULTIDRUG THERAPY - MDT)**
	
	**1. STANDARD REGIMEN FOR CHILDREN AGED 10–14 YEARS** Due to widespread primary dapsone resistance, both Multibacillary (MB) and Paucibacillary (PB) cases in children are now treated with the same 3-drug combination.
	
	**A. Multibacillary (MB) Leprosy**
	
	- **Rifampicin:** 450 mg once a month, given under supervision.
	- **Clofazimine:** 150 mg once a month, supervised; AND 50 mg every other day, self-administered.
	- **Dapsone:** 50 mg daily, self-administered.
	
	**B. Paucibacillary (PB) Leprosy**
	
	- **Rifampicin:** 450 mg once a month, given under supervision.
	- **Clofazimine:** 150 mg once a month, supervised; AND 50 mg every other day, self-administered.
	- **Dapsone:** 50 mg daily, self-administered. _(Note: Previously, only Rifampicin and Dapsone were used for PB cases)._
	
	**2. DOSAGE FOR CHILDREN UNDER 10 YEARS OF AGE**
	
	- Children under the age of 10 years should receive appropriately reduced doses of the above mentioned drugs.
	
	**3. DURATION OF TREATMENT**
	
	- **Multibacillary (MB) Leprosy:** 12 monthly blister packs to be completed within 18 months.
	- **Paucibacillary (PB) Leprosy:** 6 monthly blister packs to be completed within 9 months.
4. Syndromic management of urethral discharge.
	**SYNDROMIC MANAGEMENT OF URETHRAL DISCHARGE**
	
	**1. CAUSATIVE ORGANISMS** As dual infection is common, the syndromic treatment for urethral discharge is designed to adequately cover both of the primary pathogens:
	
	- _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_ (Gonorrhoea)
	- _Chlamydia trachomatis_ (Chlamydia)
	- _Trichomonas vaginalis_ (considered if symptoms persist after initial treatment).
	
	**2. PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT (FIRST-LINE TREATMENT)** Treatment must cover both gonococcal and chlamydial infections simultaneously for uncomplicated cases (disease limited to the anterior urethra and proctitis).
	
	- **Tab. Cefixime:** 400 mg orally, single dose.
	- **PLUS Tab. Azithromycin:** 1 gram orally, single dose, given under supervision.
	- _Alternative (if allergic to Azithromycin):_ Tab. Erythromycin 500 mg orally, four times a day for 7 days.
	
	**3. MANAGEMENT OF PERSISTENT SYMPTOMS** The patient is advised to return for follow-up after 7 days.
	
	- If discharge or only dysuria persists after 7 days (and treatment compliance was good/reinfection is unlikely), treat for _Trichomonas vaginalis_ using **Tab. Secnidazole 2 gm orally, single dose**.
	- If symptoms still persist after this, refer the patient to a higher centre as early as possible.
	
	**4. PARTNER MANAGEMENT** Notification and treatment of female partners of men with urethritis are of the highest priority to identify women at high risk of asymptomatic infections.
	
	- Examine and treat all recent partners.
	- Female partners should be treated for both gonorrhoea and chlamydia on the same lines, after ruling out pregnancy and allergies.
	
	**5. MANAGEMENT OF PREGNANT PARTNER** If the female partner is pregnant, she must be examined (per speculum and per vaginal) and treated with a safe regimen:
	
	- **Contraindicated Drugs:** Quinolones (e.g., ofloxacin, ciprofloxacin) and doxycycline are strictly contraindicated in pregnant women.
	- **For Gonorrhoea:** Cephalosporins are safe and effective. Give **Tab. Cefixime** 400 mg orally, single dose OR **Inj. Ceftriaxone** 125 mg intramuscularly.
	- **For Chlamydia:** Give **Tab. Erythromycin** 500 mg orally, four times a day for 7 days OR **Cap. Amoxicillin** 500 mg orally, three times a day for 7 days.
	
	**6. COUNSELLING AND ADVICE**
	
	- Advise strict sexual abstinence during the course of the treatment.
	- Provide condoms and educate the patient about their correct and consistent use.
	- Refer the patient for voluntary counselling and testing (VCT) for HIV, Syphilis, and Hepatitis B.
	- Schedule a return visit after 7 to 14 days to review test reports, verify clinical cure, or assess for treatment failure/reinfection.
	
	**7. LABORATORY INVESTIGATIONS (If available)** While syndromic management does not strictly rely on labs, if facilities are available, the following findings confirm the aetiology:
	
	- **Gonorrhoea:** Gram stain examination of the urethral smear showing gram-negative intracellular diplococci.
	- **Non-gonococcal urethritis (Chlamydia):** Presence of more than 5 neutrophils per oil immersion field (1000x) in the urethral smear, or more than 10 neutrophils per high power field in the sediment of the first void urine.
	1. Western blot test.
	**WESTERN BLOT TEST**
	
	**1. Purpose and Indication**
	*   The Western Blot test is primarily used as a **confirmatory test** for HIV infection.
	*   To ensure diagnostic accuracy, it is applied after an initial sensitive screening test (such as ELISA) to weed out any false-positive results. 
	
	**2. Principle of the Test**
	*   It is a highly specific test that works by detecting specific antibodies directed against key viral proteins.
	*   Specifically, it detects antibodies to the **viral core protein (p24)** and the **envelope glycoprotein (gp 41)**.
	
	**3. Diagnostic Value (Specificity)**
	*   When the Western Blot is combined with the ELISA test, the overall specificity for HIV diagnosis exceeds **99.99%**.
	
	**4. Limitations**
	*   It is technically a more difficult test to perform compared to simple rapid screening kits.
	*   It strictly requires trained and experienced laboratory workers to accurately interpret the test results.
	
	**5. Causes of "Indeterminate" Results (High-Yield)**
	An indeterminate Western blot result can occur under certain clinical conditions, which include:
	*   Early HIV infection
	*   HIV-2 infection
	*   Autoimmune diseases
	*   Pregnancy
	*   Recent administration of the tetanus toxoid vaccine
