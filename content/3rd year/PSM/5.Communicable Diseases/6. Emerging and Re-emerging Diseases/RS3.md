1. Emerging diseases
	**EMERGING INFECTIOUS DISEASES**
	
	**1. DEFINITION**
	
	- Emerging infectious diseases are defined as those whose incidence in humans has increased during the last two decades or which threaten to increase in the near future.
	- The term also encompasses newly-appearing infectious diseases, or known diseases that are rapidly spreading to new geographical areas (e.g., cholera in South America or yellow fever in Kenya).
	
	**2. FACTORS RESPONSIBLE FOR EMERGENCE (HIGH-YIELD)** The emergence of these diseases is driven by a complex interaction of several factors:
	
	1. Unplanned and under-planned urbanization.
	2. Overcrowding and rapid population growth.
	3. Poor sanitation.
	4. Inadequate public health infrastructure.
	5. Resistance to antibiotics.
	6. Increased exposure of humans to disease vectors and natural reservoirs of infection.
	7. Rapid and intense international travel.
	8. Microbial genetic mutation.
	
	**3. IMPORTANT EXAMPLES OF EMERGING DISEASES**
	
	- **COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2):** A completely new disease that emerged in late 2019 and was declared a global pandemic by the WHO on 11th March, 2020.
	- **Human Monkeypox (Mpox):** Gained global attention in May 2022 when multiple cases were reported from several non-endemic countries.
	- **Tomato Fever (Tomato Flu):** A viral infection that emerged in Kerala, India, in May 2022, primarily affecting children younger than 5 years of age. It is characterized by the eruption of red and painful blisters throughout the body.
	- **HIV/AIDS:** The existence of the Human Immunodeficiency Virus was unknown until 1983; it has since become one of the most serious global pandemics.
	- **Ebola Virus Disease:** A highly fatal haemorrhagic fever that first appeared in Zaire and Sudan in 1976 and is transmitted through contact with infected animals (e.g., fruit bats, chimpanzees) and human-to-human transmission via body fluids.
	- **Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome:** First recognized in the USA in 1993; characterized by respiratory failure with a high case fatality rate and carried by rodents, particularly deer mice.
	- **Escherichia coli O157:H7:** First reported in 1982, this strain causes severe diarrhoeal illness and sometimes kidney failure; it is closely linked to undercooked beef and unpasteurized milk.
	- **Vibrio cholerae O139:** A completely new strain of cholera that appeared in south-eastern India in 1992 and subsequently spread to other parts of Asia,.
	- **Avian Influenza (Bird Flu - H5N1):** A strain with severe pandemic potential that first appeared in humans in Hong Kong in 1997,.
	- **SARS (Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome):** Caused by a novel coronavirus, first reported from China in late 2002, with rapid international spread.
	- **Pandemic Influenza A (H1N1) 2009 (Swine Flu):** A novel influenza strain that caused a major pandemic in 2009.
	
	**4. TIMELINE OF OTHER NOTABLE EMERGING AGENTS**
	
	- **1973:** Rotavirus (major cause of infantile diarrhoea),.
	- **1976:** Cryptosporidium parvum (acute and chronic diarrhoea),.
	- **1977:** Legionella pneumophila (Legionnaires' disease),.
	- **1981:** Toxin-producing Staphylococcus aureus (Toxic shock syndrome),.
	- **1989:** Hepatitis C virus,.
	- **1990:** Hepatitis E virus,.
	- **1999:** Nipah virus,.
	
	**5. PREVENTION AND CONTROL STRATEGIES (PUBLIC HEALTH RESPONSE)** Controlling emerging diseases requires robust national and international political will, resources, and coordination. The key steps include:
	
	- **Early Recognition and Rapid Response:** The first line of defence relies on early detection, confirmation, and immediate communication,.
	- **Epidemiological Surveillance:** Strengthening disease surveillance and drug resistance surveillance mechanisms, backed by appropriate laboratory support.
	- **Investigation:** Immediate epidemiological investigations to thoroughly understand the source and mode of transmission.
	- **Implementation of Interventions:** Applying cost-effective control strategies such as early diagnosis, prompt treatment, and vector control measures.
	- **Research & Development:** Expanding research into disease agents, vectors, and developing new tools, vaccines, and adequate drugs,.
	1. Ebola fever
	**EBOLA VIRUS DISEASE (EBOLA HAEMORRHAGIC FEVER)**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION & PROBLEM STATEMENT**
	*   **Definition:** Ebola virus disease (previously known as Ebola haemorrhagic fever) is a severe, highly fatal emerging infectious disease classified under viral haemorrhagic fevers.
	*   **History:** It first appeared in 1976 in Zaire and Sudan, occurring periodically since then.
	*   **Recent Outbreaks:** A major epidemic started in December 2013 in Guinea and spread to South Africa. By April 2015, over 25,515 cases and 10,000 deaths were reported.
	*   **Mortality:** It is highly lethal, with a case fatality rate that can be as high as 70 per cent.
	
	**2. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS**
	**A. Agent Factors**
	*   **Agent:** It is caused by an RNA virus belonging to the *Filoviridae* family.
	*   **Species:** There are 5 distinct species of the virus: *Zaire ebolavirus, Reston ebolavirus, Sudan ebolavirus, Tai ebolavirus,* and *Bundibugyo ebolavirus*.
	
	**B. Host and Reservoir Factors**
	*   **Animal Reservoir:** The virus is maintained in nature in animals like chimpanzees, gorillas, monkeys, and fruit bats.
	*   **Infectivity:** Asymptomatic cases are not infective. The person is also not infective during the incubation period.
	
	**3. MODES OF TRANSMISSION**
	*   **Animal-to-Human Transmission:** Occurs through direct contact with the blood, organs, body secretions, or other body fluids of infected animals (fruit bats, chimpanzees, monkeys, etc.).
	*   **Human-to-Human Transmission:** Spreads strictly through direct contact with the blood or body fluids of an infected *symptomatic* person.
	*   **Fomite Transmission:** Exposure to objects, such as contaminated needles, that have been soiled with infected secretions.
	*   **High-Yield Note:** Ebola is *not* transmitted through the air, water, or food.
	
	**4. INCUBATION PERIOD**
	*   Ranges from **2 to 21 days**. 
	
	**5. CLINICAL FEATURES**
	The clinical presentation progresses rapidly and includes:
	*   **Initial Symptoms:** Sudden onset of fever, intense weakness, muscle pain, headache, and sore throat.
	*   **Progression:** Followed by vomiting, diarrhoea, and a rash.
	*   **Organ Involvement:** Impaired kidney and liver functions.
	*   **Haemorrhagic Signs:** In some cases, patients develop both internal and external bleeding.
	
	**6. TREATMENT, PREVENTION, AND CONTROL**
	*   **Treatment:** Currently, there is no specific curative treatment available for this disease. 
	*   **Supportive Care:** Intensive supportive care is the mainstay of management and can significantly reduce mortality.
	*   **Prevention:** Spread of the disease is prevented primarily by instituting strict and specific infection control measures (e.g., barrier nursing, proper handling of blood/secretions, and safe disposal of needles).
	*   **Vaccines:** According to the provided epidemiological data, there is no vaccine currently available against Ebola.
	*   **Public Health Response:** Because it represents a frightening "new disease - new problem" scenario, there is a critical global need to expand research into the disease agent, vectors, and the rapid development of drugs and vaccines.
1. Preventive measures in ebola virus, control of ebola disease
	
	**1. PREVENTIVE MEASURES IN EBOLA VIRUS**
	
	Ebola virus disease (Ebola haemorrhagic fever) is a severe, highly fatal emerging disease. Prevention primarily focuses on breaking the chain of transmission, as the virus is highly infectious through direct contact.
	
	- **Preventing Animal-to-Human Transmission:** Strict avoidance of direct contact with the blood, organs, body secretions, or other body fluids of infected animals (such as chimpanzees, gorillas, monkeys, and fruit bats).
	- **Preventing Human-to-Human Transmission:** Transmission must be prevented by avoiding direct contact with the blood or body fluids of an infected, _symptomatic_ person. _(High-Yield Note: The patient is NOT infective during the 2 to 21-day incubation period, and asymptomatic cases are also not infective)._
	- **Preventing Fomite Transmission:** Strict avoidance of exposure to objects (such as contaminated needles and syringes) that have been soiled with infected secretions.
	- **Mode of Spread Awareness:** It is important for preventive education to note that Ebola is _not_ transmitted through the air, water, or food.
	- **Vaccination Status:** According to the provided epidemiological data, there is currently no vaccine available against Ebola.
	
	**2. CONTROL OF EBOLA DISEASE**
	
	The control of an Ebola outbreak relies on rapid identification, strict infection control, and aggressive supportive care.
	
	- **Specific Infection Control Measures:** The alacrity of institutional infection control is paramount. The spread of the disease can be effectively prevented by instituting specific and strict infection control measures (e.g., barrier nursing, safe handling of blood/secretions, and safe disposal of needles).
	- **Clinical Management (Case Control):** Currently, there is no specific curative treatment available for this disease. The cornerstone of patient control and management is **intensive supportive care**, which has been shown to significantly reduce mortality.
	- **Epidemic Response & Investigation:** A swift public health response is critical and encompasses:
	    - Early recognition and diagnosis of the disease.
	    - Immediate epidemiological investigation to thoroughly understand the source and mode of transmission.
	    - Implementation of targeted control strategies and containment programmes.
	- **Long-Term Control & Research:** Because Ebola represents a severe "new disease - new problem" category, long-term control necessitates expanding research on the infectious agent, its evolution, and the urgent development, production, and distribution of effective drugs and vaccines.
