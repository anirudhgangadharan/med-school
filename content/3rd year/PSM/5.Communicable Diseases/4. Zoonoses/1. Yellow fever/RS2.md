1. Measures to prevent entry of yellow fever into India
	1. **MEASURES TO PREVENT THE ENTRY OF YELLOW FEVER INTO INDIA**
	
	**1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL CONTEXT: INDIA AS A "RECEPTIVE" AREA**
	
	- India is classified as a yellow fever **"receptive" area**. This means the disease does not currently exist in India, but the conditions are highly favourable for its transmission if introduced.
	- **Favourable conditions:** The Indian population is unvaccinated and highly susceptible, the primary vector (_Aedes aegypti_) is abundantly present, the climate is conducive, and the common Indian monkey (_Macacus spp._) is susceptible to the virus.
	- **Modes of Importation:** The virus could enter India through (i) infected travellers (either clinical or subclinical cases), or (ii) infected mosquitoes.
	
	**2. INTERNATIONAL MEASURES (Strict Aerial & Maritime Regulations)** To prevent the entry of the disease, the Government of India implements stringent regulations based on the World Health Organization's (WHO) International Health Regulations (IHR). These measures are primarily divided into two categories:
	
	**A. Measures for Travellers**
	
	- **Valid International Certificate of Vaccination:** All travellers, including infants aged 9 months and above, who have been exposed to the risk of yellow fever or are passing through endemic zones (in Africa or Latin America) must possess a valid international certificate of vaccination against yellow fever before entering India.
	- **Quarantine for Defaulters:** If a traveller arrives from an infected area without a valid certificate, they are compulsorily isolated in a **mosquito-proof quarantine ward for 6 days** from the date of leaving the infected area, or until their vaccination certificate becomes valid.
	- **Validity of Certificate:** The certificate becomes valid **10 days after the date of vaccination**. As per the 2016 IHR amendment, the validity of the yellow fever vaccination certificate is now for the **lifetime** of the vaccinated person (previously it was valid for 10 years); revaccination or a booster dose is no longer required for international travellers.
	
	**B. Measures for Mosquitoes (Vector Control)**
	
	- **Disinsection of Aircraft and Ships:** All aircraft and ships arriving from yellow fever endemic areas are subjected to mandatory aerosol spraying with prescribed insecticides upon arrival to destroy any potentially infected mosquitoes.
	- **Perimeter Sanitation at Ports:** All international airports and seaports must be kept strictly free from the breeding of insect vectors over an area extending at least **400 metres** around their perimeters.
	- **Aedes Aegypti Index:** The _Aedes aegypti_ index (the percentage of houses/premises showing actual breeding of _Aedes aegypti_ larvae) is strictly monitored and must be kept **below 1** (effectively zero) at all international seaports and airports.
	
	**3. THE VACCINE USED (17D Vaccine)**
	
	- **Strain:** A live attenuated vaccine prepared from the 17D strain, grown in chick embryos and freeze-dried.
	- **Dose & Route:** Administered as a single dose of 0.5 ml subcutaneously.
	- **Immunity:** Immunity begins to appear on the 7th day and lasts for life.
	- _(Note: The vaccination must be given at an officially designated centre, and the certificate must be validated with the official stamp of the Ministry of Health, Government of India)._
1. Vaccination against yellow fever and International certificate of vaccination.
	**VACCINATION AGAINST YELLOW FEVER**
	
	The most effective control strategy for yellow fever, especially in urban areas and for international travellers, is rapid active immunization of the population at risk.
	
	**1. Type of Vaccine and Strain**
	
	- **Vaccine:** The approved vaccine for international use is the **17D vaccine**.
	- **Nature:** It is a live attenuated viral vaccine prepared from a non-virulent strain (17D strain).
	- **Preparation:** It is grown in chick embryos and subsequently freeze-dried (lyophilized).
	
	**2. Dosage and Administration**
	
	- **Dose:** A single dose of **0.5 ml**, irrespective of age.
	- **Route:** Administered subcutaneously at the insertion of the deltoid muscle.
	- **Immunity:** Protective immunity begins to appear on the **7th day** after vaccination and lasts possibly for life.
	
	**3. Storage and Reconstitution (Cold Chain)**
	
	- **Storage:** The lyophilized vaccine is highly heat-sensitive and must be stored between **+5°C and -30°C** (preferably below 0°C) until reconstitution.
	- **Reconstitution:** It must be reconstituted with the sterile, cold physiological saline diluent provided.
	- **Usage:** Once reconstituted, it must be kept on ice, away from sunlight, and must be strictly **discarded if not used within half an hour**.
	
	**4. Contraindications** The vaccine is generally contraindicated in the following high-risk groups:
	
	- Children under **9 months** of age for routine immunization (or under 6 months during an epidemic).
	- **Pregnant women** (except during an outbreak when the risk of acquiring the infection is high).
	- Individuals with a severe allergy to **egg protein**.
	- Individuals with severe immunodeficiency (e.g., symptomatic HIV/AIDS) or those with a thymus disorder.
	
	**5. Adverse Reactions and Interactions**
	
	- **Adverse Effects:** Mild reactions like myalgia, headache, and low-grade fever occur in 2-5% of vaccinees 5 to 10 days after vaccination. Anaphylaxis is extremely rare and usually occurs in egg-allergic individuals.
	- **Drug Interaction:** Yellow fever and Cholera vaccines interfere with each other if given concurrently. Therefore, they should be administered at least **3 weeks apart**.

	**INTERNATIONAL CERTIFICATE OF VACCINATION**
	
	Because India is a yellow fever "receptive" area (favourable conditions for transmission exist, but the virus is absent), the Government enforces strict quarantine and vaccination regulations based on the World Health Organization's International Health Regulations (IHR).
	
	**1. General Regulations for Travellers**
	
	- All travellers, including infants aged **9 months and above**, coming from or passing through endemic zones (in Africa or Latin America) must possess a valid International Certificate of Vaccination against yellow fever before entering India.
	- India requires this certificate even if the traveller was only in transit through an infected area.
	
	**2. Validity of the Certificate**
	
	- **Commencement:** The certificate becomes valid **10 days** after the date of vaccination.
	- **Duration (High-Yield):** As per the 2014 amendment to the IHR (which entered into force on 11th July 2016), the validity of the certificate has been changed from 10 years to the **lifetime of the person vaccinated**. Revaccination or a booster dose is no longer required for international travel.
	
	**3. Quarantine for Defaulters**
	
	- If a traveller arrives without a valid certificate, they are compulsorily isolated in a **mosquito-proof quarantine ward for 6 days** from the date of leaving the infected area, or until their vaccination certificate becomes valid.
	
	**4. Official Requirements**
	
	- For the purpose of international travel, the vaccination must be administered at an **officially designated centre**.
	- The certificate is valid only if it conforms to the IHR prescribed model and is validated with the official stamp of the Ministry of Health, Government of India.
