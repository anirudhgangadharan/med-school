1. What is antigenic shift and antigenic drift? (J03)
	**ANTIGENIC SHIFT AND ANTIGENIC DRIFT**
	
	The influenza A virus is unique among viruses because its surface antigens (Haemagglutinin and Neuraminidase) are frequently subject to structural variations. These antigenic variations are of two types: Antigenic Shift (major variation) and Antigenic Drift (minor variation).
	
	Here is the high-yield breakdown for exam purposes:
	
	**1. ANTIGENIC SHIFT (Major Change)**
	
	- **Definition:** It is a sudden, complete, or major change in the antigenic structure of the influenza virus.
	- **Mechanism:** It appears to result from the genetic recombination of a human influenza virus with an animal or avian (bird) influenza virus.
	- **Epidemiological Impact:** Because this genetic recombination creates a completely new strain of the virus, the human population lacks pre-existing immunity to it. Consequently, antigenic shift can cause a major epidemic or a global pandemic involving most or all age groups.
	- **Viral Subtype:** Antigenic shift is a characteristic feature unique to the **Influenza A** virus.
	
	**2. ANTIGENIC DRIFT (Minor Change)**
	
	- **Definition:** It is a gradual and minor antigenic change in the virus that occurs continuously over a period of time.
	- **Mechanism:** It involves "point mutations" in the viral gene. This occurs due to the continuous selection pressure exerted by the natural immunity present in the host population.
	- **Epidemiological Impact:** Because the changes are gradual and minor, the population usually retains some partial immunity. Therefore, it typically leads to periodic or seasonal epidemics rather than massive global pandemics.
	- **Viral Subtype:** Antigenic drift occurs in both **Influenza A** and **Influenza B** viruses, although it occurs to a lesser degree in the B group.
	
	_(Note: Influenza C virus does not exhibit these variations and appears to be antigenically stable.)_

2. Prevention of influenza.
	**PREVENTION OF INFLUENZA**
	
	The prevention of influenza relies on a multi-pronged approach comprising active immunization (vaccination), chemoprophylaxis, strict infection control measures, and epidemiological surveillance.
	
	**1. VACCINATION (ACTIVE IMMUNIZATION)** Vaccination is the most effective strategy to prevent influenza. The WHO reviews vaccine composition annually and updates it based on circulating virus strains. Vaccines are produced using three technologies: egg-based, cell-cultured, and recombinant (egg-free).
	
	**A. Inactivated Influenza Vaccine (IIV)**
	
	- **Composition:** Available as Trivalent (contains type A H1N1, A H3N2, and type B) and Quadrivalent (contains an additional B strain for broader protection).
	- **Route and Dose:** Administered via intramuscular (IM) or intradermal routes. The adult dose is 0.5 ml, and the paediatric dose is 0.25 ml.
	- **Schedule:**
	    - One dose annually for persons 9 years of age and older.
	    - Children aged 6 months through 8 years receiving it for the first time require two doses, spaced at least 28 days apart.
	- **Efficacy & Duration:** Becomes effective 14 days after vaccination and provides immunity lasting for 6–12 months. It is 50-60% effective in preventing hospitalization and 80% effective in preventing death among the elderly.
	- **Special Formulations:** High-dose IIV and Adjuvanted IIV are specifically recommended for elderly individuals (>65 years) to overcome waning immune responses.
	- **Contraindications:** Severe egg allergy, history of anaphylaxis to vaccine components, Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS) within 6 weeks of previous flu vaccine, infants <6 months, and active moderate-to-severe illness.
	
	**B. Live Attenuated Influenza Vaccine (LAIV)**
	
	- **Composition:** Contains cold-adapted live attenuated viruses that replicate in the nasopharyngeal mucosa. It lacks preservatives like thimerosal.
	- **Route:** Administered as a single-dose nasal spray (half dose in each nostril).
	- **Indications:** Approved only for healthy, non-pregnant persons aged 2 to 49 years.
	- **Contraindications:** Children <2 years or adults >50 years, pregnant women, immunosuppressed individuals (including HIV), and persons with chronic diseases (asthma, diabetes, renal disorders, sickle cell disease). It is strictly contraindicated in children/adolescents on long-term aspirin therapy due to the risk of Reye syndrome.
	
	**C. Target Priority Groups for Vaccination (WHO)** Due to occasional vaccine shortages, the WHO recommends a strict priority order for vaccination:
	
	1. **Healthcare workers:** Top priority to protect health infrastructure and prevent nosocomial spread.
	2. **Pregnant women**.
	3. **Individuals >6 months of age with chronic medical conditions**.
	4. **Healthy young adults** (15–49 years).
	5. **Healthy children**.
	6. **Healthy adults** (49–65 years) and **elderly adults** (>65 years). _(Note: A minimum gap of 7 days must be maintained between administering a COVID-19 vaccine and an influenza vaccine.)_
	
	---
	
	**2. CHEMOPROPHYLAXIS** Antiviral drugs are used prophylactically to prevent clinical illness in highly exposed individuals.
	
	- **Drug of Choice:** Oseltamivir.
	- **Indications:** Recommended for healthcare personnel, close contacts of suspected, probable, or confirmed cases of pandemic influenza, and contacts suffering from chronic diseases.
	- **Duration:** Administered until 10 days after the last known exposure.
	- **Dosage:**
	    - Adults: 75 mg Once Daily (OD).
	    - Children: Weight-based dosing (<15 kg: 30 mg OD; 15-23 kg: 45 mg OD; 24-40 kg: 60 mg OD; >40 kg: 75 mg OD).
	
	---
	
	**3. INFECTION CONTROL PRECAUTIONS** Proper environmental and hospital infection control is crucial, as the virus spreads rapidly via droplets and fomites.
	
	- **Standard & Droplet Precautions:** Strict adherence to hand hygiene (soap and water or alcohol-based sanitizers) and respiratory etiquette (covering mouth and nose when sneezing/coughing).
	- **Use of Masks:** Symptomatic individuals should wear medical face masks in the community to limit viral shedding.
	- **Healthcare Worker Protection:** For high-risk, aerosol-generating procedures (e.g., intubation, bronchoscopy), personnel must use particulate respirators (N95, FFP2), eye protection, gowns, and gloves.
	- **Isolation:** Hospitalized influenza patients must be isolated for 7 days after the onset of illness, or 24 hours after the complete resolution of fever and respiratory symptoms, whichever duration is longer.
	
	---
	
	**4. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL SURVEILLANCE**
	
	- Continuous surveillance is necessary to monitor circulating virus strains and detect genetic mutations (antigenic shifts/drifts).
	- Surveillance data directly dictates the specific viral strains to be included in the annual influenza vaccine formulations.
	- It aids in estimating morbidity and mortality impacts and rapidly detecting sudden epidemic or pandemic outbreaks.
3. Influenza vaccine. (J10(RS2))
	**INFLUENZA VACCINE**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION**
	
	- **Purpose:** To prevent seasonal and pandemic influenza infections.
	- **Strain Selection:** The WHO reviews vaccine composition annually and updates it based on circulating virus strains.
	- **Manufacturing Technologies:** Currently produced using three technologies: egg-based, cell-cultured, and recombinant (completely egg-free).
	- **Types:** Broadly classified into Inactivated Influenza Vaccine (IIV) and Live Attenuated Influenza Vaccine (LAIV).
	
	**2. INACTIVATED INFLUENZA VACCINE (IIV)**
	
	- **Composition:**
	    - **Trivalent IIV:** Contains three inactivated viruses (Type A H1N1, Type A H3N2, and one Type B).
	    - **Quadrivalent IIV:** Contains the same as trivalent with the addition of a second Type B strain for broader protection.
	- **Dose & Route:** Administered via Intramuscular (IM) or Intradermal (ID) routes. Adult dose is 0.5 ml; paediatric dose is 0.25 ml.
	- **Schedule:**
	    - Routine: One dose annually for persons aged 9 years and older.
	    - Children (6 months through 8 years): If receiving for the first time, require two doses spaced at least 28 days apart.
	- **Efficacy & Duration:** Immunity develops 14 days post-vaccination and lasts for 6 to 12 months. It is 50–60% effective in preventing hospitalizations and 80% effective in preventing deaths among the elderly.
	- **Special Formulations for Elderly (>65 years):** High-dose IIV (HD-IIV) containing four times the HA-antigen, and Adjuvanted IIV (aIIV) are recommended to overcome waning immune responses.
	- **Contraindications:**
	    - Severe allergy to chicken eggs or vaccine components.
	    - History of severe reaction or anaphylaxis to a previous dose.
	    - History of Guillain-Barre Syndrome (GBS) within 6 weeks of a previous flu vaccine.
	    - Infants less than 6 months of age.
	    - Current moderate-to-severe illness (vaccination should be deferred).
	
	**3. LIVE ATTENUATED INFLUENZA VACCINE (LAIV)**
	
	- **Composition:** Contains cold-adapted live attenuated viruses that replicate effectively in the nasopharyngeal mucosa. Lacks preservatives like thimerosal.
	- **Route:** Administered as a single-dose nasal spray (half of the dose in each nostril).
	- **Indications:** Approved only for healthy, non-pregnant individuals between 2 to 49 years of age.
	- **Contraindications:**
	    - Children <2 years and adults ≥50 years.
	    - Pregnant women and immunosuppressed individuals (e.g., HIV).
	    - Individuals with chronic medical conditions (asthma, diabetes, renal disease, sickle cell disease).
	    - Children or adolescents on long-term aspirin therapy (due to the risk of Reye syndrome).
	    - History of GBS within 6 weeks of a previous dose or severe egg allergy.
	
	**4. WHO PRIORITY GROUPS FOR VACCINATION** Due to occasional vaccine shortages or unstoppable epidemic spread, the WHO recommends a strict priority order:
	
	1. **Healthcare Workers:** Absolute first priority to protect health infrastructure and prevent nosocomial spread.
	2. **Pregnant Women**.
	3. **Individuals >6 months with chronic medical conditions**.
	4. **Healthy young adults** (15–49 years).
	5. **Healthy children**.
	6. **Healthy adults** (49–65 years) and **Elderly adults** (>65 years).
	
	_(Note: A time difference of at least 7 days must be maintained between the administration of a COVID-19 vaccine and an influenza vaccine.)_
