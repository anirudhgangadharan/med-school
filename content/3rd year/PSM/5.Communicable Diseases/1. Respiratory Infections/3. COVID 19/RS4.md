	**1. CASE DEFINITIONS OF COVID-19**
	
	**A. Suspect Case of COVID-19** A patient is considered a suspect case if they meet any of the following criteria:
	
	2. A patient with an acute respiratory illness (fever and at least one sign/symptom of respiratory disease, e.g., cough, shortness of breath) **AND** a history of travel to or residence in a country/area/territory reporting local transmission of COVID-19 during the 14 days prior to symptom onset.
	3. A patient/health care worker with any acute respiratory illness **AND** having been in contact with a confirmed COVID-19 case in the last 14 days prior to the onset of symptoms.
	4. A patient with severe acute respiratory infection (fever and at least one sign/symptom of respiratory disease) requiring hospitalization, **AND** with no other aetiology that fully explains the clinical presentation.
	5. A case for whom testing for COVID-19 is inconclusive.
	
	**B. Confirmed Case of COVID-19** A person with laboratory confirmation of COVID-19 infection, irrespective of clinical signs and symptoms.

	**2. CLASSIFICATION OF COVID-19 ON THE BASIS OF SEVERITY**
	
	COVID-19 is clinically classified into four major categories based on disease severity:
	
	**I. Mild Disease**
	
	- Symptomatic patients meeting the case definition for COVID-19 but without any evidence of viral pneumonia or hypoxia.
	
	**II. Moderate Disease (Pneumonia)**
	
	- **Adolescent/Adult:** Clinical signs of pneumonia (fever, cough, dyspnoea, fast breathing) but no signs of severe pneumonia, including SpO2 ≥ 90% on room air.
	- **Child:** Clinical signs of non-severe pneumonia (cough or difficulty breathing + fast breathing and/or chest indrawing) and no signs of severe pneumonia. Fast breathing is defined as: >60 breaths/min (<2 months); >50 breaths/min (2-11 months); >40 breaths/min (1-5 years).
	
	**III. Severe Disease (Severe Pneumonia)**
	
	- **Adolescent/Adult:** Clinical signs of pneumonia plus one of the following: respiratory rate > 30 breaths/min, severe respiratory distress, or SpO2 < 90% on room air.
	- **Child:** Clinical signs of pneumonia plus at least one of the following: central cyanosis or SpO2 < 90%, severe respiratory distress (grunting, very severe chest indrawing), or general danger signs (inability to breastfeed/drink, lethargy, unconsciousness, convulsions).
	
	**IV. Critical Disease** Patients progressing to critical illness may present with:
	
	- **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Characterized by new or worsening respiratory symptoms and bilateral opacities on chest imaging. It is further graded as mild, moderate, or severe based on the degree of oxygenation impairment (PaO2/FiO2 ratio).
	- **Sepsis:** Acute life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to the infection.
	- **Septic Shock:** Persistent hypotension despite volume resuscitation, requiring vasopressors to maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) ≥ 65 mmHg and serum lactate level > 2 mmol/L.

	**3. PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF COVID-19**
	
	The prevention and control of COVID-19 require a comprehensive, multi-pronged approach encompassing personal protective measures, environmental controls, vaccination, and surveillance.
	
	**A. Primary Prevention (Risk Reduction & Vaccination)**
	
	- **COVID Appropriate Behaviour:** The core preventive strategy is encapsulated in the slogan _"Do gaj ki doori, mask hai jaroori"_. This includes:
	    - Maintaining a physical distance of at least 6 feet (1 metre) in public places.
	    - Wearing an appropriate and well-fitted mask.
	    - Practicing strict hand hygiene with soap and water (for 15-30 seconds) or using an alcohol-based hand sanitizer.
	    - Respiratory etiquette (covering mouth and nose while coughing and sneezing) and avoiding spitting in the open.
	    - Avoiding touching the eyes, nose, and mouth.
	- **Vaccination:** A massive COVID-19 vaccination drive has been launched. Key vaccines granted restricted emergency use in India include Covishield, Covaxin, and Sputnik V. Vaccination is prioritized for high-risk groups (healthcare workers, frontline workers, persons >50 years, and those with comorbidities) to reduce disease severity and mortality.
	- **Digital Surveillance:** Use of location-based surveillance apps like the Aarogya Setu App to help individuals assess their risk of infection by tracking contact with infected persons.
	
	**B. Secondary Prevention (Case Finding, Isolation, and Treatment)**
	
	- **Early Diagnosis:** Prompt testing using rapid antigen tests (RDTs) or the gold standard Real-Time Reverse Transcriptase Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR) upon observing symptoms.
	- **Isolation and Quarantine:**
	    - Infected individuals must be isolated for 14 days after symptom resolution to prevent spread.
	    - Contacts of confirmed cases must undergo a 14-day quarantine.
	    - Cases are managed in a three-tier system: COVID Care Centres (for mild cases), Dedicated COVID Health Centres (for moderate cases), and Dedicated COVID Hospitals (for severe cases requiring ICU/ventilator support).
	- **Home Care Management:** Mildly symptomatic patients without comorbidities can be managed at home in a well-ventilated single room. Caregivers must use medical masks and limit contact.
	
	**C. Environmental and Infection Control Measures**
	
	- **Disinfection:** Routine cleaning and decontamination of indoor spaces, high-touch surfaces, and public places using 1% sodium hypochlorite solution.
	- **Hospital Infection Control:** Healthcare workers handling COVID-19 patients must use Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) including N95 masks, heavy-duty gloves, and gowns.
	- **Dead Body Management:** Dead bodies must be handled with aseptic precautions, placed in leak-proof plastic body bags, and the exterior decontaminated with 1% hypochlorite. Autopsies and embalming should be avoided.
