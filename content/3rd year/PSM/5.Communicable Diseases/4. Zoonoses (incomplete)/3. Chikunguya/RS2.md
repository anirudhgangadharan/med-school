1. What control measures would you recommend for control of chikungunya outbreak?
	**CONTROL MEASURES FOR CHIKUNGUNYA OUTBREAK**
	
	Because the same vector (_Aedes aegypti_) is involved in the transmission of both dengue and chikungunya, the strategies for transmission risk reduction by vector control are identical for both diseases.
	
	The high-yield control measures are broadly categorized as follows:
	
	**1. VECTOR CONTROL**
	
	- **Target Species:** The _Aedes aegypti_ mosquito must be the primary target of all control activities.
	- **Comprehensive Approach:** Vector control strategies should be comprehensive and address all life stages of the _Aedes_ mosquito, from the egg to the larva and the adult.
	- **Source Reduction (Environmental Control):** Active community involvement is strictly required to keep water storage containers free of mosquitoes and to systematically eliminate other mosquito breeding places in and around houses and dwellings.
	- **Chemical Control (During Outbreaks):** When there is an active outbreak, targeted residual spraying and space spraying or fogging are recommended to rapidly knock down and reduce the adult mosquito population.
	
	**2. PERSONAL PROTECTION (PROPHYLAXIS)**
	
	- **Protective Clothing:** Individuals should wear clothing that minimizes skin exposure to avoid mosquito bites.
	- **Repellents & Screens:** The use of insect repellents and the installation of window screens are highly recommended preventive measures.
	- **Limitation of Bed-Nets:** Although insecticide-treated bed nets are a standard preventive tool for many vector-borne diseases, their utility in chikungunya is limited by the fact that the _Aedes_ mosquito primarily bites during the daytime.
	
	**3. VACCINATION**
	
	- At present, no vaccine has yet been developed that is considered suitable for use against chikungunya virus.
	
	**4. LONG-TERM MEASURES**
	
	- **Standard of Living:** Eventually, economic development will significantly reduce mosquito-borne diseases by improving the overall standard of living.
	- **Housing Improvements:** People living in houses with solid floors and roofs, window screens, and air conditioning are naturally at a lower risk of contracting the disease.
