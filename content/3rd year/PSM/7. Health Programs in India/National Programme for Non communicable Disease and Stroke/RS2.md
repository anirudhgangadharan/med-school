	**STRATEGY FOR PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF BLINDNESS** _(Under the National Programme for Control of Blindness and Visual Impairment - NPCBVI)_
	
	**1. Introduction and Goals**
	
	- Launched in 1976 as a 100% centrally sponsored scheme, incorporating the earlier trachoma control programme.
	- **Target Goal:** To reduce the prevalence of blindness to 0.25 per 1,000 by 2025 and reduce the disease burden by one-third from current levels.
	
	**2. Key Objectives**
	
	- To reduce the backlog of avoidable blindness through identification and treatment at primary, secondary, and tertiary levels.
	- To develop comprehensive eye care services and strengthen infrastructure (Regional Institutes of Ophthalmology, Medical Colleges, District Hospitals, and Vision Centres).
	- To enhance community awareness on eye care and secure the participation of voluntary organizations and private practitioners.
	- To continue signature activities: performance of cataract operations (approx. 66 lakhs/year), school eye screening, provision of free spectacles, and collection of donated eyes (50,000/year).
	
	**3. Major Strategies and Interventions (The Tiered Approach)**
	
	**A. Primary Eye Care Services**
	
	- **Grass-root delivery:** Preventive and promotive eye care services are delivered via Health and Wellness Centres (HWCs).
	- **Basic treatments:** Locally trained primary health workers (village health guides, multipurpose workers) provide on-the-spot treatment for common ailments like conjunctivitis and trachoma using essential drugs (topical tetracycline, vitamin A), and refer difficult cases.
	- **Health Education:** Conducting IEC (Information, Education, and Communication) activities for the promotion of preventive eye care, personal hygiene, and eye donation.
	
	**B. Secondary Eye Care Services**
	
	- **Cataract Surgeries:** The backbone of the programme is the reduction in the backlog of cataract (the leading cause of blindness in India) by performing surgeries in Government, NGO, and private hospitals.
	- **Camp Approach/Mobile Units:** Use of mobile ophthalmic units and tele-ophthalmology networks to expand coverage into disadvantaged, remote, and rural areas.
	- **School Eye Screening Programme:** Trained teachers screen school children (especially 10-14 years) to identify refractive errors. Suspected cases are examined by ophthalmic assistants, and free spectacles are distributed to children below the poverty line.
	- **Management of Visual Impairment:** Comprehensive management of other emerging blinding diseases such as glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, vitreo-retinal diseases, and childhood blindness.
	- **Presbyopia Correction:** Distribution of free spectacles to older persons suffering from presbyopia to enable them to undertake near work.
	- **Eye Banking:** Strengthening eye banking services and hospital retrieval programmes for the collection and utilization of donated eyes for corneal transplantation. An "Eye Donation Fortnight" is observed annually from August 25th to September 8th to promote awareness.
	
	**C. Tertiary Eye Care Services**
	
	- Established in national or regional capitals (e.g., Regional Institutes of Ophthalmology and Medical Colleges) to provide sophisticated, super-specialty eye care such as retinal detachment surgery, lasers, and corneal grafting.
	- Upgradation of skills for eye care personnel and conducting research on eye care.
	
	**D. Infrastructure Development and Capacity Building**
	
	- Grant-in-aid is provided for strengthening eye care units at primary, secondary, and tertiary levels.
	- Extensive training is provided for eye surgeons and paramedical ophthalmic assistants.
	
	**E. Integration with "Vision 2020: The Right to Sight"**
	
	- The global initiative aims to eliminate avoidable blindness. India's plan of action targets specific diseases: cataract, refractive errors, childhood blindness, corneal blindness, glaucoma, and diabetic retinopathy.
	- It implements a four-tier structure for infrastructure development: Centres of Excellence (20), Training Centres (200), Service Centres (2,000), and Vision Centres (20,000).

	**NATIONAL CANCER CONTROL PROGRAMME (NCCP)**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION & BACKGROUND**
	
	- **Launch:** The National Cancer Control Programme was originally launched in 1975-76.
	- **Revisions:** It was revised in 1984-85 and subsequently in December 2004.
	- **Integration:** During 2010, the programme was integrated into the **National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases and Stroke (NPCDCS)**.
	- **Magnitude of Problem:** Cancer is a major public health problem in India, with nearly 10 lakh new cases occurring every year and an estimated 2.8 million prevalent cases at any given point in time.
	
	**2. OBJECTIVES OF THE PROGRAMME** The core objectives are structured around the three levels of prevention:
	
	- **Primary Prevention:** Prevention of cancer through intensive health education.
	- **Secondary Prevention:** Early detection and diagnosis of common cancers (such as cancer of the cervix, mouth, breast, and tobacco-related cancers) through screening and self-examination methods.
	- **Tertiary Prevention:** Strengthening of existing institutions to provide comprehensive therapy, including palliative care.
	
	**3. SCHEMES UNDER THE REVISED PROGRAMME** To achieve its objectives, the revised programme implements the following major schemes:
	
	- **Regional Cancer Centre Scheme:** Existing regional cancer centres are strengthened to act as tertiary referral centres for complicated cases. A one-time assistance of Rs. 3 crores is provided for the purchase of equipment like cobalt units.
	- **Oncology Wing Development Scheme:** Initiated to fill geographic gaps in cancer treatment facilities across the country. Central assistance of Rs. 3 crores per institution is provided for equipment and civil work.
	- **Decentralized NGO Scheme:** Financial assistance is provided to voluntary organizations (NGOs) for undertaking health education (IEC) and early case detection activities.
	- **IEC Activities at the Central Level:** Focuses on creating mass awareness about the ill effects of tobacco consumption and giving wider publicity to Anti-Tobacco Legislation. _Note: November 7th is observed as National Cancer Awareness Day in India_.
	- **Research and Training:** Organization of training programmes for capacity building. Manuals have been developed for health professionals, cytology, palliative care, and tobacco cessation.
	
	**4. CANCER SERVICES UNDER NPCDCS (CURRENT STRATEGY)** Following its integration with NPCDCS, cancer services have been decentralized and strengthened at various levels of the health system:
	
	- **At the District Hospital Level (100 identified districts):**
	    - Common diagnostic services, basic surgery, chemotherapy, and palliative care are made available.
	    - Each district receives Rs. 1.66 crores per annum to support these services.
	    - Provision of chemotherapy drugs for 100 patients per district hospital.
	    - Establishment of day-care chemotherapy facilities and laboratory investigations including mammography.
	- **Palliative Care:** Provision of home-based palliative care for chronic, debilitating, and progressive cancer patients.
	- **Manpower Support:** Financial support is provided for contractual hiring of 1 Medical Oncologist, 1 Cytopathologist, 1 Cytopathology technician, and 2 Nurses for day care.
	- **State Cancer Institutes (SCI):** Established to provide comprehensive cancer diagnosis, treatment, and care services, acting as the apex institution within the state.
	- **Tertiary Cancer Centres (TCCs):** 45 centres are targeted to be strengthened to provide comprehensive cancer care with an assistance of Rs. 6.00 crore each.

	**SCHEMES UNDER THE NATIONAL CANCER CONTROL PROGRAMME (NCCP)**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION** The National Cancer Control Programme was revised in 1984-85 and subsequently integrated with the National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases and Stroke (NPCDCS) in 2010.
	
	**2. MAJOR SCHEMES UNDER THE REVISED PROGRAMME** The specific schemes initiated under the revised programme include:
	
	- **Regional Cancer Centre Scheme:**
	    - Aims to strengthen existing regional cancer centres to act as referral centres for complicated and difficult cases at the tertiary level.
	    - A one-time central assistance of Rs. 3 crores is provided during the plan period for strengthening these institutions.
	- **Oncology Wing Development Scheme:**
	    - Initiated to fill the geographic gaps in the availability of cancer treatment facilities across the country.
	    - Central assistance of Rs. 3 crores per institution is provided for the purchase of equipment (including a cobalt unit).
	    - A part of the grant can be used for civil work, but the concerned government/institution must provide the manpower.
	- **Decentralized NGO Scheme:**
	    - Meant for Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) activities and the early detection of cancer.
	    - Financial assistance is given to NGOs to undertake health education and early detection activities.
	- **IEC Activities at Central Level:**
	    - Initiated to provide wider publicity to Anti-Tobacco Legislation and create mass awareness regarding the ill effects of tobacco consumption.
	    - November 7th is observed as National Cancer Awareness Day under this scheme.
	- **Research and Training:**
	    - Training programmes, monitoring, and research activities are organized at the central level.
	    - Specific training manuals have been developed for capacity building at the district level, including manuals for health professionals, cytology, palliative care, and tobacco cessation.
	
	**3. NEWER CANCER SERVICES UNDER NPCDCS INTEGRATION** Following the integration with NPCDCS, additional services and schemes were introduced:
	
	- **District-Level Support:**
	    - Common diagnostic services, basic surgery, chemotherapy, and palliative care are made available at 100 district hospitals.
	    - Each district is supported with Rs. 1.66 crores per annum for chemotherapy drugs (for 100 patients), day-care chemotherapy facilities, and laboratory investigations including mammography.
	- **Home-Based Palliative Care:**
	    - Provided for chronic, debilitating, and progressive cancer patients at 100 districts.
	- **State Cancer Institutes (SCI) & Tertiary Cancer Centres (TCCs):**
	    - State Cancer Institutes provide comprehensive cancer diagnosis, treatment, and care services, acting as apex institutions for cancer activities in the state.
	    - 45 centres are to be strengthened as Tertiary Cancer Centres (TCCs) to provide comprehensive cancer care services at a cost of Rs. 6.00 crore each.
	- **Contractual Manpower Support:**
	    - Support is provided for hiring 1 Medical Oncologist, 1 Cytopathologist, 1 Cytopathology technician, and 2 Nurses for day care.
