	**CAUSES OF (PREVENTABLE) BLINDNESS IN INDIA**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION: AVOIDABLE BLINDNESS**
	
	- **Definition:** "Avoidable blindness" includes cases that are either treatable or potentially preventable.
	- **Global & National Context:** About 30 per cent of blindness globally is considered avoidable, yet a large proportion remains blind due to a lack of access to affordable care. In India, a vast majority of blindness falls under this preventable/treatable category.
	
	**2. PRINCIPAL CAUSES OF BLINDNESS IN INDIA (HIGH-YIELD DATA)** According to the **National Survey on Blindness (2015-2019)**, the proportional distribution of causes of blindness in India is as follows:
	
	- **Untreated Cataract:** 66.2% _(The absolute leading cause of blindness in India)_.
	- **Cataract Surgical Complications:** 7.4%.
	- **Non-trachomatous Corneal Opacity:** 7.2%.
	- **Other Posterior Segment Disease:** 5.9%.
	- **Glaucoma:** 5.5%.
	- **Age-Related Macular Degeneration (ARMD):** 2.8%.
	- **Uncorrected Aphakia:** 1.7%.
	- **Phthisis:** 1.2%.
	- **Trachomatous Corneal Opacity:** 0.8%.
	- **Diabetic Retinopathy:** 0.7%.
	- **Uncorrected Refractive Error:** 0.1%.
	
	**3. AGE-SPECIFIC CAUSES OF BLINDNESS** The aetiology of blindness varies significantly across different age groups:
	
	- **Childhood and Younger Age Groups:**
	    - **Malnutrition (Vitamin A Deficiency):** Severe blinding corneal destruction (keratomalacia) is largely limited to the first 4-6 years of life, often precipitated by infectious diseases like measles and diarrhoea.
	    - **Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP):** Rapidly emerging as an important cause of childhood blindness due to the improved survival of premature babies (born <30 weeks gestation and <1500g) managed in NICUs with hyperbaric oxygen.
	    - **Infections & Refractive Errors:** Trachoma, conjunctivitis, and uncorrected refractive errors are major factors.
	- **Middle Age & Elderly:**
	    - **Cataract:** Occurs more frequently with advancing age, often presenting a decade earlier in India compared to Western countries.
	    - **Systemic Disease Complications:** Glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy.
	- **Young Adults (20 to 40 Years):**
	    - **Accidents and Injuries:** Account for 1.2% of blindness overall, but are a prominent cause in this specific age demographic.
	
	**4. OTHER IMPORTANT PREVENTABLE & SOCIAL FACTORS**
	
	- **Occupational Hazards:** Factory and cottage industry workers (e.g., carpentry, stone crushing, welding) are highly prone to preventable eye injuries from flying objects, dust, radiation (welding flash), and chemical exposures.
	- **Social Determinants:** Poverty, ignorance, low standards of personal/community hygiene, and inadequate health care services heavily influence blindness rates. Blindness is twice as prevalent in poorer classes than in well-to-do populations.
	- **Meddlesome Ophthalmology:** A significant number of people lose their eyesight due to unsafe practices and treatments performed by quacks.
