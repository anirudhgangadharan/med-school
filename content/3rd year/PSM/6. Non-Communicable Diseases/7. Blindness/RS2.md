	**PLAN FOR THE PREVENTION OF BLINDNESS IN A PRIMARY HEALTH CENTRE (PHC) AREA**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION AND OBJECTIVE** Planning for the prevention of blindness at the PHC level is guided by the **National Programme for Control of Blindness and Visual Impairment (NPCB&VI)** and the **Vision 2020: The Right to Sight** initiative. The primary objective is to reduce the prevalence of avoidable blindness by providing comprehensive primary eye care services at the grass-root level.
	
	**2. INITIAL ASSESSMENT (COMMUNITY DIAGNOSIS)**
	
	- The first step is to assess the magnitude, geographic distribution, and local causes of blindness within the PHC area through epidemiological surveys.
	- The major causes to target include un-operated cataract (the principal cause), uncorrected refractive errors, glaucoma, corneal opacities, and childhood blindness (e.g., Vitamin A deficiency).
	
	**3. PRIMARY PREVENTION STRATEGIES (HEALTH PROMOTION & SPECIFIC PROTECTION)**
	
	- **Vitamin A Prophylaxis:** Administration of a massive dose of Vitamin A orally to preschool children (1 to 5 years) to prevent xerophthalmia and nutritional blindness. The dose is 100,000 IU at 9 months with the measles vaccine, and 200,000 IU every 6 months thereafter until 5 years of age.
	- **Health Education (IEC/BCC):** Conducting intensive Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaigns aimed at mothers, school children, and the general public. Topics must include the promotion of personal hygiene, good dietary habits (intake of Vitamin A-rich foods), eye safety to prevent injuries, and awareness regarding eye donation.
	- **School Eye Health Services:** Screening school children for refractive errors, squint, amblyopia, and trachoma. Teachers and "Health and Wellness Ambassadors" must be trained to identify visual defects and promote principles of good posture and proper lighting in classrooms.
	
	**4. SECONDARY PREVENTION (EARLY DIAGNOSIS & TREATMENT)**
	
	- **Primary Eye Care:** Provision of basic treatment for common eye conditions at the PHC and sub-centre level. This includes treating acute conjunctivitis, ophthalmia neonatorum, trachoma, and superficial foreign bodies using essential drugs like topical tetracycline and eye bandages.
	- **Screening and Case Finding:** Active screening for cataract and refractive errors by health workers in the community and schools.
	- **Provision of Spectacles:** Distributing free spectacles to children identified with refractive errors during school screening, and to elderly persons suffering from presbyopia.
	- **Referral System:** Prompt referral of difficult or complicated cases (e.g., mature cataracts requiring surgery, glaucoma, penetrating eye injuries, and severe corneal ulcers) to the nearest Community Health Centre (CHC), District Hospital, or Mobile Ophthalmic Units.
	
	**5. ROLE OF THE PHC HEALTH TEAM (EXAM ESSENTIALS)** For effective implementation, responsibilities are clearly distributed among the health team:
	
	- **Medical Officer (MO):** Acts as the captain of the team; provides basic diagnostic and therapeutic services, supervises field staff, manages minor ailments, and ensures timely referral of complex ophthalmic cases to higher centres.
	- **Health Worker Female (ANM) / Male (MPW):** Responsible for identifying and referring all cases of blindness (including suspected cataracts) to the MO, administering Vitamin A to children under five, and conducting health education during home visits.
	- **ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist):** Mobilizes the community, assists in identifying target beneficiaries with visual impairment, and supports IEC activities for preventive eye care and hygiene at the village level.
	
	**6. LONG-TERM MEASURES AND EVALUATION**
	
	- **Environmental Modification:** Promoting long-term measures such as safe water supply, adequate sanitation, and fly control to interrupt the transmission of eye infections like trachoma.
	- **Evaluation:** Continuous monitoring and evaluation of the program to measure the reduction in ocular diseases, assess the performance of health workers, and evaluate the coverage of cataract screening and Vitamin A distribution.

	1. Preventable blindness—causes and prevention.  
	**PREVENTABLE (AVOIDABLE) BLINDNESS**
	
	**1. INTRODUCTION & DEFINITION**
	
	- **Definition (WHO):** Blindness is defined as "visual acuity of less than 3/60 (Snellen) or its equivalent".
	- **Indian Definition (Revised):** A person who is unable to count fingers from a distance of three meters is considered blind.
	- **Avoidable Blindness:** About 30 per cent of blindness is considered avoidable, meaning it is either treatable or potentially preventable.
	
	**2. CAUSES OF BLINDNESS (HIGH-YIELD)** The causes of blindness can be classified based on geographical distribution and age groups.
	
	**A. Major Causes in India (National Survey on Blindness 2015-19):**
	
	- **Untreated Cataract:** 66.2% (The single most common cause).
	- **Corneal Opacity:** Non-trachomatous (7.4%) and Trachomatous (0.8%).
	- **Cataract Surgical Complications:** 7.2%.
	- **Posterior Segment Disease:** 5.9%.
	- **Glaucoma:** 5.5%.
	- **Uncorrected Refractive Error:** 0.1%.
	
	**B. Major Causes of Childhood Blindness:**
	
	- **Nutritional Blindness:** Vitamin A deficiency (Xerophthalmia/Keratomalacia), often precipitated by measles and diarrhoea.
	- **Infections:** Trachoma, ophthalmia neonatorum, and congenital rubella.
	- **Emerging Causes:** Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) in low birth weight babies surviving in NICUs.
	- **Congenital Conditions:** Congenital cataract and congenital glaucoma.
	
	**3. PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF BLINDNESS** The modern approach has shifted from acute intervention to "comprehensive eye-health care" integrated into the primary health care system.
	
	**A. Primary Eye Care (At Community/PHC Level):**
	
	- Delivered by peripheral health workers (Village Health Guides, MPWs, ASHAs).
	- **Interventions:** Administration of Vitamin A prophylaxis, topical tetracycline for trachoma/conjunctivitis, and treatment of superficial foreign bodies.
	- **Referral:** Early identification and referral of difficult cases (corneal ulcers, penetrating injuries, cataracts) to PHCs/CHCs.
	- **Health Education:** Promotion of personal hygiene, proper sanitation, and diets rich in Vitamin A.
	
	**B. Secondary Care (At District Hospitals/Mobile Camps):**
	
	- Involves definitive management of common blinding conditions like cataract, trichiasis, entropion, glaucoma, and ocular trauma.
	- **Camp Approach:** Mobile ophthalmic units and eye camps are highly successful for mass cataract surgeries, screening, and dispensing spectacles in rural areas.
	
	**C. Tertiary Care (At Regional Institutes/Medical Colleges):**
	
	- Provision of sophisticated eye care such as retinal detachment surgery, lasers, and corneal grafting.
	- Establishment and maintenance of **Eye Banks** for the collection and utilization of donated eyes.
	
	**4. SPECIFIC PREVENTIVE PROGRAMMES**
	
	- **Vitamin A Prophylaxis:** Administration of a massive dose of Vitamin A (200,000 IU or 110 mg of retinol palmitate) orally every 6 months to preschool children (1 to 5 years of age) to prevent nutritional blindness.
	- **School Eye Health Services:** Screening of school children (especially 10-14 years) by trained teachers and ophthalmic assistants for early detection of refractive errors, squint, amblyopia, and trachoma. Free spectacles are provided to children below the poverty line.
	- **Trachoma Control:** Blanket or selective mass treatment with 1% tetracycline or erythromycin ophthalmic ointment, combined with surgical correction of lid deformities (trichiasis/entropion). _(Note: India was declared free from trachoma in 2024)_.
	- **Occupational Eye Health:** Prevention of industrial eye hazards (dust, flying objects, radiation, welding flash) by providing protective devices and improving machine safety and illumination.
	
	**5. VISION 2020: THE RIGHT TO SIGHT**
	
	- A global initiative launched by WHO to eliminate avoidable blindness by the year 2020.
	- **Core Concept:** Recognition of sight as a fundamental human right.
	- **Target Diseases:** Cataract, refractive errors, childhood blindness (xerophthalmia), corneal blindness, diabetic retinopathy, and glaucoma.
	- **Strategy:** Focuses on human resource development, infrastructure/technology development across a four-tier health system (Centres of Excellence, Training Centres, Service Centres, and Vision Centres).
