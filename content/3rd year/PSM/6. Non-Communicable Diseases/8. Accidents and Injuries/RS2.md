1. How can we prevent road accidents?  
	**PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF ROAD TRAFFIC ACCIDENTS**
	
	Accidents don't just happen; they are caused by a complex interaction of multifactorial causes (human and environmental). Studies show that up to 98% of accidents are preventable. Because of this complexity, controlling them requires a comprehensive, intersectoral approach encompassing the following measures:
	
	**1. DATA COLLECTION AND RESEARCH (ACCIDENTOLOGY)**
	
	- **Basic Reporting System:** A robust reporting system must be established, taking police records as a fundamental starting point.
	- **Epidemiological Surveys:** National data must be supplemented by special surveys and in-depth studies to identify risk factors, environmental conditions (road, vehicle, weather), and the exact chain of events leading to the crash.
	- **Accidentology:** The future of accident prevention lies in research to gather precise information, correlate accident experiences with personal attributes and environments, and evaluate the efficiency of control measures.
	
	**2. SAFETY EDUCATION (HIGH-YIELD)**
	
	- **Core Principle:** The fatalistic attitude that accidents are "inevitable" must be eradicated. In public health, it is aptly said that _"If accident is a disease, education is its vaccine"_.
	- **Target Groups:** Safety education must begin with school children. Young people need to be educated regarding traffic rules, risk factors, and first aid.
	- **Driver Training:** Drivers require strict training in safe driving and proper vehicle maintenance.
	
	**3. PROMOTION OF SAFETY MEASURES & PROTECTIVE GEAR**
	
	- **Seat Belts:** The compulsory use of seat belts reduces the number of fatalities and non-fatal injuries by approximately 50 per cent.
	- **Safety Helmets:** Wearing crash helmets correctly reduces the risk of head injury by 30 per cent on average and fatalities by 40 per cent.
	- **Child Restraints:** Children under 12-15 years of age should be strictly prohibited from sitting in the front seats of cars.
	- **Vehicle Design Improvements:** Utilizing door locks and laminated high-penetration resistance windscreen glass significantly enhances safety.
	
	**4. CONTROL OF ALCOHOL AND DRUGS**
	
	- **Alcohol Hazard:** Alcohol drastically impairs driving ability, increases crash risk, and is the direct cause of 30 to 50 per cent of severe road accidents.
	- **Intervention:** Strict enforcement of roadside breath testing and checking of blood alcohol concentration is necessary.
	- **Drug Abuse:** Drugs that impair driving ability and cerebration, such as barbiturates, amphetamines, and cannabis, must be totally avoided.
	
	**5. ELIMINATION OF CAUSATIVE FACTORS (ENGINEERING)**
	
	- Environmental and structural factors that predispose to accidents must be sought out and eliminated.
	- Key engineering measures include the improvement of road layouts, clearly marking danger points, and the imposition of strict speed limits.
	
	**6. ENFORCEMENT OF LAWS (LEGISLATIVE MEASURES)** State enforcement of codified rules is critical to prevent accidents. These include:
	
	- Rigorous driving tests and certification of medical fitness to drive.
	- Strict enforcement of speed limits and compulsory wearing of seat-belts and crash-helmets.
	- Regular safety inspection of vehicles.
	- Periodic medical re-examination of drivers, especially those over the age of 55.
	
	**7. PRIMARY CARE AND TRAUMA MANAGEMENT**
	
	- **Continuum of Care:** Emergency care must seamlessly begin at the accident site, continue during transportation, and conclude in the hospital emergency room.
	- **Infrastructure:** An "Accident Services Organization" and at least one fully equipped, specialized trauma care hospital should be established in all major cities to maximize survival.
	
	**8. REHABILITATION SERVICES**
	
	- Survivors of road traffic accidents must be provided with comprehensive medical, social, and occupational rehabilitation.
	- The ultimate aim of these services is to prevent, reduce, or adequately compensate for the resulting disability and handicap.

Correctly installed child restraints reduce deaths among infants by approximately 70 per cent and among small children by 54 to 80 per cent. Laws in some countries mandate children under 12-15 years to sit only in the rear seats.
	- **Vehicle Design:** Safety measures built into the vehicle include the use of proper door locks, improved overall vehicle design, and the use of laminated high-penetration resistance windscreen glass.
	
	**2. SAFETY EDUCATION**
	
	- The fatalistic attitude that "accidents are inevitable" must be curbed. It is aptly said that _"if accident is a disease, education is its vaccine"_.
	- Safety education must begin with school children.
	- Drivers need adequate training in safe driving, proper maintenance of vehicles, and first aid.
	- Young people require education regarding risk factors, traffic rules, and safety precautions.
	
	**3. ENFORCEMENT OF LAWS** Strict State enforcement of legislation is critical to regulate individual behaviour and prevent accidents.
	
	- Rigorous driving tests and assessment of medical fitness to drive.
	- Strict enforcement of speed limits. Lower average traffic speeds, such as 30 km/h speed zones in residential areas and around schools, drastically reduce crash risks for vulnerable road users.
	- Compulsory wearing of seat-belts and crash-helmets.
	- Regular inspection of vehicles for roadworthiness and periodic medical re-examination of drivers over the age of 55.
	- Legislative measures and public awareness campaigns to ban distracted driving, notably the use of mobile phones and text messaging while driving.
	
	**4. CONTROL OF ALCOHOL AND DRUGS**
	
	- Alcohol impairs driving ability and is the direct cause of 30 to 50 per cent of severe road accidents. The risk of a crash increases significantly above a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.04 g/dl.
	- Strict laws establishing BAC limits of 0.05 g/dl or below are highly effective at reducing alcohol-related crashes.
	- Enforcing sobriety checkpoints and random breath testing can reduce alcohol-related crashes by about 20 per cent and is highly cost-effective.
	- Drugs that impair driving ability, such as barbiturates, amphetamines, and cannabis, must be totally avoided.
	
	**5. ELIMINATION OF CAUSATIVE FACTORS (ENVIRONMENTAL ENGINEERING)**
	
	- Factors that predispose to accidents must be sought out and eliminated. This includes the improvement of narrow or defective roads, correcting the layout of cross-roads and speed-breakers, and clearly marking danger points.
	- Ensuring proper street lighting and separating pedestrians and slow-moving vehicles from fast-moving traffic.
	
	**6. DATA COLLECTION AND ACCIDENTOLOGY**
	
	- A basic reporting system relying on police records as a starting point is essential.
	- This must be supplemented by special epidemiological surveys to collect detailed environmental data (road, vehicle, weather) to understand the chain of events leading to the crash.
	- **Accidentology:** The future of prevention lies in research to correlate accident experience with personal attributes, seek ways to alter human behaviour, make environments safer, and evaluate the efficiency of existing control measures.
	
	**7. PRIMARY CARE AND TRAUMA MANAGEMENT**
	
	- Emergency care must be seamlessly managed: beginning at the accident site, continuing during transportation, and concluding in the hospital emergency room.
	- Establishment of an "Accident Services Organization" and fully equipped specialized trauma care hospitals in all major cities.
	
	**8. REHABILITATION SERVICES**
	
	- Survivors must receive medical, social, and occupational rehabilitation to prevent, reduce, or compensate for resulting disabilities and handicaps.
	
	**9. SAFETY MEASURES FOR DOMESTIC AND INDUSTRIAL ACCIDENTS**
	
	- **Drowning:** Implementing engineering methods like building four-sided pool fences, covering wells/cisterns, emptying buckets after use, and community education on water survival skills.
	- **Burns:** Addressing hazards like inherently unsafe cookstoves, reducing reliance on open flames, and providing community first-aid training (e.g., using cool running water instead of applying ice or pastes).
	- **Falls:** Modifying nursery furniture and using window guards for children. For the elderly, prevention includes medication reviews, treating low blood pressure, correcting visual impairment, balance retraining, and providing assistive devices.
	- **Industrial Accidents:** Establishing a safety department under a competent safety engineer, applying ergonomics (fitting the machine to the worker), providing adequate job training, and conducting pre-placement medical examinations.

	**DOMESTIC ACCIDENTS AND PREVENTIVE MEASURES**
	
	**1. DEFINITION** A "domestic accident" is defined as an accident which takes place in the home or in its immediate surroundings, and generally includes all accidents not connected with traffic, vehicles, or sport.
	
	**2. ENUMERATION OF DOMESTIC ACCIDENTS (HIGH-YIELD)** The most frequent causes of domestic accidents are:
	
	2. **Drowning**.
	3. **Burns** (caused by a flame, hot liquids/scalds, electricity, crackers or fireworks, and chemicals).
	4. **Falls**.
	5. **Poisoning** (from drugs, insecticides, rat poisons, and kerosene).
	6. **Injuries from sharp or pointed instruments**.
	7. **Bites and other injuries from animals**.
	
	**3. PREVENTIVE MEASURES**
	
	**A. General Preventive Measures**
	
	- **Safety Education:** Carelessness is the predominant factor in home accidents. The problem can be tackled effectively through intensive health and safety education directed towards safety in the home.
	
	**B. Prevention of Drowning**
	
	- **Engineering Methods (Most Effective):** Implementation of safe water systems, covering of wells or open cisterns, and building four-sided pool fences or barriers to prevent access to standing water. Emptying buckets and baths immediately after use and storing them upside down is crucial.
	- **Education:** Individual and community education on drowning awareness, learning water survival skills, and ensuring the presence of lifeguards at swimming areas.
	- **Legislation:** Enacting laws that target risk factors, such as laws on alcohol use while boating or swimming.
	
	**C. Prevention of Burns**
	
	- **Hazard Reduction:** Addressing specific hazards such as inherently unsafe cookstoves, open fire cooking, and inadequate safety measures for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and electricity.
	- **Education and First-Aid:** Educating vulnerable populations and training communities in proper first-aid. Communities must be taught to use cool running water to reduce the temperature of the burn and to completely avoid applying paste, oil, haldi (turmeric), or ice to the wound.
	
	**D. Prevention of Falls**
	
	- **In Children:** Effective interventions include the engineering modifications of nursery furniture and playground equipment, alongside legislation for the use of window guards.
	- **In the Elderly:**
	    - Screening within living environments to identify risks for falls.
	    - Home assessment and environmental modifications for those with a history of falling.
	    - Clinical interventions such as medication review, treatment of low blood pressure, Vitamin D and calcium supplementation, and treatment of correctable visual impairment.
	    - Prescription of appropriate assistive devices and balance retraining or muscle strengthening by a trained health professional.
	
	**E. Prevention of Poisoning**
	
	- **Targeting High-Risk Agents:** Preventive efforts must focus on the common agents responsible for poisoning, which include pesticides, kerosene, prescription drugs, and household chemicals.
	- **Protecting Children:** Special care must be taken to prevent accidental ingestion of therapeutic drugs and kerosene, which are the leading causes of poisoning among pre-school children.
	1. What is accident proneness?  
	**ACCIDENT PRONENESS**
	
	**1. CONCEPT & SIGNIFICANCE**
	
	- **Definition:** "Accident proneness" refers to the epidemiological observation that certain individuals are inherently more prone to being involved in accidents than others.
	- **Classification:** It is categorized as a major psychological (human/host) factor in the multifactorial causation of accidents. Authorities consider human factors to be responsible for up to 85 per cent of all accidents.
	
	**2. ASSOCIATED PSYCHOLOGICAL FACTORS (HIGH-YIELD)** In the context of accident causation, psychological factors generally appear to be more important than physiological factors. The specific mental traits and conditions that contribute to a state of accident proneness include:
	
	- Carelessness and inattentiveness.
	- Overconfidence and slow cerebration (delayed decision-making).
	- Ignorance and inexperience.
	- Emotional stress.
	
	**3. PRECIPITATING & DEMOGRAPHIC FACTORS** The susceptibility of an accident-prone individual is significantly aggravated by certain physiological states and demographic variables:
	
	- **Substance Abuse:** The effect of alcohol and other drugs drastically increases susceptibility to accidents.
	- **Fatigue:** Physiological states such as fatigue directly heighten the risk.
	- **Age:** Younger age groups and the very old are notably more prone to accidents.
	- **Lack of Experience:** Experience is a crucial factor; studies show that approximately 50 per cent of employees have accidents in their first 6 months of employment.

	1. Risk factors for road traffic accidents 
	**RISK FACTORS FOR ROAD TRAFFIC ACCIDENTS (RTAs)**
	
	Accidents are a complex phenomenon of multiple causation. The risk factors for road traffic accidents can be systematically analyzed using the Epidemiological Triad: **Host (Human) factors, Agent (Vehicle) factors, and Environmental factors**.
	
	**I. HUMAN FACTORS (HOST FACTORS)** Human failure is considered the most important factor, being responsible for up to 85 to 90 per cent of all accidents.
	
	**A. Demographic Factors**
	
	- **Age:** RTAs are the absolute leading cause of death for children and young adults aged 5 to 29 years. In India, 55.5% of RTA deaths occur in individuals below 35 years of age.
	- **Sex:** Males are almost 3 times more likely to be involved and killed in a car crash than young females. In India, 85.8% of accidental deaths occur in men.
	
	**B. Specific Behavioural Risks (High-Yield)**
	
	- **Over-speeding:** This is the single largest cause of RTA deaths in India. An increase in average speed directly increases both the likelihood of a crash occurring and the severity of its consequences.
	- **Drink-Driving:** Alcohol impairs driving ability and increases crash risk. The risk of being involved in a crash increases significantly above a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.04 g/dl. It is a direct cause of 30 to 50% of severe road accidents.
	- **Distracted Driving:** Drivers using a mobile phone (including texting) are approximately four times more likely to be involved in a crash. It leads to longer braking reaction times and an impaired ability to stay in the correct lane.
	- **Lack of Protective Gear:** Non-use of seat-belts and motorcycle helmets drastically increases the risk of fatal head injuries and death.
	
	**C. Psychological & Physical Conditions**
	
	- **Physical/Medical:** Fatigue, sudden illness (e.g., heart attack), impaired vision, and lack of driving experience.
	- **Psychosocial:** Risk-taking behaviour, impulsiveness, defective judgement, aggressiveness, and heightened emotional tension.
	
	**II. AGENT FACTORS (VEHICLE FACTORS)** The vehicle acts as the primary agent of injury.
	
	- **Vehicle Condition:** Use of old, poorly maintained vehicles lacking basic safety features.
	- **Overloading:** Overloaded buses and transport vehicles compromise stability and braking (responsible for 12,000 deaths in India in 2023).
	- **High-Risk Vehicles:** A large density of highly vulnerable vehicles like two-wheelers and three-wheelers on the road.
	
	**III. ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS** These include the physical surroundings, road infrastructure, and regulatory environment.
	
	**A. Road & Infrastructure Conditions**
	
	- Defective or narrow roads.
	- Poor street lighting.
	- Defective layout of cross-roads, junctions, and unmarked speed-breakers.
	
	**B. Traffic Mix (Specific to Developing Countries)**
	
	- **Unsegregated Traffic:** In countries like India, fast-moving vehicles, slow-moving vehicles (e.g., bullock carts), large numbers of pedestrians, and stray animals all share the same unsegregated roadway, massively increasing the risk of collisions.
	
	**C. Weather & Social Factors**
	
	- Bad weather conditions reducing visibility and road grip.
	- Social pressure (e.g., travelling in groups).
	- **Regulatory Failure:** Widespread disregard for traffic rules and inadequate enforcement of existing safety laws.

	- **Common Agents:** The most frequent agents responsible for poisoning are household chemicals, kerosene, prescription drugs, and pesticides.

	- **Mechanism:** Mishandling of household tools, knives, or pointed instruments.
	- **Animals:** Bites or injuries inflicted by domestic pets or stray animals in the immediate home surroundings.

	1. State the steps in giving first aid in burns.  
	**FIRST AID MANAGEMENT OF BURNS**
	
	First aid for burns involves immediate and appropriate measures to minimize tissue damage, prevent complications, and ensure safe transport to a medical facility. The guidelines are systematically categorized into "Do's" and "Don'Ts" for optimal management.
	
	**1. THE "DO'S" (IMMEDIATE ACTIONS TO TAKE)**
	
	- **Stop the burning process:** Immediately remove the victim's clothing and irrigate the burns.
	- **Cool the burn:** Use cool running water to rapidly reduce the temperature of the burn.
	- **Extinguish flames:** Allow the person to roll on the ground, apply a blanket, or use water or other fire-extinguishing liquids to put out the fire.
	- **Manage chemical burns:** Promptly remove or dilute the chemical agent by extensively irrigating the area with large volumes of water.
	- **Ensure safe transport:** Wrap the patient carefully in a clean cloth or sheet and transport them to the nearest appropriate facility for professional medical care.
	
	**2. THE "DON'TS" (HARMFUL PRACTICES TO AVOID)**
	
	- **Do not ignore personal safety:** Never start first-aid before ensuring your own safety (e.g., switch off the electrical current, wear gloves for chemical burns, etc.).
	- **Do not apply home remedies:** Strictly avoid applying paste, oil, haldi (turmeric), or raw cotton to the burn wound.
	- **Do not apply ice:** Ice must not be applied as it deepens the tissue injury.
	- **Avoid prolonged cooling:** Do not use water for prolonged cooling as it may lead to systemic hypothermia.
	- **Do not puncture blisters:** Never open or break blisters until topical antimicrobials can be applied under sterile conditions by a health-care provider.
	- **Do not contaminate the wound:** Do not apply any unsterile material directly to the wound as it significantly increases the risk of infection.
	- **Do not use topical medications prematurely:** Avoid the application of any topical medication or ointments until the patient is under appropriate medical care.
