1. How do you measure the burden of coronary heart disease in the community.
	**MEASUREMENT OF THE BURDEN OF CORONARY HEART DISEASE (CHD) IN THE COMMUNITY**
	
	The burden of Coronary Heart Disease (CHD) can be estimated using several epidemiological parameters, each illustrating a different aspect of the disease pattern in the community.
	
	**1. PROPORTIONAL MORTALITY RATIO (PMR)**
	
	- **Definition:** It is the simplest measure, representing the proportion of all deaths currently attributed to CHD.
	- **Significance:** It highlights the relative importance of CHD as a cause of death in the population.
	- **Example:** In most western countries, CHD is held responsible for approximately 30 per cent of deaths in men and 25 per cent of deaths in women.
	
	**2. LOSS OF LIFE EXPECTANCY**
	
	- **Concept:** CHD significantly cuts short the life expectancy.
	- **Measurement:** It is calculated by estimating the average gain in life expectation that would follow the complete elimination of all cardiovascular deaths, provided other mortality rates remain unchanged.
	- **Impact:** Complete elimination would yield a benefit ranging from 3.4 to 9.4 years for men, and an even greater gain for women.
	
	**3. CHD INCIDENCE RATE**
	
	- **Definition:** It is calculated as the sum of fatal and non-fatal attack rates.
	- **Limitation:** Because CHD manifests in many different ways (angina, infarction, sudden death), accurate incidence rates are notoriously difficult to compute.
	- **Proxy Measure:** In the absence of accurate morbidity data, mortality rates are often used as a crude indicator of incidence.
	
	**4. AGE-SPECIFIC DEATH RATES**
	
	- **Utility:** It is essential to study age-specific rates when the analysis is planned to throw light on the aetiology of the disease.
	- **Significance:** A consistent rise in age-specific death rates over time suggests a true increase in the incidence of the disease in the community.
	
	**5. PREVALENCE RATE**
	
	- **Methodology:** The prevalence of CHD is typically estimated through cross-sectional surveys.
	- **Diagnostic Criteria:** These surveys utilize Electrocardiogram (ECG) changes for evidence of myocardial infarction alongside a clinical history of prolonged chest pain.
	
	**6. CASE FATALITY RATE**
	
	- **Definition:** It is defined as the proportion of CHD attacks that prove to be fatal within 28 days of the onset.
	- **Sudden Death Component:** The International Society and Federation of Cardiology defines "sudden deaths" as those occurring instantly or within an estimated 24 hours of the onset of acute symptoms.
	- **High-Yield Stats:** Data from industrialized nations indicate that 25 to 28 per cent of patients who suffer a heart attack die suddenly, and in about 55 per cent of all cardiac deaths, mortality occurs within the very first hour.
	
	**7. MEASUREMENT OF RISK FACTOR LEVELS**
	
	- Assessing the burden also involves the community-wide measurement of established risk factor levels.
	- This includes tracking the prevalence of cigarette smoking, levels of blood pressure, alcohol consumption, and serum cholesterol in the population.
	
	**8. MEDICAL CARE LEVELS**
	
	- The measurement of the levels of medical care available and utilized in the community is also a pertinent parameter for estimating the overall impact and management burden of the disease.
