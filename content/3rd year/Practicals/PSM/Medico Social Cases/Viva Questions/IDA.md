**IRON DEFICIENCY ANAEMIA VIVA: HIGH-YIELD EXAM NOTES**

**I. PUBLIC HEALTH MAGNITUDE & IMPORTANCE**

- **Burden in India (NFHS-5 data):** Iron deficiency anaemia is a massive public health problem.
    - **Children (6-59 months):** 67.1% are anaemic (64.2% urban, 68.7% rural).
    - **Pregnant women:** 52.2% are anaemic.
    - **Maternal Mortality:** 19% of maternal deaths are directly attributed to anaemia.
- **The Problem:** The most frequent cause of anaemia is iron deficiency, followed less frequently by folate and vitamin B12 deficiency.

**II. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS**

- **Agent (Dietary) Factors:**
    - _Haem-iron:_ Found in liver, meat, poultry, and fish. It is better absorbed and promotes absorption of non-haem iron.
    - _Non-haem iron:_ Found in vegetables, legumes, nuts, jaggery, and cereals. It constitutes the bulk of the Indian vegetarian diet but has poor bioavailability (less than 5%).
    - _Inhibitors of absorption:_ Phytates (in bran/cereals), oxalates (in leafy vegetables), carbonates, phosphates, and tannins (in tea).
    - _Promoters of absorption:_ Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) reduces ferric iron to ferrous iron, facilitating absorption.
- **Host Factors:**
    - _Increased Demand:_ Rapid growth phases (childhood, adolescence) and pregnancy.
    - _Iron Losses:_ Menstruating women lose ~12.5 mg per 28-day cycle. Widespread use of IUDs in family planning increases menstrual blood loss by 35-146%.
    - _Parasitic/Infectious causes:_ Hookworm infestations, malaria, and chronic bleeding (peptic ulcers, haemorrhoids).

**III. STAGES OF IRON DEFICIENCY** The progression to iron deficiency anaemia occurs in three stages:

1. **Decreased Storage:** Iron stores deplete without any other detectable abnormality.
2. **Latent Iron Deficiency:** Iron stores are exhausted, but anaemia has not occurred yet. Transferrin saturation falls <15%.
3. **Overt Iron Deficiency:** Decrease in circulating haemoglobin (Hb) due to impaired haemoglobin synthesis.

**IV. DIAGNOSIS AND EVALUATION (WHO CUT-OFFS)**

- **Diagnostic Cut-offs for Anaemia:**
    - Adult males: Hb < 13 g/dl
    - Adult females (non-pregnant): Hb < 12 g/dl
    - Adult females (pregnant): Hb < 11 g/dl
    - Children (6 months to 6 years): Hb < 11 g/dl
    - Children (6 to 14 years): Hb < 12 g/dl
- **Parameters of Iron Status:**
    - _Serum Ferritin:_ **The most sensitive tool** for evaluating iron stores. Normal is >10 mcg/L. Values <10 mcg/L indicate absent stored iron.
    - _Serum Transferrin Saturation:_ Normal is 30%. Values <16% are abnormal.
    - _Haemoglobin:_ A relatively _insensitive_ index of early depletion because it falls only in the late stages of deficiency.

**V. CONSEQUENCES OF ANAEMIA (MATERNAL & CHILD)**

- **In Pregnancy:** High incidence of premature births, low birth weight (LBW) babies, postpartum haemorrhage, puerperal sepsis, and thromboembolic phenomena.
- **In Children:** Retarded physical and cognitive development, impaired cell-mediated immunity, reduced resistance to fatigue, and increased susceptibility to infections.

**VI. PREVENTION AND CONTROL: ANAEMIA MUKT BHARAT (AMB)** The Anaemia Mukt Bharat Strategy (launched in 2018 under POSHAN Abhiyaan) utilizes the **6x6x6 strategy**: 6 target beneficiary groups, 6 interventions, and 6 institutional mechanisms.

- **6 Interventions:**
    1. Prophylactic iron and folic acid (IFA) supplementation.
    2. Deworming (Albendazole).
    3. Intensified year-round behaviour change communication (delayed cord clamping, diet diversity).
    4. Testing and treatment of anaemia using digital point-of-care methods.
    5. Mandatory provision of iron and folic acid fortified foods in public health programmes.
    6. Addressing non-nutritional causes of anaemia in endemic pockets (malaria, haemoglobinopathies, fluorosis).
- **IFA Supplementation Schedule (High-Yield):**
    - _Children (6-59 months):_ Bi-weekly, 20 mg elemental iron + 100 mcg Folic Acid (liquid drops/syrup).
    - _Children (5-9 years):_ Weekly, 45 mg elemental iron + 400 mcg Folic Acid (**Pink** tablet).
    - _Adolescents (10-19 years):_ Weekly, 60 mg elemental iron + 500 mcg Folic Acid (**Blue** tablet) under WIFS.
    - _Pregnant & Lactating Women:_ Daily, 100 mg elemental iron + 500 mcg Folic Acid (**Red** tablet) for 180 days during ANC and 180 days during PNC.

**PROBABLE VIVA QUESTIONS & ANSWERS (PSM)**

**Q1: What is the most sensitive laboratory indicator for evaluating iron deficiency in the community?** _Answer:_ Serum Ferritin is the single most sensitive tool. It reflects the size of iron stores in the body. A value below 10 mcg/L indicates an absence of stored iron.

**Q2: Why is the Indian diet, despite being sufficient in total iron content in some regions, highly prone to causing iron deficiency?** _Answer:_ The Indian diet is predominantly vegetarian, meaning the iron is "non-haem" iron. The bioavailability/absorption of non-haem iron is very poor (less than 5%) because the diet contains large amounts of inhibitors like phytates (in bran), phosphates, oxalates (in leafy vegetables), and tannins (in tea).

**Q3: What dietary advice will you give to an anaemic patient to improve iron absorption?** _Answer:_ I would advise them to consume Vitamin C rich foods (like Amla, guava, or citrus fruits) along with their meals, as ascorbic acid promotes iron absorption by reducing ferric iron to ferrous iron. I would also advise against drinking tea immediately after meals due to tannins, and suggest germinating/sprouting pulses to reduce phytate content.

**Q4: Explain the "6x6x6" strategy of Anaemia Mukt Bharat.** _Answer:_ It targets 6 beneficiary groups (children 6-59m, children 5-9y, adolescents 10-19y, women of reproductive age 15-49y, pregnant women, and lactating women) using 6 interventions (IFA supplementation, deworming, BCC/delayed cord clamping, point-of-care testing, mandatory fortified foods, and treating endemic causes like malaria), monitored by 6 institutional mechanisms.

**Q5: What are the specific IFA tablet colours given to different age groups under AMB?** _Answer:_ Children aged 5-9 years receive Pink tablets. Adolescents aged 10-19 years receive Blue tablets. Pregnant and lactating women receive Red tablets.

**Q6: What is the dose and duration of IFA prophylaxis for a pregnant woman?** _Answer:_ Under the Anaemia Mukt Bharat strategy, a pregnant woman should receive 100 mg of elemental iron and 500 mcg of folic acid daily for 180 days during the antenatal period, and continue for another 180 days during the postnatal period.

**Q7: How does family planning directly impact the prevention of iron deficiency anaemia?** _Answer:_ Repeated pregnancies and short birth intervals deplete maternal iron stores, as each pregnancy requires about 60,000 extra kcal and significant iron transfer to the foetus. Spacing births allows the mother time to replenish her iron reserves. However, IUDs should be used cautiously as they can increase menstrual blood loss.
