**DIABETES MELLITUS (DM) VIVA: HIGH-YIELD EXAM NOTES**

**I. DEFINITION AND BURDEN OF DISEASE**

- **Definition:** Diabetes describes a group of metabolic disorders characterized and identified by the presence of hyperglycaemia in the absence of treatment, resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both.
- **Magnitude in India:** India is home to 77 million diabetics, second only to China in the world. The overall prevalence is 11.8%, with 12% in males and 11.7% in females, showing no sex difference. It is higher in urban areas (10.9 to 14.2%) compared to rural areas (3.0 to 7.8%).
- **The Iceberg Phenomenon:** Diabetes is an "iceberg" disease, meaning the unknown morbidity (the submerged portion) far exceeds the known morbidity.

**II. CLASSIFICATION OF DIABETES (WHO 2019)**

1. **Type 1 diabetes:** Absolute insulin deficiency due to B-cell destruction (mostly immune-mediated); onset most common in childhood/early adulthood.
2. **Type 2 diabetes:** Most common type (>90% of cases globally); involves varying degrees of B-cell dysfunction and insulin resistance; strongly associated with overweight and obesity.
3. **Hybrid forms:** E.g., slowly evolving immune-mediated diabetes of adults, ketosis-prone type 2 diabetes.
4. **Other specific types:** Monogenic diabetes, diseases of exocrine pancreas, drug- or chemical-induced, infection-related.
5. **Unclassified diabetes**.
6. **Hyperglycaemia first detected during pregnancy:** Includes Diabetes mellitus in pregnancy and Gestational diabetes mellitus.

**III. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DETERMINANTS (RISK FACTORS)**

- **Agent Factors:** Insulin deficiency/resistance due to pancreatic disorders, beta-cell destruction (viral/chemical), genetic defects, or auto-immunity.
- **Host Factors:**
    - _Age & Sex:_ Type 2 rises steeply with age and in middle years; Type 1 is highest in 10-14 year olds.
    - _Genetic factors:_ High concordance (90%) in identical twins for Type 2. Type 1 is associated with HLA-DR3 and DR4.
    - _Obesity:_ Central adiposity (waist circumference and waist-to-hip ratio) is a powerful determinant of insulin resistance and Type 2 diabetes.
    - _Maternal diabetes:_ Offspring are often large/heavy at birth and have a high risk of developing Type 2 diabetes.
- **Environmental Factors:**
    - _Sedentary lifestyle_ and lack of physical exercise.
    - _Diet:_ High saturated fat and low dietary fibre.
    - _Malnutrition:_ Protein-energy malnutrition (PEM) in infancy can damage B-cells.
    - _Others:_ Alcohol abuse, viral infections (coxsackie B4, mumps, rubella), stress, and chemical agents.

**IV. DIAGNOSIS AND SCREENING**

- **Urine Testing (Glycosuria):** Not considered an appropriate tool for case-finding or epidemiological surveys due to lack of sensitivity (yields too many false-negatives).
- **Blood Sugar Testing (WHO Diagnostic Criteria 2019):**
    - _Fasting plasma glucose:_ $\ge$ 126 mg/dL ($\ge$ 7.0 mmol/L).
    - _2-hour post-load plasma glucose:_ $\ge$ 200 mg/dL ($\ge$ 11.1 mmol/L).
    - _HbA1c:_ $\ge$ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol).
    - _Random plasma glucose:_ $\ge$ 200 mg/dL ($\ge$ 11.1 mmol/L).
- **Target Population for Screening (High-Risk Strategy):** Adults aged 40 and over, positive family history, obese persons, women delivering babies >4.5 kg, and patients with premature atherosclerosis.

**V. PREVENTION AND CONTROL**

**1. Primary Prevention:**

- **Population Strategy:** Needs primordial prevention to stop the emergence of risk factors. It aims to maintain normal body weight, promote regular physical exercise, ensure healthy nutritional habits (high dietary fibre, low saturated fat, avoiding sweet foods), and integrate with CHD prevention programmes.
- **High-Risk Strategy:** Directed at sedentary, over-nourished, or obese individuals. It involves correcting these factors, avoiding diabetogenic drugs (e.g., oral contraceptives), and reducing atherosclerosis risk factors (smoking, high BP).

**2. Secondary Prevention:**

- **Aims:** Maintain blood glucose as close to normal as possible and maintain ideal body weight.
- **Management:** Diet alone, diet + oral hypoglycaemics, or diet + insulin.
- **Monitoring:** Routine checking of blood sugar, urine (proteins/ketones), blood pressure, vision, and foot care.
- **HbA1c:** Estimated half-yearly. It reflects the mean blood glucose levels over the previous 2-3 months.
- **Self-care:** Teaching the diabetic patient home blood glucose monitoring, foot care, adherence to diet, and recognition of hypoglycaemia.

**3. Tertiary Prevention:**

- Establishment of specialized diabetic clinics to manage and prevent severe complications like blindness (retinopathy), kidney failure (nephropathy), and amputations (diabetic foot/gangrene).

**PROBABLE VIVA QUESTIONS & ANSWERS (PSM)**

**Q1: What are the current WHO criteria for diagnosing Diabetes Mellitus?** _Answer:_ The 2019 WHO diagnostic cut-offs are: Fasting plasma glucose $\ge$ 126 mg/dL; 2-hour post-load plasma glucose $\ge$ 200 mg/dL; HbA1c $\ge$ 6.5%; or a random plasma glucose $\ge$ 200 mg/dL.

**Q2: Why is urine screening for glucose not recommended for epidemiological surveys?** _Answer:_ Urine testing lacks sensitivity, meaning it misses many milder cases of diabetes (false-negatives). It also has false-positives since normal people can occasionally have glycosuria. Therefore, blood sugar measurement is the cornerstone.

**Q3: What is "Syndrome X" or Insulin Resistance Syndrome?** _Answer:_ It is the association of hyperglycaemia, hyperinsulinaemia, dyslipidaemia, and hypertension, often exaggerated by central obesity. It predisposes the individual to severe cardiovascular complications.

**Q4: Under the National Programme (NPCDCS), what is the screening protocol for diabetes?** _Answer:_ Under the National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases and Stroke (NPCDCS), opportunistic screening is carried out for all persons above the age of 30 years and all pregnant women. It is done at the sub-centre using blood glucose strip methods (glucometers).

**Q5: What is the significance of HbA1c in diabetes management?** _Answer:_ Glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c) provides a long-term index of glucose control. It reflects the average blood glucose concentration over the lifespan of a red blood cell, which is approximately the preceding 2-3 months.

**Q6: What specific advice will you give a diabetic patient as part of secondary prevention regarding "Self-care"?** _Answer:_ The patient should be educated on home blood glucose monitoring, adherence to prescribed diet and drugs, absolute abstinence from alcohol and smoking, regular foot hygiene to prevent ulcers, and how to recognize warning signs of hypoglycaemia.

**Q7: Which anthropometric indices are the best predictors for the risk of Type 2 Diabetes?** _Answer:_ Central adiposity is the strongest predictor. Waist circumference and the waist-to-hip ratio (WHR) are more powerful determinants of subsequent risk of Type 2 diabetes than BMI alone.
