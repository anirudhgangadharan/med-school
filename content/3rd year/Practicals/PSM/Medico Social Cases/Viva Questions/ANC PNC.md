**PROBABLE VIVA QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS FOR AN ANC CASE PRESENTATION**

**Q1. What are the primary objectives of Antenatal Care (ANC)?**

- To promote, protect, and maintain the health of the mother during pregnancy.
- To detect "high-risk" cases and give them special attention.
- To foresee complications and prevent them.
- To remove anxiety and dread associated with delivery.
- To reduce maternal and infant mortality and morbidity.
- To teach the mother elements of child care, nutrition, personal hygiene, and environmental sanitation.
- To sensitize the mother to the need for family planning, including advice to cases seeking medical termination of pregnancy.

**Q2. What is the ideal and minimum recommended schedule for antenatal visits?**

- **Ideal Schedule:** Once a month during the first 7 months, twice a month during the 8th month, and weekly thereafter if everything is normal.
- **Minimum Targeted Schedule (4 Visits):**
    - _1st visit:_ Within 12 weeks (preferably as soon as pregnancy is suspected).
    - _2nd visit:_ Between 14 and 26 weeks.
    - _3rd visit:_ Between 28 and 34 weeks.
    - _4th visit:_ Between 36 weeks and term.

**Q3. How do you calculate the Expected Date of Delivery (EDD)?**

- EDD is calculated by adding 9 months and 7 days to the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP).

**Q4. What are the normal weight gain expectations during pregnancy, and what does abnormal weight gain indicate?**

- **Normal Weight Gain:** A normal woman should gain 10-12 kg during her pregnancy (on average 9-11 kg). Ideally, a pregnant woman gains around 2 kg per month after the first trimester.
- **Abnormal Weight Gain:** A low weight gain of less than 2 kg per month indicates inadequate dietary intake and should raise the suspicion of Intrauterine Growth Retardation (IUGR), resulting in a low birth weight baby. Excessive weight gain of more than 3 kg in a month should raise suspicion of pre-eclampsia or twins.

**Q5. How do you diagnose Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH) and imminent eclampsia during your physical examination?**

- **PIH Criteria:** A systolic blood pressure of 140 mmHg or more, and/or a diastolic blood pressure of 90 mmHg or more, measured on two consecutive readings taken four hours or more apart.
- **Pre-eclampsia:** High blood pressure along with the presence of albumin (proteinuria) in the urine.
- **Imminent Eclampsia (Danger Sign):** If the diastolic blood pressure of the woman is above 110 mmHg, it is a danger sign pointing towards imminent eclampsia, requiring immediate hospitalization.

**Q6. How do you estimate the gestational age based on fundal height during abdominal examination?**

- **12 weeks:** Uterine fundus is just palpable above the abdomen.
- **20 weeks:** Fundus is flat at the lower border of the umbilicus.
- **36 weeks:** Fundus is felt at the level of the xiphisternum.

**Q7. When can you normally detect foetal heart sounds (FHS) and foetal movements?**

- **FHS:** Normally heard after the 6th month (24 weeks), but best heard after the 28th week. Normal rate is 120 to 140 per minute.
- **Foetal Movements (Quickening):** Can be felt by the examiner between the 18th and 22nd weeks by gently palpating the abdomen.

**Q8. Enumerate the "High-Risk" cases in pregnancy (The Risk Approach).**

- Elderly primi (30 years and over) or elderly grandmultiparas.
- Short-statured primi (140 cm and below).
- Malpresentations (e.g., breech, transverse lie).
- Antepartum haemorrhage or threatened abortion.
- Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia.
- Anaemia, twins, or hydramnios.
- Previous stillbirth, intrauterine death, or manual removal of placenta.
- Prolonged pregnancy (14 days after expected date of delivery).
- History of previous caesarean or instrumental delivery.
- Pregnancy associated with systemic medical conditions (cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, diabetes, tuberculosis, liver disease, malaria, asthma, HIV, RTI, STI, etc.).

**Q9. What are the routine laboratory investigations advised during an antenatal check-up?**

- **At Sub-centre:** Pregnancy detection test, haemoglobin examination, urine test for albumin and sugar, rapid malaria test.
- **At PHC/CHC/FRU:** Blood group (including Rh factor), VDRL/RPR test for syphilis, HIV testing, blood sugar testing, and HBsAg for hepatitis B infection.

**Q10. What is the National prophylaxis schedule for Tetanus in a pregnant woman?**

- For an unimmunized mother, **two doses of adsorbed tetanus toxoid** should be given.
- **Schedule:** The first dose is given at 16-20 weeks, and the second dose at 20-24 weeks of pregnancy.
- **Interval:** The minimum interval between the two doses should be one month.
- **Late Registration:** No pregnant woman should be denied even one dose of tetanus toxoid if she is seen late in pregnancy.
- **Previously Immunized:** If the mother was immunized earlier, one booster dose will provide necessary cover for subsequent pregnancies during the next 5 years.

**Q11. What is the prophylactic dose for nutritional anaemia (IFA supplementation) in pregnancy?**

- The Government of India distributes tablets containing **100 mg of elemental iron and 500 mcg of folic acid** to pregnant women.
- It should be taken daily for **100 days** through antenatal clinics, primary health centres, and sub-centres.

**Q12. What specific dietary advice will you give to a pregnant woman?**

- A pregnancy in total duration consumes about **60,000 kcal** over and above normal metabolic requirements, which demands an extra **300 kcal per day**.
- The diet should meet the increased needs of the mother and foetus, emphasizing iron, folic acid, calcium, and adequate protein.

**Q13. What are the "Warning Signs" you must educate the mother to report immediately?** The mother should be given clear-cut instructions to report immediately in case of:

1. Swelling of the feet.
2. Fits (convulsions).
3. Headache.
4. Blurring of vision.
5. Bleeding or discharge per vagina.
6. Any other unusual symptoms.
