### **1. CASE: ACUTE DIARRHOEAL DISEASE (ADD)**

**A. Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI)**

- **Concept:** IMNCI is a strategy for the integrated case management of the most common childhood problems, including diarrhoea, acute respiratory infections (ARI), malaria, measles, and malnutrition.
- **Core Principles:** It promotes evidence-based assessment and a syndromic approach using colour-coded treatment:
    - **Pink (Urgent Referral):** Severe dehydration or danger signs. Pre-referral treatment is given, and the child is urgently referred to an out-patient health facility or hospital.
    - **Yellow (Treatment at Out-patient Facility):** Some dehydration. Specific local infections are treated, oral drugs (like ORS and Zinc) are given, and the caretaker is advised and taught follow-up.
    - **Green (Home Management):** No dehydration. The caretaker is counselled on home care, continuing feeding, giving oral drugs, and knowing when to return immediately.
- **Indian Adaptation:** The Indian version includes the 0-7 days age group, incorporates national guidelines on malaria, anaemia, and vitamin A supplementation, and begins training with sick young infants up to 2 months.

**B. Diarrhoeal Disease Control Programme**

- **Introduction & Objective:** Started in 1978 to reduce mortality and morbidity due to diarrhoeal diseases. In 1985-86, the National Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT) Programme was introduced.
- **Strategies:**
    - _Short-term:_ Emphasizes Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT) with the new WHO/UNICEF recommended reduced osmolarity ORS (total osmolarity 245 mOsm/L) to prevent dehydration.
    - _Long-term (Better MCH Practices):_ Promotion of exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months, appropriate weaning practices, supplementary feeding, and maternal nutrition.
    - _Preventive Strategies:_ Health education, Vitamin A supplementation, immunization against measles (can prevent up to 25% of diarrhoeal deaths in <5 children), and Rotavirus vaccination.

**C. Integrated Global Action Plan for Pneumonia & Diarrhoea (GAPPD)**

- **Concept:** A cohesive approach by WHO/UNICEF to end preventable pneumonia and diarrhoea deaths by bringing together critical services.
- **Framework (Protect, Prevent, Treat):**
    - _Protect:_ Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, adequate complementary feeding, and Vitamin A supplementation.
    - _Prevent:_ Vaccines (Rotavirus, Measles, Hib, PCV, Pertussis), safe sanitation, hand-washing with soap, and safe drinking water.
    - _Treat:_ Case management at health facilities and community level using low-osmolarity ORS, zinc, and continued feeding.
- **Goals (by 2025):** Reduce diarrhoea mortality in <5 children to fewer than 1 per 1000 live births, and reduce severe diarrhoea incidence by 75%.

### **2. CASE: ANTENATAL CARE (ANC)**

**A. Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) Programme**

- **RCH Phase I (1997):** Integrated Child Survival and Safe Motherhood (CSSM) with RTI/STD control. Key ANC interventions included early registration of pregnancy (within 12-16 weeks), provision of minimum 3 antenatal check-ups, safe delivery, and tetanus immunization.
- **RCH Phase II (2005):** Focused on reducing maternal and infant mortality. Major strategies included:
    - _Essential Obstetric Care:_ Promoting institutional delivery and skilled attendance at delivery (ANMs/LHVs trained to use emergency drugs).
    - _Emergency Obstetric Care:_ Operationalizing First Referral Units (FRUs) and 24x7 PHCs/CHCs for round-the-clock delivery services.

**B. Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)**

- **Introduction:** Launched on 12th April 2005 under the National Rural Health Mission (NRHM), modifying the National Maternity Benefit scheme.
- **Objective:** To reduce maternal and neonatal mortality by encouraging institutional delivery among poor pregnant women.
- **Key Features:** It is a 100% centrally sponsored scheme providing conditional cash assistance for delivery and post-partum care. ASHA acts as a link worker to escort pregnant women to health centres.
- **Target Groups:** In Low Performing States (LPS), all women delivering in government institutions get the benefit. In High Performing States (HPS), benefits are restricted to BPL/SC/ST women up to 2 live births. Payments are made via Direct Benefit Transfer.

**C. Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA)**

- **Concept:** Launched in June 2016 by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
- **Provisions:** All pregnant women in the country are provided a fixed day, free of cost, assured, and quality antenatal care on the **9th day of every month**.
- **Key Feature:** The campaign utilizes private sector health care providers as volunteers to provide specialist care at government facilities to identify high-risk pregnancies.

### **3. CASE: ACUTE RESPIRATORY INFECTION (ARI)**

**A. Universal Immunization Programme (UIP)**

- **Objective:** Launched in India in 1985 to reduce mortality and morbidity from vaccine-preventable diseases.
- **Relevance to ARI:** UIP directly targets respiratory pathogens. It provides immunization against Diphtheria, Pertussis, Measles, _Haemophilus influenzae_ type b (through the Pentavalent vaccine), and pneumococcal pneumonia (through the Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine - PCV, introduced in 2017).
- **Mission Indradhanush:** Launched in December 2014 to fully immunize >90% of children who are unvaccinated or partially vaccinated.

**B. IMNCI & GAPPD for ARI**

- **IMNCI Approach:** Similar to ADD, ARI is assessed for danger signs and classified. _Pink_ (severe pneumonia) requires urgent referral after the first dose of an appropriate antibiotic. _Yellow_ (pneumonia) requires treatment with local antibiotics and advice for home care. _Green_ (cough/cold) requires home management.
- **GAPPD Framework for Pneumonia:** _Protect_ (breastfeeding), _Prevent_ (Vaccines like PCV/Hib/Pertussis/Measles, reducing household air pollution), and _Treat_ (appropriate antibiotics and oxygen). The goal is to reduce pneumonia mortality to fewer than 3 per 1000 live births by 2025.

### **4. CASE: DIABETES MELLITUS (DM) & HYPERTENSION (HTN)**

**A. National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases and Stroke (NPCDCS)**

- **Objectives:** Prevent and control common NCDs through behaviour and lifestyle changes, provide early diagnosis and management, build capacity, and establish palliative care.
- **Service Delivery & Screening Levels:**
    - _Sub-Centre Level:_ Health promotion for lifestyle changes. **Opportunistic screening** of the population above 30 years using BP measurement and blood glucose strip methods. Suspected cases are referred to CHCs.
    - _CHC Level:_ NCD clinics perform diagnosis, required investigations (blood sugar, lipid profile, ECG), and stabilization of common CVD, diabetes, and stroke cases.
    - _District Hospital Level:_ NCD clinics screen persons >30 years, manage complicated cases, provide home-based palliative care, and conduct health education.
    - _Urban Health Check-up:_ Targets urban slum populations for diabetes and high blood pressure screening.
- **Guidelines for Referral & Treatment:** A random blood sugar $\ge$ 140 mg/dl or systolic BP > 140 mmHg / diastolic BP > 90 mmHg warrants referral to a medical officer for confirmation. Once diagnosed, a 1-month to 3-month supply of drugs is provided, with active follow-up by ASHA/ANM for compliance.

### **5. CASE: IRON DEFICIENCY ANAEMIA (IDA)**

**A. Nutritional Anaemia Prophylaxis Programme**

- **Concept:** Launched during the 4th Five Year Plan to prevent nutritional anaemia via the distribution of Iron and Folic Acid (IFA) tablets to pregnant women and young children through MCH centres and PHCs.
- **Dosage Guidelines (Revised):** Infants (6m-5yrs) receive 20 mg elemental iron + 100 mcg folic acid daily for 100 days. Children (6-10 yrs) receive 30 mg elemental iron + 250 mcg folic acid for 100 days. Pregnant/lactating women receive 100 mg elemental iron + 500 mcg folic acid daily for 100 days.

**B. National Iron Plus Initiative (NIPI) & WIFS**

- **NIPI:** Launched in 2013, it uses a life-cycle approach providing age and dose-specific IFA supplementation for under-5 children, 6-10 years age group, adolescents, pregnant/lactating women, and women in the reproductive age group.
- **Weekly Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation (WIFS):** Implemented for adolescent girls and boys (10-19 years) providing supervised weekly supplements of 100 mg elemental iron and 500 mcg folic acid on a fixed day, along with biannual deworming (Albendazole 400 mg).

**C. Anaemia Mukt Bharat Strategy**

- **Strategy:** Launched in 2018 under POSHAN Abhiyaan aiming for a 3% annual reduction in anaemia. It utilizes a **6x6x6 strategy**: 6 target beneficiary groups, 6 interventions, and 6 institutional mechanisms.
- **6 Interventions:** Prophylactic IFA supplementation, deworming, intensified behaviour change communication (including delayed cord clamping), point-of-care testing/treatment using digital methods, mandatory provision of fortified foods, and addressing non-nutritional causes like malaria and fluorosis.
- **Color-Coded IFA Tablets:**
    - 5-9 years: Weekly _Pink_ tablet (45 mg Fe + 400 mcg FA).
    - 10-19 years: Weekly _Blue_ tablet (60 mg Fe + 500 mcg FA).
    - Pregnant/Lactating: Daily _Red_ tablet (100 mg Fe + 500 mcg FA) for 180 days during ANC and 180 days during PNC.

### **6. CASE: PROTEIN ENERGY MALNUTRITION (PEM)**

**A. Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)**

- **Concept:** Started in 1975 to provide an integrated package of early childhood services. Delivered at the village level by _Anganwadi Workers_.
- **Target Groups:** Children <6 years, pregnant and lactating mothers, and adolescent girls (11-18 years).
- **Services:** Supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check-ups, medical referral, non-formal pre-school education, and health/nutrition education.
- **Nutritional Norms:** Normal child (6-72m) gets 500 kcal + 12-15g protein. Severely malnourished child gets 800 kcal + 20-25g protein. Pregnant/nursing mother gets 600 kcal + 18-20g protein.

**B. Nutritional Rehabilitation Centres (NRCs)**

- **Concept:** Facility-based units providing medical and nutritional care for children under 5 years with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and medical complications.
- **Management Phases:**
    1. _Stabilization Phase (1-2 days):_ Treats life-threatening problems; feeds using a "Starter diet" to promote recovery of normal metabolic function.
    2. _Transition Phase (2-3 days):_ Gradual transition from starter diet to catch-up diet once the child is clinically stable.
    3. _Rehabilitation Phase:_ Aims for rapid weight gain and physical/emotional development. Follow-up is done by ASHA and Anganwadi workers.

**C. Mid-Day Meal Programme (PM Poshan Scheme)**

- **Concept:** Launched in 1995 to universalize primary education by increasing enrolment and attendance while simultaneously impacting the nutrition of students (Class I to VIII).
- **Provisions:** Provides a cooked mid-day meal with a minimum of 300 calories and 8 to 12 grams of protein per child. Only iodized salt is to be used for cooking.

**D. Poshan Abhiyan**

- **Concept:** Launched in Dec 2017 to improve the nutritional status of children 0-6 years, adolescent girls, and pregnant/lactating mothers through the convergence of various programmes (ICDS, NHM, Swachh Bharat).
- **Targets:** Prevent and reduce stunting (2% per year), undernutrition (2% per year), anaemia (3% per year), and low birth weight (2% per year).

### **7. CASE: POSTNATAL CARE (PNC)**

**A. Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) & RMNCH+A**

- _JSY_ integrates cash assistance with post-partum care. The ASHA worker receives her incentive only after the post-natal visit to the beneficiary and ensuring the child is immunized for BCG.
- _RMNCH+A_ provides a "Continuum of Care". Postnatal priorities include home-based newborn care (HBNC), early detection of maternal/newborn illnesses, prompt referral, exclusive breastfeeding, and postpartum family planning (PPIUCD/sterilization).

**B. India Newborn Action Plan (INAP) & HBNC**

- **INAP (2014):** Aims to attain a "Single Digit Neonatal Mortality Rate" and "Single Digit Stillbirth Rate" by 2030. It includes strategic packages for immediate newborn care, care of healthy newborn, care of small/sick newborn, and care beyond newborn survival.
- **Home Based Newborn Care (HBNC):** ASHA conducts scheduled home visits up to 42 days of life (Days 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42 for institutional deliveries; extra Day 1 visit for home deliveries). ASHA checks weight, temperature, supports exclusive breastfeeding, provides cord/eye care, and identifies danger signs of sepsis for urgent referral.

**C. Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK)**

- **Concept:** Launched in 2013 for child health screening and early intervention services (0-18 years).
- **Scope (The 4 D's):** Early detection and free treatment of Defects at birth, Diseases, Deficiencies, and Development delays including disability. Includes facility-based newborn screening by ANMs/Medical Officers and community-based screening by ASHAs up to 6 weeks.

**D. Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Ashwasan (SUMAN)**

- **Concept:** Launched in October 2019 to provide assured, dignified, respectful, and quality healthcare at no cost.
- **Goal:** Zero tolerance for denial of services for every woman and newborn visiting a public health facility to end all preventable maternal and newborn deaths.

### **8. CASE: PUO / DENGUE**

**A. National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP)**

- **Concept:** The umbrella programme for the prevention and control of vector-borne diseases (Malaria, Filariasis, Kala-azar, Japanese Encephalitis, Dengue, and Chikungunya).
- **Three-Pronged Strategy:**
    1. _Disease Management:_ Early case detection, complete treatment, strengthening of referral services, and epidemic preparedness (e.g., introduction of bivalent Rapid Diagnostic Tests for malaria and NS1 antigen kits for Dengue).
    2. _Integrated Vector Management (IVM):_ Utilizing Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) in high-risk areas, use of Long-Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLINs), anti-larval measures, and environmental source reduction (crucial for _Aedes aegypti_ control in Dengue).
    3. _Supportive Interventions:_ Behaviour Change Communication (BCC) to increase community awareness, public-private partnerships, and human resource development.
- **Specific Dengue Control Measures:** Dengue has been a notifiable disease since 2016. The mid-term plan emphasizes establishing sentinel surveillance hospitals (over 521 identified) linked with Apex Referral Laboratories equipped with IgM capture ELISA and NS1 kits provided free of cost to ensure early diagnosis and containment.
