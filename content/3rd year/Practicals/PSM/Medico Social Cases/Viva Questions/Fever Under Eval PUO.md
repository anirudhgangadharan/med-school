**FEVER UNDER EVALUATION (ACUTE FEBRILE ILLNESS / PUO) VIVA: HIGH-YIELD EXAM NOTES**

**I. SYNDROMIC SURVEILLANCE OF FEVER (UNDER IDSP)** Under the Integrated Disease Surveillance Project (IDSP), paramedical health staff undertake disease surveillance based on broad categories of clinical presentation. Fever is classified and monitored under the following syndromes:

1. **Fever less than 7 days duration:** Without any localizing signs (Suspect: Malaria, Dengue).
2. **Fever with rash:** (Suspect: Measles, Dengue).
3. **Fever with altered sensorium or convulsions:** (Suspect: Japanese Encephalitis, Malaria).
4. **Fever with bleeding from skin or mucous membrane:** (Suspect: Dengue Haemorrhagic Fever, other Viral Haemorrhagic Fevers).
5. **Fever more than 7 days:** With or without localizing signs (Suspect: Typhoid, Tuberculosis, Malaria).

**Trigger Events for Outbreak Investigation (IDSP Guidelines):**

- _Fever < 7 days:_ 5 cases in 1000 population.
- _Fever with rash:_ Two similar cases in a village / 1000 population.
- _Fever with altered consciousness / bleeding / convulsions:_ Two cases in a village / 1000 population.
- _Fever > 7 days:_ More than 2 cases in a village / 1000 population.

**II. DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS OF FEVER (PUBLIC HEALTH PERSPECTIVE)** When evaluating a fever case in the community, the following major communicable diseases must be considered based on clinical and epidemiological markers:

- **Malaria:** Characterized by typical intermittent paroxysms consisting of cold, hot, and sweating stages. P. falciparum may present with irregular or continuous fever, whereas P. vivax and P. ovale have a 48-hour cycle,. Diagnosis is confirmed by microscopy (thick and thin smears) or Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs),.
- **Typhoid (Enteric Fever):** Insidious onset, ascending step-ladder fever, relative bradycardia, abdominal distension, and "rose spots" appearing on the trunk during the second week,. Diagnosis is via blood culture (gold standard) or serology (Widal, Typhidot, IDL Tubex),,.
- **Dengue Fever:** Sudden onset with chills, high fever (39°C to 40°C), intense headache, retro-orbital pain, severe muscle/joint pains, and a biphasic fever curve. NS1 antigen ELISA is used for early detection.
- **Chikungunya:** Sudden onset of fever, chills, and excruciating joint pains (arthralgia) with a morbilliform rash.
- **Leptospirosis:** Mild febrile illness or severe disease with jaundice and renal involvement (Weil's disease) often associated with history of contact with contaminated water or floods,,.
- **Non-typhoid Salmonellosis:** Can invade the blood stream and present as septicaemia with focal lesions, manifesting as pyrexia of unknown origin (PUO).
- **Tuberculosis:** Should be suspected in any patient presenting with persistent fever and/or unremitting cough for more than 2 weeks, along with weight loss or night sweats,.

**III. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL HISTORY TO ELICIT IN A FEVER CASE**

- **Environmental/Vector exposure:** History of mosquito bites, presence of stagnant water/open drains (breeding of _Culex_ for Filariasis/JE), artificial water containers (breeding of _Aedes_ for Dengue/Chikungunya),, or nearby rice fields/pigs (JE),.
- **Water and Food hygiene:** Consumption of unsafe water, unpasteurized milk, or food from outside (Typhoid, Food poisoning, Hepatitis A/E),.
- **Occupational history:** Agricultural workers, sewage workers, or exposure to animal urine (Leptospirosis),, animal handlers/abattoir workers (Brucellosis, Q fever),, or forest workers (Kyasanur Forest Disease, Scrub typhus),.
- **Travel history:** Recent travel to endemic areas (e.g., Malaria, Dengue),,.

**IV. PUBLIC HEALTH INTERVENTIONS & CONTROL MEASURES**

- **Case Finding:**
    - _Active Case Detection (ACD):_ MPWs or ASHAs perform fortnightly house-to-house visits to screen fever cases and collect blood smears.
    - _Passive Case Detection (PCD):_ Screening of fever cases reporting to sub-centres, PHCs, or hospitals.
- **Treatment Policy:** According to the revised drug policy under the National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP), there is no scope for presumptive treatment of malaria; all fever cases diagnosed by microscopy or RDT must be promptly given specific effective treatment,.
- **Vector Control:** Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) for _Anopheles_ (Malaria) and Sandflies (Kala-azar),; source reduction and anti-larval measures for _Aedes_ (Dengue),; use of Long-Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLINs).
- **Sanitation:** Purification of drinking water and safe disposal of excreta to interrupt the faecal-oral route of enteric fevers.

**PROBABLE VIVA QUESTIONS & ANSWERS (PSM)**

**Q1: How is fever categorized for surveillance under the Integrated Disease Surveillance Project (IDSP) by health workers?** _Answer:_ Under IDSP, paramedical staff conduct syndromic surveillance for fever in five categories: (1) Fever less than 7 days duration without localizing signs, (2) Fever with rash, (3) Fever with altered sensorium or convulsions, (4) Fever with bleeding from skin or mucous membrane, and (5) Fever more than 7 days duration.

**Q2: What is the "trigger event" to declare an outbreak for acute fever under IDSP?** _Answer:_ For fever of less than 7 days duration, 5 cases in a 1000 population triggers an investigation. For fever with altered consciousness, convulsions, or bleeding, just 2 cases in a village or 1000 population acts as a trigger.

**Q3: What is the current national policy for treating a suspected fever case for Malaria? Can we give presumptive chloroquine?** _Answer:_ No. According to the revised National Drug Policy on Malaria, there is no scope for presumptive treatment. All fever cases must be tested using a Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) or microscopy, and only upon confirmation should effective treatment (like ACT for falciparum or Chloroquine for vivax) be initiated,.

**Q4: A patient presents with Pyrexia of Unknown Origin (PUO). Which food-borne bacterial infection can specifically present this way with negative stool cultures but positive blood cultures?** _Answer:_ Non-typhoid salmonellae (e.g., _S. cholerae-suis_) can invade the bloodstream leading to septicaemia with focal lesions, which presents as pyrexia of unknown origin.

**Q5: Epidemiologically, how do you differentiate the vector breeding habits if a fever outbreak is suspected to be Dengue vs. Malaria?** _Answer:_ The Dengue vector, _Aedes aegypti_, is a domestic species that breeds in man-made artificial collections of clean water (overhead tanks, coolers, old tyres). The primary rural Malaria vector, _Anopheles culicifacies_, breeds in natural and man-made moving or standing water (irrigation channels, streams). _Aedes_ is a day biter, whereas _Anopheles_ bites from dusk to dawn,.

**Q6: What is the epidemiological significance of the "Infant Parasite Rate" in a community with fever cases?** _Answer:_ The infant parasite rate is the percentage of infants below one year of age showing malaria parasites in their blood. It is the most sensitive index of recent, active transmission of malaria in a locality.

**Q7: Under the National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP), when should a fever case be evaluated as a "Presumptive TB" case?** _Answer:_ Any patient presenting with persistent fever for more than 2 weeks, without a known cause, especially when accompanied by unremitting cough for >2 weeks, weight loss, or night sweats, must be evaluated as a presumptive TB case,.
