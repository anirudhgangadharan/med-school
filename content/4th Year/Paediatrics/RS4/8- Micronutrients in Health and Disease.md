 1. (Vitamin D deficiency/nutritional) Rickets — clinical features, radiological features (using a diagram), diagnosis, management, prevention.

 **NUTRITIONAL RICKETS**

**Definition** Rickets is a metabolic bone disease of growing children characterized by undermineralization of the organic matrix of bone (osteoid), most commonly caused by a nutritional deficiency of Vitamin D and/or calcium.

**Clinical Features** The peak incidence of rickets is between 6 months to 2 years of age. Features are classified by body systems:

- **General/Systemic:**
    - Irritability, restlessness, and profuse sweating over the head (especially during sleep).
    - Delayed motor milestones and waddling gait.
    - Hypotonia, laxity of ligaments (acrobatic rickets), and pot-belly with visceroptosis.
- **Head and Skull:**
    - **Craniotabes:** Earliest sign; softening of the occipital and posterior parietal bones that give in like a "ping-pong ball" when pressed.
    - **Frontal and Parietal Bossing:** Resulting in a box-like head, caput quadratum, or "hot cross bun" appearance.
    - Delayed closure of the anterior fontanelle and widened sutures.
    - Delayed dentition and enamel hypoplasia.
- **Thorax/Chest:**
    - **Rachitic Rosary:** Smooth, rounded, non-tender enlargement of the costochondral junctions.
    - **Harrison Sulcus (Groove):** A transverse depression along the lower border of the chest at the site of diaphragmatic insertion.
    - **Pigeon Chest (Pectus Carinatum):** Outward protrusion of the sternum.
- **Extremities and Spine:**
    - Widening of wrists and ankles (double malleoli sign) due to epiphyseal expansion.
    - Lower limb deformities: **Genu varum** (bow-legs) or **Genu valgum** (knock-knees).
    - Spinal deformities like kyphosis or scoliosis.

**Radiological Features** Radiological changes are characteristically and best visualized at the metaphyses of rapidly growing bones, primarily the wrist (lower ends of the radius and ulna).

- **Cupping:** Saucer-like depression of the metaphyseal margin.
- **Flaring:** Widening of the metaphyseal ends, giving a "champagne glass" appearance.
- **Fraying:** Loss of the provisional zone of calcification, causing a blurred, ragged, or "bristles in a brush" appearance.
- **Increased Joint Space:** Widened radiolucent distance between the epiphyseal center and the metaphyseal end of the shaft (due to uncalcified osteoid).
- **Osteopenia:** Generalized reduction in bone density with a ground-glass appearance and prominent trabeculae.
- **Green-stick fractures** and periosteal reaction may be noted.

**Diagnosis** Diagnosis is established through biochemical and radiological evaluation:

- **Serum Calcium:** Normal or slightly low.
- **Serum Phosphate:** Low.
- **Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP):** Elevated (a sensitive and early indicator of increased bone turnover).
- **25-Hydroxyvitamin D (25-OHD):** Markedly reduced (<12 ng/mL indicates deficiency).
- **Parathyroid Hormone (PTH):** Elevated (secondary hyperparathyroidism to compensate for low calcium).

**Management** Treatment requires the administration of Vitamin D along with adequate calcium to promote remineralization and prevent hypocalcemic tetany (hungry bone syndrome).

- **Vitamin D Therapy:**
    - _Current Daily Guidelines:_ Lower daily doses of cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3) over 12 weeks are preferred.
        - < 6 months: 2,000 IU daily.
        - 6–12 months: 2,000 IU daily (or 60,000 IU monthly as an alternative).
        - > 12 months: 3,000 IU daily (or 60,000 IU every 2 weeks for 5 doses).
            
    - _Stoss Therapy (Mega-dose):_ Historically, a single oral or intramuscular bolus of 300,000 to 600,000 IU was given. However, this is largely discouraged in current guidelines due to the risk of hypercalcemia and nephrocalcinosis.
- **Calcium Supplementation:**
    - Simultaneous oral calcium supplements (30–75 mg/kg/day, maximum 500 mg/day) must be given for at least 2 months.
- **Monitoring Progress:** Radiological healing is marked by the appearance of a dense "white line of healing" (calcification) in the metaphysis, usually seen within 10 to 14 days to 4 weeks of adequate therapy. Serum ALP levels will gradually decline.

**Prevention**

- **Sunlight Exposure:** Adequate exposure of 17–30 minutes for infants and 30–45 minutes for older children (exposing 15–40% body surface area) between 11 AM and 3 PM is highly recommended.
- **Supplementation:** All exclusively breastfed infants (or those taking < 1 liter of formula/day) should receive **400 IU/day** of Vitamin D from birth to 12 months of age.
- High-risk groups (e.g., those on anticonvulsants or with malabsorption) need 600–800 IU daily to prevent rickets.
- **Dietary:** Ensure adequate daily maintenance intake of calcium and Vitamin D post-treatment through fortified milk, dairy products, egg yolk, and fish oils.
