### **1. DEFINITION OF PUBERTY**

Puberty is defined as the sequence of biological events by which an individual undergoes rapid changes in body structure and is transformed into a young adult, attaining secondary sexual characteristics and reproductive capability.

### **2. PHYSIOLOGICAL MECHANISMS**

The onset of puberty is triggered by genetic and environmental factors that activate the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis.

- **Hypothalamus:** Kisspeptin, a hypothalamic peptide, acts as the primary regulator. It initiates pulses of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH).
- **Pituitary Gland:** GnRH stimulates the pituitary to produce gonadotropins, namely luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH).
- **Gonads & Adrenals:** Gonadotropins stimulate the gonads to produce sex steroids (estrogen in girls and testosterone in boys), which are responsible for the development of secondary sexual characteristics. Concurrently, adrenal androgens cause the development of sexual hair, acne, and underarm odor.

### **3. PHYSICAL CHANGES**

**a. Sequence of Puberty in Girls** The onset of puberty in girls typically occurs between 8 and 13 years of age.

- **Thelarche (Breast Development):** Usually the first sign of puberty.
- **Pubarche:** Appearance of pubic hair follows breast budding.
- **Growth Spurt:** Maximum height velocity occurs relatively early in puberty (Tanner stages II–III).
- **Menarche:** The onset of menstruation occurs at an average age of 12.6 years, typically 2 to 2.5 years after thelarche.

**b. Sequence of Puberty in Boys** The onset of puberty in boys typically occurs between 9 and 14 years of age.

- **Testicular Enlargement:** The earliest change is an increase in testicular volume (reaching 4 mL) or length (2.5 cm).
- **Pubarche and Penile Growth:** Appearance of pubic hair is followed by the lengthening and enlargement of the penis.
- **Spermarche:** The production of sperms occurs during mid-adolescence.
- **Growth Spurt:** Peak height velocity occurs later than in girls (typically Tanner stages III–IV).
- **Laryngeal Growth:** Manifests as the cracking of the voice during mid-puberty.

**c. Sexual Maturity Rating (SMR) / Tanner Staging** Sexual growth is clinically quantified into five stages (Stage 1 to Stage 5), where Stage 1 indicates prepubescent status and Stage 5 indicates full mature adult development. The rating is based on pubic hair and genital development in boys, and pubic hair and breast development in girls.

**d. Somatic Growth and Body Composition** During the pubertal growth spurt, adolescents attain approximately 50% of their adult weight and 25% of their adult height.

- **Boys:** Major weight gain is attributed to an exuberant increase in lean body mass (muscle tissue) and bone diameter.
- **Girls:** Weight gain is predominantly due to fat deposition in a characteristic female distribution (breasts, buttocks, thighs).

### **4. PSYCHOLOGICAL AND COGNITIVE CHANGES**

The psychological transformation involves the development of an independent identity, self-esteem, and new relationships. It progresses through three phases: **a. Early Adolescence (10–13 years)**

- **Cognitive:** Concrete thinking persists. Adolescents are highly impulsive and have a limited ability to perceive the future implications of current behavior.
- **Psychological:** They are highly self-conscious, preoccupied with their changing body image, and very sensitive to peer opinions.
- **Social:** Peer interactions are primarily within same-sex groups.

**b. Middle Adolescence (14–16 years)**

- **Cognitive:** Abstract reasoning begins to develop.
- **Psychological:** There is a surge in emotional autonomy and sexual drive, leading to experimentation and dating.
- **Social:** Acceptance by the peer group becomes critically important. The adolescent struggles for independence, frequently leading to conflicts and challenges against parental authority.

**c. Late Adolescence (17–20 years)**

- **Cognitive & Psychological:** Characterized by idealism, absolutism, and a relatively stable body image. The individual establishes a distinct identity and makes long-term career decisions.
- **Social:** Intimacy and personal relations take precedence over peer group conformity.

### **5. COMMON ADOLESCENT EATING DISORDERS**

Adolescents, particularly girls, are highly vulnerable to eating disorders due to rapid hormonal changes, body image concerns driven by distorted perceptions, and extreme societal/peer pressure for slimness.

**a. Anorexia Nervosa**

- **Definition:** An eating disorder characterized by a distorted body image and an intense fear of becoming fat, leading the adolescent to impose severe dietetic restrictions on themselves.
- **Clinical Features:** The subject remains under-weight (body weight <85% of expected) but still perceives themselves as fat. It may involve restricted eating or increased physical activity combined with self-induced vomiting, laxatives, or diuretics.
- **Complications:** Gross malnutrition leads to widespread systemic disturbances, including electrolyte imbalances, postural hypotension, cardiac arrhythmias, congestive cardiac failure (CCF), bone marrow hypoplasia, hypothermia, constipation, secondary amenorrhea, and dry skin with fine lanugo hair.

**b. Bulimia**

- **Definition:** Characterized by recurrent episodes of binge eating (eating in large quantities) alternating with inappropriate compensatory behaviors to prevent weight gain.
- **Clinical Features:** Compensatory tactics include self-induced vomiting and the misuse of laxatives or diuretics. These episodes typically occur at least twice a week for 3 months. It primarily affects females aged 10–19 years.
- **Comorbidities:** Highly associated with underlying depression, anxiety, suicidal ideation, and obsessive-compulsive disorders.

**c. Management of Eating Disorders** The management of both anorexia nervosa and bulimia requires a comprehensive, multidisciplinary approach:

- **Nutritional Rehabilitation:** Supervised restoration of dietary intake to correct malnutrition and systemic complications.
- **Psychotherapy:** Focused on resolving distorted body perceptions, building self-esteem, and managing underlying anxiety or familial conflicts.
- **Pharmacotherapy:** Antidepressant agents may be required for associated mood disorders or depression.
