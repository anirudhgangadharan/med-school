### **1. OVERVIEW OF BEHAVIORAL PROBLEMS**

Behavioral problems represent a significant deviation from normal behavior and are often relatively stable and internalized. The etiology of behavioral disorders is multifactorial, including maladjustment at home and/or school, and faulty emotional environments. Faulty parental attitudes, such as overprotection, rejection, unrealistic expectations, overcriticism, and marital disharmony, are the single most vital cause of behavioral difficulties.

### **2. THUMB SUCKING**

- **Definition & Age of Onset:** Thumb sucking is a common habit problem that is considered normal in infants and toddlers. It typically peaks by 18–21 months of age and usually disappears by the age of 4 years.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** The cause is generally rooted in a kind of insecurity, conflict, or hostility, and the child seemingly draws a sense of pleasure and comfort from this self-stimulation.
- **Complications:** While harmless initially, its persistence beyond 3 to 4 years of age is socially unacceptable and can lead to dental malalignment, such as an "open bite" and distortion of the maxillary arch and incisor teeth.
- **Management:** For children below 4 years, parents should be reassured and advised not to be "fussy" over this benign problem. Beyond 4 years of age, the child should be motivated to refrain from the habit using both positive and negative reinforcements.

### **3. NAIL BITING**

- **Definition & Age of Onset:** Nail biting is a habit phenomenon typically demonstrated by children beyond 4 years of age. Unlike thumb sucking, it may continue up to adolescence and even into later adult life.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Similar to thumb sucking, it stems from insecurity, hostility, or suppressed tension.
- **Management:** Treatment primarily consists of reassurance to the parents, advising them to avoid making a fuss or causing undue anxiety over the problem.

### **4. FEEDING PROBLEMS**

Feeding problems are exceptionally common in infants and young children, often stemming from poor mothering, faulty techniques, or psychological conflicts.

- **Regurgitation (Posseting):** The phenomenon of bringing up a little of the swallowed feed along with swallowed air. If the child habitually chews the regurgitated milk, it is called "rumination," which risks aspiration and makes the baby smelly. It is often due to poor feeding techniques leading to excessive wind swallowing.
- **Underfeeding ("Bottle Baby Disease"):** Often results from highly diluted artificial formulas due to ignorance or economic considerations. The baby feeds quickly, is restless, cries excessively, and shows poor weight gain or failure to thrive.
- **Overfeeding:** Forcible feeding of large amounts causes regurgitation, projectile vomiting, excessive crying, fatty diarrhea, and infantile obesity.
- **Bottle Addiction:** Occurs when parents fail to replace the bottle with a cup and spoon at 6 months; continuing bottle feeding beyond one year is unwise.
- **Fussy Eating:** Many normal or thin children are perceived as "fussy" by parents who chase them with food bowls and indulge in forcible feeding, which the child resents. Parents must completely stop forcible feeding, cajoling, or bribing, as their anxiety is usually unfounded.

### **5. BREATH-HOLDING SPELLS (INFANTILE SYNCOPE)**

- **Definition:** These are reflex events initiated by a provocation such as anger, frustration, or pain making the child cry. They account for a significant percentage of psychosomatic disorders and occur in up to 27% of otherwise normal children. The spells are rare before 6 months of age, peak at 2 years, and abate by 5 years of age.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** They are caused by a genetically determined dysregulation of autonomic nervous system reflexes, often triggered by disciplinary conflicts. Iron deficiency anemia (IDA) is strongly associated, as it adversely affects autonomic compensatory functions.
- **Types:**
    - _Cyanotic Type (Common):_ The child cries, stops at full expiration, and holds their breath, followed by cyanosis, momentary unconsciousness, convulsive twitching, and limpness. The mechanism involves a Valsalva maneuver raising intrathoracic pressure and decreasing cerebral circulation.
    - _Pallid Type:_ The child develops pallor instead of cyanosis, secondary to cardiac asystole (similar to a vasovagal attack).
- **Differential Diagnosis:** Must be differentiated from epilepsy (in seizures, cyanosis follows convulsions, and the EEG is abnormal) and cardiac arrhythmias like long QT syndrome.
- **Management:**
    - Parental reassurance is essential; parents should remain calm, lay the child sideways so secretions can drain, and avoid picking the child up (which decreases blood flow to the brain).
    - Parents must avoid showing undue concern or giving in to the child's demands, which reinforces the behavior.
    - Screen for and treat coexisting iron deficiency anemia.

### **6. TEMPER TANTRUMS**

- **Definition:** Temper tantrums are a child's response to physical or emotional challenges using attention-seeking tactics like yelling, biting, crying, kicking, pushing, throwing objects, hitting, and head banging.
- **Age of Onset:** Tantrums typically begin at 18–36 months of age and gradually subside by the age of 3–6 years.
- **Management:**
    - Parents must be counseled to handle the behavioral problem strategically by staying calm, firm, and consistent, ensuring the child is unable to take advantage of the behavior.
    - The child should be protected from injuring himself or others.
    - Techniques such as distraction and 'time out' are very useful.

### **7. PICA (GEOPHAGIA)**

- **Definition:** Pica refers to the persistent ingestion of non-nutritive, nonedible substances such as plaster, charcoal, paint, soil, earth, sand, and ice (pagophagia) for at least 1 month. It is inappropriate to the child's developmental level and cultural practice. Pica is considered normal mouthing behavior up to 2 years of age, but is deviant thereafter.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** It is common in lower socioeconomic statuses and is strongly associated with malnutrition, iron deficiency anemia, developmental delay, maternal deprivation, parental neglect, and psychosocial stress.
- **Complications:** Children with pica are at a high risk for lead poisoning and intestinal parasitic infestations. Some children pull out and swallow their hair (trichotillomania), which can form a large lump in the stomach known as a trichobezoar.
- **Management:**
    - Behavior modification and alleviating psychosocial stress in the family.
    - Screening for and treating lead poisoning.
    - Deworming for parasitic infestations and initiating iron and vitamin supplementation.
