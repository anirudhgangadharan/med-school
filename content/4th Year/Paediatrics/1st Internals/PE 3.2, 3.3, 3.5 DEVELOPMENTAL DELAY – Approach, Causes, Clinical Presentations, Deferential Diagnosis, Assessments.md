### **1. DEFINITION**

Global developmental delay is defined as a delay in acquiring milestones in two or more of the following domains: gross and fine motor, speech and language, cognition, socio-personal, and activities of daily living. For children above 5 years of age, the term intellectual disability is used, replacing the older term mental retardation. Developmental delay is estimated to be present in about 10% of children, with the estimated prevalence of global developmental delay specifically being 2.5–5%. It indicates that the child fails to attain the key milestones by the expected age range.

### **2. ETIOLOGY AND CAUSES**

An underlying etiology can be identified in approximately 70% of patients with developmental disorders.

- **Antenatal Causes:** These factors predominate in developed countries.
    - _Syndromes & Chromosomal Disorders:_ Down syndrome, Fragile X syndrome, Rett syndrome, Turner syndrome.
    - _Congenital/Structural:_ Cortical malformations.
    - _Intrauterine Infections:_ TORCH/STORCH infections (Toxoplasmosis, Rubella, Cytomegalovirus, Herpes, Syphilis).
    - _Metabolic:_ Inborn errors of metabolism (aminoacidopathy, organic acidemia, mitochondrial disorder, urea cycle disorder).
    - _Others:_ Teratogen exposure, neuromuscular disorders, bad obstetric history (abortions), and maternal illnesses like toxemias.
- **Perinatal/Neonatal Causes:** These are more common in the developing world.
    - Hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy (HIE), birth asphyxia, and kernicterus.
    - Neonatal meningitis, encephalitis, and hypoglycemic brain injury.
    - Low birth weight (LBW), prematurity, multiple pregnancy, intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), and neonatal seizures.
    - Hypothyroidism.
- **Postnatal Causes:**
    - Infections (post-meningitis/encephalitis sequelae), after head trauma.
    - Deficiency of vitamin B12, iodine, or B1; toxins such as lead.
    - Chronic protein-energy malnutrition (PEM).
- **Social/Environmental Factors:**
    - Poor socioeconomic status, teenage mother, mentally retarded parent(s), and severe emotional deprivation.

### **3. CLINICAL PRESENTATIONS**

- **Primary Features:** The hallmark is a failure to meet age-appropriate expectations, presenting as delayed speech, language disabilities, and delayed motor milestones.
- **Behavioral Associations:** Affected children often present with hyperactivity, poor memory, poor attention and concentration, distractibility, emotional instability, sleep problems, impulsiveness, and clumsy/awkward movements.
- **Comorbidities:** Common comorbidities include seizures, vision abnormalities, hearing impairment, speech disorders, feeding problems, sleep disturbances, and contractures or bedsores.
- **Dysmorphic/Atypical Features:** Certain physical features strongly correlate with developmental delay and underlying genetic/chromosomal syndromes. These include microcrania or macrocrania, abnormal hair (double whorl, white lock), microphthalmia, slanting eyes, hypertelorism, epicanthal folds, Brushfield spots, large testicles, ambiguous genitalia, and single palmar (Simian) creases.

### **4. DEVELOPMENTAL DEVIANCE AND DISSOCIATION**

- **Deviance:** This is defined as the acquisition of milestones in a sequence that is different from usual. An example is a child with cerebral palsy who may show early standing with support secondary to increased extensor tone, or a child who directly starts walking without ever sitting or crawling.
- **Dissociation:** This refers to the acquisition of developmental milestones in various domains at differing rates.
    - Normal development in all domains except language may indicate an isolated hearing deficit.
    - Normal social and language milestones but significantly delayed motor milestones may indicate rickets or a neuromuscular problem.

### **5. DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS**

Developmental delay must be differentiated from "pseudo-mental retardation" and specific functional impairments, which may be secondary to:

- **Psychiatric/Behavioral Problems:** Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD).
- **Sensory Deficits:** Severe, unrecognized speech, hearing, or vision problems.
- **Deprivation:** Severe cultural, educational, environmental, emotional, or sensory deprivation.
- **Motor Disabilities:** Isolated chronic myopathies, specific neuromuscular disorders, or physical handicaps (like isolated cerebral palsy) where cognition is spared.
- **Specific Learning Disabilities:** Dyslexia, dysgraphia, and dyscalculia where intelligence is preserved but specific academic processing is impaired.

### **6. APPROACH AND ASSESSMENTS**

A multidisciplinary approach involving a pediatrician, neurologist, geneticist, psychologist, speech therapist, occupational therapist, and social worker is essential to achieve maximum cognitive potential.

**a. History and Physical Examination**

- **History Taking:** Provide detailed questioning regarding risk factors (prenatal, perinatal, and postnatal events). It is critical to determine the rate of acquisition of milestones and strongly differentiate pure delay from _regression_ (the loss of already achieved milestones, which points to progressive neurometabolic/neurodegenerative disorders). Enquire about consanguinity, family history of delay, maternal illnesses, and toxemias.
- **Physical Examination:** Evaluate physical growth, exact head circumference (to rule out microcephaly or hydrocephalus), and examine for dysmorphic features. Look for stigmata of intrauterine infections (e.g., microcephaly, cataracts, abnormal orientation of ears/eyes) and signs of hypothyroidism. A comprehensive neurologic examination must include assessment of muscle tone, abnormal movements, and the presence or persistence of primitive reflexes.

**b. Developmental Screening and Definitive Tests**

- **Developmental Quotient (DQ):** Calculated as `(Developmental Age / Chronological Age) x 100`. A DQ below 70% is an indication for a detailed evaluation by a trained child psychologist.
- **Screening Tests:**
    - _Denver Developmental Screening Test (DDST) / Denver II:_ Assesses gross motor, fine motor adaptive, language, and personal-social behavior in children up to 3 years.
    - _Phatak's Baroda Screening Test:_ Evaluates gross motor, fine motor, and cognitive domains for children up to 30 months; widely used in India.
    - _Trivandrum Development Screening Chart (TDSC):_ 17 or 51 test items (depending on the version) for children aged 0-6 years.
    - _Ages and Stages Questionnaire (ASQ-3):_ Parent-completed screening for children 1 month to 5.5 years.
    - _Goodenough-Harris Drawing Test:_ Determines mental age based on child's drawing of a man (for ages 3-15).
- **Definitive Tests:** Used once screening is abnormal to accurately define impairments. Examples include the Development Assessment Scale for Indian Infants (DASII), Bayley Scale for Infant Development IV, Wechsler Intelligence Scale, and Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scales.

**c. Investigations** Investigations are tailored based on clinical clues from the history and examination.

- **Neuroimaging:** MRI (preferred over CT) is indicated specifically if there is abnormal head size, microcephaly, macrocephaly, or an abnormality on neurological examination.
- **Metabolic Tests:** Ammonia, bicarbonate, lactate, sugar, blood TMS (tandem mass spectrometry), and urine GCMS are indicated if there are clues such as recurrent encephalopathy, vomiting, seizures, regression of milestones, or organomegaly.
- **Genetic Studies:** Cytogenetic studies and karyotyping are indicated for developmental delay without a known cause, irrespective of dysmorphic features, or if a specific syndrome (like Down or Fragile X) is suspected.
- **Other Tests:** Thyroid function tests, blood and urine lead levels, and micronutrient levels if clinically indicated. Electrophysiological tests (EEG for seizures, VEP/BERA for visual/auditory assessments, EMG/NCV) are done where specifically indicated.
