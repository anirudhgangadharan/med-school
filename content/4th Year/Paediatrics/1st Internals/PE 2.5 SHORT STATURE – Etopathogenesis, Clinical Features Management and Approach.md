### **1. DEFINITION**

Short stature is defined as a length or height below the 3rd percentile for age and sex, or more than two standard deviations (SD) below the mean height for that age according to population standards. It is also suspected when the height velocity is lower than expected for the child's age (<25th percentile).

### **2. ETIOPATHOGENESIS (CLASSIFICATION OF CAUSES)**

Short stature is broadly classified into proportionate and disproportionate types based on the upper segment to lower segment (US:LS) ratio.

**a. Proportionate Short Stature** The body proportions correspond to the child's chronological age or height age.

- **Normal Variants:**
    - _Familial (Genetic) Short Stature:_ Runs in families; inherited trait.
    - _Constitutional Delay of Growth and Puberty (CDGP):_ Delayed skeletal growth and delayed puberty, often with a family history of "late maturers".
- **Prenatal Origin:**
    - _Intrauterine Growth Retardation (IUGR) / Small for Gestational Age (SGA):_ Due to maternal diabetes, toxemia, infections (TORCH), or teratogens.
    - _Chromosomal Anomalies:_ Turner syndrome (45,X), Down syndrome.
    - _Dysmorphic Syndromes:_ Silver-Russell syndrome, Prader-Willi syndrome.
- **Postnatal Origin / Pathological:**
    - _Nutritional:_ Chronic protein-energy malnutrition (PEM) leading to nutritional dwarfing.
    - _Systemic/Organic Diseases:_
        - Gastrointestinal: Celiac disease, cystic fibrosis, chronic parasitic infestations, liver cirrhosis.
        - Cardiovascular/Respiratory: Congenital heart disease, severe asthma, tuberculosis.
        - Renal: Chronic kidney disease, renal tubular acidosis.
        - Hematological: Chronic anemias (e.g., Thalassemia major).
    - _Endocrinal:_ Growth Hormone Deficiency (GHD), hypothyroidism, Cushing syndrome, poorly controlled diabetes mellitus, pseudohypoparathyroidism.
    - _Psychosocial Dwarfism:_ Emotional deprivation or child abuse/neglect causing functional hypopituitarism.
    - _Drug-Induced:_ Prolonged use of corticosteroids.

**b. Disproportionate Short Stature** The US:LS ratio is abnormal for the child's age.

- **Short Limbs:** Achondroplasia (rhizomelic shortening), severe congenital hypothyroidism, osteogenesis imperfecta, and severe rickets.
- **Short Trunk:** Mucopolysaccharidoses (e.g., Morquio disease, Hurler syndrome), caries spine, hemivertebrae, and severe scoliosis.

### **3. CLINICAL FEATURES OF IMPORTANT TYPES**

- **Familial Short Stature:** The child is born with normal weight/length, but falls to a lower percentile early on and grows parallel to the 3rd percentile. Bone age is equal to chronological age, puberty occurs at a normal time, and the final adult height is short but appropriate for the mid-parental target height.
- **Constitutional Delay of Growth and Puberty (CDGP):** The child appears normal but has a "delayed" growth pattern. Height velocity is normal, but bone age is significantly delayed (less than chronological age). Puberty is delayed, but the child eventually reaches a normal adult height.
- **Growth Hormone Deficiency (GHD):** The child has proportionate short stature, a highly reduced height velocity (<4 cm/yr), delayed bone age, and characteristic "doll-like" facies with frontal bossing, a depressed nasal bridge, prominent philtrum, and central obesity (high subcutaneous fat).
- **Hypothyroidism:** Often presents with disproportionate short stature (infantile US:LS ratio), delayed bone age, myxedematous skin, coarse features, cold intolerance, and mental retardation (if congenital).
- **Turner Syndrome (45,X):** Should be suspected in any short girl. Associated features include a webbed neck, shield chest with widely spaced nipples, increased carrying angle (cubitus valgus), primary amenorrhea, and coarctation of the aorta.
- **Achondroplasia:** Autosomal dominant skeletal dysplasia causing severe disproportionate short stature with proximal limb shortening (rhizomelia), a large head, prominent forehead, depressed nasal bridge, lumbar lordosis, and stubby fingers.

### **4. APPROACH TO ASSESSMENT**

**a. History**

- Obtain exact birth weight and gestational age to rule out SGA.
- Detailed dietary history to assess calorie and protein intake.
- History of chronic symptoms: Polyuria, chronic diarrhea, greasy stools, recurrent chest infections, or headaches/visual problems.
- Family history: Measure the exact heights of parents and siblings to calculate genetic potential. Elicit history of delayed puberty in parents.

**b. Anthropometry and Clinical Examination**

- **Height Measurement:** For children under 2 years, measure supine (recumbent) length using an infantometer. For children over 2 years, measure standing height using a stadiometer, ensuring the head is in the Frankfurt horizontal plane.
- **Target Height (Mid-Parental Height):** Calculates the child's genetic potential.
    - Boys: `(Mother's height + Father's height + 13 cm) / 2` OR `[(Mother + Father)/2] + 6.5 cm`.
    - Girls: `(Mother's height + Father's height - 13 cm) / 2` OR `[(Mother + Father)/2] - 6.5 cm`.

**c. Evaluation of Growth Velocity and Body Proportions**

- **Height Velocity:** Calculated from two accurate height readings at least 6 months (preferably 1 year) apart. A velocity of <4 cm/year between 5 years of age and the onset of puberty is strongly indicative of a pathological short stature.
- **Body Proportions (US:LS Ratio):** The Upper Segment (US) is measured from the top of the head to the pubic symphysis, and the Lower Segment (LS) from the pubic symphysis to the floor. The normal US:LS ratio is 1.7 at birth, 1.3 at 3 years, and approaches 1.0 by 10-12 years of age. An increased ratio for age indicates short-limbed dwarfism or hypothyroidism.
- **Arm Span:** Normally equals height at age 10; < height in early childhood, > height after 12 years. Span > height is seen in spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia.

### **5. INVESTIGATIVE WORK-UP (TIERED APPROACH)**

Investigations are indicated if the height is >2 SD below the mean, height velocity is <4 cm/yr, or there are clinical signs of organic/syndromic disease.

- **Level 1 (Essential Screening):**
    - _Bone Age Assessment:_ Radiograph of the left hand and wrist (or knee/ankle in neonates). Bone age equals chronological age in familial short stature but is delayed in constitutional delay, GHD, hypothyroidism, and malnutrition.
    - _Complete Hemogram with ESR:_ To rule out chronic anemia and infections.
    - _Urinalysis:_ Including microscopy, osmolality, and pH (to rule out RTA and chronic kidney disease).
    - _Stool Examination:_ For parasites (ova/cysts), steatorrhea, and occult blood.
    - _Blood Chemistry:_ Urea, creatinine, electrolytes, bicarbonate, pH, calcium, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, fasting glucose, albumin, and transaminases.
- **Level 2 Investigations (If Level 1 is abnormal or clinical suspicion is high):**
    - _Thyroid Profile:_ Serum T4 and TSH (to rule out hypothyroidism).
    - _Karyotype:_ Mandatory in all short girls to rule out Turner syndrome (45,X), even if typical dysmorphic features are absent.
    - _Celiac Serology (Anti-tTG antibodies):_ To rule out occult celiac disease.
- **Level 3 Investigations (Endocrine/Specialized):**
    - _GH Axis:_ Serum IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 levels.
    - _Provocative GH Testing:_ Required to confirm GHD if IGF-1 is low. Peak GH <10 ng/ml confirms deficiency.
    - _Imaging:_ MRI brain (focused on the pituitary and hypothalamus) if GHD is confirmed, to rule out space-occupying lesions like craniopharyngioma.

### **6. MANAGEMENT**

The management is strictly based on correcting the underlying etiology.

- **Nutritional and Chronic Systemic Diseases:** Dietary rehabilitation with balanced macro- and micronutrients leads to rapid catch-up growth. Treat underlying chronic diseases (e.g., gluten-free diet for celiac disease, alkali therapy for RTA, wormicidals for parasitosis).
- **Endocrinopathies:**
    - _Hypothyroidism:_ Immediate and lifelong replacement with oral levothyroxine.
    - _Growth Hormone Deficiency (GHD):_ Treatment with daily subcutaneous injections of recombinant human Growth Hormone (rhGH) at a dose of 0.07 to 0.1 IU/kg/day. Therapy must be started before epiphyseal fusion and monitored via IGF-1 levels.
- **Syndromic/Genetic Causes:**
    - _Turner Syndrome:_ High-dose rhGH therapy is approved and significantly improves final adult height. Low-dose estrogen is added later for pubertal induction.
    - _SGA without catch-up growth:_ rhGH therapy is approved for children born SGA who fail to show catch-up growth by 2-4 years of age.
- **Normal Variants (Familial and CDGP):** Counseling, reassurance, and annual monitoring of height velocity. No specific medical therapy is required. Ensure parents understand that CDGP children will have delayed puberty but will achieve normal target height.
- **Skeletal Dysplasias:** Reassurance and orthopedic management. Limb lengthening procedures (Ilizarov technique) are offered at specialized orthopedic centers but are expensive and carry high risks.
