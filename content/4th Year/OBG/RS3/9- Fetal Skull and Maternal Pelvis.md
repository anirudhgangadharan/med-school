1. Diameters of fetal skull.
**DIAMETERS OF FETAL SKULL**

The engaging diameter of the fetal skull depends entirely on the attitude (degree of flexion or extension) of the head. The diameters of obstetric significance are broadly classified into anteroposterior and transverse diameters.

**I. ANTEROPOSTERIOR DIAMETERS** The anteroposterior diameters of the head which may engage during labour include the following:

- **Suboccipitobregmatic (9.5 cm / 3¾ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ Extends from the nape of the neck to the centre of the bregma.
    - _Attitude & Presentation:_ It is the engaging diameter in a head with **complete flexion** (Vertex presentation).
- **Suboccipitofrontal (10 cm / 4 inches):**
    - _Extent:_ Extends from the nape of the neck to the anterior end of the anterior fontanel or the centre of the sinciput.
    - _Attitude & Presentation:_ It is the engaging diameter in a head with **incomplete flexion** (Vertex presentation).
- **Occipitofrontal (11.5 cm / 4½ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ Extends from the occipital eminence to the root of the nose (glabella).
    - _Attitude & Presentation:_ It is the engaging diameter in a head with **marked deflexion** (Vertex presentation).
- **Mentovertical (14 cm / 5½ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ Extends from the midpoint of the chin to the highest point on the sagittal suture.
    - _Attitude & Presentation:_ It is the engaging diameter in a head with **partial extension** (Brow presentation).
- **Submentovertical (11.5 cm / 4½ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ Extends from the junction of the floor of the mouth and neck to the highest point on the sagittal suture.
    - _Attitude & Presentation:_ It is the engaging diameter in a head with **incomplete extension** (Face presentation).
- **Submentobregmatic (9.5 cm / 3¾ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ Extends from the junction of the floor of the mouth and neck to the centre of the bregma.
    - _Attitude & Presentation:_ It is the engaging diameter in a head with **complete extension** (Face presentation).

**II. TRANSVERSE DIAMETERS** The transverse diameters which are concerned in the mechanism of labour include the following:

- **Biparietal diameter (9.5 cm / 3¾ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ It extends between the two parietal eminences.
    - _Significance:_ Whatever may be the position of the head, this transverse diameter nearly always engages.
- **Super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm / 3½ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ It extends from a point placed below one parietal eminence to a point placed above the other parietal eminence on the opposite side.
- **Bitemporal diameter (8 cm / 3¼ inches):**
    - _Extent:_ It is the distance between the anteroinferior ends of the coronal suture.
- **Bimastoid diameter (7.5 cm / 3 inches):**
    - _Extent:_ It is the distance between the tips of the mastoid processes.
    - _Significance:_ This diameter is incompressible, and it is impossible to reduce the length of the bimastoid diameter by any obstetrical operation.
2. Moulding of fetal head—degrees, importance
**MOULDING OF FETAL HEAD**

**Definition** Moulding is defined as the alteration of the shape of the forecoming fetal head while passing through the resistant birth passage during labor.

**Mechanism of Moulding**

- There is a compression of the engaging diameter of the head with a corresponding elongation of the diameter at a right angle to it.
- For example, in a well-flexed head of an anterior vertex presentation, the engaging suboccipitobregmatic diameter is compressed, causing an elongation of the head in the mentovertical diameter.
- During this process, the parietal bones tend to overlap the adjacent bones: the occipital bone behind, the frontal bones in front, and the temporal bones at the sides.
- In the first vertex position, the right parietal bone tends to override the left one, whereas the reverse occurs in the second vertex position.

**Characteristics**

- There is very little alteration in the actual size or volume of the head because the contents inside the skull are incompressible, although a small amount of cerebrospinal fluid and blood does escape out during the process.
- During a normal delivery, an alteration of 4 mm in the skull diameter commonly occurs.
- Moulding is a temporary phenomenon and disappears within a few hours after birth.

**Degrees (Grading) of Moulding** Clinically, moulding is classified into three gradings based on the relationship of the skull bones at the suture lines:

- **Grade 1:** The bones are touching but not overlapping.
- **Grade 2:** The bones are overlapping but can be easily separated.
- **Grade 3:** There is fixed, irreducible overlapping of the bones.

**Clinical Importance and Significance**

- **Facilitates Normal Delivery:** Slight moulding is an inevitable and beneficial physiological process. It reduces the engaging diameters and enables the head to pass more easily through the birth canal.
- **Diagnostic Aid:** The specific shape of the moulding provides useful retrospective information about the position the head occupied in the pelvis during labor.
- **Indicator of Disproportion (Pathological Moulding):** Extreme moulding (Grade 3) is typically seen in cases of cephalopelvic disproportion. This is dangerous as it may produce severe intracranial disturbances, such as tearing of the tentorium cerebelli or potentially fatal subdural hemorrhage.
3. Caput succedaneum (features to differentiate it from cephalhematoma).
**CAPUT SUCCEDANEUM**

**Definition and Nature** Caput succedaneum is the formation of a localized, diffuse, boggy swelling on the fetal scalp due to the stagnation of fluid (effusion of serum) in the layers of the scalp beneath the girdle of contact. The girdle of contact may be the bony pelvis, the dilating cervical ring, or the vulval ring.

**Mechanism of Formation** During labor, as the fetal head descends to press over the dilating cervix, the overlying scalp is free from pressure, but the tissues in contact with the full circumference of the girdle of contact are highly compressed. This compression causes significant obstruction to the venous return and lymphatic drainage from the unsupported area of the scalp. The resulting stagnation of fluid leads to the appearance of scalp edema. This phenomenon usually occurs after the rupture of the fetal membranes.

**Clinical Significance in Obstetrics**

- **Duration of Labor:** The presence and size of the caput signify the static position of the head for a prolonged period of time during the labor process.
- **Position of the Head:** The precise location of the caput gives a reliable idea about the position the head occupied in the maternal pelvis during labor. For example, in a left-sided position, the caput forms on the right parietal bone, whereas in a right-sided position, it forms on the left parietal bone.
- **Degree of Flexion:** With increasing flexion of the fetal head, the caput is placed more posteriorly.
- **Resolution:** It is a physiological occurrence that disappears spontaneously within a few hours (typically 24 hours) after birth without requiring any active treatment.

---

**FEATURES TO DIFFERENTIATE CAPUT SUCCEDANEUM FROM CEPHALHEMATOMA**

Differentiation is critically important for neonatal assessment. The defining differences are outlined below:

**1. Time of Appearance**

- **Caput Succedaneum:** Present immediately at birth.
- **Cephalhematoma:** Appears hours after birth.

**2. Nature and Feel of the Swelling**

- **Caput Succedaneum:** Feels soft, boggy, and is compressible.
- **Cephalhematoma:** Feels soft but is distinctly incompressible.

**3. Extent and Anatomical Boundaries**

- **Caput Succedaneum:** Diffuse swelling that spreads over and freely crosses the cranial suture lines.
- **Cephalhematoma:** Strictly limited to the area of a single bone; it does not cross the suture lines because the bleeding is subperiosteal and the pericranium is attached at the suture edges.

**4. Mobility and Position**

- **Caput Succedaneum:** Tends to change position and moves to the dependent part of the scalp.
- **Cephalhematoma:** Does not change position and remains fixed to one specific site.

**5. Progression and Clinical Course (With Observation)**

- **Caput Succedaneum:** Gradually reduces in size and disappears completely within a few hours to 24 hours.
- **Cephalhematoma:** Increases in size with time as bleeding accumulates and takes weeks or even months to disappear fully.

**6. Underlying Pathology (Definition)**

- **Caput Succedaneum:** It is an accumulation of fluid/serum causing localized edema in the scalp tissues.
- **Cephalhematoma:** It is a frank hemorrhage occurring underneath the periosteum of the skull bone(s).

**7. Etiology and Associated Risks**

- **Caput Succedaneum:** Caused by physiological pressure from the dilating cervix causing venous and lymphatic obstruction.
- **Cephalhematoma:** Caused by mechanical trauma to the skull due to prolonged pressure on the head during labor or following operative deliveries such as forceps or ventouse (vacuum) extraction. It is also often associated with underlying fractures of the skull bone(s).

4. Anatomical conjugate.
**ANATOMICAL CONJUGATE**

**Definition and Synonyms**

- The anatomical conjugate is the anteroposterior diameter of the pelvic inlet.
- It is also synonymously known as the **True Conjugate** or **Conjugate Vera**.

**Anatomical Landmarks**

- It is defined as the distance between the midpoint of the sacral promontory and the inner margin of the upper border of the symphysis pubis.

**Normal Measurement**

- In a normal female pelvis, it measures **11 cm** (approximately 4¼ inches).

**Clinical Significance and Estimation**

- **Not the shortest diameter:** Although it is the true anatomical anteroposterior diameter of the inlet, it is not the shortest diameter in this plane (the _obstetric conjugate_ is the shortest, measuring 10 cm).
- **Indirect Measurement:** In routine clinical and obstetric practice, the true anatomical conjugate cannot be estimated directly.
- **Calculation:** Its measurement is clinically inferred by measuring the **diagonal conjugate** (the distance from the lower border of the symphysis pubis to the sacral promontory) during a pelvic examination.
- To obtain the length of the anatomical conjugate, **1.2 cm (½ inch) is subtracted from the diagonal conjugate**. This deduction is made to specifically allow for the inclination, thickness, and height of the symphysis pubis.
