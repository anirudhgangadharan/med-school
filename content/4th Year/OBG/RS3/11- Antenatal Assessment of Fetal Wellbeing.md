1. Diagnosis of fetal distress.
**DIAGNOSIS OF FETAL DISTRESS (NONREASSURING FETAL STATUS)**

**DEFINITION** Fetal distress is an ill-defined term used to express intrauterine fetal jeopardy, which is primarily a result of intrauterine fetal hypoxia. In modern obstetrics, it is preferably termed "Nonreassuring Fetal Status" (NRFS). It is characterized by fetal heart rate (FHR) tachycardia or bradycardia, reduced FHR variability, decelerations, and the absence of spontaneous or elicited accelerations.

The diagnostic methods for intrapartum fetal evaluation are broadly classified into three categories: Clinical, Biophysical, and Biochemical.

**I. CLINICAL METHODS** Clinical diagnosis relies on intermittent auscultation and observation of the amniotic fluid.

- **Abnormal Fetal Heart Rate (FHR):**
    - **Tachycardia:** An increase in FHR to over 160 beats per minute lasting for more than 10 minutes.
    - **Bradycardia:** A decrease in the FHR to less than 100 beats per minute.
    - **Irregularity and Slow Recovery:** FHR irregularity, or an FHR that takes a long time to come back to its normal baseline rate after a uterine contraction passes off, indicates distress.
- **Meconium-Stained Amniotic Fluid (MSAF):**
    - Meconium in the liquor amnii is a potential sign of fetal hypoxia.
    - **Pathogenesis:** Hypoxia leads to a vagal response, which causes increased intestinal peristaltic activity and relaxation of the anal sphincter, resulting in the passage of meconium.
    - The presence of thick meconium along with a nonreassuring FHR pattern necessitates urgent intervention.

**II. BIOPHYSICAL METHODS (Continuous Electronic Fetal Monitoring - EFM / CTG)** Continuous electronic fetal monitoring (Cardiotocography - CTG) is utilized to accurately monitor uterine contractions and FHR to detect hypoxia early. A pathological (Category III) tracing indicates fetal compromise.

- **Absent or Reduced Baseline Variability:** Decreased or absent baseline variability (<5 bpm) lasting for >50 minutes denotes a hypoxic fetus.
- **Absence of Accelerations:** The absence of FHR accelerations, particularly when combined with reduced variability, is a significant marker of hypoxia.
- **Late Decelerations:** These are uniform, U-shaped periodic slowings of the FHR with reduced variability, beginning >20 seconds after the onset of a uterine contraction. The nadir occurs after the peak of the contraction, suggesting uteroplacental insufficiency and fetal hypoxia. Repeated late decelerations increase the risk of hypoxemia and low Apgar scores.
- **Variable Decelerations (with concerning features):** These are variable in shape (often U or V-shaped) and suggest fetal hypoxia/acidosis if they present with concerning characteristics such as: lasting >60 seconds, lacking "shouldering" (pre/post-deceleration accelerations), failing to return to baseline, showing a biphasic 'W' shape, or presenting with reduced baseline variability. It primarily indicates umbilical cord compression.
- **Prolonged Decelerations:** An abrupt decrease in FHR (>=15 bpm) below the baseline lasting >3 minutes but <10 minutes. It indicates profound hypoxia and acidosis requiring emergent intervention.
- **Sinusoidal Pattern:** A stable baseline FHR exhibiting a smooth, undulating, sine-wave like pattern (3-5 cycles/min) with fixed or absent variability lasting >=30 minutes. It is strongly associated with severe fetal hypoxia or fetal anemia.

**III. FETAL STIMULATION TESTS (Adjuncts to EFM)** These tests are performed when the CTG is nonreassuring to rule out metabolic acidosis.

- **Vibroacoustic Stimulation (VAS):** An electronic larynx is placed on the maternal abdomen; any resulting FHR acceleration indicates the absence of fetal acidosis.
- **Fetal Scalp Stimulation:** A gentle digital stroke of the fetal scalp is performed; the presence of FHR acceleration suggests a normal blood pH.

**IV. BIOCHEMICAL METHODS** Biochemical tests are the gold standard to corroborate the significance of fetal CTG abnormalities by directly detecting fetal hypoxia and metabolic acidemia.

- **Fetal Scalp Blood Sampling (FBS):**
    - Blood is obtained from the fetal scalp via a small lancet incision through a dilated cervix (4-5 cm) to estimate pH and base excess.
    - **pH < 7.20:** Abnormal. Indicates true fetal acidosis and mandates urgent delivery.
    - **pH 7.21 - 7.25:** Borderline. The test needs to be repeated within 30 minutes to detect any deteriorating changes.
    - **pH > 7.25:** Reassuring. Labor progress can be safely monitored.
- **Fetal Blood Lactate Estimation:** Often correlated with FBS values. A lactate level of >4.9 is abnormal, 4.2-4.9 is borderline, and <4.1 is considered normal.

**V. FETAL ELECTROCARDIOGRAM (ECG) / ST-SEGMENT ANALYSIS (STAN)**

- Hypoxia causes an increase in the fetal T-wave amplitude.
- A significant increase in the T/QRS ratio (>0.25) or the presence of more than two consecutive biphasic ST complexes on the fetal ECG, when combined with a nonreassuring FHR tracing, indicates fetal metabolic acidosis.


2. Nonstress test—indications and interpretation.
**NON-STRESS TEST (NST)**

**DEFINITION AND PRINCIPLE** The Non-Stress Test (NST) is a primary, non-invasive biophysical screening method for antenatal fetal surveillance. It involves the continuous electronic monitoring of the fetal heart rate (FHR) along with the recording of fetal movements using cardiotocography.

- **Principle:** The test evaluates the reflex-mediated association of FHR accelerations with fetal movements. The observation of FHR accelerations in response to fetal activity indicates a healthy, well-oxygenated fetus.
- **Objective:** It is highly valuable to identify fetal "wellness" rather than illness.

**INDICATIONS** NST is used to assess fetal well-being in complicated or high-risk pregnancies, typically starting after 30 weeks of gestation, and is often performed twice weekly. Key indications include:

- **Maternal Medical Disorders:** Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, preexisting or gestational diabetes mellitus.
- **Fetal Complications:** Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR), postmaturity (prolonged/post-term pregnancy).
- **Decreased Fetal Movements:** A maternal report of diminished or absent daily fetal movement count (DFMC) mandates immediate NST evaluation.
- **Placental/Cord Abnormalities:** Cases like vasa previa require twice-weekly NST to assess for signs of cord compression.

**INTERPRETATION OF NST** The tracing is evaluated over a 20-minute observation window and is interpreted as follows:

**1. Reactive (Reassuring) NST:**

- **Criteria:** The presence of two or more accelerations of the FHR, each rising by more than 15 beats per minute above the baseline and lasting for longer than 15 seconds, within a 20-minute observation period.
- **Inference:** Indicates a healthy, non-acidotic fetus with a low risk of fetal asphyxia. It allows for the continuation of routine monitoring.

**2. Non-reactive (Non-reassuring) NST:**

- **Criteria:** The absence of any fetal reactivity (accelerations) during the observation period.
- **Inference:** May indicate fetal hypoxia, but can also be caused by a physiological quiet fetal sleep state, maternal medications (like sedatives), or extreme prematurity.

**CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE AND PREDICTIVE VALUE**

- **Reactive NST:** Highly reassuring. It is associated with a perinatal death rate of only about 5 per 1,000. The test has an extremely low false-negative rate of less than 1% (0.5%).
- **Non-reactive NST:** Associated with a significantly higher perinatal death rate of about 40 per 1,000. However, it has a high false-positive rate of over 50%, meaning many non-reactive fetuses are actually healthy.

**MANAGEMENT OF A NON-REACTIVE NST** Because a non-reactive trace is often due to a fetal sleep cycle (non-REM sleep) and has a high false-positive rate, sequential steps must be taken:

- **Vibroacoustic Stimulation (VAS):** An electronic larynx is used over the maternal abdomen to change the fetal state from quiet sleep to active (REM) sleep. A reactive NST after VAS confirms a healthy fetus and avoids unnecessary interventions.
- **Further Surveillance:** If the NST remains non-reactive or abnormal, the patient must be subjected to a detailed Fetal Biophysical Profile (BPP) or a Modified BPP (NST + Amniotic Fluid Index), along with umbilical artery Doppler flow velocimetry to accurately rule out fetal hypoxia and formulate a delivery plan.


3. Fetal biophysical profile (BPP).
**FETAL BIOPHYSICAL PROFILE (BPP)**

**Definition and Principle**

- Fetal Biophysical Profile (BPP) is a screening test used to detect uteroplacental insufficiency.
- **Pathophysiology:** Fetal biophysical activities are initiated, modulated, and regulated through the fetal central nervous system (CNS). The fetal CNS is highly sensitive to diminished oxygenation. Hypoxia leads to metabolic acidosis, which causes CNS depression, subsequently altering fetal biophysical activity.
- The biophysical activities that appear first in fetal development are the last to disappear during fetal hypoxia. BPP correlates well with fetal acid-base status.

**Parameters and Scoring (Manning Modified, 1992)** The BPP evaluates five parameters over a 30-minute observation period using real-time ultrasonography and cardiotocography. Each parameter is awarded a score of 2 if normal and 0 if abnormal, yielding a maximum total score of 10.

1. **Non-Stress Test (NST):** A reactive pattern scores 2.
2. **Fetal Breathing Movements:** $\ge$ 1 episode lasting >30 seconds scores 2. (Hypoxemia decreases fetal breathing movements).
3. **Gross Body Movements:** $\ge$ 3 discrete body/limb movements scores 2.
4. **Fetal Muscle Tone:** $\ge$ 1 episode of active extension (limb or trunk) with a return to flexion, or opening and closing of the hand scores 2.
5. **Amniotic Fluid Volume:** $\ge$ 1 pocket measuring 2 cm in two perpendicular planes (2 x 2 cm pocket) scores 2.

**Interpretation and Clinical Management**

- **Score 8–10 (Normal):** Indicates a low risk of fetal asphyxia (1 per 1,000). The false-negative rate of a normal BPP is less than 0.1%. **Management:** Repeat testing at weekly intervals or as clinically indicated.
- **Score 6 (Equivocal):** Suspicious for chronic asphyxia. **Management:** If $\ge$ 36 weeks, deliver. If <36 weeks and L/S ratio is <2.0, repeat the test in 4–6 hours.
- **Score 4 (Abnormal):** Suspect chronic asphyxia. A score of 4 or less is strongly associated with fetal acidemia, high risks of stillbirth, and perinatal mortality. **Management:** If $\ge$ 36 weeks, deliver. If <32 weeks, repeat testing in 4–6 hours.
- **Score 0–2 (Abnormal):** Strongly suspect severe asphyxia. **Management:** Extend testing to 120 minutes; if the score remains persistently $\le$ 4, deliver immediately regardless of gestational age.

**Indications and Test Frequency**

- **Indications:** A non-reactive NST or any high-risk pregnancy (e.g., intrauterine growth restriction, post-dated pregnancy).
- **Frequency:** Usually performed weekly following a normal NST, and twice weekly following an abnormal test.

**Modified Biophysical Profile**

- A modified BPP consists of only the Non-Stress Test (NST) and ultrasonographically determined Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI).
- It takes less time to perform, has a lower false-positive rate compared to an isolated NST, and offers a high positive predictive value.
- The modified BPP is considered abnormal (non-reassuring) when the NST is non-reactive and/or the AFI is <5.



4. Cardiotocography
**CARDIOTOCOGRAPHY (CTG) / ELECTRONIC FETAL MONITORING (EFM)**

**DEFINITION AND PRINCIPLE** Cardiotocography is a method of continuous electronic fetal monitoring (EFM) that involves the simultaneous recording of fetal heart action and uterine contractions. The primary objective is to detect intrapartum fetal hypoxia early, explain its mechanism, and initiate appropriate management to prevent fetal organ injury or death.

**INDICATIONS** Continuous EFM is not routinely recommended for low-risk women but is strictly indicated for high-risk pregnancies.

- **Maternal Indications:** Hypertensive disorders, diabetes, previous cesarean delivery, induction/augmentation of labor with oxytocin, antepartum hemorrhage (APH), premature rupture of membranes (PROM), pyrexia, and fresh bleeding in labor.
- **Fetal Indications:** Fetal growth restriction (FGR), oligohydramnios, multiple pregnancy, thick meconium-stained liquor, and abnormal fetal heart rate (FHR) detected on intermittent auscultation.

**METHODS OF MONITORING** **1. External (Non-invasive):**

- Continuous tracing is obtained by placing two transducers on the maternal abdomen.
- An ultrasound Doppler transducer detects the FHR via cardiac motion, and a tocodynamometer records the frequency and duration of uterine contractions.
- The standard paper speed for recording is 1 cm/min. Telemetry (wireless transmission) can be used to allow maternal mobility.

**2. Internal (Invasive):**

- Fetal ECG is recorded directly by applying a spiral-pointed scalp electrode to the fetal scalp after rupturing the fetal membranes.
- Intrauterine pressure is measured simultaneously by passing a catheter into the uterine cavity.
- _Contraindications:_ Active genital herpes infection, HIV, or a very preterm fetus (<32 weeks).

**INTERPRETATION OF CTG TRACE (THE 4 KEY PARAMETERS)** Interpretation of the CTG must always be correlated with the clinical picture. A healthy fetus is characterized by a stable baseline, normal variability, and the presence of accelerations.

**1. Baseline Fetal Heart Rate (FHR):**

- It is the mean FHR excluding accelerations and decelerations.
- **Normal Baseline:** 110–160 beats per minute (bpm).
- **Fetal Tachycardia:** FHR >160 bpm lasting for >10 minutes. Causes include maternal infection/pyrexia, prematurity, fetal anemia, and fetal hypoxia.
- **Fetal Bradycardia:** FHR <100 or 110 bpm lasting for >10 minutes.

**2. Baseline Variability:**

- Refers to the continuous oscillation of the baseline FHR, predominantly modulated by the parasympathetic (vagus) nerve.
- **Normal (Moderate) Variability:** 5–25 bpm.
- **Reduced Variability:** <5 bpm. If it persists for >50 minutes, it strongly denotes a hypoxic fetus. Non-hypoxic causes include fetal sleep cycles, prematurity, congenital malformations, and maternal administration of CNS depressants (e.g., pethidine, tranquilizers).

**3. Accelerations:**

- Defined as a transient increase in FHR by 15 bpm or more, lasting for at least 15 seconds.
- Presence of accelerations denotes an intact neurohormonal and cardiovascular reflex, indicating a healthy, well-oxygenated fetus without acidosis.

**4. Decelerations:** Transient decrease in FHR below the baseline by 15 bpm or more, lasting at least 15 seconds. They are classified into three main types based on their relationship with uterine contractions:

- **Early Decelerations (Type I):** Uniform, repetitive, U-shaped slowing that mirrors the uterine contraction (the nadir of the deceleration coincides with the peak of the contraction). They are caused by fetal head compression (vagal nerve activation) and are clinically benign, not indicating hypoxia.
- **Late Decelerations (Type II):** Uniform, U-shaped slowing where the onset, nadir, and recovery occur _after_ the start, peak, and end of the uterine contraction, respectively. The nadir is typically >20 seconds after the contraction's peak. This is a highly pathological sign indicating uteroplacental insufficiency and fetal hypoxia/acidosis. Causes include placental abruption, postmaturity, hypertension, and uterine hyperstimulation.
- **Variable Decelerations:** V-shaped, intermittent decelerations that vary in shape, depth, and timing relative to contractions. They are the most common type and are caused by umbilical cord compression. They become concerning (suggesting hypoxia/acidosis) if they last >60 seconds, lack initial "shouldering" (compensatory accelerations), have a biphasic (W) shape, or are associated with reduced baseline variability.
- **Prolonged Decelerations:** Abrupt decrease in FHR (>=15 bpm) lasting >3 minutes but <10 minutes. This indicates profound hypoxia/acidosis and requires emergent intervention. Common causes include placental abruption, uterine rupture, cord prolapse, and uterine hyperstimulation.

**SINUSOIDAL PATTERN**

- Resembles a smooth sine wave with a frequency of 3–5 cycles/min, lasting for >=30 minutes.
- Characterized by a stable baseline, fixed or absent variability (<=5 bpm), and absent accelerations.
- It is highly pathological and is classically associated with severe fetal anemia (e.g., Rh-isoimmunization), fetomaternal hemorrhage, ruptured vasa previa, or profound fetal hypoxia/acidosis.

**CATEGORIZATION OF CTG TRACES (RCOG / NICE / ACOG GUIDELINES)**

- **Category I (Normal / Reassuring):** Baseline FHR 110–160 bpm, moderate variability (5–25 bpm), no late, variable, or prolonged decelerations. Early decelerations and accelerations may or may not be present.
- **Category II (Suspicious / Indeterminate):** Tracings that do not fit into Category I or III. For example, tracings with one non-reassuring feature while the rest remain reassuring.
- **Category III (Pathological / Abnormal):** Features absent baseline variability coupled with any of the following: recurrent late decelerations, recurrent variable decelerations, or bradycardia (for at least 10 minutes). A sinusoidal pattern lasting >=20-30 minutes also falls into this category.

**ADVANTAGES AND LIMITATIONS OF CTG** **Advantages over Clinical Monitoring:**

- Provides an accurate, continuous record of uterine contractions and fetal heart rate.
- Can detect fetal hypoxia early and explain its exact mechanism (e.g., cord compression vs. uteroplacental insufficiency).
- Reduces the incidence of intrapartum fetal death by threefold and neonatal seizures by 50%.
- Serves as an objective medicolegal record.

**Limitations and Drawbacks:**

- **High False-Positive Rate:** The CTG has low specificity and a low positive predictive value for true metabolic acidosis.
- **Increased Operative Interference:** Due to false-positive interpretation, continuous CTG is associated with a 63% increase in cesarean delivery rates and a 15% increase in operative vaginal deliveries.
- Prone to inter-observer and intra-observer disagreement during interpretation.
- Requires expensive equipment, trained personnel, and confines the mother to the bed (unless telemetry is used).

**ADJUNCT MANAGEMENT FOR ABNORMAL CTG** If a non-reassuring FHR pattern is identified, it must be corroborated with adjunct tests to confirm true fetal acidosis and avoid unnecessary cesarean deliveries:

1. **Fetal Stimulation Tests:** Vibroacoustic stimulation (VAS) or digital fetal scalp stimulation. Evocation of an FHR acceleration confirms the absence of fetal acidosis.
2. **Fetal Scalp Blood Sampling (FBS):** Direct measurement of fetal capillary blood pH. A pH <7.20 indicates definite fetal acidosis requiring urgent delivery, while a pH >7.25 is reassuring.
3. **Fetal ECG (ST-segment analysis / STAN):** Combined with CTG, an increase in the T/QRS ratio or biphasic ST segments signals myocardial hypoxia and impending metabolic acidosis.

4. Daily fetal movement count (DFMC).
**DAILY FETAL MOVEMENT COUNT (DFMC)**

**Definition and Significance** The Daily Fetal Movement Count (DFMC), often referred to as the "kick count," is a simple, non-invasive, and widely used first-line clinical screening test for monitoring antenatal fetal wellbeing. It is an ideal method for both high-risk and low-risk patients to evaluate the condition of the fetus on a daily basis.

**Principle** The physiological principle behind DFMC is that there is a noticeable decrease in fetal movements when the fetus experiences hypoxemia (hypoxia). A complete loss of fetal movements is an ominous sign and is commonly followed by the disappearance of the Fetal Heart Rate (FHR) within the next 24 hours.

**Time of Initiation** The daily counting of fetal movements should be started at 28 weeks of pregnancy and continued until delivery.

**Methods of Counting** There are two primary methods recommended for recording fetal movements:

**1. Cardiff 'Count 10' Formula:**

- The pregnant woman is instructed to start counting fetal movements beginning at 9 a.m..
- The counting is concluded for the day as soon as 10 movements are perceived.
- **Warning Signs:** The patient is strictly instructed to report to the physician immediately if:
    - Less than 10 movements occur during a 12-hour period on 2 successive days.
    - No movement is perceived even after 12 hours in a single day.

**2. Daily Fetal Movement Count (DFMC) / 3-Hour Method:**

- The woman is advised to count fetal movements for 1 hour each during the morning, noon, and evening.
- The total number of movements counted during these 3 hours is summed up and multiplied by 4 to give the daily (12-hour) fetal movement count.
- **Warning Signs:** A diminution of the number of 'kicks' to less than 10 in 12 hours, or less than 3 in each hour, is a strong indicator of fetal compromise.

**Interpretation and Clinical Actions**

- **Normal Profile:** A healthy fetus should exhibit a minimum of 10 movements in a 12-hour period. Mothers typically perceive about 88% of the actual fetal movements that can be detected by Doppler imaging.
- **Action on Decreased Count:** If the DFMC result is ominous (decreased counting rate), it warrants immediate further evaluation of the fetus. The patient must be subjected to a Non-Stress Test (NST) and a Fetal Biophysical Profile (BPP) to accurately assess fetal acid-base status and rule out hypoxia.

**Factors Affecting Fetal Movements and Maternal Perception** It is important to evaluate a decreased count in the context of clinical factors. Maternal perception of fetal movements may be reduced in the following conditions:

- Fetal sleep (quiet state).
- Fetal congenital anomalies (specifically Central Nervous System anomalies).
- Maternal factors: Anterior placenta, hydramnios (polyhydramnios), and obesity.
- Substance use: Administration of certain drugs (like narcotics) and chronic maternal smoking.
- True fetal hypoxia.
- _(Note: Conversely, maternal hypoglycemia is associated with an increase in fetal movements)._

7. Biophysical profile.
**FETAL BIOPHYSICAL PROFILE (BPP)**

**INTRODUCTION AND PRINCIPLE** The Fetal Biophysical Profile (BPP) is a comprehensive, noninvasive screening test utilized primarily for the assessment of fetal wellbeing and the detection of uteroplacental insufficiency.

- **Principle:** The fetal biophysical activities are initiated, modulated, and regulated through the fetal Central Nervous System (CNS). The fetal CNS is highly sensitive to diminished oxygenation.
- **Pathophysiology:** Fetal hypoxia leads to metabolic acidosis, which results in CNS depression. This CNS depression manifests clinically as specific, observable changes in fetal biophysical activities.
- The biophysical activities that appear first during fetal development are the last to disappear during states of fetal hypoxia. BPP correlates well with the fetal acid-base status.

**COMPONENTS OF BIOPHYSICAL PROFILE (Manning's Modified Scoring)** The BPP utilizes real-time ultrasonography to evaluate specific biophysical variables over a 30-minute observation period. It comprises 5 parameters. Each parameter is awarded a score of 2 if normal criteria are met, and 0 if abnormal. The maximum possible score is 10.

**1. Non-Stress Test (NST)**

- **Normal (Score 2):** Reactive pattern (i.e., presence of FHR accelerations associated with fetal movements).
- **Abnormal (Score 0):** Non-reactive pattern.

**2. Fetal Breathing Movements**

- **Normal (Score 2):** $\ge$ 1 episode of fetal breathing lasting for > 30 seconds within the 30-minute observation window.
- **Abnormal (Score 0):** Absent or $\le$ 1 episode lasting < 30 seconds.

**3. Gross Body Movements**

- **Normal (Score 2):** $\ge$ 3 discrete body/limb movements.
- **Abnormal (Score 0):** $\le$ 2 discrete movements.

**4. Fetal Muscle Tone**

- **Normal (Score 2):** $\ge$ 1 episode of active extension (of limb or trunk) with a subsequent return to flexion; opening and closing of the hand is also considered normal.
- **Abnormal (Score 0):** Slow extension with partial return to flexion, or absent fetal movement.

**5. Amniotic Fluid Volume**

- **Normal (Score 2):** $\ge$ 1 pocket of amniotic fluid measuring at least 2 cm in two perpendicular planes (i.e., a 2 x 2 cm pocket).
- **Abnormal (Score 0):** Either no pockets or largest pocket < 2 cm.

**INTERPRETATION AND OBSTETRIC MANAGEMENT** The total score guides the clinical management of the pregnancy.

- **Score 8-10 (Normal):** Indicates a normal, healthy fetus with a very low risk of fetal asphyxia (1 per 1000).
    - _Management:_ Repeat the testing at weekly intervals or more frequently depending on the clinical scenario.
- **Score 6 (Equivocal):** Suspicious for chronic asphyxia.
    - _Management:_ If the gestational age is $\ge$ 36 weeks, delivery is indicated. If L/S ratio is < 2.0 (indicating lung immaturity), repeat the test in 4-6 hours.
- **Score 4 (Abnormal):** Suspicion of chronic asphyxia is high.
    - _Management:_ If $\ge$ 36 weeks, deliver. If < 32 weeks, repeat testing in 4-6 hours. An abnormal score of 4 or less is strongly associated with fetal acidemia and carries high risks of stillbirth and perinatal mortality.
- **Score 0-2 (Highly Abnormal):** Strongly suspect asphyxia.
    - _Management:_ Test for an extended period of 120 minutes. If the score persistently remains $\le$ 4, deliver immediately regardless of the gestational age.

**INDICATIONS AND FREQUENCY**

- **Indications:** It is primarily indicated in high-risk pregnancies or following a non-reactive NST.
- **Frequency:** Done weekly after a normal NST, and twice weekly following an abnormal test.

**CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE**

- **Predictive Value:** BPP using real-time ultrasonography has a high predictive value. The false-negative rate of a normal BPP (meaning fetal death occurs within 1 week of a normal score) is extremely low, at about 0.1%.
- **Correlation:** BPP significantly correlates with the fetal acid-base status and accurately identifies fetuses at risk of asphyxia.

**MODIFIED BIOPHYSICAL PROFILE** Because a full BPP is time-consuming, a modified version is often employed in clinical practice.

- **Components:** It consists of only two parameters: the Non-Stress Test (NST) and ultrasonographically determined Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI).
- **Inference:** The modified BPP is considered abnormal (nonreassuring) when the NST is non-reactive and/or the AFI is < 5.
- **Advantages:** It takes less time to perform compared to the full BPP, possesses a lower false-positive rate than an NST alone, and has a high positive predictive value.

8. Nonstress test (NST)—methods and interpretation.

**NONSTRESS TEST (NST)**

**Definition and Principle**

- The Nonstress Test (NST) involves the continuous electronic monitoring of the fetal heart rate (FHR) along with the recording of fetal movements using cardiotocography.
- **Principle:** The test is based on the observed association of FHR accelerations with fetal movements. Fetal cardiac accelerations are associated with fetal movements in more than 85% of the time. These accelerations are presumably reflex-mediated, and their presence reliably indicates a healthy, well-oxygenated fetus.
- **Objective:** The primary value of the NST is to identify fetal _wellness_ rather than illness. It is widely used as a primary biophysical screening test for uteroplacental insufficiency.

**Methodology**

- **Timing:** Testing should generally be started after 30 weeks of pregnancy.
- **Frequency:** It is recommended to be performed twice weekly in complicated or high-risk pregnancies, such as those with intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) or diabetes mellitus.
- **Observation Period:** The fetal heart rate and movements are continuously monitored for a standard 20-minute observation period.
- **Vibroacoustic Stimulation (VAS):** Fetal sleep can cause a lack of movements and accelerations. VAS can be safely used to change the fetal sleep state from quiet (non-REM) sleep to active (REM) sleep. A reactive NST following harmless VAS stimulation confirms a healthy, reactive fetus.

**Interpretation** The results of the NST are interpreted into two main categories based on FHR reactivity:

**1. Reactive (Reassuring) Pattern**

- **Criteria:** A tracing is considered reactive when there are **two or more accelerations** of the FHR within a 20-minute observation period. Each acceleration must be **more than 15 beats per minute above the baseline** and last **longer than 15 seconds in duration**.
- **Inference:** Indicates a healthy fetus with intact central nervous system and cardiovascular reflex responses.
- **Prognosis:** It is highly reassuring and is associated with a very low perinatal death rate of about 5 per 1,000.

**2. Non-reactive (Non-reassuring) Pattern**

- **Criteria:** Characterized by the absence of any fetal reactivity (accelerations) during the monitoring period.
- **Inference:** It is a non-reassuring sign that may indicate fetal compromise, hypoxemia, or merely a fetal sleep state.
- **Prognosis:** A persistently non-reactive NST is associated with a significantly higher perinatal death rate of about 40 per 1,000.

**Clinical Significance and Limitations**

- **Accuracy:** The NST has a very low false-negative rate of 0.5% (or <1%), meaning a reactive test is highly reliable in predicting fetal wellbeing.
- **Limitation:** It has a high false-positive rate of 50% (or >50%), meaning many non-reactive fetuses are actually healthy (often just asleep).
- **Management of Non-reactive NST:** Because of the high false-positive rate, a non-reactive NST mandates further evaluation. The patient should be subjected to a Fetal Biophysical Profile (BPP) or a Modified Biophysical Profile (which combines the NST with ultrasonographically determined Amniotic Fluid Index) to conclusively rule out fetal asphyxia.
