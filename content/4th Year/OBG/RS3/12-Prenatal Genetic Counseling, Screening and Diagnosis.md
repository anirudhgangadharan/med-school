1. Triple test.
**TRIPLE TEST**

**Definition and Purpose**

- The triple test is a second-trimester maternal serum biochemical screening test utilized primarily for the prenatal detection of Down's syndrome (Trisomy 21).

**Timing of the Test**

- It is optimally performed between **15 and 18 weeks** of gestation.

**Components (Biochemical Analytes)** The test measures the levels of three specific biochemical markers in the maternal blood:

1. **MSAFP (Maternal Serum Alpha Fetoprotein)**
2. **hCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin)**
3. **uE3 (Unconjugated Estriol)**

**Interpretation of Values (in Down's Syndrome)** In a pregnancy affected by Trisomy 21 (Down's syndrome), the classical pattern of these serum analytes is altered as follows:

- **hCG levels:** Markedly **increased**.
- **uE3 levels:** Significantly **reduced**.
- **MSAFP levels:** Significantly **reduced**. _(Note: Conversely, an elevated MSAFP level is indicative of open Neural Tube Defects (NTDs))._

**Clinical Management and Follow-up**

- **Risk Adjustment:** The values of these analytes are converted into Multiples of the Median (MOM) and adjustments are made for maternal age, maternal weight, and ethnic group to calculate the exact risk.
- **Confirmatory Testing:** The triple test is strictly a screening test. Women who are evaluated as "screen positive" (high risk) must be offered invasive prenatal diagnostic procedures (such as genetic amniocentesis or cordocentesis) to obtain fetal tissues for definitive karyotyping and confirmation of the chromosomal anomaly.

2. Chorionic villus sampling—define, indications
**CHORIONIC VILLUS SAMPLING (CVS)**

**Definition** Chorionic villus sampling (CVS) is an invasive prenatal diagnostic procedure where a small sample of fetal trophoblast cells (villi) is aspirated from the chorion frondosum under ultrasonographic guidance,. It is primarily performed to obtain fetal tissue for the definitive diagnosis of genetic and chromosomal disorders.

**Timing of the Procedure**

- **Transcervical route (TC-CVS):** Performed between 10 and 13 weeks of gestation.
- **Transabdominal route (TA-CVS):** Performed from 10 weeks of gestation up to term.
- _Crucial Precaution:_ The procedure must strictly be performed after 9 completed weeks of gestation to avoid the risk of oromandibular limb reduction deformities (LRD),.

**Indications** CVS is offered to couples who are at a high risk of having a child with an inherited disorder. The specific indications include:

- **Confirmation of Screen-Positive Cases:** It is indicated as a definitive diagnostic test when non-invasive prenatal screening (such as the first-trimester combined test using Nuchal Translucency, free $\beta$-hCG, and PAPP-A, or the cell-free DNA test) shows a high risk for fetal aneuploidy,,,.
- **Cytogenetic Diagnosis (Chromosomal Anomalies):** To diagnose numerical or structural chromosomal defects, such as Trisomy 21 (Down's syndrome), Monosomy X (Turner's syndrome), Trisomy 18, and Trisomy 13,.
- **DNA Analysis (Single Gene Disorders):** To diagnose specific inherited single gene mutations using DNA probes and Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) amplification or chromosomal microarrays. It is highly indicated for conditions like cystic fibrosis, Tay-Sachs disease, and $\beta$-thalassemia.

**Major Advantages of CVS**

- **Rapid Results:** Cytogenetic results from direct preparation can be obtained within 24 to 48 hours, while tissue culture takes about 10-14 days,,.
- **Early Safe Termination:** Because the diagnosis is made early in the first trimester, it allows the parents to opt for a safer and psychologically less traumatic termination of pregnancy if the fetus is found to be severely affected,.

**Complications and Limitations (High-Yield Additions)**

- **Fetal Loss:** The procedure carries a fetal loss risk of approximately 1-2% (some estimates cite 0.2-0.5%),.
- **False Positives:** There is a 2-3% chance of false-positive results due to confined placental mosaicism or maternal cell contamination. In such equivocal cases, a follow-up amniocentesis is required to confirm the diagnosis.
- **Fetomaternal Hemorrhage:** It can cause fetomaternal bleeding; therefore, an administration of 50 $\mu$g of anti-D immunoglobulin is mandatory for Rh-negative, unimmunized women following the procedure,.
- **Contraindications for Transcervical Route:** TC-CVS is avoided in women with active genital herpes, cervicitis, vaginal bleeding, cervical myoma, or an acutely angulated uterus. It should also be avoided in HIV-positive women.
