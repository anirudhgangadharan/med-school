1. Functions of Placenta
**FUNCTIONS OF PLACENTA**

The placenta is a highly specialized organ that serves as a vital link between the mother and the developing fetus. It performs multiple essential roles, achieving fetomaternal exchange of gases, nutrients, and waste products through mechanisms like simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport, endocytosis, and bulk flow.

The important functions of the placenta are detailed below:

**1. RESPIRATORY FUNCTION**

- Although fetal respiratory movements begin early, there is no gaseous exchange in the fetal lungs.
- Intake of oxygen and output of carbon dioxide take place by simple diffusion across the fetal membrane.
- The driving force for this exchange is the partial pressure gradient between the maternal and fetal circulations.
- The placenta supplies oxygen to the fetus at a rate of 8 mL/kg/min, facilitated by a cord blood flow of 165–330 mL/min.

**2. NUTRITIVE FUNCTION** The fetus obtains all its nutritional requirements from the maternal blood.

- **Carbohydrates:** Glucose is the principal source of energy and is transferred across the placenta by facilitated diffusion using specific transporter proteins (GLUT-1) located in the syncytiotrophoblast.
- **Lipids:** Triglycerides and fatty acids are directly transported to the fetus in early pregnancy. Cholesterol is also capable of direct transfer across the placenta, giving fetal fat a dual origin.
- **Proteins and Amino Acids:** Amino acids are transferred by active transport against a concentration gradient via an ATPase enzymatic mechanism. Essential proteins like maternal Immunoglobulin G (IgG) cross via endocytosis to provide passive immunity to the fetus.
- **Water and Electrolytes:** Water, sodium, potassium, and chloride cross the placental membrane by simple diffusion. In contrast, calcium, phosphorus, and iron are transferred via active transport against the concentration gradient.
- **Vitamins:** Water-soluble vitamins cross by active transport, whereas fat-soluble vitamins are transferred slowly, keeping their levels low in the fetal blood.
- **Hormones:** Insulin, thyroid hormones, and adrenal steroids cross at a very slow rate. Parathormone and calcitonin do not cross the placenta.

**3. EXCRETORY FUNCTION**

- Waste products generated by fetal metabolism, such as urea, uric acid, and creatinine, are excreted into the maternal blood through simple diffusion.

**4. ENDOCRINE FUNCTION** The placenta acts as a temporary new endocrine organ (powerhouse) to maintain the pregnancy, taking over the function of the corpus luteum by 6-8 weeks (luteal-placental shift). The syncytiotrophoblast is the principal site for the synthesis of both protein and steroid hormones.

- **Protein Hormones:**
    - _Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG):_ Maintains the corpus luteum until 6 weeks, stimulates fetal Leydig cells to produce testosterone, stimulates maternal thyroid and adrenal steroidogenesis, and possesses immunosuppressive activity.
    - _Human Placental Lactogen (hPL):_ Has growth-promoting and lactogenic effects. It antagonizes maternal insulin action, causing maternal lipolysis to ensure a continuous transfer of glucose and amino acids to the fetus.
    - _Pregnancy-Specific $\beta$-1 Glycoprotein (PS $\beta$-1G) & Early Pregnancy Factor (EPF):_ Act as potent immunosuppressors to prevent the immunorejection of the conceptus.
    - _Pregnancy Associated Plasma Protein-A (PAPP-A):_ Regulates the bioavailability of insulin-like growth factors (IGF), aiding normal fetal growth.
    - _Growth Factors:_ Includes Inhibin, activin, IGF-1 and 2, TGF-$\beta$, and EGF, which have varied paracrine, steroidogenic, and immunosuppressive roles.
- **Steroid Hormones:**
    - _Estrogen (primarily Estriol):_ Synthesized by the fetoplacental unit. It causes hypertrophy and hyperplasia of the uterine myometrium to accommodate the growing fetus and aids in the development of the breasts.
    - _Progesterone:_ Synthesized from maternal cholesterol. It maintains uterine quiescence by stabilizing lysosomal membranes and inhibiting prostaglandin synthesis, stimulates decidual changes, and aids in protecting the conceptus via immunomodulation.

**5. ENZYMATIC FUNCTION**

- Numerous enzymes are elaborated by the placenta.
- Important enzymes include diamine oxidase (which inactivates circulatory pressor amines), oxytocinase (which neutralizes oxytocin), and phospholipase A2 (which synthesizes arachidonic acid).

**6. BARRIER FUNCTION**

- The fetal membrane acts as a protective barrier holding back substances with a high molecular weight (greater than 500 daltons).
- _Clinical limitations:_ The barrier is not perfect. Maternal infections caused by viruses (rubella, chickenpox, cytomegalovirus), bacteria (_Treponema pallidum_, _Tubercle bacillus_), and protozoa (_Toxoplasma_, malaria) can be transmitted across the placenta to the fetus. Additionally, almost any drug administered during pregnancy can cross the placental barrier and potentially exert deleterious effects on the fetus.

**7. IMMUNOLOGICAL FUNCTION**

- Despite the fetus and placenta containing paternally determined antigens foreign to the mother, the conceptus is not rejected as an allograft.
- Placental hormones, proteins (like SP1 and EPF), PAPP-A, steroids, and hCG offer immunological protection through their immunosuppressive effects.
- The placenta induces a shift in the maternal immune response from cell-mediated (T helper 1) to humoral (T helper 2) immunity, which is highly beneficial to the maintenance of pregnancy.
- Decidual Natural Killer (NK) cells interact with extravillous trophoblast HLA Class I molecules. This interaction regulates the depth of trophoblastic invasion into the spiral arteries, preventing morbid adhesion of the placenta (e.g., placenta accreta).
1. **AMNIOTIC FLUID INDEX (AFI)**

**Definition** The Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI) is a standardized, non-invasive ultrasonographic method used to assess and quantify the volume of amniotic fluid in the uterine cavity. It is a vital parameter for diagnosing amniotic fluid volume disorders such as oligohydramnios and polyhydramnios.

**Method of Measurement (Four Quadrant Technique)**

- The maternal abdomen is artificially divided into four quadrants using the umbilicus, symphysis pubis, and the uterine fundus as anatomical reference points.
- Under ultrasound guidance, the single deepest (largest) vertical pocket of amniotic fluid is measured in each of the four quadrants.
- The sum of these four measurements (in centimeters) gives the total Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI).

**Normal Values**

- **Normal Range:** An AFI between 5 cm and 24 cm is considered normal.
- These values represent the volume falling between the 5th and 95th percentiles for gestational age, evaluated typically from 28 to 40 weeks.

**Abnormal Values and Clinical Interpretation**

- **Oligohydramnios:** Defined as an AFI of less than 5 cm (below the 5th percentile). It strongly suggests decreased fetal renal perfusion and oliguria due to placental insufficiency. An AFI $\le$ 5 cm is associated with an increased risk of umbilical cord compression, abnormal CTG, meconium-stained liquor, and overall perinatal mortality and morbidity.
- **Borderline Oligohydramnios:** Defined as an AFI between 5 cm and 8 cm.
- **Polyhydramnios (Hydramnios):** Sonographic diagnosis is made when the AFI is more than 25 cm (above the 95th percentile for gestational age).

**Clinical Significance & Obstetric Applications**

- **Fetal Wellbeing Assessment:** AFI is a core component of the "Modified Biophysical Profile," which combines the Non-Stress Test (NST) and AFI to rapidly and effectively screen for uteroplacental insufficiency.
- **High-Risk Pregnancy Surveillance:** Serial AFI measurements (often twice weekly) are strictly indicated in the monitoring of prolonged/post-term pregnancies, Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), and multiple pregnancies.
- **Guiding Obstetric Interventions:**
    - An AFI $\le$ 5 cm in a compromised or post-term fetus is a direct indication for induction of labor or cesarean delivery to prevent intrauterine fetal death.
    - In the management of severe polyhydramnios via therapeutic amniocentesis (amnioreduction), fluid is slowly decompressed and drainage is typically stopped when the AFI drops below 25 cm.
- _Note:_ While AFI is a widely used index, current guidelines note that the Maximum Vertical Pocket (MVP) measurement is sometimes considered superior to AFI for accurately diagnosing oligohydramnios.
1. **VELAMENTOUS INSERTION OF CORD**

**Definition and Anatomy**

- It is an abnormality of umbilical cord attachment where the cord is attached to the fetal membranes instead of directly into the placental disc.
- The branching umbilical vessels traverse unprotected between the membranes (amnion and chorion) for a varying distance before they reach and supply the placenta.

**Clinical Significance and Complications**

- **Vasa Previa:** A highly critical condition that arises if the leash of these unprotected blood vessels happens to traverse through the membranes overlying the internal cervical os, positioning them directly in front of the fetal presenting part.
- **Rupture and Fetal Hemorrhage:** When the membranes rupture (spontaneously or artificially), the overlying umbilical vessels are torn, leading to sudden vaginal bleeding.
- **Fetal Exsanguination:** Because the bleeding is entirely from the fetal circulation, even a minimal blood loss can rapidly result in fetal exsanguination, severe hypoxia, and fetal death. Fetal mortality in vasa previa can be as high as 50%.
- **Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR):** Velamentous cord insertion is also recognized as an umbilical cord factor associated with the development of FGR.

**Diagnosis**

- **Antenatal Diagnosis:** The condition and the presence of associated vasa previa can be accurately diagnosed using ultrasonography with color Doppler and pulse Doppler mapping.
- **Intrapartum Diagnosis:** Vaginal bleeding accompanying the rupture of membranes, often immediately followed by severe fetal distress (manifesting as fetal tachycardia or a sinusoidal fetal heart rate tracing), is highly suggestive.
- **Biochemical Tests:** Detection of fetal blood in the vaginal aspirate confirms the diagnosis. This is done by detecting nucleated red blood cells using **Singer's alkali denaturation test** or identifying fetal hemoglobin via the **Apt test**.

**Management**

- **Antenatal:** If diagnosed antenatally, the patient requires close monitoring, often needing hospital admission between 28-32 weeks, administration of corticosteroids for fetal lung maturity, and an elective cesarean delivery before the onset of labor (around 34 to 36 weeks) to prevent catastrophic rupture.
- **In the presence of Fetal Bleeding (Ruptured Vasa Previa):**
    - **Live Fetus:** Urgent and expeditious delivery is essential (category-I emergency cesarean section) to salvage the baby.
    - **Neonatal Resuscitation:** The infant's hemoglobin should be estimated immediately at birth, and preparations for an urgent neonatal blood transfusion must be carried out. Immediate cord clamping should be done to prevent further neonatal hypovolemia.
    - **Dead Fetus:** If fetal death has already occurred, conservative management is followed, and spontaneous vaginal delivery is awaited.
