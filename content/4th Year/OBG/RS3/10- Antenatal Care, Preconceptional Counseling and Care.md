1. Antenatal care—objectives, components, importance.
**DEFINITION** Antenatal (prenatal) care refers to the systematic supervision, including examination and advice, of a woman during pregnancy. It is a care continuum that begins before pregnancy and concludes at delivery and the postpartum period.

**AIMS AND OBJECTIVES** The ultimate objective is to ensure a normal pregnancy culminating in the delivery of a healthy baby from a healthy mother. The specific aims are:

- To screen and identify 'high-risk' cases.
- To prevent, detect, and treat any maternal or fetal complications at the earliest.
- To ensure continued risk assessment and provide ongoing primary preventive healthcare.
- To educate the mother about the physiology of pregnancy and labor (via mothercraft classes) to alleviate fear and improve her psychology.
- To discuss with the couple regarding the place, time, and mode of delivery, and the care of the newborn.
- To motivate the couple about family planning and provide appropriate advice.

**COMPONENTS OF ANTENATAL CARE** The antenatal care bundle comprises careful history taking, general and obstetrical examinations, necessary investigations, and antenatal advice.

**1. The First Visit** The first visit should ideally not be deferred beyond the second missed period.

- **History Taking:** Includes vital statistics, maternal age (extremes of age like teenage or $\ge$35 years are obstetric risk factors), gravida and parity, detailed history of present and past pregnancies, menstrual history (to calculate Expected Date of Delivery using Naegele's formula), and past medical, surgical, personal, and family history.
- **General Physical Examination:** Assessment of build, nutrition, height (short stature $\le$152.4 cm may indicate small pelvis), pallor, jaundice, edema of the legs, and blood pressure.
- **Systemic and Obstetric Examination:** Examination of the heart, lungs, and breasts. Abdominal examination assesses the fundal height in relation to the period of amenorrhea. Routine vaginal examination before 12 weeks is generally replaced by ultrasound.
- **Routine Investigations:** Blood (hemoglobin, hematocrit, ABO & Rh grouping, blood glucose, VDRL) and urine analysis (protein and sugar).
- **Special Investigations:** Serological tests (rubella, HBV, HIV), prenatal genetic screening, and ultrasonography. An early USG helps confirm viability, accurate dating, number of fetuses, and gross anomalies.
- **Immunization:** Tetanus toxoid (0.5 mL IM) is given in 2 doses at a 6-week interval for unprotected women; previously immunized women receive a single booster dose in the last trimester.

**2. Subsequent Visits**

- **Schedule:** Checkups are generally done at 4-week intervals up to 28 weeks, 2-week intervals up to 36 weeks, and weekly thereafter until delivery. The WHO recommends at least 4 visits in resource-limited settings (at 16, 24-28, 32, and 36 weeks).
- **Objectives:** To assess fetal wellbeing, lie, presentation, position, amniotic fluid volume, fetal growth, and screen for anemia or pre-eclampsia.
- **Clinical Assessment:** In each visit, maternal weight, pallor, edema, and blood pressure are recorded. Abdominal examination is performed to note the symphysis fundal height, fetal parts, fetal movements, and fetal heart sounds.

**3. Antenatal Advice**

- **Diet:** Should be light, nutritious, easily digestible, and rich in protein, minerals, and vitamins. Caloric requirement increases by 300 Kcal/day during the second half of pregnancy.
- **Supplementation:** Routine iron therapy (60 mg elemental iron) is needed from 16 weeks onwards. Supplementary vitamins, calcium, and folic acid are also advised.
- **Rest and Sleep:** The patient requires about 10 hours of sleep (8 hours at night and 2 hours during the day), especially in the last 6 weeks.
- **Bowel and Hygiene:** Advice is given to avoid constipation through diet. Comfortable, loose clothing and good dental care are recommended.
- **Travel and Habits:** Long journeys with jerks should be avoided in the first trimester and the last 6 weeks. Smoking and alcohol must be strictly avoided as they can cause fetal maldevelopment and growth restriction.
- **Warning Signs:** Women must be counseled to report immediately if they experience intense headache, visual changes, severe abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding, leakage of fluid, or decreased fetal movements.

**IMPORTANCE / VALUES OF ANTENATAL CARE** The value of systematic antenatal supervision is globally recognized as the cornerstone of safe motherhood.

- **Screening and Risk Assessment:** It is vital to screen and identify high-risk cases so that medical disorders and obstetric complications can be sorted out and managed at the earliest.
- **Educational Opportunity:** It provides an opportunity to boost the mother's confidence, remove her fear of the unknown, and make her realize that childbirth is a physiological process.
- **Implementation of Preventive Care:** Advice regarding diet, drugs, immunization, and family planning is better accepted during pregnancy than in the nonpregnant state.
- **Reduction of Mortality and Morbidity:** High-quality antenatal care directly results in a marked reduction in maternal mortality (reduced to about one-seventh) and morbidity. It similarly causes a significant reduction in perinatal mortality (about one-fifth) and morbidity.
- **Strategic Role:** In the continuum of obstetric care, "antenatal care is said to be the strategy; the intranatal care is the tactic in obstetrics," making one indispensable from the other for a successful obstetric outcome.


2. Preconceptional counseling.
**PRECONCEPTIONAL COUNSELING AND CARE**

**Definition** When a couple is seen and counseled about pregnancy, its course, and outcome well before the time of actual conception, it is called preconceptional counseling.

**Aim** The primary aim is to ensure that a woman enters pregnancy with an optimal state of health which would be safe both for herself and the fetus.

**Objectives**

- To reduce the risk of adverse health effects for the woman, fetus, and neonate.
- To optimize health and provide education about a healthy pregnancy.
- To detect and optimally manage chronic medical illnesses (such as diabetes, hypertension, psychiatric illness, and thyroid disease) before pregnancy, as they have adverse effects on pregnancy outcomes.
- To thoroughly review all the medications that the woman is currently taking.
- To assess the immunization status of women of reproductive age and provide appropriate vaccine coverage.
- To screen for sexually transmitted infections (STIs), exposure to infectious diseases (like Zika virus), use of alcohol, nicotine products, opioids, and risks of intimate partner violence.

**Key Recommendations and Interventions**

- **Nutritional Supplementation:** The woman should start prepregnancy folic acid supplementation. A dose of 400 $\mu$g (0.4 mg) daily is advised for all women planning pregnancy, starting 4 weeks before conception and continuing for at least 3 months thereafter. A higher dose (5 mg a day) is advised for high-risk situations (such as a previous child with neural tube defects, multiple pregnancy, or women on anticonvulsant therapy) to significantly reduce the incidence of neural tube defects. The dietary adequacy of calcium, iron, and vitamins (A, B12, D, and other nutrients) should also be maintained.
- **Weight Management:** Women are encouraged to start their pregnancy with a normal Body Mass Index (BMI).
- **Genetic Counseling:** Risks of inheritable diseases (like cystic fibrosis and thalassemia) and the importance of prenatal diagnosis for chromosomal and genetic diseases are discussed with the parents. Preconceptional genetic counseling and prenatal diagnosis are proven interventions to manage fetal congenital malformations and chromosomal anomalies.
- **Educational Classes:** Prenatal educational classes are highly valuable and include discussions regarding delivery, timing, methods, and possible interventions (such as ventouse/forceps or cesarean delivery).

**Role in Specific Medical Disorders (High-Yield Examples)**

- **Diabetes Mellitus:** The goal is to achieve tight glycemic control before the onset of pregnancy. Women with preexisting diabetes should be advised to achieve an HbA1c $\le$ 6.5% prior to conception, which brings down the risk of major fetal congenital malformations to 0.8–2%.
- **Heart Disease:** Preconceptional evaluation is essential to assess the etiology, functional capacity, severity of hypertension, and the possible outcome of pregnancy.
- **Epilepsy:** Counseling involves discussing the risk of inheritance, replacing polytherapy with monotherapy if possible, and initiating folic acid supplementation at a higher dose of 4 mg/day. Valproic acid has an increased risk of major fetal congenital malformations and is managed accordingly.
- **Thyroid Dysfunction:** Considering the hazards during pregnancy, adequate treatment should be instituted to bring the thyroid function profile to normal; it is critical that the patient is euthyroid at the time of conception.
- **Renal Transplant:** Conception is not advised within the first year following a renal transplant because the risk of acute rejection is 10–15%, and teratogenic medications (like ACE inhibitors and mycophenolate mofetil) must be discontinued before pregnancy.
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Conception is ideally planned during a period of disease quiescence (sustained remission), as the disease can flare up at any time in pregnancy.

**Healthcare Providers and Limitations**

- The counseling should be conducted by primary healthcare providers, taking the help of an obstetrician, physician, and geneticist when required.
- **Limitations:** Currently, only a small percentage of women take advantage of preconceptual care, largely because many pregnancies remain unplanned. It is important to create more awareness, as preconceptional checkups help in the early detection and prevention of risk factors.

3. Antenatal care—objectives, components, visits schedule (recommended in each trimester), current recommendation, importance
**ANTENATAL CARE**

**Definition** Systematic supervision (examination and advice) of a woman during pregnancy is called antenatal (prenatal) care. It is a preventive healthcare measure in continuum that starts before pregnancy and ends at delivery and the postpartum period.

**Aims and Objectives** The primary objective of antenatal care is to ensure a normal pregnancy with the delivery of a healthy baby from a healthy mother. The specific aims include:

- To screen the 'high-risk' cases.
- To prevent or to detect and treat at the earliest any complication.
- To ensure continued risk assessment and to provide ongoing primary preventive health care.
- To educate the mother about the physiology of pregnancy and labor by demonstrations, charts, and diagrams (mothercraft classes), so that fear is removed and psychology is improved.
- To discuss with the couple about the place, time, and mode of delivery provisionally, and the care of the newborn.
- To motivate the couple about the need for family planning and provide appropriate advice to couples seeking medical termination of pregnancy.

**Components of Antenatal Care (Antenatal Care Bundles)** The routine prenatal care is recorded in a standardized proforma and consists of three main bundles:

1. Careful history taking and examinations (both general and obstetrical).
2. Carrying out necessary investigations.
3. Advice given to the pregnant woman (regarding diet, drugs, hygiene, etc.).

**Antenatal Visits Schedule** The first visit should ideally not be deferred beyond the second missed period.

- **Ideal / Standard Schedule:**
    
    - Checkups are done at an interval of 4 weeks up to 28 weeks of pregnancy.
    - At an interval of 2 weeks up to 36 weeks.
    - Thereafter, weekly visits till delivery.
    - _Note:_ This should be flexible depending on the need and convenience of the patient.
- **Current WHO Recommendation (For Developing Countries / Low Resource Settings):**
    
    - As per WHO recommendations, the visits may be curtailed to a minimum of 4 visits to ensure essential obstetric care.
    - **1st visit:** Between 12-14 weeks (First Trimester).
    - **2nd visit:** Between 14-26 weeks (Second Trimester).
    - **3rd visit:** Between 28-34 weeks (Third Trimester).
    - **4th visit:** Between 36-40 weeks (Last Trimester).

**Values and Importance of Antenatal Care** The chief values of antenatal supervision are:

- **Screening of high-risk cases:** Medical disorders and obstetric complications are sorted out at the earliest.
- **Continued Risk Assessment:** The risk assessment is a continuous process, and the detection of high-risk factors deserves no credit unless proper steps are taken to rectify them (e.g., admission, investigation, treatment).
- **Improved Maternal Outcomes:** The net effect of antenatal care is a marked reduction in maternal mortality (about one-seventh) and morbidity.
- **Improved Perinatal Outcomes:** There is a significant reduction in perinatal mortality (about one-fifth) and morbidity.
- **Acceptance of Advice:** During pregnancy, advice regarding diet, drugs, family planning guidance, and immunization schedules are better followed than in the nonpregnant state.
- **Psychological Boost:** It acts as an educational opportunity to make the patient realize that childbirth is a physiological process, removing the fear of the unknown, and boosting the psychology so the patient finds herself confident during labor.
- _Limitation to note:_ Good antenatal care alone cannot reduce maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity unless the woman receives good care during labor (intranatal care) and the postnatal period. Antenatal care is the strategy, and intranatal care is the tactic in obstetrics; one is indispensable from the other.


3. Routine antenatal (laboratory tests) investigations (in 8 weeks primigravida) and antenatal advice.
**ROUTINE ANTENATAL INVESTIGATIONS (AT 8 WEEKS PRIMIGRAVIDA / FIRST VISIT)**

The primary objectives of the first antenatal visit are to assess the health status of the mother and fetus, obtain baseline investigations, and organize continued obstetric care.

**1. Blood Tests**

- **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Indices:** Hemoglobin (Hb) and hematocrit are estimated to screen for anemia.
- **Blood Grouping:** ABO and Rh blood typing along with an antibody screen are mandatory.
- **Infection Screening (Serology):** VDRL (for syphilis), Hepatitis B Virus (HBV), and Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) testing (with consent).
- **Blood Glucose:** Random or fasting blood glucose testing is performed to screen for preexisting or overt diabetes.
- **Rubella Immunity:** Serological tests for rubella antibodies to detect maternal immunity.
- **Hemoglobinopathies:** Hemoglobin electrophoresis is advised if the patient is at risk for conditions like thalassemia or sickle cell trait.

**2. Urine Tests**

- **Urine Routine & Microscopic:** Urine is tested (via dipstick) for the presence of protein and sugar.
- **Urine Culture:** Performed at the first visit to screen for asymptomatic bacteriuria (which carries a 25% risk of progressing to acute pyelonephritis if left untreated).

**3. Cytology & Genetics**

- **Cervical Cytology:** A Pap smear (cervical cytology) is obtained as a routine protocol, especially if the woman has not been screened previously.
- **Genetic Testing:** Offered based on the patient's personal or family history.

**4. Ultrasonography (First Trimester Scan)**

- Transvaginal Sonography (TVS) or transabdominal ultrasound is performed to confirm gestational age (dating), confirm viability (cardiac activity), determine the number of fetuses, and evaluate for any gross fetal anomalies or adnexal pathology.

---

**ANTENATAL ADVICE**

The goal of antenatal advice is to maintain or improve the maternal health status to the optimum level till delivery and to remove the fear of the unknown by educating the mother.

**1. Diet and Nutrition**

- The diet should be light, nutritious, easily digestible, and rich in proteins, minerals, and vitamins.
- It should include at least half to 1 liter of milk daily (as 1 liter provides about 1 g of calcium), plenty of green vegetables, and fruits.
- A woman with a normal pre-pregnancy Body Mass Index (BMI) should eat adequately to achieve an optimum weight gain of 11-16 kg.
- **Folic Acid:** Supplementation with 5 mg of folic acid daily up to 12 weeks of pregnancy is strongly advised to reduce the incidence of neural tube defects.

**2. Rest and Sleep**

- The patient may continue her usual activities; however, excessive and strenuous work must be avoided, especially in the first trimester.
- On average, the patient needs about 10 hours of sleep (8 hours at night and 2 hours at noon).
- Recreational exercise (low impact, such as brisk walking) is permitted as long as she feels comfortable.

**3. Bowel Care**

- Constipation is common in pregnancy due to progesterone effects and can cause backache or increase the risk of abortion. Regular bowel habits should be maintained through adequate fluid intake, green vegetables, and fruits.

**4. Clothing, Shoes, and Belt**

- The patient should wear loose, comfortable garments.
- High-heeled shoes and constricting belts must be avoided, as the center of balance alters in advanced pregnancy.

**5. Dental and Breast Care**

- Good dental and oral hygiene should be maintained. If dental extraction or filling is required, it is preferably done in the second trimester.
- A well-fitting brassiere should be worn to provide relief from breast engorgement and discomfort.

**6. Coitus**

- Coitus is generally not restricted during normal pregnancy. However, women with an increased risk of miscarriage or preterm labor should avoid it, as prostaglandins and oxytocin released during coitus may stimulate uterine contractions.

**7. Travel**

- Traveling in vehicles with jerks should be avoided, particularly in the first trimester and the last 6 weeks.
- The rail route is preferable to the bus route.
- Air travel in a pressurized aircraft is generally safe up to 36 weeks, but prolonged sitting in any vehicle should be avoided due to the increased risk of venous stasis and thromboembolism. The seat belt should be fastened under the abdomen.

**8. Addictions (Smoking and Alcohol)**

- Smoking should be completely stopped as it is associated with smaller babies and an increased chance of abortion.
- Alcohol consumption must be drastically curtailed or completely avoided to prevent fetal maldevelopment or growth restriction.

**9. Drugs and Medications**

- Almost all drugs given to the mother cross the placenta. The patient must be advised not to take any over-the-counter medications without consulting her physician, keeping the possibility of teratogenicity in mind.

**10. Warning Signs (When to Report to the Hospital)**

- The patient must be educated on warning signs and instructed to report to the physician immediately if she develops: intense headache, visual disturbances, severe/excessive vomiting, disturbed sleep, epigastric pain, scanty urination, painful uterine contractions, sudden gush of watery fluid per vaginam (rupture of membranes), or any active vaginal bleeding.
4. Investigations in first trimester of pregnancy
**INVESTIGATIONS IN THE FIRST TRIMESTER OF PREGNANCY**

The investigations during the first trimester (first 12 weeks) are systematically directed towards confirming the pregnancy, accurately establishing the gestational age, assessing the maternal health status, and screening for fetal anomalies.

**I. ROUTINE MATERNAL BLOOD & URINE INVESTIGATIONS** During the initial obstetric visit, baseline investigations are ordered to evaluate the health status of the mother:

- **Blood Grouping and Typing:** Blood for ABO and Rh typing, and antibody screen to identify Rh-negative, non-immunized mothers.
- **Hematological Indices:** Complete Blood Count (CBC), hemoglobin, and hematocrit. Hemoglobin electrophoresis is advised if the woman is at risk for hemoglobinopathy.
- **Metabolic Status:** Blood glucose estimation (fasting and postprandial/OGTT or HbA1c) to screen for Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) or overt diabetes.
- **Infectious Disease Screening:** Serological tests for Syphilis (VDRL), Hepatitis B Virus (HBV), Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) (with consent at VCTC), and testing for Rubella antibody immunity.
- **Urine Analysis:** Routine urinalysis for protein and sugar (dipstick), and urine culture to rule out asymptomatic bacteriuria and urinary tract infection.

**II. PREGNANCY CONFIRMATION TESTS (IMMUNOLOGICAL)** Immunological tests depend on the detection of the unique $\beta$-subunit of Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG) in maternal urine or serum using specific antibodies.

- **ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay):** A highly sensitive and specific test capable of detecting hCG in serum at levels as low as 1-2 mIU/mL. It can detect pregnancy as early as 5 days before the first missed period (or 8-9 days post-fertilization).
- **Two-Site Sandwich Immunoassay (Card Tests):** A rapid screening test that yields results in 4-5 minutes and becomes positive typically on the first day of the missed period.

**III. FIRST TRIMESTER ULTRASONOGRAPHY (TVS)** Transvaginal Ultrasonography (TVS) is the most accurate modality for establishing early pregnancy parameters and gestational age (variation $\pm$ 5 days).

- **Aims:** (1) Confirm intrauterine pregnancy and viability, (2) provide accurate dating, (3) detect the number of fetuses (and chorionicity in twins), (4) diagnose early pregnancy failure or ectopic/molar pregnancy, and (5) screen for gross fetal anomalies or adnexal pathology.
- **Key Chronological Findings:**
    - **4.5 to 5 weeks:** Appearance of the gestational sac.
    - **5.5 weeks:** Yolk sac becomes visible.
    - **6 weeks:** Fetal pole (CRL $\ge$4-5 mm) with visible embryonic cardiac activity.
- **Gestational Dating:** Measurement of the Crown-Rump Length (CRL) between 7 and 12 weeks is the most accurate parameter for predicting the expected date of delivery.

**IV. FIRST TRIMESTER ANEUPLOIDY SCREENING (11-14 WEEKS)** Noninvasive combined screening is offered between 11 and 14 weeks to evaluate the risk of chromosomal abnormalities (e.g., Trisomy 21).

- **Biophysical Markers (USG):**
    - **Nuchal Translucency (NT):** Increased fetal nuchal skin thickness ($\ge$ 3 mm) is a strong marker for chromosomal anomalies, cardiac defects, and skeletal dysplasia.
    - **Nasal Bone:** Absence of the nasal bone is associated with Down's syndrome.
- **Biochemical Markers:**
    - **Free $\beta$-hCG:** Levels are increased in Trisomy 21.
    - **Pregnancy-Associated Plasma Protein-A (PAPP-A):** Levels are reduced in Trisomy 21.

**V. NONINVASIVE PRENATAL TESTING (NIPT) / CELL-FREE DNA**

- **Principle:** Small fetal DNA fragments (cell-free DNA or cf-DNA) are detected and sequenced from maternal plasma or whole blood.
- **Timing & Accuracy:** The test is generally performed from 10 weeks of pregnancy onward. It has a high detection rate (>98%) for Trisomy 21, 18, and 13, with a false-positive rate of 0.1-0.5%.

**VI. INVASIVE PRENATAL DIAGNOSTIC PROCEDURES** If a woman is screen-positive for genetic/chromosomal risks, invasive diagnostic testing is offered to obtain fetal tissues for definitive karyotyping.

- **Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS):** Performed between 10 and 13 weeks transcervically, or transabdominally from 10 weeks onwards. Cytogenetic results can be obtained within 24-48 hours, allowing for safe termination in the first trimester if required. Early genetic amniocentesis (11-14 weeks) is not recommended due to high cell culture failure rates and complications.

**VII. OTHER ROUTINE INVESTIGATIONS**

- **Cervical Cytology (Pap Smear) / HPV Screening:** Evaluated as a routine protocol at the initial obstetric visit to exclude abnormal cervical cells (dysplasia/malignancy).

5. Nutrition in pregnancy and lactation.
**NUTRITION IN PREGNANCY AND LACTATION**

**I. AIMS AND OBJECTIVES OF NUTRITION** The diet during pregnancy and lactation should be adequate to provide for:

- Maintenance of good maternal health.
- Optimum fetal growth and development.
- The strength and vitality required by the mother during labor.
- Successful lactation post-delivery.

**II. GENERAL PRINCIPLES OF DIET**

- The diet should ideally be light, nutritious, easily digestible, and rich in proteins, minerals, and vitamins.
- It should consist of the principal food of the woman's choice, supplemented by at least half a liter to 1 liter of milk daily (1 liter of milk provides approximately 1 gram of calcium).
- Plenty of green vegetables and fruits should be included.
- At least half of the total protein intake should be first-class proteins (containing all essential amino acids), and the majority of fat should be of animal origin (which contains vitamins A and D).
- During lactation, the mother should drink at least 1 extra liter of fluid per day to compensate for the fluid lost in breast milk.

**III. CALORIC AND MACRONUTRIENT REQUIREMENTS** **1. Energy (Calories):**

- **Pregnancy:** The basal metabolic rate increases by 30%. There is an increased calorie requirement of approximately 300 Kcal/day over the non-pregnant state during the second half of pregnancy. The recommended daily allowance is 2,500 Kcal.
- **Lactation:** A healthy mother produces 500–900 mL of breast milk daily, providing the baby with about 75 kcal/dL. This requires an additional 750 Kcal/day for the mother, which is met either through her diet or from the 5 kg of fat stored during pregnancy. The recommended daily allowance during lactation is 2,600 Kcal.

**2. Proteins:**

- Pregnancy is a state of positive nitrogen balance. At term, the fetus and placenta contain about 500 g of protein, and the maternal body stores an additional 500 g (in the uterus, breasts, and maternal blood).
- The daily protein requirement increases from 50 g in the non-pregnant state to 71 g during both the second half of pregnancy and lactation.

**3. Fats and Carbohydrates:**

- An average of 3–4 kg of fat is stored during pregnancy, mostly in the abdominal wall, breasts, hips, and thighs, to serve as a reserve for lactation.
- Glucose is the principal source of fetal energy and is transferred across the placenta via facilitated diffusion. In conditions like Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM), carbohydrates should be restricted to 40–50% of total calories, prioritizing complex carbohydrates with a low glycemic index.

**IV. MICRONUTRIENT REQUIREMENTS (MINERALS & VITAMINS)** **1. Iron:**

- **Pregnancy:** The total extra iron requirement is approximately 1,000 mg (300 mg for the fetus and placenta, 500 mg for expanded maternal red cell mass, and 200 mg for obligatory losses). During the second half of pregnancy, the daily iron requirement increases significantly to 6–7 mg/day. Because diet alone is insufficient (an adequate diet contains ~18-20 mg of iron, with only 10-20% absorption), pregnancy is an inevitable iron-deficiency state. Routine oral supplementation of 60 mg elemental iron (e.g., ferrous sulfate) is necessary from 16 weeks onwards.
- **Lactation:** The dietary allowance of iron during lactation is 30 mg/day (some of which must be supplemented).

**2. Calcium:**

- The growing fetus requires about 28 g of calcium, 80% of which is drained from the mother in the third trimester for fetal bone mineralization.
- **Pregnancy:** The recommended daily intake is 1,000 mg (compared to 500 mg in the non-pregnant state). Calcium absorption from the maternal intestine is doubled due to increased levels of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3.
- **Lactation:** The requirement increases further to 1,500 mg/day due to calcium loss in breast milk. Bone mineral density decreases during breastfeeding but returns to normal within 12 months after cessation.

**3. Folic Acid & Vitamin B12:**

- Folic acid requirements increase from 400 µg to 600 µg daily during pregnancy to support the expanded red cell volume and fetal growth.
- Pre-conceptional supplementation of 400 µg/day (or 5 mg/day in high-risk cases) starting 4 weeks before conception and continuing up to 12 weeks of pregnancy is strongly advised to prevent neural tube defects (NTDs).
- Vitamin B12 requirements increase from 2.4 µg to 2.6 µg daily during pregnancy.

**4. Other Essential Micronutrients (Daily Allowances):**

- **Zinc:** Increases from 8 mg to 15 mg in pregnancy, and 19 mg in lactation.
- **Iodine:** Increases from 150 µg to 220 µg in pregnancy, and 290 µg in lactation.
- **Vitamin A:** Increases from 700 µg to 1,000 µg in pregnancy, and 1,300 µg in lactation.
- **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C):** Increases from 75 mg to 120 mg in pregnancy.

**V. MATERNAL WEIGHT GAIN CONSIDERATIONS**

- Dietary advice should be realistic and based on the woman's pre-pregnancy Body Mass Index (BMI).
- **Normal BMI (18.5–24.9 kg/m²):** Recommended total weight gain is 11–16 kg (25–35 lbs).
- **Underweight BMI (<18.5 kg/m²):** Recommended weight gain is 28–40 lbs (up to 18 kg).
- **Overweight/Obese BMI (≥25 kg/m²):** Recommended weight gain is strictly limited to 7 kg (15–25 lbs for overweight, 11–20 lbs for obese classes). Intentional weight loss during pregnancy is not recommended.

**VI. NUTRITION IN SPECIAL CONDITIONS (GESTATIONAL DIABETES)**

- **Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT):** Caloric intake is restricted based on weight—30 kcal/kg/day for normal-weight women, 24 kcal/kg/day for overweight women, and 12 kcal/kg/day for morbidly obese women.
- The diet is divided into three main meals (breakfast 25%, lunch 30%, dinner 30%) and three snacks to prevent fasting hypoglycemia and postprandial hyperglycemia. Fiber-containing diets (complex carbohydrates) are increased.
