### **1. DEFINITION AND CLASSIFICATION**

- **Definition:** Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder resulting from either insulin deficiency or peripheral tissue resistance. Pregnancy acts as a diabetogenic state characterized by increased insulin resistance (due to hormones like hPL, cortisol, prolactin, and TNF-alpha) to ensure a continuous supply of glucose to the fetus.
- **Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM):** Defined as carbohydrate intolerance of variable severity with onset or first recognition during the present pregnancy.
- **Overt Diabetes:** Pre-existing diabetes (Type 1 or Type 2) or diabetes detected for the first time during pregnancy with classic symptoms and high blood glucose levels.

### **2. DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (GDM AND OVERT DIABETES)**

All pregnant women should be screened for GDM between 24 and 28 weeks of gestation. Early screening is indicated for those with high-risk factors (e.g., prior macrosomic baby, obesity, PCOS, family history).

- **Criteria for GDM (IADPSG - 75g Oral Glucose Tolerance Test):** Diagnosis is made if one or more of the following thresholds are met or exceeded:
    - Fasting Plasma Glucose: $\ge$ 92 mg/dL.
    - 1-hour post-glucose: $\ge$ 180 mg/dL.
    - 2-hour post-glucose: $\ge$ 153 mg/dL.
- **Criteria for Overt Diabetes (ADA):**
    - Fasting plasma glucose $\ge$ 126 mg/dL.
    - 2-hour post-glucose (75g) $\ge$ 200 mg/dL.
    - HbA1c $\ge$ 6.5%.

### **3. ADVERSE EFFECTS AND COMPLICATIONS**

**Maternal Complications**

- **During Pregnancy:**
    - Increased incidence of pre-eclampsia (25%).
    - Polyhydramnios (25-50%) due to fetal hyperglycemia leading to fetal polyuria.
    - Recurrent spontaneous abortions and preterm labor (26%).
    - Infections like urinary tract infections and vulvovaginitis.
    - Worsening of diabetic retinopathy and diabetic nephropathy.
    - Ketoacidosis, especially triggered by hyperemesis or infection.
- **During Labor:**
    - Prolongation of labor due to a big baby.
    - Increased risk of shoulder dystocia and perineal injuries.
    - High rates of operative interference (Cesarean delivery) and postpartum hemorrhage (PPH).
- **Puerperium:** Puerperal sepsis, lactation failure, and a 50% risk of developing overt Type-2 diabetes over the next 5-20 years for GDM patients.

**Fetal and Neonatal Complications**

- **Fetal Macrosomia (40-50%):** Birth weight >4 kg. Caused by maternal hyperglycemia leading to fetal hyperinsulinemia, which stimulates fetal fat accumulation and growth (Pedersen hypothesis).
- **Congenital Malformations (6-10%):** Due to poor glycemic control during organogenesis (first trimester). Includes cardiovascular defects (VSD, ASD, Coarctation), CNS defects (Neural tube defects, Anencephaly), and skeletal anomalies (Caudal regression syndrome, Sacral agenesis).
- **Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD):** Due to hypoxia, lactic acidemia, placental insufficiency, or ketoacidosis.
- **Neonatal Complications:**
    - _Hypoglycemia (50%)_ due to fetal hyperinsulinemia following cord clamping.
    - _Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)_ because hyperinsulinemia blocks the action of cortisol in surfactant synthesis.
    - Hyperbilirubinemia, polycythemia, hypocalcemia, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

### **4. MANAGEMENT DURING PREGNANCY (ANTENATAL CARE)**

**Preconceptional Counseling**

- Goal is tight glycemic control (HbA1c $\le$ 6.5%) before conception to significantly reduce the risk of major fetal malformations (down to 0.8-2%).
- High-dose folic acid (5 mg/day) supplementation to prevent neural tube defects.

**Antenatal Supervision and Fetal Monitoring**

- First-trimester combined screening (11-14 weeks) and a detailed high-resolution ultrasound with fetal echocardiography at 20-22 weeks are vital to detect structural anomalies.
- Serial USG every 2-3 weeks for fetal growth profile (to detect macrosomia or IUGR) and amniotic fluid volume assessment.
- Start low-dose aspirin (150 mg daily) from 12 weeks to reduce the high risk of pre-eclampsia.
- Fetal wellbeing assessment (NST, BPP) is initiated from 28 weeks onwards.

**Diet, Exercise, and Pharmacotherapy**

- **Diet (Medical Nutrition Therapy):** 30 kcal/kg for normal weight, restricted to 24 kcal/kg for overweight. Carbohydrates should be 40-50% (complex carbs preferred), protein 20%, and fat 30-40%. Follow a 3-meal, 3-snack regimen.
- **Exercise:** Brisk walking for 30 minutes daily (5 days a week) improves insulin sensitivity.
- **Target Blood Glucose:** Fasting $\le$ 95 mg/dL, 1-hour postprandial $\le$ 140 mg/dL, 2-hour postprandial $\le$ 120 mg/dL, and HbA1c $\le$ 6%.
- **Pharmacotherapy:** If targets are not met with diet and exercise, insulin is the primary and preferred medication as it does not cross the placenta. Oral agents like Metformin and Glibenclamide are also safe and effective first-line treatments.

### **5. MANAGEMENT DURING LABOR AND DELIVERY**

**Timing and Mode of Delivery**

- **Timing:** Well-controlled GDM on diet can be delivered at 39 to 40 weeks. Patients on insulin are planned for delivery at 39 to 39 6/7 weeks. If suboptimal control, vascular disease, or obstetric complications (like pre-eclampsia) are present, delivery is planned at 37 to 38 weeks.
- **Mode of Delivery:** Vaginal delivery is favored. However, Cesarean delivery is indicated (approx. 50% of cases) for fetal macrosomia (>4 kg), worsening hypertension, fetal compromise, or severe proliferative retinopathy to avoid shoulder dystocia and birth injuries.

**Glycemic Control During Labor**

- Procedure is scheduled for early morning. Morning insulin is omitted, and breakfast is withheld.
- Start IV normal saline. Once active labor begins, change to 5% dextrose at 100 mL/hour to maintain capillary blood glucose levels around 100 mg/dL.
- Blood glucose is monitored hourly. If glucose exceeds 110 mg/dL, a regular (short-acting) insulin infusion is administered.
- Epidural analgesia is ideal for pain relief.

**Puerperium and Neonatal Care**

- Following delivery, insulin requirements fall dramatically. The dose must be readjusted or reverted to the pre-pregnant level based on blood glucose checks.
- The baby must be attended by a neonatologist at birth and kept under observation (NICU) to detect and treat hypoglycemia, RDS, or birth injuries immediately.
- Early breastfeeding (within 30-60 minutes) is encouraged to combat neonatal hypoglycemia. Breastfeeding mothers require an additional 500 kcal/day in their diet.
- GDM patients should undergo a 75g 2-hour OGTT at 4-12 weeks postpartum to screen for persistent diabetes.
