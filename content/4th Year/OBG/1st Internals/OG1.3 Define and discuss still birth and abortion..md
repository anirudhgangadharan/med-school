### **1. Stillbirth**

**Definition**

- A stillbirth is defined as the birth of a newborn after the 28th completed week of gestation (weighing 1000 g or more) who does not breathe or show any sign of life after delivery.
- It includes two types:
    - **Antepartum deaths (macerated stillbirths):** The dead fetus undergoes an aseptic degenerative process called maceration in utero,.
    - **Intrapartum deaths (fresh stillbirths):** Death occurs during labor and delivery,.

**Stillbirth Rate**

- It is expressed as the number of stillbirths per 1000 total births (live births and stillbirths).
- The risk of stillbirth increases significantly with prolonged/post-term pregnancy, rising from 1 in 1000 at 37 weeks to 3-5 in 1000 at 42 weeks and beyond.

**Causes and Predisposing Factors** In about 20-35% of cases, the exact cause remains unexplained/idiopathic,,. Known causes include:

- **Fetal Causes (25-40%):** Chromosomal abnormalities, major structural anomalies, non-immune hydrops, fetal growth restriction (FGR), and congenital infections.
- **Placental/Umbilical Causes (20-35%):** Antepartum hemorrhage (abruptio placentae and placenta previa causing acute placental insufficiency), cord accidents (prolapse, true knot), twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), and chronic placental insufficiency.
- **Maternal Causes (5-10%):** Hypertensive disorders in pregnancy, diabetes mellitus, severe anemia, maternal infections (malaria, syphilis, toxoplasmosis, parvovirus), and acquired/inherited thrombophilias,.
- **Intrapartum Complications:** Birth asphyxia, obstructed labor, and traumatic delivery,.

**Evaluation and Investigations for a Stillbirth** A thorough evaluation is essential to find the cause and counsel the parents for future pregnancies,,:

- **Maternal Laboratory Tests:**
    - _Routine:_ Complete blood count, Kleihauer-Betke test (for fetomaternal hemorrhage), VDRL (syphilis), TSH, fasting glucose/HbA1c, Lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin antibody, and TORCH/Parvovirus B19 screening.
    - _Selected:_ Parental karyotyping, Homocysteine levels, and inherited thrombophilia screening.
- **Fetal Evaluation:** Whole-body photograph, skeletal X-ray (for dysplasia), fetal autopsy, and chromosomal studies (using fetal skin, cartilage, or placental tissue block).
- **Placental/Cord Examination:** Look for true knots, entanglement, number of vessels, meconium staining, and send the placenta for histopathological examination.

**Management and Future Pregnancy Counseling**

- **Immediate Management:** Expectant management allows spontaneous onset of labor in about 80% of cases within 7-10 days. Active management involves induction of labor, usually using a combination of mifepristone and misoprostol (PGE1) or oxytocin.
- **Bereavement Care:** Breaking the bad news gently, showing empathy, allowing the parents to hold the infant, and providing psychological support are crucial,.
- **Future Counseling:** Risk of recurrence varies from 0 to 8%. Preconceptional counseling, strict antenatal surveillance, early dating scans, targeted anomaly scans, and planned delivery at 38 weeks are recommended in subsequent pregnancies,.

### **2. Abortion (Miscarriage)**

**Definition**

- Abortion is the expulsion or extraction of an embryo or fetus weighing 500 g or less from its mother, independent of whether the cord has been cut or the placenta is attached.
- This weight roughly corresponds to 22 weeks (154 days) of gestation.
- The term **miscarriage** is the recommended medical terminology for spontaneous abortion.

**Classification** Abortions are broadly classified into:

1. **Spontaneous Miscarriage:**
    - _Isolated (Sporadic)_
    - _Recurrent (3 or more consecutive losses)_
    - _Clinical Types:_ Threatened, Inevitable, Complete, Incomplete, Missed, and Septic.
2. **Induced Abortion:**
    - _Legal (Medical Termination of Pregnancy - MTP)_
    - _Illegal / Unsafe Abortion_

**Etiology** The exact cause is often difficult to pinpoint and can be multifactorial,.

- **First Trimester Causes:**
    - _Genetic/Chromosomal (50%):_ Autosomal trisomies, monosomy X (45,X - most common single defect), triploidy, and structural rearrangements,.
    - _Endocrine Disorders:_ Luteal phase defect (LPD), uncontrolled diabetes, thyroid abnormalities.
    - _Immunological Factors:_ Autoimmune (Antiphospholipid antibodies) and alloimmune disorders,.
    - _Infections:_ Viral (Rubella, CMV), Parasitic (Toxoplasma), Bacterial (Chlamydia, Ureaplasma),.
- **Second Trimester Causes:**
    - _Anatomical Abnormalities:_ Cervical incompetence (congenital or acquired), Müllerian fusion defects (bicornuate or septate uterus), uterine fibroids (submucous), and intrauterine adhesions (Asherman's syndrome),.
    - _Maternal Medical Illnesses:_ Chronic hypertension, renal disease, severe anemia.

**Mechanism of Miscarriage**

- **Before 8 weeks:** The ovum, surrounded by villi and decidua, is typically expelled intact.
- **8 to 14 weeks:** Expulsion of the fetus often occurs leaving behind the placenta and membranes (incomplete miscarriage).
- **Beyond 14 weeks:** The process mimics a 'mini labor' where the fetus is expelled first, followed by the placenta.

**Clinical Types, Diagnostic Features, and Management** _(Adapted from High-Yield Table 16.1 & Text)_,

- **1. Threatened Miscarriage:**
    - _Features:_ Slight vaginal bleeding, mild pelvic pain, external os is **closed**, and uterine size corresponds to dates. USG shows a live fetus.
    - _Management:_ Expectant/conservative management (rest, reassurance, avoidance of strenuous activity),.
- **2. Inevitable Miscarriage:**
    - _Features:_ Increased vaginal bleeding, severe colicky lower abdominal pain, external os is **open**, and products of conception may be felt. Fetus is often dead.
    - _Management:_ Resuscitation followed by active evacuation of the uterus,.
- **3. Incomplete Miscarriage:**
    - _Features:_ Heavy vaginal bleeding, open os, and expulsion of a fleshy mass with a part of the conceptus retained inside. Uterus is smaller than the period of amenorrhea.
    - _Management:_ Resuscitation, Evacuation of Retained Products of Conception (ERPC) surgically (D&E/MVA) or medically using Misoprostol,,,.
- **4. Complete Miscarriage:**
    - _Features:_ Expulsion of products _en masse_. Bleeding is trace or absent, abdominal pain subsides, os is **closed**, and the uterine cavity is empty on USG.
    - _Management:_ No active intervention required if USG confirms an empty cavity,.
- **5. Missed Miscarriage (Early Fetal Demise):**
    - _Features:_ Fetus is dead but retained in utero. Trace brownish vaginal bleeding, regression of pregnancy symptoms, closed os, and uterus is smaller than dates. USG shows an anembryonic sac or fetus without cardiac activity.
    - _Management:_ Expectant management, medical management (Misoprostol 800 µg vaginally), or surgical evacuation (Suction Evacuation),,,.
- **6. Septic Abortion:**
    - _Features:_ Any abortion associated with clinical evidence of uterine infection. Manifests with purulent foul-smelling vaginal discharge, pelvic pain, and fever (>100.4°F/38°C).
    - _Management:_ High vaginal swab for culture, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, and prompt surgical evacuation of the uterus to remove the septic focus,,,.
