### **1. Definition**

**Antepartum Hemorrhage (APH):** Bleeding from or into the genital tract after the 20th week of pregnancy but before the birth of the baby (first and second stages of labor included). The 28th week is arbitrarily taken as the lower limit of fetal viability. **Placenta Previa:** A condition where the placenta is implanted partially or completely over the lower uterine segment, over and adjacent to the internal cervical os.

### **2. Classification**

The revised classification by ACOG and ACR (2014) defines two main types:

- **True Placenta Previa:** The placenta completely covers the internal os.
- **Low-Lying Placenta:** The placenta lies within 2 cm of the internal os but does not cover it.
- _Dangerous Placenta Previa:_ This is the name given to a Type II posterior placenta previa. Because of the curved birth canal, the major thickness of the placenta overlies the sacral promontory, diminishing the anteroposterior diameter of the inlet, preventing head engagement, and hindering effective compression of the separated placenta to stop bleeding.

### **3. Aetiology and Risk Factors**

The exact cause of implantation of the placenta in the lower segment is not definitively known, but several theories and risk factors are postulated.

- **Dropping down theory:** The fertilized ovum drops down and implants in the lower segment due to poor decidual reaction in the upper uterine segment.
- **Defective decidua:** The chorionic villi spread over a wide area in the uterine wall for nourishment, encroaching onto the lower segment.
- **Hyperplacentosis:** A big surface area of the placenta, such as in twin pregnancies, may encroach onto the lower segment.

**High-Risk Factors:**

- Multiparity and maternal age >35 years (4-fold increase).
- Prior cesarean delivery (CD) or uterine surgery (myomectomy, hysterotomy). The risk of placenta previa increases from 0.9% with one prior CD to 3% with three or more prior CDs.
- Prior curettage or prior placenta previa.
- Multiple pregnancy and pregnancy following Assisted Reproductive Techniques (ART).
- Asian race and maternal smoking (causes placental hypertrophy to compensate for CO-induced hypoxemia).

### **4. Pathogenesis (Mechanism of Bleeding)**

As placental growth slows down in the later months and the lower uterine segment progressively enlarges, the inelastic placenta is sheared off the wall of the lower segment. This shearing opens up the uteroplacental vessels, leading to an episode of bleeding.

- **Inevitable Bleeding:** Because it is a physiological phenomenon of lower segment stretching that leads to placental separation, the bleeding is considered inevitable.
- **Provoked Bleeding:** Separation can also be provoked by trauma, including vaginal examination or coitus.
- The blood lost is almost always maternal, though fetal blood may escape from torn villi if the placenta is separated by trauma.
- **Spontaneous Control of Bleeding:** Bleeding is controlled by thrombosis of the open sinuses, mechanical pressure applied by the presenting part, and placental infarction.

### **5. Clinical Features**

**Symptoms:**

- **Vaginal Bleeding:** This is the _only_ symptom of placenta previa. The classical features of this bleeding are that it is **sudden in onset, painless, apparently causeless, and recurrent**.
- In about one-third of cases, there is a history of a slight "warning hemorrhage".
- Bleeding is unrelated to activity, often occurring during sleep, leaving the patient to wake up in a pool of blood.

**Signs:**

- **General Condition:** The patient's general condition and degree of pallor are proportionate to the amount of visible blood loss.
- **Abdominal Examination:**
    - The size of the uterus is proportionate to the period of gestation.
    - The uterus feels relaxed, soft, and elastic without any localized tenderness.
    - Malpresentations (breech, transverse, unstable lie) are frequent.
    - The head is high and floating, out of proportion to the gestational age, and cannot be pushed into the pelvis.
    - _Stallworthy's sign:_ Slowing of the fetal heart rate upon pressing the head down into the pelvis, which recovers promptly when pressure is released, is suggestive of a posterior low-lying placenta.
- **Vulval Inspection:** Bleeding is typically bright red.
- **Vaginal Examination:** **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** as it may provoke severe hemorrhage.

### **6. Diagnosis and Ultrasonography**

Painless and recurrent vaginal bleeding in the second half of pregnancy should be taken as placenta previa unless proven otherwise. Ultrasonography is the diagnostic technique of choice.

- **Transvaginal Sonography (TVS):** This is the safest and most accurate method (virtually 100%). The transducer is inserted without touching the cervix, providing superior resolution to classify if the placenta is within 2 cm or >2 cm from the undilated internal cervical os.
- **Transabdominal Sonography (TAS):** Accuracy is nearly 100% after 30 weeks, but false-positive results can occur due to a full bladder or myometrial contractions.
- **Color Doppler / 3D Power Doppler:** Shows diffuse vascular lakes with turbulent flow in hypoechoic areas near the cervix.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** Superior to USG for diagnosing posterior placenta previa and identifying cases of morbidly adherent placenta (Placenta Accreta Spectrum) with deep myometrial or bladder invasion.

### **7. Differential Diagnosis**

Placenta previa must be fundamentally differentiated from **Abruptio Placentae**:

- _Bleeding:_ Painless, recurrent, and bright red in Placenta Previa vs. painful, continuous, and dark colored in Abruptio Placentae.
- _General condition:_ Proportionate to visible blood loss in Previa vs. out of proportion (shock) in concealed Abruptio Placentae.
- _Uterine Feel:_ Soft and relaxed in Previa vs. tense, tender, and woody hard in Abruptio Placentae.
- _Fetal Heart Sound (FHS):_ Usually present in Previa vs. often absent (fetal death) in severe Abruptio Placentae.
- Other differentials include local cervicovaginal lesions (cervical polyp, carcinoma), vasa previa, and circumvallate placenta.

### **8. Management**

**A. Immediate Attention on Admission**

- All cases of APH must be admitted to an equipped hospital.
- Resuscitation with IV fluids (Ringer's solution) and arrangement of cross-matched blood.
- Assess blood loss, check vital signs (pulse, BP), and perform gentle abdominal examination (check uterine tone and FHS).
- Send blood for CBC, ABO and Rh grouping, and coagulation profile.

**B. Expectant Management (Macafee Regime)**

- **Indications:** Pregnancy is preterm (<37 weeks), there is no active bleeding, the patient is hemodynamically stable, and fetal status is reassuring (normal CTG and USG).
- **Conduct:**
    - Strict bed rest with washroom privileges.
    - Supplementary hematinics and blood transfusions if the patient is anemic.
    - Serial USG every 2-3 weeks to monitor fetal growth and placental migration.
    - Use of tocolytics (e.g., magnesium sulfate) if bleeding is associated with uterine contractions.
    - Corticosteroid therapy (betamethasone/dexamethasone) is given to accelerate fetal lung maturity if gestational age is <34 weeks.

**C. Active Management and Mode of Delivery**

- **Indications:** Active continuous bleeding, pregnancy >37 weeks, patient in labor, exsanguinated state, non-reassuring fetal heart rate, or gross fetal malformations.
- **Cesarean Delivery (CD):** The majority of women with true placenta previa (covering the os) or a low-lying placenta (edge within 2 cm of the internal os) are delivered by cesarean section. CD is done between 36 and 37 weeks for asymptomatic cases, or emergently if there is active hemorrhage.
- **Vaginal Delivery:** May only be considered if the placental edge is clearly >2 cm away from the internal os and there are no other contraindications.

**D. Management of Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS)** If placenta previa is complicated by PAS (morbidly adherent placenta due to absent decidua basalis), especially in women with prior cesarean sections:

- A multidisciplinary team approach (obstetrician, anesthetist, urologist, transfusion specialist) is vital.
- Delivery is planned at 34-36 weeks at a tertiary center.
- **No attempt should be made to separate the placenta manually**, as it will cause catastrophic hemorrhage.
- Management often requires a planned peripartum hysterectomy. Conservative approaches may include uterine artery embolization or leaving the placenta in situ under antibiotic cover if fertility preservation is strongly desired.
