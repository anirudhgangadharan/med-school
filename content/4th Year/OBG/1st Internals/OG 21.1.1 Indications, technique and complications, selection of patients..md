### **PART A: PERMANENT CONTRACEPTION (STERILIZATION)**

Female sterilization (tubectomy/tubal occlusion) is the most popular terminal method of contraception worldwide. Male sterilization (vasectomy) is an equally effective, less morbid alternative.

#### **1. Female Sterilization (Tubectomy / Laparoscopic Sterilization)**

**Indications**

- **Family Planning Purposes:** This is the principal indication in most developing countries.
- **Socioeconomic:** Adopted when an individual/couple has achieved their desired number of children.
- **Medicosurgical Indications:**
    - Medical diseases such as severe heart disease, diabetes, chronic renal disease, and hypertension, which are likely to worsen if repeated pregnancies occur.
    - During a third repeat cesarean section or following surgical repair of genital prolapse to avoid future complications.
- **Risk-reducing Salpingectomy:** Serves as a preventive measure against serous ovarian and peritoneal cancers.

**Selection of Patients**

- Women who have completed their family and voluntarily consent to the permanent nature of the procedure.
- **For the Laparoscopic route:** Patients must be hemodynamically stable. Absolute contraindications include severe cardiopulmonary disease, advanced malignancy, intestinal obstruction, and generalized peritonitis. Relative contraindications include large pelvic tumors, morbid obesity, and extensive peritoneal adhesions.
- **For the Vaginal route:** Interval cases with a normal-sized uterus (<12 weeks) are most suited.

**Technique**

- **Laparoscopic Sterilization (Single Puncture Technique):**
    - _Premedication:_ Pethidine hydrochloride (75-100 mg), phenergan (25 mg), and atropine sulfate (0.65 mg) are given intramuscularly 30 minutes prior.
    - _Position & Anesthesia:_ The patient is placed in a low lithotomy position with buttocks protruding slightly. Local anesthesia (10 mL of 1% lignocaine) is infiltrated at the subumbilical puncture site, though general anesthesia is generally preferred.
    - _Procedure:_ A pneumoperitoneum is created. The operating table is placed in the Trendelenburg position. The tubes are visualized and occluded using electrocoagulation or by applying clips/rings.
- **Vaginal Sterilization:**
    - Accessed through a posterior colpotomy. It leaves no abdominal scar and requires a short hospital stay, making it convenient for obese women.

**Complications**

- **Immediate:** Hemorrhage (revealed or broad ligament hematoma), wound infection, peritonitis (rare), and injury to adjacent viscera (rectum, bowel, or blood vessels).
- **Anesthetic/Laparoscopic Hazards:** Gas embolism, hypercarbia, metabolic acidosis, cardiac arrhythmias, and hypoventilation (due to pneumoperitoneum and Trendelenburg position).
- **Late/Remote:** Dyspareunia, incisional hernia, and a small failure rate (recanalization) which may lead to normal or ectopic pregnancies.

#### **2. Male Sterilization (Vasectomy)**

**Indications & Selection of Candidates**

- **Indications:** A permanent method for terminal contraception/family planning.
- **Selection:** Ideal candidates are sexually active and psychologically adjusted husbands who have achieved their desired number of children.

**Technique**

- The procedure is highly cost-effective, involves minimal equipment, and requires a very short hospital stay. It is generally performed under local anesthesia in an outpatient setting (e.g., No-Scalpel Vasectomy).

**Complications and Drawbacks**

- Immediate and late complications are significantly fewer compared to female sterilization.
- The failure rate is minimal (0.15%), and the chance of a successful reversal anastomosis operation is fair (70-80%).
- Frigidity or impotency, when it occurs, is almost entirely psychological.
- **Major Drawback:** Sterility is not immediate. Additional contraceptive protection is required for about 2-3 months following the operation until the semen becomes completely free of sperm.

### **PART B: ASSISTED REPRODUCTIVE TECHNOLOGY (ART)**

#### **3. In Vitro Fertilization (IVF)**

**Indications** IVF is indicated for a wide variety of fertility challenges. The primary indications include:

- Fallopian tube blockage or absence.
- Unexplained infertility.
- Endometriosis.
- Oligospermia or azoospermia (requiring Testicular Sperm Extraction - TESE).
- Advanced reproductive age and fertility preservation.
- Multiple infertility factors (combined female and male factors).
- Failed ovulation induction.
- Ovarian failure (necessitating donor oocyte IVF).
- Women with normal ovaries but no functional uterus (e.g., Müllerian agenesis).
- Couples with a high genetic risk (utilizing IVF with Preimplantation Genetic Diagnosis - PGD).

**Selection of Patients (Ideal Candidate Criteria)** Proper patient selection improves the success rate of the cycle. Ideal parameters include:

- Age < 35 years.
- Presence of adequate ovarian reserve (Day 3 serum FSH < 10 IU/L).
- Husband with a normal seminogram (unless advanced male-factor techniques like ICSI/TESE are planned).
- The couple must be screened and test negative for HIV and hepatitis.
- A normal uterine cavity, evaluated beforehand by hysteroscopy or sonohysterography.

**Technique (Principal Steps of an ART Cycle)** The standard IVF cycle proceeds through the following sequential steps:

1. **Downregulation:** Using a GnRH agonist to prevent a premature LH surge.
2. **Controlled Ovarian Stimulation (COS):** Using gonadotropins to stimulate the development of multiple follicles.
3. **Monitoring:** Tracking follicular growth via transvaginal ultrasonography and serum estradiol levels.
4. **Oocyte Retrieval:** Aspiration of the eggs from the mature follicles.
5. **Fertilization _in vitro_:** Exposing the retrieved oocytes to sperm in the laboratory (Standard IVF or ICSI).
6. **Transfer:** Placing the resulting gametes or embryos directly into the uterine cavity.
7. **Luteal Support:** Administering progesterone to support the endometrial lining for implantation.

**Complications and Reasons for Poor Outcome**

- While IVF is largely safe, complications can arise from the stimulation or retrieval processes (e.g., Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome, multiple gestations).
- Poor IVF outcomes or cycle failures are more commonly observed in older women (due to poor oocyte quality), women with premature ovarian failure, or those who fail to respond to superovulation regimens (poor ovarian reserve). Such cases may require donor oocytes.
