### **1. Definition of Abortion (Miscarriage)**

Abortion is defined as the expulsion or extraction of an embryo or fetus weighing 500 g or less from its mother, independent of whether the cord has been cut or the placenta is attached. This weight corresponds approximately to 22 weeks (154 days) of gestation. The term "miscarriage" is the recommended medical terminology for spontaneous abortion.

### **2. Classification of Abortion**

Abortions are broadly classified into two main categories: spontaneous and induced.

- **Spontaneous Miscarriage:** This can be either isolated (sporadic) or recurrent. The clinical types of spontaneous miscarriage include threatened, inevitable, complete, incomplete, missed, and septic.
- **Induced Abortion:** This is classified into legal (Medical Termination of Pregnancy - MTP) and illegal (unsafe) abortions.

### **3. Aetiology of Miscarriage**

**First Trimester Causes:**

- **Genetic factors (50%):** Chromosomal abnormalities are the most common cause, with 95% resulting from errors in maternal gametogenesis. These include autosomal trisomies, monosomy X (45,X), triploidy, and structural chromosomal rearrangements (translocation, deletion, inversion).
- **Endocrine and metabolic factors (10-15%):** Luteal Phase Defect (LPD) leads to deficient progesterone secretion, preventing adequate support for implantation and placentation. Overt hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, and poorly controlled diabetes mellitus also increase the risk of fetal loss.
- **Anatomical abnormalities (3-38%):** Cervicouterine factors include cervical incompetence, congenital malformations (bicornuate or septate uterus), submucous uterine fibroids, and intrauterine adhesions (Asherman's syndrome).
- **Infections (5%):** Transplacental fetal infections can be caused by viruses (Rubella, Cytomegalovirus, HIV), parasites (Toxoplasma, Malaria), or bacteria (Ureaplasma, Chlamydia).
- **Immunological disorders (5-10%):** Antiphospholipid Antibody Syndrome (APAS) causes fetal hypoxia through thrombosis of the uteroplacental vascular bed, while an abnormal T-helper 1 (Th1) pro-inflammatory cytokine response can also lead to rejection of the conceptus.
- **Maternal medical illness:** Conditions such as cyanotic heart disease and hemoglobinopathies are associated with early miscarriage.
- **Thrombophilias:** Inherited thrombophilias cause intravascular and placental intervillous thrombosis.
- **Unexplained:** In a majority of cases, the exact cause remains unknown, though the risk generally increases with maternal age.

**Second Trimester Causes:**

- **Anatomic abnormalities:** This includes congenital or acquired cervical incompetence, Mullerian fusion defects, uterine synechiae, and uterine fibroids.
- **Maternal medical illness**.
- **Unexplained**.

### **4. Threatened Miscarriage**

**Definition:** It is a clinical entity where the process of miscarriage has started but has not progressed to a state from which recovery is impossible.

**Management:**

- The patient should be on bed rest (pelvic rest) for a few days until the bleeding stops, though prolonged restriction of activity has no therapeutic value.
- Treatment with progesterone improves the outcome by inducing immunomodulation to shift the Th1 (pro-inflammatory response) to a Th2 (anti-inflammatory response).
- Anti-D gamma globulin must be administered to Rh-negative nonimmunized women.
- The patient is advised on discharge to follow up with a repeat sonography at 2-3 weeks.

### **5. Inevitable Miscarriage**

**Definition:** It is the clinical type where the presence of bleeding, cervical dilation, and abdominal pain indicates impending abortion, meaning the expulsion of the conceptus is certain.

**Management:**

- The management is aimed at accelerating the process of expulsion and maintaining strict asepsis.
- Blood loss is corrected by intravenous fluid therapy and blood transfusion.
- Excessive bleeding should be promptly controlled by administering methergine 0.2 mg or oxytocin 10 IU intramuscularly if the uterus size is less than 12 weeks.
- **Before 12 weeks:** Dilatation and evacuation (D&E) or suction evacuation followed by curettage of the uterine cavity is performed under analgesia or general anesthesia.
- **After 12 weeks:** Uterine contractions are accelerated by an oxytocin drip. If the placenta remains unseparated after fetal expulsion, digital separation followed by evacuation under general anesthesia is required.

### **6. Incomplete Miscarriage**

**Definition:** When the entire products of conception are not expelled, and instead a part of it is left inside the uterine cavity, it is called an incomplete miscarriage. It is the most common type encountered among hospitalized patients.

**Management:**

- The patient should be resuscitated before any active surgical treatment is undertaken.
- In recent cases, Evacuation of the Retained Products of Conception (ERPC) is the standard treatment.
- **Early abortion:** Dilatation and evacuation (D&E) or Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA) is performed under analgesia or general anesthesia.
- **Late abortion:** The uterus is evacuated under general anesthesia, and the retained products are removed by ovum forceps or a blunt curette.
- **Medical management:** Misoprostol 200 mg vaginally every 4 hours can be used, which presents fewer complications compared to surgical methods.

### **7. Missed Miscarriage**

**Definition:** When the fetus is dead and retained inside the uterus for a variable period, it is called a missed miscarriage or early fetal demise.

**Management:**

- **Expectant management:** Many women expel the conceptus spontaneously without intervention.
- **Medical management:** Prostaglandin E1 (misoprostol) 800 µg is given vaginally in the posterior fornix and repeated after 24 hours if needed, with expulsion usually occurring within 48 hours.
- **Surgical management:** Suction evacuation or dilatation and evacuation is performed as a definitive treatment or if medical methods fail.
- **Uterus more than 12 weeks:** Induction of abortion is achieved using a combined regimen of 200 mg oral mifepristone followed 48 hours later by 800 mg misoprostol via buccal, vaginal, or sublingual routes.

### **8. Septic Abortion**

**Definition:** Any abortion associated with clinical evidences of infection of the uterus and its contents is called a septic abortion. It is diagnosed when there is a temperature rise of at least 100.4°F (38°C) for 24 hours or more, offensive or purulent vaginal discharge, and lower abdominal pain and tenderness.

**Management:**

- **General Management:** Hospitalization in isolation is essential for all cases of septic abortion. A high vaginal or cervical swab must be taken for culture, drug sensitivity testing, and Gram stain.
- **Principles of Management:** The goal is to control sepsis, remove the source of infection, provide supportive therapy to restore normal homeostatic metabolism, and assess the treatment response.
- **Antimicrobial Therapy:** Ideal regimens should cover Gram-positive, Gram-negative, aerobic, and anaerobic organisms. A combination of piperacillin-tazobactam or carbapenem plus intravenous clindamycin provides the broadest range of microbial coverage.
- **Supportive Therapy:** Blood transfusion is frequently needed to improve anemia and overall body resistance.
- **Surgery (Evacuation):** To remove the nidus of infection, evacuation of the uterus should be performed at a convenient time within 24 hours following antibiotic therapy.
- **Advanced Surgery:** If a pelvic abscess forms, a posterior colpotomy is performed to drain the pus. In severe cases (Grade III) with generalized peritonitis, endotoxic shock, or unresponsive uterine infection, laparotomy and hysterectomy must be performed regardless of parity.
