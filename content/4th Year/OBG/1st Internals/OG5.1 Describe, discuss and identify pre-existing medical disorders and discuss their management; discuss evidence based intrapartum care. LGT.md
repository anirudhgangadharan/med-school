### **1. PRE-EXISTING MEDICAL DISORDERS IN PREGNANCY**

**Hematological Disorders (Anemia & Platelet Disorders)**

- **Anemia:** Anemia (Hb <8 g/dL) is a major indirect cause of maternal and perinatal mortality. Causes include iron deficiency, megaloblastic, dimorphic, aplastic, and hemoglobinopathies (sickle cell, thalassemia).
- **Management:** Routine iron-folic acid supplementation. Blood transfusion is used for severe anemia or acute hemorrhage.
- **Intrapartum Care:** Conduct vaginal delivery preferentially. Provide continuous oxygen therapy (5-6 L/min) to maintain PaO2 >94%, avoid fluid overload to prevent pulmonary edema, and utilize epidural analgesia. Apply prophylactic forceps or ventouse to shorten the second stage of labor and prevent maternal exhaustion. Strict Active Management of the Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL) with oxytocin is mandatory to prevent postpartum hemorrhage (PPH).
- **Platelet Disorders:** Gestational thrombocytopenia may present and requires monitoring of platelet counts.

**Heart Disease in Pregnancy**

- Pregnancy induces a hyperdynamic circulation, which can precipitate congestive cardiac failure, pulmonary edema, and arrhythmias in women with pre-existing heart disease. Risk stratification is done using the WHO classification (Class I to IV).
- **Management:** Multidisciplinary team care (obstetrician, cardiologist, neonatologist). Ensure adequate rest, and treat exacerbating factors like infections and anemia.
- **Intrapartum Care:** Place the patient in a lateral recumbent position to minimize aortocaval compression. Administer oxygen and prophylactic antibiotics against bacterial endocarditis. Restrict IV fluids to less than 75 mL/hour to prevent fluid overload. Epidural analgesia is the preferred method for pain relief. The second stage is cut short by prophylactic forceps or ventouse. In the third stage, AMTSL is performed using oxytocin, but ergometrine is strictly contraindicated. Postpartum diuretics may be required.

**Pregestational Diabetes Mellitus (Overt Diabetes)**

- Associated with high maternal and fetal risks, including fetal macrosomia (>4 kg), congenital malformations (especially cardiovascular), polyhydramnios, pre-eclampsia, preterm labor, and unexplained intrauterine fetal death (IUFD).
- **Management:** Preconceptional counseling is vital to achieve tight glycemic control (HbA1c ≤ 6.5%) and initiate high-dose folic acid (5 mg/day) to reduce the risk of major fetal malformations. Antenatal care involves diet, insulin therapy, and frequent self-monitoring of blood glucose. Perform targeted ultrasonography (USG) and fetal echocardiography at 20-22 weeks. Fetal surveillance (NST, BPP) begins at 28 weeks. Low-dose aspirin (150 mg/day) is started at 12 weeks to prevent pre-eclampsia. Cesarean delivery is indicated for macrosomia or obstetric complications.

**Chronic Hypertension**

- Defined as hypertension antedating pregnancy or recognized before 20 weeks of gestation. It carries a 20% risk of superimposed pre-eclampsia, and increases the risks of placental abruption, Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), and preterm birth.
- **Management:** Adjust antihypertensive medications (avoiding ACE inhibitors/ARBs which are teratogenic). Conduct regular BP monitoring and serial USG for fetal growth and umbilical artery Doppler velocimetry. Aim to deliver at 37 weeks, or earlier (34 weeks) if severe complications arise.

**Thyroid Dysfunctions**

- **Hyperthyroidism:** Managed medically. Propylthiouracil (PTU) is preferred in the first trimester, and Methimazole is used thereafter. Monitor the fetus for signs of goiter or neonatal thyrotoxicosis.
- **Hypothyroidism:** Often caused by Hashimoto's thyroiditis. If untreated, it is associated with pre-eclampsia, placental abruption, LBW, miscarriage, and deficient intellectual development of the child. Managed by maintaining a euthyroid state with levothyroxine.

**Epilepsy**

- Antiepileptic polytherapy significantly increases the risk of congenital anomalies.
- **Management:** Preconceptional counseling aims to shift the patient to monotherapy (e.g., Levetiracetam, Lamotrigine) at the lowest effective dose. High-dose folic acid supplementation is required.

### **2. EVIDENCE-BASED INTRAPARTUM CARE (WHO GUIDELINES)**

The WHO provides evidence-based recommendations to ensure a positive childbirth experience, emphasizing minimal routine interference in normal labor.

**Respectful Maternity Care & Companionship**

- Care must maintain the woman's dignity, privacy, and confidentiality, ensuring freedom from harm and verbal or physical abuse.
- A companion of choice (family member or partner) is recommended throughout labor to provide emotional support. This decreases the duration of labor and reduces the need for analgesia or operative delivery.
- Effective, culturally acceptable communication between care providers and the woman is essential.

**First Stage of Labor Care**

- **Definitions:** The _latent phase_ is characterized by painful contractions and slower cervical dilatation up to 5 cm. The _active phase_ begins at 5 cm with regular contractions and rapid cervical dilatation.
- **Admission Procedures:** Routine clinical pelvimetry, perineal/pubic shaving, and administration of enemas are NOT recommended for healthy pregnant women.
- **Fetal Monitoring:** Intermittent auscultation using a Doppler ultrasound device or Pinard stethoscope is recommended. Continuous electronic fetal monitoring (CTG) is NOT recommended for healthy women undergoing spontaneous labor.
- **Maternal Care:** Encourage mobility and an upright posture, which significantly reduces the duration of the first stage and the risk of cesarean delivery. Oral fluid and food intake are recommended for women at low risk.
- **Pain Relief:** Epidural analgesia, parenteral opioids (fentanyl, pethidine), and relaxation/manual techniques (massage, warm packs) are recommended based on maternal preference.
- **Interventions:** Early amniotomy combined with early oxytocin augmentation, as well as the use of antispasmodic agents to prevent delay in labor, are NOT recommended.

**Second Stage of Labor Care**

- Defined as the period between full cervical dilatation and the birth of the baby.
- Women should be encouraged to follow their own urge to bear down (push).
- Application of manual fundal pressure to facilitate childbirth is explicitly NOT recommended.

**Third Stage of Labor Care (AMTSL)**

- **Prophylactic Uterotonics:** Administration of uterotonics is recommended for all births to prevent PPH. Oxytocin (10 IU, IM/IV) is the drug of choice.
- **Delayed Cord Clamping:** Clamping the umbilical cord should be delayed (not earlier than 1 minute after birth) to improve infant health and nutrition outcomes.
- **Controlled Cord Traction (CCT):** Recommended only in settings where skilled birth attendants are available.
- **Uterine Massage:** Sustained uterine massage is NOT recommended as a PPH prevention intervention in women who have already received prophylactic oxytocin.

**WHO Labor Care Guide (Partograph)**

- The traditional partograph has been updated to the WHO Labor Care Guide.
- The active phase of labor now starts at 5 cm of cervical dilatation (previously 4 cm).
- The fixed 1 cm/hour 'alert line' and corresponding 'action line' have been removed. They are replaced with evidence-based time limits at each centimeter of cervical dilatation to account for natural variability in labor progress.
- It mandates recording of maternal vitals, fetal heart rate, labor progress (cervical dilatation and head descent), medications, and shared decision-making plans.

### **3. LOWER GENITAL TRACT (LGT) DISORDERS & DEFENSE IN PREGNANCY**

**LGT Defense Mechanisms** The lower genital tract encompasses the vulva, vagina, and cervix, which have natural barriers against ascending infections.

- **Vulvar Defense:** Maintained by the anatomical apposition of the labia and the fungicidal action of apocrine gland secretions (undecylenic acid).
- **Vaginal Defense:** Under the high estrogen influence of pregnancy, the vaginal epithelium thickens and accumulates glycogen. The commensal _Lactobacillus acidophilus_ (Doderlein's bacilli) converts this glycogen into lactic acid, maintaining a highly acidic vaginal pH (3.5–5.5). This acidic environment actively suppresses the multiplication of pathogenic organisms.
- **Cervical Defense:** A thick, tenacious mucus plug seals the cervical canal. This mucus is rich in immunoglobulins (IgG and IgA) and possesses bacteriolytic properties, acting as a physical and chemical barrier against ascending infections.

**Pre-existing LGT Infections & Management** Infections of the LGT must be identified and managed as they can cause maternal morbidity and severe obstetric complications.

- **Bacterial Vaginosis (BV):**
    - _Pathology:_ Characterized by a decrease in lactobacilli and an overgrowth of organisms like _Gardnerella vaginalis_ and anaerobes. It produces a homogeneous, grayish-white, malodorous (fishy) discharge with a pH >4.5, and is diagnosed by the presence of 'clue cells' on wet mount.
    - _Complications:_ Strongly associated with preterm labor, preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM), and chorioamnionitis.
    - _Management:_ Oral Metronidazole is highly effective.
- **Trichomonas Vaginalis:**
    - _Pathology:_ A flagellated protozoan causing sudden, profuse, offensive, greenish-yellow, frothy vaginal discharge with intense vulvar pruritus.
    - _Management:_ Diagnosis by hanging drop preparation. Treated with Metronidazole (treating the partner is also required).
- **Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (Moniliasis):**
    - _Pathology:_ Fungal infection by _Candida albicans_, highly prevalent during pregnancy due to increased glycogen and in women with diabetes. Presents with intense pruritus, soreness, and a thick, curdy white discharge.
    - _Management:_ Intravaginal fungicidal preparations of the azole group (e.g., clotrimazole, miconazole) are safe and effective.
- **Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs):**
    - Pathogens like _Chlamydia trachomatis_ and _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_ can ascend to cause upper tract infections or transmit to the neonate. They should be screened and treated empirically (e.g., erythromycin/azithromycin for Chlamydia, ceftriaxone for Gonorrhea).
    - _Herpes Genitalis:_ Caused by HSV type 1 or 2. Active genital herpes infection during the third trimester is an absolute indication for elective cesarean delivery to prevent fatal neonatal transmission. Suppressive therapy with Acyclovir may be used.
    - _Syphilis:_ Screened via VDRL/RPR; managed actively with Benzathine Penicillin to prevent congenital syphilis.
