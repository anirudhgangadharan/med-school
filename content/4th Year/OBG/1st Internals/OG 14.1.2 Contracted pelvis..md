### **1. Introduction and Definition**

- **Anatomical Definition:** A pelvis in which the essential diameters of one or more planes are shortened by 0.5 cm.
- **Obstetric Definition:** An alteration in the size and/or shape of the pelvis of sufficient degree as to alter the normal mechanism of labor in an average-size baby.

### **2. Types of Parent Pelves**

The female pelvis is divided into four parent types based on the shape of the inlet, though intermediate forms (e.g., gyne-android) are more common:

- **Gynecoid (50%):** Round inlet, favorable obstetric outcome.
- **Anthropoid (25%):** Long oval inlet.
- **Android (20%):** Triangular inlet, typical male type pelvis.
- **Platypelloid (5%):** Flat inlet.

### **3. Etiology and Types of Contracted Pelvis**

Minor variations in size and shape are common, whereas gross deformities are now rare.

- **Nutritional and Environmental Defects:**
    - _Rachitic flat pelvis:_ Causes a "reniform" shape of the inlet by pushing the sacral promontory downwards and forwards.
    - _Osteomalacic pelvis:_ Softening of bones due to calcium/Vitamin D deficiency leads to inward pushing of lateral walls, creating a triradiate shape.
- **Disease or Trauma:** Tuberculosis of pelvic joints/spines, or pelvic fractures.
- **Asymmetrical / Obliquely Contracted Pelvis:**
    - _Naegele's pelvis:_ Arrested development of one ala of the sacrum.
    - _Scoliotic pelvis:_ Scoliosis in the lumbar region pushes the acetabulum inwards on the weight-bearing side.
- **Symmetrical Deformities:**
    - _Robert's pelvis:_ Transversely contracted; both alae are absent.
    - _Kyphotic pelvis:_ Extreme funneling of the pelvis due to backward tilt of the upper sacrum and forward tilt of the lower sacrum.
- **Generally Contracted Pelvis:** Shape remains unaltered, but all diameters in the inlet, cavity, and outlet are proportionately shortened.

### **4. Diagnosis of Contracted Pelvis**

**A. History**

- _Medical History:_ Past history of fractures, rickets, or bone tuberculosis.
- _Obstetric History:_ A previous uncomplicated vaginal delivery of an average-size baby largely rules out pelvic contraction. History of prolonged labor followed by difficult instrumental or cesarean delivery strongly suggests contracted pelvis.

**B. Physical Examination**

- _Stature:_ A short woman (< 5 ft) is likely to have a small pelvis.
- _Dystocia Dystrophia Syndrome:_ Seen in stockily built, obese women with a bull neck, broad shoulders, and male hair distribution. They often have an android pelvis, subfertility, and are prone to prolonged labor and occipitoposterior positions.
- _Abdominal Inspection:_ Pendulous abdomen in a primigravida is highly suspicious of inlet contraction. Non-engagement of the head in a primigravida at the onset of labor must raise suspicion of disproportion.

**C. Clinical Pelvimetry** Done in early labor or after 37 weeks to note pelvic adequacy. Key features evaluated include:

- _Inlet:_ Diagonal conjugate, palpability of the sacral promontory.
- _Cavity:_ Curve of the sacrum, presence of convergent sidewalls, prominent ischial spines, and narrowed sacrosciatic notches.
- _Outlet:_ Subpubic angle, pubic arch, and Transverse Diameter of the Outlet (TDO), which normally accommodates four knuckles.

**D. Imaging**

- _Intrapartum Ultrasonography (IUSG):_ Highly useful for assessing pelvic dimensions, fetal presentation, position, and station without radiation risks.
- _MRI:_ Superior for assessing bony pelvis and fetal head volume without ionizing radiation.

### **5. Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD)**

**Definition and Criteria** CPD is the disparity between the fetal head and the maternal pelvis causing dystocia.

- _Inlet Contraction:_ Suspected when the obstetric conjugate is <10 cm, transverse diameter is <12 cm, or diagonal conjugate is <11 cm.
- _Midpelvic Contraction:_ Suspected when the sum of interischial spinous and posterior sagittal diameters is 13.0 cm or less.
- _Outlet Contraction:_ Interischial tuberous diameter is 8 cm or less.

**Diagnosis of CPD at the Brim** The fetal head is the best pelvimeter.

- _Abdominal Method:_ Pushing the head downwards and backwards. If it cannot be pushed down and the parietal bone overhangs the symphysis pubis, severe disproportion is present.
- _Abdominovaginal Method (Muller-Munro Kerr):_ Superior to the abdominal method. The vaginal fingers are placed at the level of the ischial spines while the external hand pushes the head down. The thumb of the vaginal hand assesses the degree of overlapping of the parietal bone over the symphysis pubis.

### **6. Effects of Contracted Pelvis**

**On Pregnancy:**

- Pendulous abdomen due to high floating head.
- Increased frequency of malpresentations (breech, transverse) by 3-4 times, and unstable lie.

**On Labor:**

- Increased incidence of early and premature rupture of membranes.
- High risk of cord prolapse due to ill-fitting presenting part.
- Slow cervical dilatation and prolonged labor.
- Increased maternal trauma (cervical tear, uterine rupture), postpartum hemorrhage, and sepsis.
- _Fetal Hazards:_ Hypoxia, acidosis, intracranial hemorrhage (due to supermolding), and increased perinatal mortality.

### **7. Mechanism of Labor in Flat Pelvis**

If the head negotiates the contracted brim in a flat pelvis, it usually faces no difficulty in the cavity and outlet. The mechanism involves:

- _Engagement in Transverse Diameter:_ The sagittal suture lies in the transverse diameter.
- _Deflexion:_ The head remains deflexed, delaying engagement.
- _Lateralization of Occiput:_ The head shifts laterally so the biparietal diameter occupies the wider sacrocotyloid diameter.
- _Exaggerated Parietal Presentation (Asynclitism):_ The head engages such that the super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm) passes through the brim instead of the biparietal diameter (9.5 cm). Extreme molding is common.

### **8. Management of Contracted Pelvis**

**A. Management of Inlet Contraction** The management depends on the degree of contraction, fetal size, and complicating factors.

- **Elective Cesarean Section:** Indicated for severe disproportion (obstetric conjugate < 7.5 cm) or when associated with complicating factors (e.g., elderly primigravida, malpresentation, postmaturity, pre-eclampsia, prior cesarean).
- **Trial of Labor:** Aimed at avoiding unnecessary cesarean sections in cases of minor to moderate disproportion. It is the conduction of spontaneous labor in an institution under strict supervision.
    - _Prerequisites:_ Favorable vertex presentation (anterior parietal), adequate midpelvis and outlet, absence of obstetric/medical complications, and strict partographic monitoring.
    - _Unfavorable signs during trial:_ Arrest of cervical dilatation or fetal descent despite oxytocin, abnormal uterine action, and early rupture of membranes. If the trial fails, emergency cesarean section is performed.

**B. Management of Midpelvic and Outlet Disproportion** Isolated outlet contraction without midpelvic contraction is rare.

- Diagnosis is made during the active phase of labor or second stage.
- _Elective Cesarean Section:_ Recommended for simultaneous contraction of transverse and anteroposterior diameters of the midpelvis.
- _Vaginal Delivery:_ In minor contractions, molding and adaptation may allow descent. Delivery is accomplished using forceps or ventouse with a deep mediolateral episiotomy to prevent severe perineal injuries, especially if the pubic arch is narrow.
- If there is complete cessation of progress (arrest disorder) for more than 2 hours in the active phase, cesarean delivery is mandated.
