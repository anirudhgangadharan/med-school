### **1. Introduction and Incidence**

Urinary Tract Infection (UTI) is a common medical complication in pregnancy.

- **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ASB):** The overall incidence during pregnancy ranges between 5 and 10%.
- **Acute Pyelonephritis:** The overall incidence in pregnancy is between 1 and 3%.
- **High-Risk Factors:** It is more common in primigravidae, and a previous history of UTI increases the chance by 50%. Presence of ASB increases the risk of symptomatic infection by 25%.

### **2. Effect of Pregnancy on UTI (Pathogenesis & Predisposing Factors)**

Pregnancy induces significant physiological changes in the urinary tract that predispose women to stasis and subsequent infection:

- **Hormonal Effects:** High progesterone levels lead to low ureteral peristalsis and relaxation of smooth muscles.
- **Anatomical Changes:** There is physiological dilatation of the urinary collecting system, renal pelvis, and ureters, increasing the risk of UTI.
- **Mechanical Compression:** Dextrorotation of the enlarging gravid uterus causes compression of the right ureter against the pelvic brim. Consequently, pyelonephritis occurs much more frequently on the right side (70-80%) compared to the left (10-15%).
- **Vesicoureteral Reflux:** Stasis of urine in the bladder allows multiplication of bacteria, and laxity of the vesicoureteral sphincters (due to edema) facilitates the influx of infected urine into the ureters and renal pelves.

### **3. Causative Organisms**

The most common offending organism is _Escherichia coli_, responsible for 70-90% of cases. Other pathogens include:

- _Klebsiella pneumoniae_ (10%)
- _Proteus_, _Pseudomonas_, and _Enterobacter_
- _Staphylococcus aureus_ and Group B Streptococcus (GBS).

### **4. Clinical Types and Features**

Depending on the presentation and site of infection, UTIs in pregnancy are categorized into:

**A. Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ASB)**

- **Definition:** Presence of actively multiplying bacteria (count > $10^5$/mL of the same species) in a midstream clean-catch specimen of urine on two occasions, without any symptoms of urinary infection.

**B. Acute Cystitis (Lower UTI)**

- **Symptoms:** Dysuria, urgency, frequency, suprapubic pain, and dribbling.
- **Signs:** Hematuria may be present. Suprapubic tenderness is often noted.

**C. Acute Pyelonephritis (Upper UTI)**

- **Onset:** Acute onset, usually appearing beyond the 16th week of pregnancy.
- **Symptoms:** Acute aching pain over the loins, fever with chills and rigor, frequency of micturition, dysuria, anorexia, nausea, and vomiting.
- **Signs:** The patient looks ill and toxic with a dry tongue. The pulse rate is proportionate to the high temperature. There is varying degree of loin tenderness (renal angle tenderness).

### **5. Detection and Investigations**

- **Urine Analysis (Dipstick):** Screening for nitrites and leukocyte esterase; if either is positive, it indicates infection and necessitates a culture.
- **Urine Microscopy:** Midstream clean-catch or catheterized urine reveals plenty of pus cells (leukocytes) and red blood cells (RBCs).
- **Urine Culture & Sensitivity:** Gold standard. A colony count > $10^5$/mL in midstream urine or > $10^2$/mL in catheterized urine confirms the diagnosis and identifies antibiotic sensitivity.
- **Blood Investigations:** Complete blood count (CBC) shows neutrophilic leukocytosis (WBC >15,000/$mm^3$). Serum urea, creatinine, and electrolytes should be evaluated to assess renal function.
- **Blood Culture:** Indicated in severe infections or if fever is associated with chills and rigor to rule out bacteremia.
- **Ultrasonography:** Renal and pelvic USG may be evaluated if the patient is not responding to therapy, to rule out urinary tract obstruction or calculi.

### **6. Impact of UTI on Pregnancy (Complications)**

**Maternal Complications:**

- If left untreated, 25% of women with ASB will develop acute pyelonephritis, usually in the third trimester.
- Anemia and hypertension are frequently associated with recurrent UTIs.
- Severe acute pyelonephritis can lead to septicemia, endotoxic (septic) shock, and acute renal failure (AKI).
- Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) may develop due to endotoxin-induced alveolar capillary membrane damage.

**Fetal and Neonatal Complications:**

- Increased risk of miscarriage and preterm labor.
- Premature rupture of membranes (PROM) and chorioamnionitis.
- Low birth weight (prematurity and dysmaturity).
- Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD) caused by maternal hyperpyrexia or severe acidosis.

### **7. Management**

**A. Management of Asymptomatic Bacteriuria & Acute Cystitis**

- **Antimicrobial Therapy:** Appropriate oral antibiotics safe for the mother and fetus are prescribed for 10-14 days. Options include:
    - Nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID.
    - Amoxicillin 500 mg TID.
    - Cephalexin 500 mg TID. _(Note: Ampicillin resistance is increasingly common, so nitrofurantoin is uniformly effective against common uropathogens)_.
- **Hydration:** Plenty of fluid intake should be encouraged.

**B. Management of Acute Pyelonephritis**

- **Hospitalization:** Essential for severe cases.
- **Hydration and Monitoring:** Intravenous crystalloid fluids (Ringer's solution) to correct dehydration. Monitor urine output ($\geq$ 60 mL/hr), blood pressure, and temperature.
- **Symptomatic Relief:** Acetaminophen is given for hyperpyrexia.
- **Intravenous Antibiotics:** Empirical IV antibiotic therapy is started immediately for 48 hours until culture reports are available. Regimens include Ceftriaxone, Aminoglycosides (e.g., Gentamicin), Cefazolin, or Aztreonam.
- **Transition to Oral:** Once the patient is afebrile and responding, transition to oral antibiotics for another 10-14 days.

**C. Follow-up and Suppressive Therapy**

- **Test of Cure:** A repeat urine culture is performed 2 weeks after completing antimicrobial therapy to confirm cure. Routine urine cultures are then repeated in each trimester.
- **Suppressive Therapy:** To prevent recurrence (which occurs in 30-40% of cases), antimicrobial suppression therapy is continued until the end of pregnancy. Nitrofurantoin 100 mg or 50 mg daily at bedtime is highly effective.
