### **1. ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY OF THE PLACENTA**

**Gross Anatomy and Development**

- **Type:** The human placenta is discoid (disk-shaped), deciduate (covered externally by decidual cells), labyrinthine (complexly branched villous tree), and hemochorial (maternal blood directly contacts the chorionic trophoblasts).
- **Development:** Begins at the 6th week and is completed by the 12th week. It develops from two sources: the fetal component from the chorion frondosum and the maternal component from the decidua basalis.
- **Anatomy at Term:** Circular disk measuring 15–20 cm in diameter and about 3 cm thick at its center. It weighs about 500 g (a 1:6 ratio to the weight of the fetus) and occupies ~30% of the uterine wall.
- **Surfaces:**
    - _Fetal surface (Chorionic plate):_ Smooth and glistening, covered by the amnion. The umbilical cord attaches near the center, and radiating umbilical vessels are visible beneath the amnion.
    - _Maternal surface (Basal plate):_ Rough, spongy, and dull red. Mapped into 15–20 convex polygonal areas called lobes or cotyledons, which are separated by decidual fissures (septa).

**Structure of the Placenta**

- **Chorionic Plate:** Inner plate lined by amniotic membrane. From within outward, it consists of primitive mesenchymal tissue, cytotrophoblast, and syncytiotrophoblast. Stem villi arise from this plate.
- **Basal Plate:** Outer maternal boundary consisting of the compact and spongy layers of the decidua basalis, Nitabuch’s layer (fibrinoid degeneration), cytotrophoblastic shell, and syncytiotrophoblast.
- **Intervillous Space:** Lies between the two plates, bounded internally by the chorionic plate and externally by the basal plate. It is lined entirely by syncytiotrophoblast and is filled with 150 mL of slow-flowing maternal blood.
- **Stem Villi:** The functional unit of the placenta is the fetal cotyledon (placentome), derived from a major primary stem villus.

**Placental Circulation and Barrier**

- **Uteroplacental (Maternal) Circulation:** Driven by 120–200 spiral arteries that pierce the basal plate. The blood flow in the intervillous space at term is 500–600 mL/minute, completely replacing the blood 3–4 times per minute.
- **Fetoplacental Circulation:** Two umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated blood to the placenta, and one umbilical vein returns oxygenated blood to the fetus. Fetal blood flow is about 400 mL/minute.
- **Placental Barrier:** Separates maternal and fetal blood. It is ~0.025 mm thick and consists of: (1) syncytiotrophoblast, (2) cytotrophoblast, (3) basement membrane, (4) stromal tissue, and (5) fetal capillary endothelium. Near term, it thins to 0.002 mm (vasculosyncytial membrane) to facilitate exchange.

**Functions of the Placenta**

- **Respiratory:** Oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange occurs via simple diffusion, driven by a partial pressure gradient.
- **Excretory:** Waste products like urea, uric acid, and creatinine are excreted into maternal blood via simple diffusion.
- **Nutritive:** Glucose transfers via facilitated diffusion (using GLUT-1 transporter proteins). Amino acids, calcium, phosphorus, and iron transfer via active transport against concentration gradients.
- **Endocrine:** A temporary endocrine gland synthesizing protein hormones (hCG, hPL) and steroid hormones (estrogen, progesterone) essential for pregnancy maintenance.
- **Barrier & Immunological:** Prevents the transmission of substances >500 daltons. Modulates maternal immune response (via HLA-G interaction, Th-2 cytokine shift, and immunosuppressive proteins) to prevent graft rejection of the fetus.

### **2. ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY OF FETAL MEMBRANES**

**Chorion**

- **Anatomy:** The outermost fetal membrane. It is thicker than the amnion, friable, and shaggy on both sides. It ends at the margin of the placenta and represents the remnant of the chorion laeve.
- **Function:** Forms the outer boundary of the amniotic cavity, merges with the placental margins, and fuses with the decidua capsularis and parietalis.

**Amnion**

- **Anatomy:** The innermost fetal membrane, smooth and shiny, directly in contact with the liquor amnii. Fully formed, it is 0.02–0.5 mm thick.
- **Histology:** From within outward: (1) cuboidal epithelium, (2) basement membrane, (3) reticular compact layer, (4) fibroblastic layer, and (5) spongy layer. The amnion is devoid of blood vessels, nerves, and lymphatics.
- **Functions:**
    1. Contributes to amniotic fluid formation.
    2. Maintains a sterile environment, preventing ascending uterine infections.
    3. Facilitates cervical dilatation during labor by forming a hydrostatic wedge.
    4. Serves as a rich source of glycerophospholipids and arachidonic acid, precursors for prostaglandin E2 and F2α.

### **3. AMNIOTIC FLUID (LIQUOR AMNII)**

**Origin, Circulation, and Volume**

- **Origin:** Mixed maternal and fetal origins. It is derived from maternal serum transudation, amniotic epithelium secretion, fetal urine (400–1200 mL/day at term), and transudation from the fetal respiratory tract.
- **Circulation:** Highly dynamic; the water in the amniotic fluid is completely replaced every 3 hours.
- **Volume:** Measures ~50 mL at 12 weeks, 400 mL at 20 weeks, reaches a peak of 1 Liter at 36–38 weeks, and decreases to 600–800 mL at term.

**Physical Features and Composition**

- **Physical Features:** Faintly alkaline, hypotonic to maternal serum with a low specific gravity of 1.010, and an osmolarity of ~250 mOsmol/L (indicating fetal maturity).
- **Normal Color:** Colorless in early pregnancy, becoming pale straw-colored with white flakes of vernix caseosa near term.
- **Abnormal Colors:**
    - _Green (Meconium stained):_ Suggests fetal distress.
    - _Golden:_ Rh-incompatibility (excess bilirubin).
    - _Greenish-yellow (Saffron):_ Postmaturity.
    - _Dark brown (Tobacco juice):_ Intrauterine fetal death (IUD).
- **Composition:** Contains 98-99% water and 1-2% solids. Solids include proteins (0.3 mg%), glucose (20 mg%), urea (30 mg%), electrolytes (sodium, chloride, potassium), and suspended particles (lanugo, exfoliated fetal epithelial cells).

**Clinical Significance and Functions**

- **Pregnancy:** Acts as a shock absorber, regulates fetal temperature, and distends the sac allowing free fetal growth and movement.
- **Labor:** Forms a hydrostatic wedge for cervical dilatation, prevents umbilical cord compression, and flushes the birth canal with its bactericidal action upon rupture.
- **Clinical Importance:** Estimation of Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI) diagnoses polyhydramnios or oligohydramnios. Amniocentesis helps test fetal maturity, karyotyping, and anomalies.

### **4. ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY OF THE LOWER GENITAL TRACT (LGT)**

**Vulva (External Genitalia)**

- **Mons Veneris:** A pad of subcutaneous adipose tissue over the pubis covered by hair.
- **Labia Majora:** Two skin elevations bounding the vulva laterally, containing sebaceous and sweat glands, hair follicles, and adipose tissue (rich venous plexus). Homologous to the male scrotum.
- **Labia Minora:** Two thin, fat-free, hairless folds within the labia majora. They divide anteriorly to enclose the clitoris (prepuce and frenulum) and fuse posteriorly to form the fourchette. Homologous to the ventral aspect of the penis.
- **Clitoris:** A 2.5 cm cylindrical erectile body consisting of a glans, a body, and two crura. Highly sensitive and analogous to the male penis.
- **Vestibule:** A triangular space bounded by the clitoris anteriorly, the fourchette posteriorly, and labia minora laterally. Contains four openings:
    - _Urethral opening:_ 1–1.5 cm below the pubic arch.
    - _Vaginal orifice:_ Partially closed by the hymen (a mucous membrane septum).
    - _Bartholin’s ducts (2):_ Ducts of the Bartholin's glands (greater vestibular glands). The glands (0.5 cm) sit in the superficial perineal pouch and secrete alkaline lubricating mucus during sexual excitement. Homologous to male bulbourethral glands.

**Vagina (Internal Genitalia)**

- **Anatomy:** A highly distensible fibromusculomembranous sheath extending from the vulva to the uterus. Directed upward and backward at a 45° angle. The anterior wall is 7 cm long, and the posterior wall is 9 cm long.
- **Fornices:** Four clefts at the vaginal vault around the cervix (one anterior, one deep posterior, two lateral).
- **Histological Structure:** Four layers from within outward:
    1. _Mucous coat:_ Stratified squamous epithelium without any secreting glands.
    2. _Submucous layer:_ Loose areolar vascular tissue.
    3. _Muscular layer:_ Inner circular and outer longitudinal muscles.
    4. _Fibrous coat:_ Derived from the highly vascular endopelvic fascia.
- **Vaginal Secretion & Flora:** The secretion is composed of tissue fluid, transudate, cervical mucus, and epithelial debris. During the reproductive period, under estrogenic influence, epithelial cells accumulate glycogen. _Doderlein’s bacilli_ (Lactobacillus acidophilus) convert this glycogen into lactic acid, maintaining an acidic pH of 4.0–5.5.

**Defense Mechanisms of the LGT**

- **Vulvar Defense:** Anatomical apposition of the labia and fungicidal action of apocrine gland secretions (undecylenic acid) offer high natural resistance.
- **Vaginal Defense:** The acidic ecosystem (pH 4.0–5.5) maintained by Doderlein’s bacilli is a powerful physiological barrier that suppresses the multiplication of pathogenic bacteria, safeguarding both the lower and upper reproductive tracts from ascending infection. Anatomical apposition of the anterior and posterior vaginal walls also acts as a physical barrier.
