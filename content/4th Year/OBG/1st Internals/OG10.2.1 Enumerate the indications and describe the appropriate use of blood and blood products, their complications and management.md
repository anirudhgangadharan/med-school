### **1. Indications for Blood Transfusion**

**A. Obstetrical Indications**

- **Severe Anemia in Pregnancy:** Specially in later months (>36 weeks) to improve the anemic state and oxygen-carrying capacity before the patient goes into labor.
- **Refractory Anemia:** Cases resistant to oral or parenteral iron therapy.
- **Hemorrhagic Shock / Acute Blood Loss:** Due to ruptured ectopic pregnancy, placenta previa, abruptio placentae (especially concealed type where at least 1 L of blood is minimally required), or postpartum hemorrhage (PPH).
- **Operative Delivery:** To compensate for significant blood loss during cesarean section, manual removal of placenta, or destructive operations.
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), HELLP syndrome, or dilutional coagulopathy requiring specific blood components.
- **Associated Infections:** Severe puerperal sepsis or septic abortion.

**B. Gynecological Indications**

- **Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB) / Dysfunctional Uterine Bleeding (DUB):** In adolescent or reproductive age women presenting with severe, heavy bleeding causing acute anemia.
- **Major Gynecological Surgeries:** To replace blood loss during extended surgeries (e.g., hysterectomy, pelvic floor repair).
- **Pelvic Trauma / Hematomas:** Significant vulvovaginal or broad ligament hematomas requiring surgical exploration and resuscitation.

**C. Fetal and Neonatal Indications**

- **Intrauterine Fetal Transfusion (IUFT):** Indicated for severe fetal anemia (hematocrit <30% or hemoglobin deficit $\geq$ 2 g/dL) primarily due to Rh alloimmunization.
- **Exchange Transfusion:** Life-saving in newborns with severe Hemolytic Disease of the Fetus and Newborn (HDFN) to prevent kernicterus.
- **Neonatal Hemorrhagic Anemia:** Secondary to vasa previa, abruptio placentae, or twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome.

### **2. Appropriate Use of Blood and Blood Products**

Different clinical scenarios require specific blood components to optimize therapeutic efficacy and reduce volume overload risks.

- **Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC):**
    - _Characteristics:_ Most effective for improving oxygen-carrying capacity. One unit contains about 300 mL (250 mL RBC + 50 mL plasma). It contains minimal fibrinogen and no platelets.
    - _Effect:_ One unit raises hemoglobin by 1 g/dL and hematocrit by 3%.
    - _Shelf Life:_ 35 days. Must be ABO and Rh compatible.
- **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP):**
    - _Characteristics:_ Extracted from whole blood. Contains all clotting factors and fibrinogen. One unit is 250 mL.
    - _Use:_ Indicated for consumptive (DIC) and dilutional coagulopathy. Should not be used merely as a volume expander.
    - _Effect:_ One unit raises fibrinogen by 5-10 mg/dL.
- **Cryoprecipitate:**
    - _Characteristics:_ Thawed from FFP. Highly concentrated with fibrinogen, factor VIII, factor XIII, and von Willebrand's factor. Low volume (40 mL per unit).
    - _Effect:_ Each 10-15 mL contains 200 mg of fibrinogen. Preferred when rapid fibrinogen replacement is needed without volume overload.
- **Platelet Concentrates:**
    - _Characteristics:_ Single donor concentrates preferred. Shelf life is 5 days.
    - _Use:_ Indicated when platelet count is <50,000/mL with active bleeding. Transfused rapidly over 10 minutes.
    - _Effect:_ One unit (50 mL) raises the platelet count by 7,500/mL to 10,000/mL. Usually, 5-10 units are transfused at once.
- **Whole Blood:**
    - _Characteristics:_ Used less commonly today, but beneficial in acute massive hemorrhage to restore both volume and procoagulants.
    - _Effect:_ 500 mL of fresh blood raises blood volume, increases fibrinogen by roughly 12.5 mg/100 mL, and adds 10,000-15,000 platelets/mm³.
- **Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP):**
    - Used in severe PPH or hemorrhagic shock.
    - _Regimen:_ 5 units of PRBCs, 3 units of FFP, and 1 unit of platelet concentrate (Ratio 5:3:1). Cryoprecipitate is added if fibrinogen is <1 g/L.
- **Blood for Fetal/Neonatal Transfusion:**
    - _IUFT:_ Group 'O', Rh-negative packed cells (hematocrit 78-90%), cross-matched with mother, fresh, CMV negative, leukoreduced, and irradiated (25 Gy).
    - _Exchange Transfusion:_ Rh-negative whole blood (ABO identical to baby or Group O), fresh (<7 days old), cross-matched with maternal or infant serum. Volume is 160 mL/kg (double-volume exchange).

### **3. Complications of Blood Transfusion**

**A. Immune-Mediated Reactions**

- **Febrile non-hemolytic reactions** (most common).
- **Allergic and Anaphylactic reactions.**
- **Hemolytic transfusion reactions** (due to ABO/Rh incompatibility).
- **Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI):** An immune-mediated condition causing sudden pulmonary capillary permeability, leading to Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS).

**B. Transfusion-Related Infections**

- **Viral:** Hepatitis B (HBV), Hepatitis C (HCV), Cytomegalovirus (CMV), and Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV).
- **Parasitic:** Malaria.
- **Bacterial contamination** leading to septic shock.

**C. Other/Metabolic Complications**

- **Circulatory (Fluid) Overload:** Leading to congestive cardiac failure and pulmonary edema, especially in severely anemic or pre-eclamptic pregnant women whose myocardial reserve is compromised.
- **Hypothermia:** Due to massive transfusion of refrigerated blood.
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Hyperkalemia (from older stored blood) and hypocalcemia (due to citrate toxicity binding free calcium).
- **Acidosis**.

**D. Specific Complications in Fetus/Neonate**

- **Exchange Transfusion:** Air embolism, thrombosis, hypervolemia, RDS, cardiac arrhythmias, hypothermia, thrombocytopenia, coagulopathies, and necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC).
- **Intrauterine Transfusion:** Fetal injury, volume overload, preterm labor, and fetomaternal hemorrhage.

### **4. Management and Precautions**

**A. Pre-Transfusion Precautions**

- **Proper Cross-Matching:** Ensure exact ABO and Rh compatibility. Cross-match with maternal serum for intrauterine or exchange transfusions.
- **Component Therapy:** Strictly use PRBCs for chronic anemia to avoid circulatory overload; reserve FFP and platelets for specific coagulopathies.
- **Prophylactic Medications:**
    - Administer an antihistamine (e.g., Promethazine/Phenergan 25 mg IM) to prevent allergic reactions.
    - Administer a loop diuretic (e.g., Furosemide/Lasix 20-40 mg IM/IV) at least 2 hours prior to, or during, transfusion in severely anemic patients to prevent volume overload and cardiac failure.

**B. Intra-Transfusion Monitoring**

- **Drip Rate:** Should be extremely slow in severe anemia (about 10 drops per minute) to allow time for circulatory readjustment.
- **Vitals:** Closely observe pulse, respiration, blood pressure, and specifically listen for basal lung crepitations (early sign of pulmonary edema).
- **Volume Limits:** In chronic anemia, do not exceed 80-100 mL at a time, and do not repeat within 24 hours.

**C. Management of Complications**

- **Fluid Overload / Cardiac Failure:** Stop transfusion immediately. Administer high-flow oxygen, IV Furosemide (40-80 mg) to reduce preload, and place the patient in a propped-up position.
- **TRALI / ARDS:** Requires intensive care admission, endotracheal intubation, mechanical ventilation, and circulatory volume support.
- **Hemolytic Reaction:** Stop transfusion immediately. Maintain urine output with vigorous IV crystalloid hydration and loop diuretics to prevent acute kidney injury (acute tubular necrosis/cortical necrosis). Send blood/urine samples for investigations.
- **Metabolic Derangements (Massive Transfusion):** Administer 10% Calcium Gluconate IV slowly to treat citrate-induced hypocalcemia. Monitor ECG for hyperkalemia.
