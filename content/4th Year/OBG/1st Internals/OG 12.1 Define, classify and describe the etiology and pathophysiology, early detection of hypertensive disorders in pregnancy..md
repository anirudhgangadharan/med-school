### **1. DEFINITION OF HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS IN PREGNANCY**

Hypertension is one of the most common medical complications of pregnancy.

- **Pre-eclampsia (PE):** Defined as the new onset of hypertension and either proteinuria or other signs of end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks of gestation in a previously normotensive woman.
- **Diagnostic Criteria for Hypertension:** A systolic BP $\ge$ 140 mm Hg or a diastolic BP $\ge$ 90 mm Hg, measured two times at least 4 hours apart after 20 weeks of gestation.

### **2. CLASSIFICATION OF HYPERTENSION IN PREGNANCY (ACOG)**

According to the ACOG Task Force, hypertensive disorders in pregnancy are classified into four main categories:

1. **Gestational Hypertension (GH):** Hypertension developing after 20 weeks of gestation in a previously normotensive woman, with NO proteinuria and NO signs of end-organ damage.
2. **Pre-eclampsia (PE) and Eclampsia:**
    - _Pre-eclampsia:_ Gestational hypertension with proteinuria (urinary excretion of $\ge$ 0.3 g protein/24 hours, or dipstick $\ge$ 1+) or signs of end-organ dysfunction.
    - _Eclampsia:_ The occurrence of grand mal seizures and/or coma in a woman with pre-eclampsia.
3. **Chronic Hypertension (CH):** Systolic BP $\ge$ 140 mm Hg and/or diastolic BP $\ge$ 90 mm Hg present _before_ 20 weeks of gestation and persisting longer than 12 weeks postpartum.
4. **Superimposed Pre-eclampsia or Eclampsia:** The occurrence of new-onset proteinuria ($\ge$ 0.5 g/24 hours) or end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks in a woman with pre-existing chronic hypertension.

### **3. ETIOLOGY AND ETIOPATHOGENESIS**

While the exact etiology remains obscure, the underlying basic pathology is **endothelial dysfunction and intense vasospasm** resulting from abnormal placentation.

**Risk Factors**

- Primigravida (especially young or elderly).
- Placental abnormalities: Hyperplacentosis (molar pregnancy, multiple pregnancy, diabetes) or placental ischemia.
- Family history of PE, pre-existing vascular disease, chronic kidney disease, or obesity (BMI >35).
- Thrombophilias (e.g., antiphospholipid syndrome, factor V Leiden mutation).

**Mechanism of Abnormal Placentation**

- In a normal pregnancy, endovascular trophoblasts invade the myometrial segments of spiral arteries (between 16-18 weeks), replacing the musculoelastic media with fibrinoid material to create a low-resistance, high-flow system.
- In pre-eclampsia, there is a **failure of the second wave of endovascular trophoblast migration**. The myometrial segment retains its musculoelastic media and remains responsive to vasoconstrictor stimuli, leading to decreased uteroplacental blood supply and chronic placental hypoxia.

**Endothelial Dysfunction and Vasospasm** The chronic placental hypoxia releases factors into the maternal circulation, causing a systemic inflammatory response and widespread endothelial damage. This results in critical biochemical imbalances:

- **Imbalance of Vasomotor Agents:** There is an absolute or relative deficiency of vasodilators (Prostacyclin/PGI2, Nitric Oxide) and an increased synthesis of potent vasoconstrictors (Thromboxane/TXA2, Endothelin-1) and increased sensitivity to Angiotensin-II.
- **Imbalance of Angiogenic Factors:** The hypoxic trophoblast overproduces antiangiogenic proteins like **sFlt-1** (soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase 1) and **soluble endoglin**. sFlt-1 binds to and neutralizes circulating Placental Growth Factor (PlGF) and VEGF, inducing severe endothelial dysfunction and preventing placental angiogenesis.
- **Oxidative Stress:** Release of lipid peroxides, superoxide radicals, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) further damages the maternal endothelium.

### **4. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY AND ORGAN DYSFUNCTION**

The intense vasospasm and endothelial capillary leak result in multiorgan dysfunction:

- **Uteroplacental Bed:** Premature aging of the placenta, acute red and white infarcts, syncytial degeneration, and acute atherosis of spiral arteries with obliteration of the lumen. This leads to Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR), oligohydramnios, and placental abruption.
- **Renal System:** Renal vasospasm reduces the Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR). Endothelial swelling (glomerular endotheliosis) leads to proteinuria, oliguria, and elevated serum uric acid (>4.5 mg/dL) and creatinine levels. Salt and water retention cause generalized edema.
- **Hepatic System:** Periportal hemorrhagic necrosis and ischemia elevate liver enzymes (AST, ALT, LDH). Severe cases may develop subcapsular hematoma or HELLP syndrome (Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets).
- **Central Nervous System:** Vasospasm, ischemia, and increased capillary permeability cause cerebral edema, leading to headache, visual disturbances (cortical blindness, retinal detachment), Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES), and Eclamptic seizures.
- **Hematological and Cardiovascular Systems:** Endothelial leak causes hemoconcentration (raised hematocrit) and pulmonary edema. Activation of the coagulation cascade causes thrombocytopenia and Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).

### **5. EARLY DETECTION AND SCREENING (PREDICTION)**

Although pre-eclampsia cannot be completely prevented, early identification of high-risk women is crucial. No single test is entirely reliable, but a combination of clinical, biophysical, and biochemical markers is utilized.

- **Multimarker Screening (11-13 Weeks):** A combination of maternal factors, Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP), Uterine Artery Pulsatility Index (UtA-PI), and maternal serum Placental Growth Factor (PlGF) and PAPP-A.
- **Doppler Ultrasound:** The presence of a **diastolic notch** in the uterine artery at 24 weeks of gestation, or an elevated UtA-PI (>95th percentile), indicates high resistance in the uteroplacental circulation.
- **Biochemical Markers:** Increased maternal serum levels of antiangiogenic factors (sFlt-1) and decreased levels of angiogenic factors (PlGF, PAPP-A) predict the onset of endothelial dysfunction.
- **Renal Markers:** A rise in serum uric acid (hyperuricemia) or the appearance of microalbuminuria can predict the development of pre-eclampsia.
- **Roll Over Test (28-32 Weeks):** Blood pressure is measured in the lateral position and then in the supine position. An increase of 20 mm Hg in diastolic pressure from the side to the back position is considered a positive test.
- **Average MAP:** A Mean Arterial Pressure >90 mm Hg in the second trimester is highly predictive of future onset.
