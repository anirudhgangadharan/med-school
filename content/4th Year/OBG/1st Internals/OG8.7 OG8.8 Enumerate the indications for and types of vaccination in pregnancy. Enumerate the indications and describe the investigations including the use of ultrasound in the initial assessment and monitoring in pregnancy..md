### **1. Vaccination in Pregnancy**

**General Principles**

- The primary indication for vaccination in pregnancy is to protect the mother and confer passive immunity to the neonate.
- Live attenuated vaccines are strictly contraindicated during pregnancy because of the potential risk of teratogenicity and fetal infection. They should be administered either before or more than 4 weeks after pregnancy.

**Recommended Routine Vaccines**

- **Tetanus Toxoid (TT):** Immunization protects both mother and neonate against tetanus. In unprotected women, 0.5 mL TT is given intramuscularly (IM) for two doses at a 6-week interval, with the first dose given between 16 and 24 weeks. For previously immunized women, a single booster dose of 0.5 mL IM is given in the last trimester.
- **Tdap (Tetanus, Diphtheria, and Pertussis):** Recommended in each pregnancy between 27 and 36 weeks.
- **Influenza Vaccine:** The inactivated influenza vaccine is safe and recommended during the influenza season. The intranasal live virus vaccine must not be used.
- **COVID-19 Vaccine:** COVID vaccination in pregnancy is considered safe beyond 13 weeks (first trimester).

**Vaccines in Special/High-Risk Circumstances**

- **Hepatitis A and B:** Administered as in the non-pregnant state. Recombinant Hepatitis B vaccine is safe and recommended if the woman is not immune or exposed. Household contacts of Hepatitis A should receive immune prophylaxis (immunoglobulin and HAV vaccine).
- **Pneumococcal Vaccine:** Indicated only for pregnant women at a high risk of infections, such as those with heart disease, HIV infection, diabetes mellitus, or sickle cell disease.
- **Rabies Vaccine:** Given as in the non-pregnant state following exposure.

**Contraindicated Vaccines**

- **Live Viral Vaccines:** Measles, Mumps, Rubella (MMR), Varicella (Chickenpox), and Yellow fever vaccines are absolutely contraindicated.
- **Human Papillomavirus (HPV) Vaccine:** Not recommended during pregnancy.

### **2. Initial Assessment and Monitoring in Pregnancy**

**Objectives and Indications**

- To assess the health status of the mother and the fetus.
- To assess the exact fetal gestational age and obtain baseline investigations.
- To screen for 'high-risk' cases and detect/treat early complications.
- To organize continuous obstetric care, risk assessment, and provision of primary preventive health care.

**Routine Laboratory Investigations**

- **Blood:** Complete blood count (CBC), hematocrit, ABO and Rh grouping, blood glucose (fasting and postprandial), and VDRL for syphilis.
- **Urine:** Urinalysis for culture, and dipstick testing for protein and sugar at every antenatal visit.

**Special Investigations**

- **Serology:** Screening for Rubella, Hepatitis B Virus (HBV), and HIV (using an opt-out approach with consent).
- **Hemoglobinopathies:** Hemoglobin electrophoresis if the woman is at risk.
- **Prenatal Genetic Screening:** First-trimester combined test (at 11-14 weeks) utilizing ultrasound and biochemical markers (free $\beta$-hCG and PAPP-A) for aneuploidy screening.
- **Cervical Cytology:** Pap smear screening is done as a routine protocol to every woman attending the antenatal clinic, especially those not screened before.

**Use of Ultrasound in Initial Assessment** First-trimester Transvaginal Ultrasonography (TVS) is the most accurate method to calculate gestational age and evaluate early pregnancy.

- **Indications:** Confirming intrauterine pregnancy, ruling out ectopic or molar pregnancy, evaluating vaginal bleeding, screening for aneuploidy, and assessing pelvic masses.
- **Gestational Age & Dating:** A gestational sac is visible as early as 5 weeks, the yolk sac at 5.5 weeks, and the fetal pole with cardiac activity at 6 weeks. Measuring the Crown-Rump Length (CRL) between 7 and 12 weeks is the most accurate method for pregnancy dating (variation $\pm$ 5 days).
- **Fetal Number and Chorionicity:** Determines the number of fetuses and their chorionicity (e.g., detecting the T sign or lambda/twin peak sign).
- **Gross Fetal Anomalies:** Detecting early major structural anomalies (e.g., anencephaly).
- **Aneuploidy Screening:** Measuring Nuchal Translucency (NT) and assessing the nasal bone between 11 and 14 weeks.

**Antenatal Monitoring Protocol**

- **Visit Schedule:** Routine checkups are ideally done at 4-week intervals up to 28 weeks, 2-week intervals up to 36 weeks, and weekly thereafter until delivery.
- **Clinical Monitoring:** At every visit, maternal weight, pallor, blood pressure, and edema are recorded. Abdominal examination evaluates fundal height, fetal growth, fetal movements, lie, presentation, position, and fetal heart sounds.
- **Repeat Investigations:** Hemoglobin estimation is repeated at the 28th and 36th weeks. Screening for Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) is done at 24-28 weeks.
- **Mid-trimester Ultrasound:** A detailed fetal anatomy survey (anomaly scan) and placental localization are performed between 18 and 22 weeks.
- **Third-trimester Ultrasound:** Used at 36 weeks to verify fetal presentation, assess the fetal growth profile (BPD, HC, AC, FL), and measure the amniotic fluid volume.
