### **1. Definition of Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancy**

- **Pre-eclampsia (PE):** It is a progressive multisystem disorder defined as the new onset of hypertension and either proteinuria or other signs of end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks of gestation in a previously normotensive woman. (Note: It may rarely appear before 20 weeks in cases of hydatidiform mole and multiple pregnancy).
- **Gestational Hypertension (GH):** A sustained rise of blood pressure to 140/90 mm Hg or more on at least two occasions 4 or more hours apart, beyond the 20th week of pregnancy or within the first 48 hours of delivery, in a previously normotensive woman without proteinuria or end-organ damage.

### **2. Classification of Hypertensive Disorders**

According to the ACOG Task Force, hypertensive disorders in pregnancy are classified as follows:

- **Gestational Hypertension (GH):** Systolic BP $\ge$ 140 mm Hg or diastolic BP $\ge$ 90 mm Hg (or both) measured twice at least 4 hours apart after 20 weeks of gestation, with _no proteinuria_ and no signs of end-organ damage.
- **Pre-eclampsia (PE):** Gestational hypertension with proteinuria (urinary excretion of $\ge$ 0.3 g protein/24 hours, Urine protein-creatinine ratio >0.3 mg, or Dipstick $\ge$ 1+). It can also be diagnosed without proteinuria if there is new-onset end-organ dysfunction.
- **Eclampsia:** The occurrence of grand mal seizures (generalized tonic-clonic convulsions) and/or coma in a woman with pre-eclampsia.
- **HELLP Syndrome:** A severe form of pre-eclampsia characterized by **H**emolysis, **E**levated **L**iver enzymes, and **L**ow **P**latelet counts. It can be diagnosed without hypertension or proteinuria.
- **Chronic Hypertension:** Systolic BP $\ge$ 140 mm Hg and/or diastolic $\ge$ 90 mm Hg present _before_ 20 weeks of gestation and persisting longer than 12 weeks postpartum.
- **Superimposed Pre-eclampsia or Eclampsia:** Occurrence of new-onset proteinuria (>0.5 g/24 hours) or end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks of gestation in women with pre-existing chronic hypertension.

### **3. Etiology and Risk Factors**

The exact etiology remains obscure, but several high-risk and etiopathological factors predispose a woman to developing the disease:

- **Maternal Factors:** Primigravida (young or elderly), advanced maternal age, obesity (BMI >35 kg/m$^2$), and interpregnancy interval $\ge$ 7 years.
- **Genetic/Family History:** Family history of hypertension, pre-eclampsia, or diabetes mellitus. It is considered a polygenic disorder.
- **Placental Abnormalities:** Hyperplacentosis causing excessive exposure to chorionic villi (e.g., molar pregnancy, multiple pregnancy, diabetes) or placental ischemia.
- **Medical Conditions:** Pre-existing vascular disease, chronic kidney disease, auto-immune disorders (SLE), and thrombophilias (antiphospholipid syndrome, protein C/S deficiency, factor V Leiden).
- **Others:** New paternity, pregnancy following ART/IVF, and dietary deficiencies.

### **4. Pathogenesis (Etiopathogenesis)**

The basic pathology is **endothelial dysfunction and intense vasospasm** resulting from abnormal placentation.

- **Failure of Trophoblastic Invasion:** In a normal pregnancy, endovascular trophoblasts invade the spiral arterioles in two waves (10-12 weeks and 16-18 weeks), replacing the musculoelastic media with fibrinoid material and converting them into a low-resistance, high-flow system. In pre-eclampsia, the second wave of trophoblast migration fails. The spiral arteries retain their musculoelastic media and remain responsive to vasoconstrictor stimuli, leading to a reduction in blood supply to the fetoplacental unit (chronic placental hypoxia).
- **Imbalance of Vasodilators and Vasoconstrictors:**
    - _Prostaglandins:_ Relative or absolute deficiency of the vasodilator Prostacyclin (PGI$_2$) and increased synthesis of the potent vasoconstrictor Thromboxane (TXA$_2$).
    - _Nitric Oxide (NO):_ Deficiency of this potent vasodilator contributes to hypertension.
    - _Vascular Sensitivity:_ Increased vascular sensitivity to the pressor agent Angiotensin-II and increased synthesis of Endothelin-1 (a potent vasoconstrictor).
- **Imbalance of Angiogenic and Antiangiogenic Factors:** The hypoxic placenta overproduces antiangiogenic factors, namely soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFlt-1) and soluble endoglin. sFlt-1 binds and neutralizes Placental Growth Factor (PlGF) and Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF), leading to severe endothelial cell dysfunction.
- **Oxidative Stress and Inflammation:** Chronic placental hypoxia activates apoptotic pathways, releasing lipid peroxides, Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS), and superoxide radicals. Cytokines (TNF-$\alpha$, IL-6) from activated leukocytes further cause systemic inflammatory response and endothelial injury.

### **5. Pathophysiology and Net Effects**

The cascade of endothelial dysfunction and vasospasm leads to widespread end-organ damage:

- **Vasospasm:** Causes hypertension and multiorgan hypoperfusion.
    - _Uteroplacental bed:_ Leads to IUGR and placental abruption.
    - _Kidneys:_ Glomerular endothelial damage causes oliguria and proteinuria.
    - _Liver:_ Ischemia leads to HELLP syndrome, necrosis, and subcapsular hematoma.
    - _Brain:_ Causes seizures (eclampsia) and Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES).
- **Endothelial Dysfunction (Capillary Leak):** Results in ascites, generalized edema, proteinuria, pleural effusion, and pulmonary edema.
- **Activation of Coagulation System:** Leads to microangiopathic hemolysis, thrombocytopenia, and Disseminated Intravascular Coagulopathy (DIC).

### **6. Early Detection (Screening and Prediction)**

Pre-eclampsia is not totally preventable, but early detection of high-risk factors and prediction tests can guide timely intervention.

- **Multimarker Screening (First Trimester - 11 to 13 weeks):** The most effective predictive model combines maternal factors with Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP), Uterine Artery Pulsatility Index (UtA-PI), maternal serum Pregnancy Associated Plasma Protein-A (PAPP-A), and Placental Growth Factor (PLGF). This combined screening detects a high percentage of preterm PE cases.
- **Doppler Ultrasound:** The presence of a "diastolic notch" in the uterine artery at 24 weeks of gestation indicates high blood flow resistance and predicts the possible development of IUGR and pre-eclampsia.
- **Biomarkers:**
    - Increased maternal serum levels of sFlt-1 and decreased PLGF strongly predict the onset of endothelial dysfunction.
    - Other markers under study include increased levels of leptin, TNF-$\alpha$, homocysteine, and detection of cell-free fetal DNA (ff-DNA) in early maternal plasma.
- **Clinical and Renal Markers:**
    - Development of microalbuminuria and hyperuricemia (rise in serum uric acid).
    - An average Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) >90 mm Hg in the second trimester.
    - _Roll-over Test:_ Measured between 28 and 32 weeks. An increase of 20 mm Hg in diastolic pressure when the patient rolls from her side to her back (supine position) indicates a positive test and higher risk for developing hypertension.
