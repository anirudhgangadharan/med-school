### **1. Birth Rate**

- **Concept:** While a specific definition of birth rate is broadly related to population dynamics, the aim of family welfare planning is to bring down the birth rate to a realistic minimum to ensure a better standard of living.
- **Parameters:** In India, the Total Fertility Rate (TFR) is currently 2.4, and the national target is to reduce it to 2.0. Slowing population growth conserves resources, improves health, and reduces maternal and infant mortality rates.

### **2. Maternal Mortality**

- **Definition:** Death of a woman while pregnant or within 42 days of the termination of pregnancy, irrespective of the duration and the site of pregnancy, from any cause related to or aggravated by the pregnancy or its management, but not from accidental or incidental causes.
- **Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR):** Expressed as the number of maternal deaths per 100,000 live births. In India, the MMR has significantly declined to 99 per 100,000 live births (2020). The Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) target is to reduce global MMR to <70/100,000 live births by 2030.
- **Maternal Mortality Rate:** Indicates the number of maternal deaths divided by the number of women of reproductive age (15-49 years), expressed per 100,000 women per year.

**Classifications and Causes of Maternal Death:**

1. **Direct Obstetric Deaths (75%):** Resulting from complications of pregnancy, delivery, or management.
    - Hemorrhage (20-25%)
    - Hypertension / Eclampsia (15-20%)
    - Sepsis (11-15%)
    - Unsafe abortion (8-13%)
    - Obstructed labor (8-9%)
2. **Indirect Obstetric Deaths (25%):** Due to pre-existing conditions aggravated by pregnancy (e.g., anemia, cardiac disease, viral hepatitis). Anemia is the most important single indirect cause in developing countries.
3. **Non-obstetric (Fortuitous) Deaths:** Due to incidental causes like accidents.

**The Three Delays Model:**

- _Delay-I:_ Delay in recognizing the problem and deciding to seek care.
- _Delay-II:_ Delay in reaching the health facility.
- _Delay-III:_ Delay in receiving treatment at the center.

### **3. Maternal Morbidity**

- **Definition:** Obstetric morbidity originates from any cause related to pregnancy, childbirth, or puerperium (usually up to 42 days after confinement). It is estimated that for every 1 maternal death, at least 15 more women suffer from severe morbidities.
- **Parameters of Morbidity:** Fever >100.4°F (>24 hours), BP >140/90 mm Hg, recurrent vaginal bleeding, Hb <10.5 g/dL, and asymptomatic bacteriuria.
- **Classifications:**
    - _Direct - Temporary:_ e.g., APH, PPH, eclampsia, obstructed labor.
    - _Direct - Chronic (Permanent):_ e.g., Vesicovaginal fistula (VVF), rectovaginal fistula (RVF), uterine prolapse, Sheehan's syndrome.
    - _Indirect:_ Aggravation of pre-existing diseases like malaria, hepatitis, or tuberculosis.
- **Maternal Near Miss (SAMM):** A woman who experienced a severe life-threatening complication but survived during pregnancy, childbirth, or within 42 days postpartum.

### **4. Perinatal Mortality and Morbidity**

- **Perinatal Mortality Definition:** Deaths among fetuses weighing 1000 g or more at birth (28 weeks of gestation) who die before or during delivery or within the first 7 days of delivery. It is expressed as deaths per 1000 total births. (Note: WHO limit of viability is 500 g / 22 weeks, but 1000 g / 28 weeks is used for international LMIC comparisons).
- **Incidence:** The rate is much higher in developing countries (e.g., 26/1000 in India) compared to <10/1000 in developed nations.
- **Causes of Perinatal Mortality:**
    - Infection (33%)
    - Birth asphyxia and trauma (28%)
    - Preterm birth and/or LBW (24%)
    - Congenital malformations (15%)
- **Antepartum fetal death causes:** Chronic hypoxia (30%), pregnancy complications (30%), congenital malformations (15%), infections (5%), unexplained (20%).
- **Perinatal Morbidity:** Implies major illness of the neonate from birth to the first 4 weeks of life. Important causes are prematurity/LBW, birth asphyxia, trauma, and congenital malformations.

### **5. Neonatal Mortality**

- **Definition:** Death of the infant within 28 days after birth. Neonatal Mortality Rate is the number of such deaths per 1000 live births. The SDG target is to reduce neonatal mortality to 12/1000 live births by 2030.
- **Causes:** About two-thirds of neonatal deaths are related to prematurity. Specific causes include:
    - Prematurity and LBW (48.1%)
    - Birth asphyxia and trauma (12.9%)
    - Neonatal pneumonia (12.0%)
    - Sepsis (5.4%)
    - Congenital malformation (4.0%)

### **6. Perinatal and Neonatal Mortality and Morbidity Audit**

- **Definition and Purpose:** An audit is a systematic critical analysis of the quality of medical care. Maternal mortality conferences and audits evaluate the causes of death and avoidable factors to improve clinical care.
- **Tools for Data Collection:** Include the Confidential Enquiry into Maternal Death (CEMD) and Maternal Deaths Surveillance and Response (MDSR).
- **Structuring an Audit:**
    - Must be structured beforehand based on available resources, personnel, and motivation.
    - Requires proper documentation and confidentiality; it serves as an educational tool.
    - _Audit Cycle:_ Analyze the existing practice -> Compare with the standard (best practice) -> Identify problems -> Implement change.
- **Perinatal Audit:** Continued study of perinatal mortality problems relies on demographic studies, regular interdepartmental clinical meetings, autopsy studies of perinatal deaths, and pathological research.

### **7. Stillbirth**

- **Definition:** A stillbirth is the birth of a newborn after the 28th completed week (weighing 1000 g or more) who does not breathe or show any sign of life after delivery. It includes antepartum deaths (macerated) and intrapartum deaths (fresh stillbirths).
- **Rate:** Expressed as the number of such deaths per 1000 total births. The risk significantly increases in post-term pregnancies (from 0.4/1000 at 37 weeks to 11.5/1000 at 43 weeks).
- **Causes:**
    - Birth asphyxia and trauma (30%)
    - Pregnancy complications such as placental abruption, hypertension, diabetes (30%)
    - Others, including congenital malformations and unexplained causes (40%).

### **8. Abortion (Miscarriage)**

- **Definition:** Expulsion or extraction of an embryo or fetus weighing 500 g or less from its mother, independent of whether the cord has been cut or the placenta is attached. This weight corresponds approximately to 22 weeks (154 days) of gestation. The term "miscarriage" is recommended for spontaneous abortion.
- **Incidence:** Around 10-20% of clinical pregnancies end in miscarriage, with 75% occurring before the 16th week.
- **Classification:**
    - _Spontaneous:_ Isolated (sporadic) or Recurrent.
    - _Clinical types of spontaneous abortion:_ Threatened, Inevitable, Complete, Incomplete, Missed, and Septic.
    - _Induced:_ Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) for therapeutic, eugenic, humanitarian, or social indications.
- **Etiology:**
    - _First Trimester (most common):_ Genetic factors/chromosomal abnormalities (50%), endocrine disorders (luteal phase defect, thyroid issues, diabetes), immunological disorders, infection, and unexplained causes.
    - _Second Trimester:_ Anatomic abnormalities (cervical incompetence, bicornuate/septate uterus, uterine fibroids, synechiae), maternal medical illnesses, inherited thrombophilias, and unexplained factors.
