### **1. CHANGES IN THE GENITAL TRACT**

**Vulva and Vagina**

- **Vulva:** Becomes edematous and more vascular, with superficial varicosities often appearing in multiparae. The labia minora become pigmented and hypertrophied.
- **Vagina:** The walls become hypertrophied, edematous, and highly vascular, leading to a bluish coloration of the mucosa known as Jacquemier's or Chadwick's sign. The anterior vaginal wall increases in length.
- **Vaginal Secretion and Cytology:** Secretions become copious, thin, and curdy white, with an acidic pH (3.5–6.0) resulting from _Lactobacillus acidophilus_ converting glycogen into lactic acid under high estrogen levels. This acidic pH prevents the multiplication of pathogenic organisms. Cytology shows a preponderance of navicular cells in clusters.

**Uterus (Body and Isthmus)**

- **Growth and Capacity:** The nonpregnant uterus weighs 60 g with a 5-10 mL capacity; at term, it weighs 900-1,000 g, measures 35 cm in length, and its capacity increases by 500-1,000 times.
- **Muscle Changes:** Hypertrophy and hyperplasia of muscle fibers occur early under estrogen and progesterone influence, while stretching dominates beyond 20 weeks. Muscle fibers are arranged in outer longitudinal, inner circular, and intermediate interlacing layers (forming the "living ligature" that occludes blood vessels to prevent hemorrhage postpartum).
- **Shape and Position:** The shape changes from pyriform to globular by 12 weeks, back to ovoid by 28 weeks, and becomes spherical beyond 36 weeks. As it enlarges, the uterus usually rotates on its long axis to the right, known as dextrorotation.
- **Braxton-Hicks Contractions:** Spontaneous, irregular, infrequent, spasmodic, and painless contractions occur without affecting cervical dilatation.
- **Isthmus:** Hypertrophies and elongates to 3 times its original length in the first trimester. Beyond 12 weeks, it unfolds and is incorporated into the uterine cavity to form the lower uterine segment.

**Cervix**

- **Consistency and Color:** Marked softening (Goodell's sign) is evident as early as 6 weeks. It takes on a bluish discoloration due to increased vascularity.
- **Hypertrophy:** There is marked hypertrophy and hyperplasia of the glands, which occupy about half the bulk of the cervix, along with hypertrophy of elastic and connective tissues.
- **Secretions:** A copious, tenacious mucous plug seals the cervical canal. This mucus is rich in Immunoglobulin G (IgG) and IgA.

**Fallopian Tubes and Ovaries**

- **Fallopian Tubes:** The tube becomes placed almost vertically, congested, and its muscles undergo hypertrophy.
- **Ovaries:** The corpus luteum reaches its maximum growth and function at the 8th week (measuring about 2.5 cm) and secretes estrogen and progesterone to maintain the early pregnancy. The normal ovarian and uterine cycles remain suspended.

### **2. CHANGES IN THE CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM**

**Anatomical Changes and Heart Sounds**

- **Displacement:** Elevation of the diaphragm pushes the heart upward and outward with a slight rotation to the left. The apex beat shifts to the 4th intercostal space, about 2.5 cm outside the midclavicular line.
- **Murmurs:** A systolic murmur may be audible in the apical or pulmonary area due to decreased blood viscosity and torsion of the great vessels. A continuous hissing 'mammary murmur' may be heard over the tricuspid area due to increased blood flow to the breasts.
- **ECG Changes:** Left axis deviation, Q waves in leads II, III, and aVF, and flat or inverted T-waves in leads III and V1-V3 are noted.

**Cardiac Output (CO)**

- **Increase:** CO increases by 40-50%, starting from the 5th week and peaking at 30-34 weeks. It remains static until term when measured in the lateral recumbent position.
- **Labor and Postpartum:** CO increases further during labor (+50%) and immediately following delivery (+70%) due to autotransfusion of blood from the contracting uterus into the systemic circulation.
- **Determinants:** The rise is chiefly affected by an increase in stroke volume (+27%) and heart rate (+17% or an increase of about 15 beats per minute).

**Blood Pressure (BP) and Venous Pressure**

- **Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR):** SVR decreases by 21% due to the smooth muscle relaxing effects of progesterone, nitric oxide, and prostaglandins.
- **Blood Pressure:** Despite increased CO, the overall maternal BP decreases because of the fall in SVR. There is a mid-pregnancy drop in diastolic pressure and Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) by 5-10 mm Hg.
- **Venous Pressure:** Femoral venous pressure is markedly raised (from 8-10 cm water to 25 cm lying down, and 80-100 cm standing) due to the gravid uterus compressing the common iliac veins. This causes physiological edema and predisposes to varicose veins and deep vein thrombosis.

**Supine Hypotension Syndrome**

- In late pregnancy, the gravid uterus compresses the inferior vena cava in the supine position. In about 10% of cases, collateral circulation fails to compensate, leading to severe hypotension, tachycardia, and syncope. Normal BP is quickly restored by turning the patient to the lateral position.

**Regional Blood Flow**

- **Uterus:** Uterine blood flow increases from 50 mL/min to about 750 mL/min near term (accounting for about 15% of total CO).
- **Other Organs:** Pulmonary blood flow increases by 2,500 mL/min, renal blood flow increases by 400 mL/min, and skin/mucous membrane blood flow increases to a maximum of 500 mL/min.

### **3. CHANGES IN THE RESPIRATORY SYSTEM**

**Anatomical Changes**

- **Chest Expansion:** The diaphragm is elevated by 4 cm. The subcostal angle increases from 68° to 103°, the transverse diameter of the chest expands by 2 cm, and the chest circumference increases by 5-7 cm.
- **Diaphragmatic Breathing:** Diaphragmatic excursion increases by 1-2 cm, transitioning the primary mode of respiration to diaphragmatic breathing.
- **Mucosa:** The mucosa of the nasopharynx becomes hyperemic and edematous, occasionally causing nasal stuffiness and epistaxis.

**Lung Volumes and Capacities**

- **Decreased Volumes:** Total Lung Capacity (TLC) is reduced by 5%, and Residual Volume (RV) is diminished by 20%. Functional Residual Capacity (FRC) decreases by 20%.
- **Increased Volumes:** Tidal Volume (TV) increases by 40% (from 500 mL to 700 mL), and Inspiratory Capacity (IC) increases by 10%. Vital Capacity (VC) remains practically unaltered.

**Ventilation and Acid-Base Balance**

- **Hyperventilation:** The respiratory rate remains unchanged (15/min), but the Minute Ventilation increases by 40% (7.5 to 10.5 L/min) due to the increased tidal volume. This state of hyperventilation is driven by progesterone acting on the respiratory center.
- **Gas Exchange:** Arterial pCO2 falls from 40 mm Hg to 30-32 mm Hg, while arterial pO2 rises from 95 mm Hg to 105-106 mm Hg. This gradient facilitates the transfer of CO2 from the fetus to the mother, and O2 from the mother to the fetus.
- **Acid-Base Balance:** Pregnancy is a state of compensated respiratory alkalosis. The pH rises slightly (7.40 to 7.42). Partial renal compensation occurs through the increased excretion of bicarbonate, resulting in a decreased plasma HCO3- level (26 to 22 mmol/L).
