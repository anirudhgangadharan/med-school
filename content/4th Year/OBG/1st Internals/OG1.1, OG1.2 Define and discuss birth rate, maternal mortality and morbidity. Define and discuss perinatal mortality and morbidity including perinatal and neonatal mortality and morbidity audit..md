### **1. Birth Rate**

- **Concept:** Controlling conception and bringing the birth rate down to a realistic minimum during a given period is the primary objective of family planning.
- **Goals:** Slowing population growth helps prevent pregnancies that are too early, too frequent, or too many, thereby significantly reducing maternal and infant mortality rates. The Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) and National Population Policy target a Total Fertility Rate (TFR) of <2.1 by 2030.

### **2. Maternal Mortality**

**Definition and Ratios**

- **Maternal Death:** Death of a woman while pregnant or within 42 days of the termination of pregnancy, irrespective of the duration and the site of pregnancy, from any cause related to or aggravated by the pregnancy or its management, but not from accidental or incidental causes.
- **Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR):** Expressed as the number of maternal deaths per 100,000 live births. In India, the MMR has declined to 99 per 100,000 live births (2020),. The global SDG target aims to reduce the MMR to less than 70 per 100,000 live births by 2030,.
- **Maternal Mortality Rate:** The number of maternal deaths divided by the number of women of reproductive age (15-49 years), expressed per 100,000 women per year.

**Causes of Maternal Mortality**

1. **Direct Obstetric Deaths (75%):** Resulting from complications of pregnancy, delivery, or their management.
    - Hemorrhage (20-25%),.
    - Hypertension / Eclampsia (15-20%).
    - Sepsis / Infection (11-15%),.
    - Unsafe abortion (8-13%),.
    - Obstructed labor (8-9%),.
2. **Indirect Obstetric Deaths (25%):** Resulting from pre-existing conditions aggravated by the physiological effects of pregnancy. Anemia is the most important single indirect cause in developing countries (15-20%),,. Other causes include cardiac disease and viral hepatitis,.
3. **Non-obstetric (Fortuitous) Deaths:** Due to incidental causes like accidents or infectious diseases like typhoid.

**Factors and The "Three Delays"** Maternal mortality reflects social disadvantage, often stemming from ignorance, poor nutrition, un-regulated fertility, and inadequate maternity services,,. The Government of India identifies "Three Delays" that increase maternal deaths:

- _Delay-I:_ Delay in recognizing the problem and deciding to seek care (lack of birth preparedness).
- _Delay-II:_ Delay in reaching the health facility due to poor transport or referral mechanisms.
- _Delay-III:_ Delay in receiving treatment at the center due to lack of trained personnel, supplies, or facility readiness.

### **3. Maternal Morbidity and Maternal Near Miss**

**Definition and Parameters**

- **Definition:** Obstetric morbidity originates from any cause related to pregnancy, childbirth, or puerperium, usually up to 42 days after confinement. For every 1 maternal death, it is estimated that at least 15-16 women suffer from severe morbidities,,.
- **Parameters:** Fever >100.4°F (>24 hours), Blood pressure >140/90 mm Hg, recurrent vaginal bleeding, Hemoglobin <10.5 g/dL, and asymptomatic bacteriuria.

**Classification of Obstetric Morbidity**

1. **Direct - Temporary:** Conditions like Antepartum Hemorrhage (APH), Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH), eclampsia, obstructed labor, rupture of the uterus, ectopic or molar pregnancy, and sepsis.
2. **Direct - Chronic (Permanent):** Long-term sequelae including Vesicovaginal Fistula (VVF), Rectovaginal Fistula (RVF), dyspareunia, uterine prolapse, secondary infertility, obstetric palsy, and Sheehan's syndrome.
3. **Indirect:** Expressions of pre-existing diseases aggravated by pregnancy, such as malaria, hepatitis, tuberculosis, and anemia.

**Maternal Near Miss (SAMM)**

- A "Maternal Near Miss" or Severe Acute Maternal Morbidity (SAMM) is defined as a woman who nearly died but survived a severe complication during pregnancy, childbirth, or within 42 days of termination.
- Clinical criteria include acute cyanosis, loss of consciousness >12 hours, gasping, and Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).

### **4. Perinatal Mortality and Morbidity**

**Definition of Perinatal Mortality**

- Perinatal mortality refers to deaths among fetuses weighing 1000 g or more at birth (28 weeks of gestation) who die before or during delivery, or within the first 7 days of delivery.
- It is expressed as deaths per 1000 total births (live and stillbirths),. The rate in India is approximately 26/1000.

**Causes and Predisposing Factors**

- **Important Causes:** Infection (33%), Birth asphyxia and trauma (28%), Preterm birth/LBW (24%), and Congenital malformations (15%).
- **Antepartum Fetal Deaths:** Primarily caused by chronic hypoxia (30%), pregnancy complications such as placental abruption or hypertension (30%), congenital malformations (15%), infections (5%), and unexplained causes (20%),.
- **Predisposing Factors:** Extremes of maternal age, high parity, poor nutrition, medical disorders (anemia, diabetes), obstetric complications (APH, PROM), and fetoplacental factors (twins, Fetal Growth Restriction),,.

**Perinatal Morbidity**

- Implies major illness of the neonate from birth up to the first 4 weeks of life.
- Important causes include prematurity and low birth weight, birth asphyxia and birth trauma, and congenital malformations.

### **5. Neonatal Mortality**

- **Definition:** Death of an infant within the first 28 days of life. The Neonatal Mortality Rate is expressed per 1000 live births. The SDG target is to reduce neonatal mortality to 12 per 1000 live births by 2030,,.
- **Causes:** The vast majority of deaths occur within the first 48 hours. Two-thirds of neonatal deaths are related to prematurity. Specific causes include:
    - Prematurity and low birth weight (48.1%).
    - Birth asphyxia and trauma (12.9%).
    - Neonatal pneumonia (12.0%).
    - Sepsis (5.4%).
    - Congenital malformation (4.0%).

### **6. Perinatal, Neonatal, and Maternal Mortality and Morbidity Audit**

**Definition and Purpose of Audit**

- An audit (clinical review) is an effective tool used to evaluate medical practice and indicate where change is essential for betterment.
- In obstetrics, audits scrutinize the work done by health professionals to detect avoidable factors in maternal and perinatal deaths, aiming to improve the quality of future care,.

**Structuring an Audit**

- An audit must be structured based on available personnel, finance, and resources.
- It requires setting a standard (best practice), establishing indicators (e.g., BP measurement timings), and setting targets to include maximum patient numbers,.
- Proper documentation, strict confidentiality, and treating the audit as an educational tool rather than a punitive one are essential.

**Perinatal and Maternal Audit Tools**

- **Perinatal Audit:** Continued study of perinatal mortality involves demographic tracking, regular interdepartmental clinical meetings, pathological research, and routine autopsy studies of all perinatal deaths.
- **Maternal Audit:** Relies on robust tools for quality data collection to identify trends and avert future deaths. These include the Confidential Enquiry into Maternal Death (CEMD) and Maternal Deaths Surveillance and Response (MDSR) programs,. Maternal mortality conferences evaluate exact causes of death and highlight preventable factors, ensuring continuous medical education for obstetric staff.
